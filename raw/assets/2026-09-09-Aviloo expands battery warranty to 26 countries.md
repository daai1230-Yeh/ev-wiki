# Aviloo expands battery warranty to 26 countries

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-09-08 12:30

---

Aviloo now offers its battery warranty for used electric cars in 26 European countries. The free coverage is linked to a qualifying flash battery test by the Austrian company.

Aviloo's battery warranty was initially launched in June 2026 in France and Sweden, soon after adding countries such as Germany, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, Austria, Finland, Belgium, Switzerland, Ireland, Norway, and Denmark. Since September, according to Aviloo, the battery warranty has also been available in the Czech Republic, Spain, Italy, Portugal, Slovakia, Iceland, Cyprus, Greece, Bulgaria, Slovenia, Luxembourg, Hungary, Poland, and Liechtenstein. The warranty is directly linked to Aviloo's Flash Test. Any test that meets the required criteria automatically receives the battery warranty at no additional cost, the company states.

## Battery warranty is linked to flash test

Using the Flash Test, Aviloo determines the State of Health (SoH) of the high-voltage battery via the OBD interface. The service targets car dealerships, used car dealers, and leasing companies, and is designed to assess the condition of a used electric vehicle's battery independently of the manufacturer's status display. In early September, Aviloo emphasised in its own analysis that age and mileage alone only provide limited insights into battery health. For the first Aviloo Certified EV Battery Report, the company evaluated more than 500,000 tests from 20 electric vehicle models, according to its own statements. The results were generally positive, finding that, after 150,000 kilometres, the median SoH of the models examined ranged between 87 and 94 per cent.

At the same time, the analysis revealed significant differences between individual vehicles of the same model. For example, with the Hyundai Ioniq 5 featuring a 72.6-kWh battery, the median SoH after 150,000 kilometres was 92.8 per cent, but the range between individual vehicles exceeded twelve percentage points. For other models, the variation was even greater.

These results thus provide the argumentative basis for Aviloo's business model. An individual battery test is intended to provide more insight into the condition of a used electric vehicle than age and mileage alone. However, it is important to note that the underlying data comes from Aviloo itself, and the company is commercially active in battery diagnostics.

The now-expanded warranty takes a step further by combining Aviloo's condition check with battery protection. For the used car trade in particular, such a combination could help reduce uncertainties about the condition of the vehicle's most expensive component. Based on the information published so far, it is not yet possible to assess the extent of this protection, for which the specific warranty terms would be decisive.
