# Kia launches Carens EV in Indonesia as its first locally built electric model

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 2026-11-17

---

A year after introducing the Carens Clavis EV in India, Kia has launched the model in Indonesia, where it is simply called the Carens EV. The three-row MPV is the company's first locally assembled electric model in the country.

Kia does not own a factory in Indonesia and has teamed up with Hyundai to localise the Carens EV. The company imports CKD kits from its factory in Anantapur, Andhra Pradesh, India, and supplies them to Hyundai, which assembles the vehicles at its plant in Cikarang, West Java.

Fransiscus Soerjopranoto, COO of Hyundai’s Indonesian subsidiary, said that the company’s local factory is operating at around half of its installed capacity of 150,000 units. Assembling Kia’s Carens EV, along with a corporate cousin called Neira, scheduled to debut on 20 November, will help the company improve capacity utilisation, he added. Kia says the Carens EV has reached a local content level of 80 per cent, well ahead of the Indonesian government’s 2030 deadline.

Indonesian customers can choose between standard- and long-range versions of the Carens EV. Both feature a single permanent magnet synchronous motor driving the front wheels and are available with six or seven seats. In the standard-range version, the motor produces 134 PS (99 kW) and 255 Nm of torque and draws power from a 42 kWh NMC battery pack. The long-range version increases the motor’s output to 171 PS (126 kW), while torque remains unchanged. It also comes with a larger 51.4 kWh NMC battery pack.

HLI Green Power, a joint venture between Hyundai Motor Group and LG Energy Solution, manufactures the battery cells for the Carens EV and supplies them to Hyundai Energy Indonesia (HEI), which assembles them into packs. The standard-range version with the 42 kWh battery pack can travel up to 404 kilometres on a single charge, while the long-range version with the 51.4 kWh battery pack offers a range of up to 490 kilometres. Both figures are based on the Modified Indian Driving Cycle (MIDC). Customers can charge both battery packs at up to 11 kW using an AC charger or at up to 100 kW via a DC charger.

On-road prices in Jakarta start at 379 million rupiah (approximately €18,800) for the standard-range version and 422 million rupiah (approximately €20,900) for the long-range version. Commenting on the launch of the new electric MPV, Kia Sales Indonesia CEO Jong Sung Park said: “Carens has been present in Indonesia since 2000 as one of Kia’s MPV choices for families, spanning a total of six generations. Today, we are introducing its electric version. For families beginning to consider an electric vehicle, we want them to be able to make the switch through a model they already know.”
