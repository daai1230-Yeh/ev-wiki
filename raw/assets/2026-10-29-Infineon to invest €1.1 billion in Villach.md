# Infineon to invest €1.1 billion in Villach

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-10-28

---

Villach, Austria is one of the most important locations for Infineon. The market leader in chip manufacturing has now established the world's first high-volume production of GaN power semiconductors on larger 300-mm wafers at its Austrian location. Production is set to start in 2027. By 2034, around 1.1 billion euros are expected to flow into the existing infrastructure in Villach.

Infineon achieved a breakthrough in the production of gallium nitride (GaN) power semiconductors in 2024. The transition from 200- to 300-millimetre wafers boosted production volume by a factor of 2.3 and made high-volume manufacturing possible. Infineon plans to invest around €1.1 billion in Villach by 2034, utilising and modernising existing cleanroom facilities. Infineon Austria currently employs around 5,800 people, approximately 4,800 of whom work at the plant.

"By establishing high-volume manufacturing on 300-millimetre wafers, we will be the first company in the world to bring gallium nitride power electronics into volume production," said Jochen Hanebeck, CEO of Infineon Technologies AG.

High-volume production is scheduled for the end of 2027, serving AI data centres, electric vehicles, renewable energy, robotics and consumer electronics. Industry analysts forecast annual growth of around 35% for GaN power semiconductors between 2025 and 2031, reaching approximately US$3.5 billion. GaN switches and converts electricity far more efficiently than conventional silicon.

Austrian Chancellor Christian Stocker called it one of the largest single investments in the history of Austria's high-tech industry. The project is supported by the European Chips Act; the European Commission has already approved state aid totalling €319 million, with formal funding approval from the Austrian Ministry of Economic Affairs expected to follow.

Austrian Economic Minister Wolfgang Hattmannsdorfer noted that, statistically, every job in the chip industry can provide six additional jobs across the broader economy. Sabine Herlitschka, CEO of Infineon Technologies Austria AG, stressed the link between research and industrial value creation and Europe's competitiveness and strategic autonomy in microelectronics.
