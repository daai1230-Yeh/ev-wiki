# Xiaomi SkyNomad unveils range extender SUVs for China

**Author**: Kasimir Hupe | **Date**: 06.08.2026

---

Xiaomi now has range-extender (EREV) vehicles in its portfolio. On July 30 the company unveiled its second vehicle series, SkyNomad, in Beijing — two large SUVs now available for pre-order. Chinese media estimate reservations have already surpassed 100,000.

**Pricing**: The seven-seater SkyNomad N90 Max is priced at 299,900 yuan (~€39,200), while the five-seater N70 Max is priced at 259,900 yuan (~€34,000). Both are top "Max" trims; "Pro" and base variants follow at the official September launch. The N90 Max undercuts Tesla's six-seat Model Y L (339,900 yuan in China); the N70 Max is close to Li Auto's new Li L6 (249,800 yuan).

**Background**: Reported in May under the codename Kunlun N3, both SUVs are built on Xiaomi's Kunlun platform, developed over three and a half years since early 2023.

**How EREVs work**: Propulsion is always electric; if the battery depletes, a compact combustion engine kicks in solely as a generator, never driving the wheels directly — convenient for buyers without home charging, though a bridging technology. Xiaomi opted for unusually large batteries to offset the typical EREV drawbacks.

**N70 Max specs**: 52 kWh LFP or 76 kWh NCM battery options, up to 505 km electric range (CLTC), 1,705 km combined range on the N90; combined output 310 kW (416 hp) dual motor AWD; depleted-battery fuel consumption 6.26 L/100km (WLTC) plus 15.85 kWh electric. 0-100 km/h in 5.9 seconds for the N90.

**N90 Max (flagship)**: 5.28 m long, 1.99 m wide, 1.82 m tall, 3.08 m wheelbase, drag coefficient 0.255. Two motors, 310 kW combined, 0–100 km/h in 5.9s, top speed 190 km/h. 76 kWh CALB ternary battery, 464 km CLTC electric range, 1,705 km combined range. 15-minute charge adds 285 km; 20–80% in 18 minutes. Suspension shared with SU7/YU7 (double wishbone front, multi-link rear, 5-level air suspension).

Interior: 2+2+3 seven-seat layout, up to 1.55 m second-row legroom, four zero-gravity seats, rotating front seats, flat sleeping area of 2.30 m when configured, 1,831-litre boot (all seats up), 34 storage compartments, 9-litre fridge, 25 speakers. Safety: 90.4% high-strength steel/aluminium body, 12 airbags standard, 750mm wading depth. Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 cockpit chip, Nvidia Drive Thor (700 TOPS) for ADAS, front + rear-facing LiDAR.

**N70 Max**: 4.96 m long, 1.99 m wide, 1.76 m tall, 2.95 m wheelbase — about 32cm shorter than the N90, five seats instead of seven, priced ~40,000 yuan (~€5,200) lower. Offers longer electric range than the N90 (up to 505 km CLTC / 380 km WLTC on the larger battery) — Xiaomi claims best-in-class. Dual motor AWD (210 kW front + 100 kW rear = 310 kW combined). No rear LiDAR or third row, but shares the flat floor, long seat rails, and fridge storage unit.

**Demand**: Xiaomi's development-inside-out design freed up cabin space (1.26m front overhang housing five drivetrain components, extra 14.5cm rear interior space, lowered floor). Chinese portal Cailian counted 200–300 deposits per outlet across sampled stores, suggesting 100,000+ reservations system-wide (unofficial estimate); 4,800 deposits in Beijing alone on Aug 2, 30,000+ across Beijing-Tianjin region.

**Targets vs. reality**: Xiaomi targets 550,000 deliveries for 2026 but managed only ~220,000 from January–July (SU7 + YU7 combined ~30,000/month). Meeting the annual target would require 60,000+ cars/month for the rest of the year, even as China's EREV segment shrank ~15% YoY in H1 (450,000 units, per CPCA). CEO Lei Jun believes the market remains far from saturated.

Production is underway at Xiaomi's Beijing plant phase two; the N90 debuts publicly at the Chengdu Auto Show on August 21. Key suppliers: Harbin Dongan (range extender), Hesai and RoboSense (LiDAR), Bosch (brakes).
