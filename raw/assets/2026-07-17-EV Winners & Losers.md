# EV Winners & Losers

**Author**: Tim Levin, Mack Hogan, Rob Stumpf (InsideEVs Critical Materials newsletter) | **Date**: 2026-07-17

---

## Plug In: EV Sales Are A Hot Mess

There's finally some good news about the EV market in the U.S. — kinda sorta. In the second quarter, EV sales hit their highest point since the federal tax credit ended last September: 247,226 sales, according to Cox Automotive. But dig a little deeper, and some sellers of EVs are clearly a lot happier right now than others.

Broadly, EV sales were down 20.5% year-over-year in Q2 — not great, but better than the sharper declines of recent quarters. For the first half, EV sales were down 23.8%. The data is noisy, and individual car brands run the gamut.

**Winners:**

Toyota is zigging while rivals are zagging. For years it wasn't serious about EVs, focusing on hybrids instead. In the first half its EV sales more than doubled, largely on the back of the updated bZ, which moved 17,553 units. This time last year, Toyota trailed VW, Rivian, Nissan, Kia, Honda, GMC, Ford, Cadillac, BMW, and Audi — now it's ahead of all of them. Lexus also nearly doubled its sales in the first half, and Subaru doubled sales in Q2.

Cadillac lifted EV sales 10% in the first half, buoyed by the Vistiq and Optiq. Hyundai declined only about 5% — a feat in this environment — with Ioniq 5 sales rising 9%, making it America's favorite non-Tesla EV in the first half, helped by a nearly $10,000 price cut. Rivian was up 13.7% to 21,770 vehicles sold on strong commercial van performance, and raised its full-year guidance to up to 70,000 units.

**Losers:**

Demand has been hit by the end of the tax credit, especially for brands that haven't cut prices, plus model cuts and pared-back supply. Acura is down 99% year-to-date after canceling its only EV and its planned replacement. Honda was cut roughly in half and completely exited the U.S. EV game this week, announcing the Prologue will end production this year.

Jeep EV sales fell 94.3% from an already low baseline (a Recon EV and Wagoneer EREV are due this year). Dodge Charger EV was down nearly 88% to just over 500 units, as Stellantis — which also axed its plug-in hybrids — appears to be backing out of EVs. Nissan plunged 88.6% despite the new Leaf launch, with the volume-selling Ariya now dead. Volkswagen is down nearly 70% to under 4,000 units as the ID.Buzz skips a model year and the ID.4 is canceled. Ford's EV sales dropped over 57% after canceling the F-150 Lightning last year. BMW EV sales dropped about half, though new models are coming. Audi sales dropped 85% to just 1,697 units.

Tesla outperformed the market, with sales down only 10.9% per Cox estimates — though 2025 was already a historically bad year for the company.

**Takeaway:** The EV market is still kind of a hot mess. Companies that were the most serious EV contenders in the pre-policy-whiplash era are trying to stay on top; others, like Toyota, see an opening to gain ground. Given Honda's decision to axe its only U.S. EV, the shakeout isn't over yet.

## Get Fully Charged — News Roundup

**EV News:** Bentley is skipping fake engine noises in favor of a "concerto" of drums, viola, and bass in its EVs. Honda's Prologue — its last EV in the U.S. — has officially been discontinued, following the Acura ZDX and planned 0 Series EVs; Honda will have no EV on offer in the U.S. for the foreseeable future. A report claiming Lucid was weighing bankruptcy or a take-private sent its stock down over 50% before the company denied the claims as "completely false." Volvo could be bringing back not just a sedan but a wagon to its U.S. lineup. Affordable Chinese EVs are coming to Canada, though not the U.S. A new EV startup, Chip, emerged from stealth with a $15,000 low-speed EV offering optional remote parking. California's EV rebate program has named its 13 participating automakers.

**Battery and Charging News:** Automakers that built EV battery factories anticipating a boom are now pivoting some of that capacity to energy storage for AI data centers and utilities. MG says its next-gen plug-in hybrids will get semi-solid-state batteries, claiming it will be first to mass-produce them at scale. GM will roll out a managed charging program via a WeaveGrid partnership, joining Ford, Hyundai, Kia, Rivian, Stellantis, Tesla, and Toyota in offering off-peak charging incentives.

**Autonomy and Tech News:** San Francisco's mayor is calling for tougher statewide rules on autonomous vehicles after Waymos reportedly ran out of power and blocked streets during July 4th traffic. Robotaxi companies are increasingly calling 911 for riders who fall asleep in vehicles — a phenomenon emergency responders now call "sleepers." Geely unveiled a new electric drivetrain combining 12 functions into a single 165-pound, 328-horsepower package with 30% less wiring than a traditional drive unit.

## Driver's Seat: Cadillac Escalade IQL

A quick review take: the 9,000-pound, 750-horsepower Escalade IQL delivers 460 miles of range, an opulent and spacious interior, and four-wheel steering that makes its size manageable — but it's cartoonishly huge, expensive (as-tested price of $156,315), and worse to drive than Cadillac's other three-row EV, the Vistiq, at 1.5x the cost. Verdict: over-the-top in every way, but for many Americans, exactly the EV they'll actually want.
