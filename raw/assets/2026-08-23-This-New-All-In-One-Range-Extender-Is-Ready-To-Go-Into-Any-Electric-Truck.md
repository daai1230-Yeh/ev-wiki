# This New All-In-One Range Extender Is Ready To Go Into Any Electric Truck

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: Aug 22, 2026

---

*Mahle says its new range extender can fit in the same space as a battery module, increasing driving range to roughly 500 miles.*

**Key points**

- German company Mahle has unveiled an all-in-one solution that can be easily integrated into existing electric trucks.
- The unit includes a compact diesel engine, a generator, a fuel tank, an AdBlue tank, and all thermal and fluid management systems.
- Mahle says its solution can replace about a third of the battery capacity of an existing electric truck.

Electric trucking is slowly becoming more mainstream, but battery-powered big rigs are probably not for everybody yet. Tesla and Mercedes-Benz have proved you can squeeze over 400 miles of range out of an electric truck, but some companies and drivers still see that as an inconvenience.

Germany's Mahle has come up with a solution: add a diesel engine. The company's new range extender system packs everything into a single enclosure that can be fitted into the same space used by a couple of battery modules on an electric truck tractor.

The all-in-one enclosure has a compact diesel engine, a generator that powers the high-voltage battery, plus a fuel tank, an AdBlue tank, an exhaust gas treatment system, and a cooling system.

By taking out roughly a third of the battery capacity, the rear axle load can be reduced by roughly 881 pounds (400 kg), while total vehicle weight drops by about 1,322 lbs (600 kg). This means the truck can carry heavier loads, while total range can exceed 500 miles (800 km) — about 250 miles from the battery and 250 miles from the range extender.

The generator has no physical connection to the driven wheels, instead sending between 110 kW and 130 kW to the electric drive system. It uses no rare earth elements. Because the diesel engine can run on biofuels like HVO100 biodiesel, CO2 emissions are "virtually zero," according to the company. Under real-world conditions with conventional fuel, Mahle claims about 80% less CO2 compared to a conventional diesel truck.

> "Our range extender is the key to disconnecting the market ramp-up of battery-electric trucks from the inadequate pace of infrastructure development," said Marco Warth, VP of Mahle Corporate Research and Advanced Engineering. "If the charging station is busy, or alternative routes are called for as a result of weather conditions or congestion, and infrastructure reaches its limits, other fleets can be stranded. An e-truck with a range extender just carries on."

The plug-and-play range extender will be unveiled next month at the IAA Transportation show in Hanover, September 15–20.
