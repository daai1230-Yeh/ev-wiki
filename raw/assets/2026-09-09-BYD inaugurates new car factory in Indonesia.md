# BYD inaugurates new car factory in Indonesia

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 2026-09-08 15:00

---

BYD has commenced operations at its new car factory in Indonesia, located in the Subang Smartpolitan Industrial Park, West Java. This is the company's third auto plant in Southeast Asia, following those it opened in Thailand in 2024 and in Cambodia in 2025.

Spanning 126 hectares, BYD's Indonesian factory offers an annual production capacity of 150,000 units. The Chinese automaker is currently employing over 5,000 employees at the new plant and could potentially increase that figure to over 20,000. The Chinese automotive company has built facilities for four manufacturing processes at this site: stamping, welding, painting, and assembly.

The first vehicle to roll off the assembly line at BYD's Indonesian plant was the M6 DM plug-in hybrid MPV, which can travel up to 110 kilometres in EV mode, as per NEDC. The company also builds the Atto 1 – marketed as the Dolphin Surf in Europe – at the new facility. The city car was Indonesia's best-selling electric car in 2025, registering 22,582 units.

According to a report from CNN Indonesia, BYD will start building the M6 EV and the D9 locally later this year. While the Chinese automotive giant generally gives a choice of plug-in hybrid and purely electric variants for the D9 in its international offerings, in Indonesia, only purely electric variants will be on offer. As agreed to adhere to Indonesia's local production drive, BYD has entirely stopped importing cars into Indonesia and has not announced any plans to localise the other models in its local line-up: Dolphin, Atto 3, Seal, and Sealion 7, which all but points to their temporary, if not permanent, discontinuation.

BYD is one of six companies that utilised a government scheme, available from February 2024 to December 2025, that fully exempted imported electric cars from import duty and luxury goods sales tax. It imported 92,000 units under its 100,000-unit quota and needs to offset that volume through local production by 31 December 2027.

Luther Panjaitan, the Head of Public and Government Relations at BYD Motor Indonesia, suggested that the company will not import additional units of the Dolphin, Atto 3, Seal, and Sealion 7 as it needs to focus on locally produced products. However, the local subsidiary is studying BYD's Racco electric kei car, and Panjaitan indicated that the preliminary research study had yielded a 'very positive' response.

Liu Xueliang, the Vice President of BYD and the General Manager of BYD Asia-Pacific Auto Sales Division, said at the inauguration ceremony that the company has over 100 sales outlets in Indonesia and aims to grow that figure to over 200 next year. Agus Gumiwang Kartasasmita, the Minister of Industry of Indonesia, said that the country's cumulative EV sales reached 486,231 units in April 2026, with cars accounting for 223,000 units. In the first half of 2026, Indonesia registered 70,095 units in EV sales, up 16.06 per cent year-on-year.
