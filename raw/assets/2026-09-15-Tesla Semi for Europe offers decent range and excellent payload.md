# Tesla Semi for Europe offers decent range and excellent payload

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 12.09.2026

---

Tesla has released the performance data for its electric truck, the Semi, in Europe ahead of IAA Transportation. The figures broadly correspond to the US version with the smaller battery. Customers can expect up to 800 kW of power, around 550 kilometres of range and a relatively high payload. In addition, Tesla is planning its own truck charging network in Europe.

Tesla Germany GmbH has revealed details of the specification of the European Tesla Semi and has also launched a dedicated German webpage for the electric truck. However, the company remains tight-lipped about the market launch itself. Tesla only states that it will deliver the Semi to fleets across Europe, with customer deliveries set to begin in 2027. What is clear is that Tesla plans to introduce a network of so-called Semi Service Centres and licensed workshops to provide service coverage.

Tesla is bringing the 'Standard Range' version to Europe. Whether the 'Long Range' version, with its range of up to 800 kilometres, will follow later, as it has in the US, remains unclear.

**Central driving position**

The Semi features an unusual cab design, most notably its central driving position, which Tesla says provides 'improved visibility'. The cab is also said to offer enough headroom to stand, a coat hook, and a heated and ventilated seat, with acoustic glass and sound-insulating materials used to reduce road noise.

In terms of axle configuration, the Semi is a 6×4 tractor unit with three axles, the rear two driven. The European version adopts the powertrain of the US model, featuring three independent motors across the two rear axles, with total output stated at up to 800 kW.

**Charges at up to 800 kW DC**

Tesla has not disclosed battery details ahead of the trade fair, but states the European Semi will support megawatt charging at up to 800 kW DC, allowing enough energy to be added in half an hour to recharge to 60 per cent. This strongly suggests the same 548 kWh battery as the US version, built on 4680 cylindrical cells with NCMA chemistry at Tesla's Nevada plant.

For the European version, Tesla specifies an estimated range of 550 kilometres, while the US version achieves approximately 325 miles (around 523 kilometres). Tesla Germany states energy consumption of 1.0 kWh/km, versus 1.7 kWh/mile (1.1 kWh/km) in the US — a difference explained by different weight limits and truck speeds between the two markets. In the US the Semi is a Class 8 truck up to 80,000 lb (~36.3 tonnes); in Europe it is limited to 40 tonnes with motorway speeds usually restricted to 80-90 km/h.

**Payload-optimised for Europe**

Tesla notes the 550 km range and consumption figures are estimates based on a gross train weight of 40 tonnes, a speed of 80 km/h and 20°C on a flat road, using a special 'Aero Kit' and a dry van semi-trailer to California standards.

Tesla has also revealed a kerb weight of under 9.1 tonnes and an electric power take-off (ePTO) of up to 25 kW. Assuming a standard European trailer with a gross weight of around 6.3 tonnes, the European Semi would offer a payload of approximately 24.6 to 26.6 tonnes, depending on whether the 40-tonne limit or the special e-truck weight allowance of up to 42 tonnes applies.

The 550 kilometres quoted is not a new benchmark in Europe: Volvo Trucks leads with its FH Aero Electric (up to 700 km), followed by Renault Trucks' E-Tech T 780 (up to 660 km). For its European debut, Tesla has opted for a smaller battery in exchange for higher payload, along with megawatt charging at up to 800 kW.

Tesla distinguishes between 'Megachargers' at up to 800 kW and 'Basechargers' at 120 kW for depot and overnight charging, the latter likely using CCS systems, said to "increase the Semi's range by up to 60 per cent in four hours."

**Operating costs and service network**

Tesla Germany markets the imported model as a fully electric tractor unit with lower operating costs than conventional trucks, highlighting remote diagnostics, over-the-air software updates and reduced maintenance requirements. For service and repairs, Tesla plans Semi Service Centres, supplemented by 'Mobile Service on-site' support and Tesla-certified repair workshops carrying spare parts.

Tesla also plans to make its fleet management platform, Tesla for Business, available to future truck customers, allowing users to manage vehicle and driver access, view real-time data on charging status, tyre pressure and energy consumption, and process invoices.

The Tesla Semi will be on display at IAA Transportation in Hanover from 14 to 20 September, at the Tesla stand in Hall 12, Stand B01, and Hall 14/15, Stand TD07.
