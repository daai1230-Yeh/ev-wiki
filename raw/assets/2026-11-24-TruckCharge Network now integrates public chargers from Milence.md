# TruckCharge Network now integrates public chargers from Milence

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-11-23

---

Daimler Truck reports that its TruckCharge network now covers 21 markets and has recently integrated Milence's charging points. The initiative began in 2025 as a semi-public electric truck charging network with locations at dealers and customers. In addition to depot chargers, the integration of public chargers is also becoming increasingly important.

In March 2025, Daimler Truck announced its intention to establish Europe’s largest semi-public truck charging network under its TruckCharge brand as part of its plans. A year later, the first pilot customers were introduced: transport company Wessels in Rhede, North Rhine-Westphalia, and logistics provider Diez with its charging park in Dettingen unter Teck, Baden-Württemberg. The official rollout in Germany and Austria began in the first quarter of 2026.

The company now reports that “an increasing number of charging points are active at customer and dealer depots as well as at other locations within the network”, and that the offering now covers 21 European countries. Daimler Truck declines to specify the exact number of available charging points upon request. However, it is clear that the TruckCharge initiative is no longer intended to be a purely semi-public charging network limited to customer or dealer sites. Since August 2026, Milence, as an operator of public charging infrastructure, has been integrated into the TruckCharge Network. “This gives customers access to around 300 fast-charging points suitable for trucks across Europe without additional roaming fees,” Daimler Truck stated. This naturally also expands the network’s scale: Milence alone is active in nine European markets.

From the outset, it was important to Daimler Truck that the initiative, introduced in 2025 as a semi-public network, should be understood as “a complement to public charging offerings, including those from Milence”. After all, alongside Traton and the Volvo Group, the Stuttgart-based company has been part of the Milence joint venture since 2022 and did not want to create competition under its own roof. Now, it is evident that both sides are not pursuing a parallel existence but rather an integrated system.

The goal remains “to provide transport companies with access to additional charging options beyond their own depots and to bring together different charging offerings within one network,” as Daimler Truck emphasised. The TruckCharge brand continues to bundle additional services, such as consulting and planning for charging solutions, as well as digital services.

Alexander Müller, Head of eMobility Solutions at Mercedes-Benz Trucks, summarised: “With the TruckCharge Network, we are creating additional charging options for our customers where they are needed in day-to-day transport operations. The key is to connect different forms of charging infrastructure, from customer depots and our own locations to public offerings such as Milence. At the same time, we enable operators to make available capacity from their own charging infrastructure accessible to additional users in a targeted manner. In this way, a European network is growing step by step, making electric long-haul transport easier to plan.”

In practice, available charging points can be found via the TruckCharge Connect Portal, where access requests can also be submitted. Meanwhile, operators of charging infrastructure can use an integrated Charge Point Management System to centrally manage their charging points, controlling access, availability, and terms, for example. This allows them to determine which capacities they reserve for their own fleet and which they make available to other users.

From the beginning, Daimler Truck stated its aim to expand the TruckCharge Network to more than 3,000 charging points in Europe by 2030. The company reaffirms this target in its latest announcement – including public chargers.

For its part, Milence is not only opening up to TruckCharge but has recently also announced a cooperation with the similarly named provider Depotcharge. Here, too, the focus is on closer integration between depot charging and public charging processes: Depotcharge fleet customers will initially gain access to Milence’s public charging hubs in Germany. Usage and billing are handled via the Depotcharge platform.
