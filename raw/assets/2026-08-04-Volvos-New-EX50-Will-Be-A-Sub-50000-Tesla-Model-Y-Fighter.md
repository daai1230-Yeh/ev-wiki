# Volvo's New EX50 Will Be A Sub-$50,000 Tesla Model Y Fighter: Report

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-08-03

---

It will replace the EX40 and EX30 and ride on the software-defined SPA3 platform.

Volvo is reportedly preparing a new electric crossover to replace the aging EX40 and compete in the hotly contested compact electric SUV segment dominated by the Tesla Model Y. The new model will be called the EX50 and is expected to arrive in the U.S. by the end of 2027 with a starting price in the high $40,000 range, according to an Automotive News report citing people familiar with the plans.

The EX50 would become Volvo's new entry-level EV as the EX30 is phased out after the 2026 model year, priced roughly $10,000 below the new EX60 midsize crossover shipping in the U.S. later this year. A Volvo spokesperson declined to comment on future products but said EVs are "where we see the market growing."

Volvo, majority owned by China's Geely Group, has faced growing challenges over the past year — the Trump administration's tariffs and policy changes have hurt its U.S. business, while it also competes with burgeoning Chinese automakers in Europe. Volvo has rolled back some of its ambitious all-EV goals but still considers EVs central to its product mix.

The EX50 will ride on Volvo's new software-defined SPA3 architecture, the same platform underpinning the EX60, which has received largely positive early reviews. Volvo is increasing production at its Torslanda, Sweden plant to meet stronger-than-expected demand. SPA3 is a significant step forward from SPA2, which underpins the EX90 — Volvo's flagship SUV that has been plagued by software and infotainment issues since launch. Volvo says lessons from the EX90 informed SPA3's development.

While no specs have been announced, if the EX60 is any indication, the EX50 should offer substantially faster charging than the EX30 and EX40, along with improved interior packaging and greater efficiency. In size, the EX50 will reportedly slot alongside crossovers like the Hyundai Ioniq 5 and Ford Mustang Mach-E, and compete with entry-level luxury EVs including the Cadillac Optiq and upcoming Mercedes-Benz GLA EV. Production will reportedly take place at Volvo's new factory in Kosice, Slovakia.
