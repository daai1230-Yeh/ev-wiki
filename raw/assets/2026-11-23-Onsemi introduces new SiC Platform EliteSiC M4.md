# Onsemi introduces new SiC Platform: EliteSiC M4

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-20

---

US semiconductor manufacturer Onsemi has introduced the fourth generation of its silicon carbide technology with EliteSiC M4. The new platform is set to increase power density by up to 30 per cent and reduce losses by up to 35 per cent. This could benefit, among other things, the drivetrain systems of electric vehicles.

Onsemi has introduced a new generation of silicon carbide (SiC) semiconductors with its EliteSiC M4 platform. Based on a trench architecture, the platform is designed to reduce both conduction and switching losses compared to its predecessor. The technology targets electric vehicle manufacturers, enabling them to develop more compact and efficient traction inverters.

According to Onsemi, EliteSiC M4 delivers up to 30 per cent higher power density with up to 35 per cent lower losses. As a result, inverters can achieve the same power output in a smaller form factor while requiring less cooling. In electric vehicles, these components convert the direct current from the battery into alternating current for the electric motor. Higher efficiency can reduce energy consumption and improve range.

Technically, Onsemi has revised the semiconductor structure. The specific on-resistance is reduced by 20 to 50 per cent compared to the previous generation. Additionally, the manufacturer reports a 67 per cent improvement in switching characteristics for hard-switching applications and a 50 per cent improvement for soft-switching applications. These figures refer to technical parameters of the semiconductors and do not directly translate to a corresponding reduction in losses across the entire powertrain system.

## Onsemi focuses on lower resistance

The new platform also supports various existing package types, including top-side cooled SMD packages and power modules. For demanding applications, Onsemi plans to qualify the technology for operating temperatures of 185°C and above. Automotive supplier Schaeffler also emphasises the importance of higher efficiency and power density in traction inverters. Harald Hartel, Vice President of the eMotor Controls Business Unit at Schaeffler, sees additional opportunities to optimise inverters in terms of range, heat generation, and installation space.

A first variant of the new platform is already available: Onsemi offers the EliteSiC M4 as a 750-volt bare chip for traction inverters in electric vehicles. Further versions for voltages up to 1,200 volts are expected to follow from 2027.

As early as September 2026, Onsemi presented the Embedded Power Platform (EPP), a new architecture for power electronics. Unlike EliteSiC M4, EPP focuses primarily on the integration and packaging of various semiconductor technologies. For traction inverters, Onsemi promised up to four times higher power density and up to 15 per cent lower power losses at the time. Subaru is one of the first development partners and is evaluating the technology for future electrified vehicle architectures.

While EPP aims to improve the integration of different components within a shared architecture, EliteSiC M4 targets the electrical properties of the silicon carbide semiconductors themselves. Whether and how the two technologies might be combined in the future remains unclear from the current announcements.

In addition to electromobility, EliteSiC M4 is also targeted at data centres and energy applications, such as photovoltaic systems and battery storage. In these areas, the lower conversion losses are expected to contribute to more efficient and compact power electronics systems.
