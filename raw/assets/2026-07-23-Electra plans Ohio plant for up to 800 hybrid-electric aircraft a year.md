# Electra plans Ohio plant for up to 800 hybrid-electric aircraft a year

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 22.07.2026

---

Electra will invest 850 million US dollars in its first production facility for the hybrid-electric EL9 Ultra Short. In the US state of Ohio, up to 800 aircraft are to be built annually in the long term, creating nearly 2,000 jobs.

US aircraft developer Electra has selected Springfield, Ohio, as the site for the future series production of its EL9 Ultra Short aircraft. The company plans to build the new manufacturing facility on a 39-hectare site at AirPark Ohio, adjacent to Springfield-Beckley Municipal Airport. According to Electra, the project is expected to create nearly 2,000 jobs.

Planning for the facility will begin immediately, while construction is scheduled to start in 2027. In its initial phase, the plant is designed to produce up to 400 aircraft per year, with capacity expected to double to 800 units annually in a later expansion. According to Electra, the company evaluated more than 140 potential locations before selecting Springfield, citing its existing test infrastructure and proximity to aviation and research facilities.

The EL9 is a nine-seat fixed-wing aircraft with a hybrid-electric propulsion system. Unlike eVTOLs, it uses conventional take-off and landing but is designed to operate from extremely short runways thanks to its blown-lift technology. Eight electric motors accelerate airflow over the wings and flaps to increase lift. According to Electra, the aircraft requires a take-off run of just 46 metres.

The propulsion system combines batteries with a turbogenerator. While the batteries primarily provide power for take-off and landing, the turbogenerator supplies energy during cruise flight. Electra targets a cruising speed of 324 km/h and a ferry range of nearly 2,040 kilometres, plus reserves. Alternatively, the EL9 can carry up to 1.36 tonnes of cargo instead of nine passengers. The company is developing the TG600 turbogenerator with Safran Helicopter Engines under an initial agreement covering 250 units. Electra sees potential applications for the aircraft in regional passenger transport, cargo operations, military logistics and humanitarian missions.

The aircraft has been submitted to the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) for type certification under Part 23. The first flight is scheduled for late 2027 or early 2028. Bristow Group has already been identified as the launch customer, and Electra reports over 2,200 letters of intent from more than 60 potential customers.

Electra's production plans come at a time when US companies are also expanding infrastructure for fully battery-electric aircraft. Recently, Archer Aviation, BETA Technologies and Macquarie Capital announced that, as part of the ACES consortium, they aim to equip more than 250 sites with interoperable CCS charging infrastructure by 2030.
