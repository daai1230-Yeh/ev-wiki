# Battery-electric trucks gain ground in key markets; electric buses near-ubiquitous

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-10-29

---

The latest ACEA figures show that European commercial vehicle markets are growing, amid disproportionate demand for electric trucks and buses. In the first nine months of the year, electric trucks saw a 50 per cent sales increase, while electric buses rose by 38 per cent. The starting level for electric trucks remains modest, whereas electric buses are now almost standard in orders.

Across all powertrain types, the EU's markets grew by 7.5 per cent to 245,079 trucks (3.5 tonnes and above) and by 15.2 per cent to 32,695 buses. ACEA describes truck growth as a "recovery from a low base" and the bus market as performing strongly.

In the first three quarters of 2026, diesel accounted for 91.7 per cent of EU truck registrations (+6.6 per cent). Plug-in electric trucks increased 49.9 per cent to a 5.3 per cent share (3.8 per cent last year) – one in every 20 new trucks. Germany (+77.2 per cent), France (+60.2 per cent) and the Netherlands (+57.5 per cent) led, together accounting for 74 per cent of registrations in this segment.

## Bus market

E-bus registrations grew 37.6 per cent, lifting market share from 22.1 to 26.4 per cent. Italy, now the largest market in total registrations, saw e-bus growth of +116 per cent; Poland recorded 1,000 new e-buses (+557 per cent). Diesel and non-plug-in hybrid buses also rose (+5 and +24 per cent), with shares of 58.4 and 6.9 per cent.

## Medium-duty e-trucks reach 20 per cent registration share

Medium-duty trucks (3.5–16 t) grew only slightly to around 39,000 units (+2.5 per cent), but plug-in vehicles (7,753 units, +44 per cent) reached 19.8 per cent of registrations versus 14.1 per cent a year earlier. Germany registered 3,879 medium-duty e-trucks, exactly 50 per cent of the EU total (+72 per cent). Others: Netherlands 1,195 (+40 per cent), France 974 (+86 per cent), Spain 320 (+36 per cent), Poland 135 (+90 per cent); Italy (200, -56 per cent) and Belgium (42, -58 per cent) fell sharply. In the UK 2,960 e-trucks up to 16 t (+55 per cent); Norway 445 (+104 per cent), Iceland 94 (+571 per cent).

## Germany leads in heavy-duty e-trucks

Heavy-duty trucks (16 t+): of 205,857 registrations (+8.5 per cent), 5,120 were e-trucks – a 2.5 per cent share (1.7 per cent last year). Germany had 1,722 (+91 per cent), 33.6 per cent of the EU total; Netherlands 1,022 (+84 per cent), Austria 393 (+84 per cent), Denmark 271 (+62 per cent). Switzerland recorded 605 heavy-duty e-trucks (+62 per cent), a 24 per cent e-share, the highest in Europe; Germany stands at 4.2 per cent. Norway (14.4 per cent), the Netherlands (11.1 per cent) and Sweden (10.3 per cent) also have double-digit shares.

## Italy becomes EU's largest bus market

Of 32,695 new EU buses, 8,633 were plug-in electric (26.4 per cent, versus 23.8 per cent for full-year 2025). Overall growth is driven by Italy (+34.4 per cent), France (+10.5 per cent) and Germany (+10.1 per cent), each with over 5,000 registrations; Poland rose 69 per cent to ~3,100, the EU's fourth-largest bus market.

Italy leads e-bus registrations with 1,970 (+116 per cent), followed by Poland (999, +557 per cent), Netherlands (783, +86 per cent), France (665, +33 per cent) and Portugal (642, +379 per cent). Germany registered 836 (-30 per cent from 1,202), Sweden and Belgium fell -40 and -71 per cent. The UK lost its top spot in plug-in buses with 1,727 units (-22.7 per cent) behind Italy; Italy's e-share is 37 per cent, the UK's 30 per cent. Highest e-bus penetration: Romania (82 per cent) and the Netherlands (75 per cent).
