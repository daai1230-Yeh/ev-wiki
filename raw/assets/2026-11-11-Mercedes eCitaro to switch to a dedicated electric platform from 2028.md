# Mercedes eCitaro to switch to a dedicated electric platform from 2028

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-11-10

---

Daimler Buses has announced the second generation eCitaro. From 2028, the electric city bus will be based on a dedicated electric platform, utilise LFP batteries – and even handle some intercity routes. We attended the presentation of the near-series prototype in Munich.

Daimler Buses has been producing the battery-electric Mercedes-Benz eCitaro in series since 2018. Exactly ten years after its debut, the successful e-city bus is set to be replaced by a successor. At its own event in Munich, Daimler Buses has now unveiled a near-production study of the new eCitaro. The key message: the brand is positioning the eCitaro as its flagship city bus. The second generation will no longer share its platform with the combustion-engine Citaro but will instead stand on a dedicated battery-electric vehicle platform. Furthermore, from 2030 onwards, no more diesel Citaros will be sold in Europe. The spotlight today, therefore, belongs to Mercedes' new city bus flagship.

At the presentation in the Bavarian capital, Daimler Buses already provided plenty of details about it. At the heart of the new electric architecture is a new battery technology featuring LFP cell chemistry and a modular compact battery kit, as the manufacturer puts it. In the new eCitaro, the battery packs will be smaller but more numerous. They will be housed in the underfloor and on the roof. There are also significant developments in the motors – more on the drivetrain details to follow.

## Three low-floor and four low-entry versions

For context, it is important to note that the new BEV platform supports vehicle lengths from ten to just over 18 metres. The body shell, roof, and underfloor are designed in such a modular way that seven variants are possible: three low-floor city buses of different lengths (10.63, 12.13, and 18.13 metres) and four low-entry versions (10.63, 12.34, 13.08, and 14.68 metres). The latter can cover both urban and intercity routes – a first for the eCitaro.

Interestingly, all seven variants share the same front and rear modules. Daimler Buses refers to this as a newly created 'modular kit principle'. This system will be showcased in its final form at the next Busworld Europe in Brussels: for the trade fair in October 2027, Daimler Buses has announced the official world premiere of the second model generation.

## eCitaro battery: departure from NMC cell chemistry

How much has changed in ten years of e-bus development is already clearly evident in the Munich prototype – above all in Daimler Buses' U-turn on batteries: over the past eight years, the current eCitaro has received three new battery generations during its product lifecycle (NMC 1 to NMC 4), most recently this year. Although Daimler Buses switched suppliers before the last battery change – from Akasol to BMZ Polen – the brand with the star has always remained loyal to nickel-manganese-cobalt chemistry.

Now we know: Daimler Buses has been planning the second eCitaro generation for some time, aware that the more cost-effective LFP cell chemistry could be embedded in a major product overhaul. In Mercedes' future e-city bus, the LFP cells are packaged in relatively small units of approximately 50 kWh each. "In the solo bus, depending on the desired battery configuration, an additional two, four, six, or eight battery packs can be mounted on the roof," the company states. The total energy content thus amounts to around 300 to 600 kWh for solo buses and 400 to 700 kWh for articulated buses. Daimler Buses does not specify whether these are gross or net values. In any case, with LFP batteries, almost the entire gross energy content can typically be utilised.

## Energy content: a gross / net calculation

For comparison: the current eCitaro with NMC-4 battery technology offers up to 666 kWh, and the articulated bus variant, the eCitaro G, offers up to 777 kWh—both gross. The individual battery packs are 111 kWh each, with the solo bus accommodating four to six and the articulated bus four to seven of these packs. Daimler Buses specifies the range here as up to 600 km for the solo bus and up to 450 km for the articulated bus. For the second generation, the manufacturer has not yet provided a range figure. However, it is unlikely that the new model will have a shorter range: net capacity is the deciding factor, the successor promises a lower kerb weight, and it would be marketing-wise difficult to present a shorter range in 2028 as progress.

Charging power is also rising: up to 400 kW DC is possible via CCS plug, while other charging methods using pantographs or charging rails can deliver up to 450 kW. It is still too early for more detailed information on the charging curve and charging times.

## Two fundamentally different drivetrain layouts

As a low-floor bus, the series will feature the ZF AxTrax 2 LF e-portal axle with wheel-hub electric motors and up to 360 kW of system power – significantly more than the approximately 280 kW peak power of the current e-portal axle. The new e-axle in the eCitaro G means it will no longer require two driven axles (2x 250 kW peak) but only one with 360 kW, making it lighter.

Another innovation is the MMTI system (Modular Multi Torque Interface), which can individually control the recuperation and braking behaviour for each wheel, expected to improve energy efficiency, reduce brake wear and enhance driving stability.

The low-entry variants will not feature an e-axle but instead a central motor combined with Mercedes-Benz's own RO 440 rear axle, allowing speeds of up to 100 km/h "for intercity use". No power rating has been given for the central motor.

## A certain resemblance to the Mercedes eIntouro?

The eIntouro – Mercedes' earlier foray into electric intercity transport – is set to celebrate its delivery launch this month. It shares its platform with the combustion-engine Intouro but features an electric central drive (ZF CeTrax 2 dual CX332, 320 kW continuous, 24,700 Nm), an RO-440 rear axle and two 207 kWh LFP packs from CATL, with a range of approximately 500 km.

The near-series eCitaro prototype also introduces a new multi-layer 'sandwich roof' of around 42 mm (vs. approx. 70 mm for the steel roof), reducing weight by around 100 kg for the 12-metre solo bus and approximately 150 kg for the 18-metre articulated bus.

## Three-quarters of a tonne less kerb weight

Overall, the eCitaro is set to be around 750 kilograms lighter than its current counterpart (comparing a future solo bus with eight LFP compact packs to a current eCitaro with five NMC4 packs). A continuously straight side wall allows standardisation of roof and underfloor across all variants; defined crash zones protecting the high-voltage technology at the rear remain.

## Externally angular with references to the 1970s

The study stands out with its pronounced rectangular cuboid shape, with design references to the Omnibus O321 and the W123 car series, plus camera mirrors and a three-stripe light bar. Inside: a fully low-floor passenger compartment, two 12-inch displays (Multimedia Cockpit Interactive), and the Thermal Exchange Module (ThexMO) using propane (R290) as refrigerant. Via Omniplus, Daimler Buses will provide OTA updates and activation of additional vehicle functions as a new recurring revenue pillar.

## New eCitaro also to be produced in Central Europe

The new eCitaro is being developed primarily in Mannheim and is set to be produced in Germany, France and the Czech Republic. Daimler Buses intends to offer only electrically powered city buses in its European new vehicle business from 2030 onwards. According to in-house evaluations, in 2025 two out of every three city buses sold in Europe were already electrically powered; the company has delivered over 4,600 electric vehicles to customers across Europe to date.
