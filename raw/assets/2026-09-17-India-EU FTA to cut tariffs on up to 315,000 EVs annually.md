# India-EU FTA to cut tariffs on up to 315,000 EVs annually

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 2026-09-16 15:15

---

The European Commission has published tariff schedules under its proposed Free Trade Agreement (FTA) with India, revealing that the deal could cut tariffs on up to 315,000 EVs traded between the two markets annually. Should the FTA enter into force on these terms, the combined annual quota will reach this figure in phases.

Both the EU and India are taking a cautious approach towards opening their markets to imported EVs. Under the proposed terms of the agreement, they would begin reducing the customs duty on qualifying EV imports from each other only in the FTA's fifth implementation year, with reductions taking effect on 1 January each year.

The European Commission is proposing to cut the customs duty on up to 50,000 India-made BEVs, PHEVs, and any other passenger vehicles except ICE vehicles and HEVs imported to the EU to 8 per cent in the fifth year of implementation. This quota would include up to 27,500 vehicles with a CIF value of up to €40,000; 16,250 vehicles with a CIF value of above €40,000 and up to €60,000; and 6,250 vehicles with a CIF value exceeding €60,000. The proposed terms do not state any condition under which the entire quota cannot be used entirely for BEVs.

The EU would reduce the tariff by two percentage points each year from the fifth year, bringing it to zero in the ninth year. It would also increase the combined annual quota in stages, bringing it to 225,000 vehicles from the fourteenth year onward.

India would allow importing relatively fewer EVs from the EU at preferential customs duty rates, which would be comparatively much higher initially. Moreover, unlike the EU, India would exclude cars with a CIF value below €20,000 from the concession, aiming to protect domestic EV manufacturing. In the FTA's fifth implementation year, it is proposing to reduce the customs duty on up to 20,000 BEVs, PHEVs, and any other passenger vehicles except ICE vehicles and HEVs from currently as high as 110 per cent to 30 per cent.

The quota would include 6,667 vehicles with a CIF value of €20,000 to less than €40,000, 6,666 vehicles with a CIF value of €40,000 to less than €60,000, and 6,667 vehicles with a CIF value of €60,000 or higher. The proposed terms in this case also have no restriction on utilising the entire quota for BEVs.

India is proposing to reduce the customs duty by four percentage points annually, from 30 per cent in the fifth year to 10 per cent in the tenth year. It would also increase the combined annual quota progressively, allowing imports of up to 90,000 vehicles starting in the fourteenth year.

The EU and India concluded FTA negotiations on 27 January 2026. On 11 September, the European Commission submitted proposals for the agreement's signature and conclusion to the Council of the EU. Once the Council authorises the EU to sign, the EU and India can sign the agreement.

The FTA would then need the European Parliament's consent and the Council's final decision on concluding the deal. India would also have to ratify the agreement before the FTA can enter into force. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has said the two sides aim to sign the FTA by the end of 2026, potentially allowing it to come into effect in 2027.
