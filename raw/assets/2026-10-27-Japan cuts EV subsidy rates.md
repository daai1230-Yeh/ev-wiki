# Japan cuts EV subsidy rates

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-10-26

---

The Japanese government has decided to reduce state purchase subsidies for EVs. From January 1, 2027 onwards, the maximum subsidy limits for newly registered electric cars, Kei-EVs, plug-in hybrids, and fuel cell vehicles will decrease significantly. This is due to high demand for the subsidy program in Japan.

Japan's Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) announced on 21 October a revision of the subsidy programme for so-called Clean Energy Vehicles (CEV). The new subsidy rates will apply from January 2027 to vehicles registered from that date onwards, provided the current subsidy programme is extended beyond that point. With this adjustment, the government aims to stretch available funds as far as possible.

The maximum subsidy for battery-electric passenger cars will be reduced from the current 1.25 million yen (approx. €7,000) to 750,000 yen (~€4,200). For electric Kei cars, the subsidy ceiling will be lowered from 550,000 yen (~€3,100) to 440,000 yen (~€2,500). For plug-in hybrids, the maximum subsidy will drop from 800,000 yen (~€4,500) to 480,000 yen (~€2,700). Fuel cell vehicles will now receive a maximum of 870,000 yen (~€4,900), down from the previous 1.45 million yen (~€8,200). These amounts refer to the highest subsidy tier and do not include additional bonuses for specific manufacturer measures.

## Japan: EV subsidy rates drop significantly

The cuts vary depending on the type of powertrain. For regular electric cars, plug-in hybrids, and fuel cell vehicles, the new ceiling is 60 per cent of the previous amount. For Kei EVs, it is 80 per cent. The actual subsidy amount continues to depend on the specific vehicle model and its evaluation based on the programme's criteria.

The Next Generation Vehicle Promotion Center has published an updated list of model-specific subsidy amounts for registrations from 1 January 2027. Example changes for five popular models:

| BEV Model | Subsidy until end of 2026 | Subsidy from 2027 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Toyota bZ4X | 1.30M yen (~€7,300) | 780,000 yen (~€4,400) | −520,000 yen (~€2,900) |
| Nissan Leaf | 1.29M yen (~€7,300) | 774,000 yen (~€4,400) | −516,000 yen (~€2,900) |
| Tesla Model Y | 1.27M yen (~€7,200) | 762,000 yen (~€4,300) | −508,000 yen (~€2,900) |
| Nissan Sakura (Kei-EV) | 580,000 yen (~€3,300) | 458,000 yen (~€2,600) | −122,000 yen (~€700) |
| BYD Atto 3 / Dolphin | 150,000 yen (~€800) | 90,000 yen (~€500) | −60,000 yen (~€300) |

Japan is also adjusting bonuses for manufacturers' environmental measures, such as using lower-CO₂ steel. The maximum bonus for regular electric cars falls from 50,000 yen (~€300) to 30,000 yen (~€200); for Kei EVs from 30,000 yen (~€200) to 18,000 yen (~€100).

## Subsidy reduction due to high demand

The funds of the current programme are expected to be exhausted before the originally planned end of the application period. The adjustment affects the CEV subsidy programme based on the supplementary budget for Japan's fiscal year 2025. It has not yet been determined whether or to what extent Japan will introduce a new subsidy programme afterwards.

The 2025 supplementary budget allocated approximately 110 billion yen (~€624 million). The surge in demand was triggered by an increase in subsidy amounts in January 2026 (ceiling for conventional electric passenger cars, including bonuses, raised from 900,000 yen in 2025 to up to 1.3 million yen). According to Biggo Finance, in September 2026 BEV sales in the domestic market reached 16,074 units, up 88.6 per cent year on year.
