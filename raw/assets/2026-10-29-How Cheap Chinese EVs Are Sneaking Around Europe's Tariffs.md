# How Cheap Chinese EVs Are Sneaking Around Europe's Tariffs

**Author**: Rob Stumpf | **Date**: 2026-10-27

---

One Chinese online car marketplace found a loophole to bring cheap, used Chinese EVs to Europeans. The key is buying used cars instead of new ones.

China EV Marketplace, a company that exports Chinese vehicles and parts, told CarNewsChina that its used-car sales to Europe rose more than 280% year over year. Through the end of September it exported 7,900 cars to the EU. Those vehicles let buyers avoid import duties of up to 45.3% on Chinese EVs; vehicles with over 6,000 km are only subject to 10% tariffs, according to the company.

The marketplace has more than 2,600 used cars listed from brands like BYD, Geely, Nio, Xiaomi and Zeekr. The cheapest is a 2022 Chery QQ Little Ant at around $4,000; the priciest a Zeekr 009 luxury van at $120,000. Shanghai-based buyers find used, low-mileage EVs and broker them overseas. Inspection, shipping, import and homologation fees still apply, but prices stay low enough to attract buyers. The process is slow: up to three weeks to prepare for shipping, up to three months to reach a local port, followed by certification, customs and local shipping. The company can also supply a charging adapter between Chinese and European plug standards. The workaround does not work in the U.S.

Europe's new import cap of 350,000 new Chinese-sourced plug-in hybrids also pushes buyers toward used cars. "Tariffs have already weakened demand for new Chinese BEVs among our European customers... Pre-owned cars are exempt from the additional BEV tariffs and from the new hybrid (PHEV) quota," said COO Jakub Gersl. The loophole exists mostly for Europe and a few other markets, though Canada and Mexico may not be far off.
