# Ford's new EV truck Fathom aims to compete under $30K

**Author**: Breana Noble, The Detroit News | **Date**: 06.08.2026

---

The Ford Fathom is coming as an affordable electric vehicle option: Ford's first vehicle built on its new Universal Electric Vehicle (UEV) Platform, a midsize truck starting at $28,350 plus $1,595 destination with the standard-range battery.

Kay Hart, GM of Model e (Ford's EV division): the target buyer's motivations "look a little different from the traditional truck customer's," which guided every decision on the vehicle — "the name had to do more than sound good. It had to capture a mindset, shifting the focus from specs to a spirit of adaptability."

Customers can sign up for updates now; pre-orders open early 2027 with first deliveries later that year. Naming took nine months, with archived names like Galaxie, Taurus and Ranchero considered and rejected. Consultant Tu Le (Sino Auto Insights) on the price point: it sits close to Slate Auto's barebones EV truck (~$26,400 with destination), while Chinese automakers like BYD continue pushing subsidized, tech-heavy low-cost EVs that Ford CEO Jim Farley calls an "existential threat."

**Vehicle**: Five-seat midsize truck, similar size to the Maverick but more passenger room than a RAV4. 0–60 mph time targeted to match the Mustang EcoBoost. Uses a 48-volt electrical system (like Cybertruck) instead of traditional 12-volt. Targets roughly 300 miles of range on higher trims with a third of the battery cells of Ford's current EVs (not the standard-range base battery). Bidirectional charging can power a home (duration undisclosed — the first-gen F-150 Lightning could power a house up to six days). BlueCruise Level 2+ hands-free highway driving standard; the UEV platform will also support Ford's planned Level 3 eyes-off-the-road system in 2028. High-res touchscreen with embedded Apple Maps, plus Android Auto/CarPlay support, and a digital key.

**Market context**: EV demand has lagged expectations — Mustang Mach-E US sales are down 50% through July, not helped by the end of federal tax credits. Fathom slots just above Ford's Maverick ($28,145) and well below the Mach-E ($37,795) and Bronco Sport ($31,845). S&P Global Mobility analyst Stephanie Brinley notes Ford has a history of price creep (Maverick started under $20,000 in 2022; F-150 Lightning's price rose $20,000 from its sub-$40,000 debut) and cautions it's unclear whether midsize/compact truck buyers want electric — full-size EV pickups haven't gained significant traction.

**Manufacturing**: The UEV platform comes from Ford's California-based EV hardware/software "skunkworks" team (launched 2022, deliberately kept small and separate from Ford's legacy bureaucracy). Ford is investing $2 billion in Louisville Assembly, replacing the traditional moving assembly line with a new tree-shaped design combining three sub-assemblies and cutting parts by 20% — securing 2,200 hourly jobs. Large single-piece aluminum unicastings replace many smaller parts, assembled separately front/rear and combined with a structural battery pack. Batteries come from the $3 billion BlueOval Battery Park Michigan (Marshall), producing cobalt/nickel-free LFP cells under license from China's CATL — making Ford the first US automaker to build LFP batteries domestically.
