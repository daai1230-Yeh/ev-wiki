# Schwarz Group 'forced' to order combustion engine vehicles again

**Author**: Peter Schwierz | **Date**: 27.07.2026

---

The Schwarz Group has temporarily removed fully electric cars from its company car list in Germany. Employees of Lidl and Kaufland's parent company can no longer select a battery-electric company car for new orders — and thus cannot benefit from the tax advantages associated with private use. The company cites a volatile market environment and shifting regulatory frameworks.

According to Welt, the measure is primarily driven by resale values: unlike many companies, the Schwarz Group purchases company cars outright and sells them later on the used car market, and the lower residual values of EVs compared to combustion vehicles directly affect the fleet's economics. Demand for used electric cars has also been weaker than for comparable combustion vehicles, partly because rapid technological advances make EVs just a few years old appear outdated.

The decision applies only to Germany; in other European countries the group plans to continue integrating new EVs into its fleet, and its overarching net-zero-by-2050 climate strategy remains in place, including continued charging infrastructure expansion (over 24,000 charging points across 6,973 Lidl/Kaufland locations by the end of 2025) and partial transition of logistics to electric trucks.

The article notes this contradicts Lidl Belgium and Luxembourg's plan to fully electrify its company car fleet by 2030, and explores whether the Schwarz Group's near-exclusive reliance on BMW Group vehicles — a relationship dating to a 2018 switch from Audi — plus long delivery times for electric BMWs (roughly three to 14 months) may be contributing factors alongside the residual-value argument. Newer data suggests demand for used EVs is no longer uniformly weak in mid-2026: 6.4% of June 2026 registrations were battery-electric, and AutoScout24 recorded a 39% rise in enquiries for used EVs in March, though the market remains segmented, with affordable compact models in demand and pricier or older models harder to sell.
