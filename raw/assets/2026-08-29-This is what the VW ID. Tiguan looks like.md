# This is what the VW ID. Tiguan looks like

**Author**: electrive | **Date**: 2026-08-27

---

The VW ID.4 is expected to be renamed the ID. Tiguan after its facelift. The new name is considered confirmed. And the exterior design has also been leaked ahead of the world premiere – even in moving images: A well-known auto YouTuber has sneaked a peek of VW's hope-bearer in a short video – partially wrapped, but with clearly visible features.

The successor to the ID.4 will celebrate its world premiere in October, but interest in the revamped electric SUV is already immense. Spy shots have been circulating for months, and at the beginning of the year, IG Metall Emden confirmed the name change to ID. Tiguan — a long-anticipated move, widely expected following the introduction of the ID. Polo and the analogous switch from ID.3 to ID. Neo. Earlier this year, journalists — including the editorial team from electrive — were also given a preview of the production model during Volkswagen's week-long press event in Hamburg. But due to an embargo, no reports could be published at the time.

There has been a lot of speculation about the design, with suggestions that Volkswagen might align the ID.4's distinctive electric look more closely with the Tiguan's exterior, even though the ID. Tiguan is intended to remain recognisable as a standalone model. This now appears to be the case: well-known YouTuber Thomas Majchrzak has published a short video on his channel "Autogefühl," briefly showcasing the partially camouflaged successor from the front and rear. Visible through the foil, the front end becomes more angular and features a continuous light signature—both hallmarks of Volkswagen's current design language, as seen in recently launched vehicles for the Chinese market. The rear view also signals a departure from the existing ID.4 design, appearing less fragmented and featuring angular lights.

Volkswagen will fully unveil the ID.4's successor on 9 October. The world premiere will also reveal the complete technical specifications. Those familiar with VW's recent model updates will know that the technology is being overhauled: the electric SUV will switch to the updated MEB+ platform, receive a new entry-level motor, and the base variant's batteries are expected to adopt the more cost-effective LFP cell chemistry. The recessed door handles will also be replaced with traditional pull handles.

The revamped model will be built in Emden. At the beginning of the year, the IG Metall trade union stated that production of the ID. Tiguan in Emden is expected to continue until the end of 2031. The ID.4 was originally produced exclusively at VW's Zwickau plant in Saxony, which the group had fully converted to manufacture MEB-platform electric vehicles. Later, an additional production line for the ID.4 was established in Emden, initially running alongside the internal combustion models Passat and Arteon (both in Germany).

The Arteon did not receive a successor, and the Passat relocated to Bratislava with its generational change. This gap was filled by the electric ID.7, including its estate variant. There are also rumours about this MEB model being renamed ID. Passat—but this has not been confirmed by either VW or IG Metall.

The ID. Tiguan will definitely be produced exclusively in Emden. ID.4 production in Zwickau is being phased out, while the Saxon plant will retain the Audi Q4 e-tron and likely also the Cupra Born and partially the VW ID.3. With the facelift, all ID. Tiguan models will come from Lower Saxony. The ID.5 SUV coupé, built in Zwickau, will be discontinued and will not relocate to the plant at the mouth of the Ems in the North Sea.

According to Volkswagen, the shift to familiar model names with the ID. prefix is intended to add emotional appeal to the electric models and create a closer connection to the established model ranges—something that the ID.3 or ID.4 never fully achieved. The first model of this new era was confirmed around the IAA Mobility 2025, when the name ID. Polo was announced for the near-production study of the small electric car—originally planned as the ID.2. The accompanying SUV model was renamed from ID.s2 X to ID. Cross, similarly referencing the internal combustion T-Cross, which is positioned as an SUV in the Polo segment.
