# GM announces new efficient EV platform

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-10-22

---

GM has streamlined its development processes and introduced a new platform in a shorter timeframe, with the first production vehicles set to debut "very soon". From 2028, vehicles on the platform will also feature significantly more efficient batteries and a faster, centralised computing system.

General Motors has unveiled the fourth generation of its battery-electric vehicle platform. The new design is said to be more efficient, cost-effective, and require fewer components. At the 'GM Forward' event in New York, GM President Mark Reuss described the launch as "very imminent", with a primary focus on high-volume models such as crossover SUVs.

Reuss said: "When the U.S. market is ready for EVs in large numbers, we will be there for them." Rival Ford is developing a comparable 'Universal Electric Vehicle Platform', where a pickup named 'Fathom' is set to enter production at the end of next year at around US$30,000.

## Faster development processes established

GM integrated teams that typically work sequentially, shortening the vehicle development cycle from five years to roughly half the time. The EV base reduces vehicle weight by 15 per cent, improves aerodynamics by 25 per cent, and cuts powertrain components by 40 per cent. GM is adopting new lithium-manganese-rich (LMR) batteries with prismatic, flat cells, increasing overall range by 20 per cent per kilowatt-hour and allowing smaller, cheaper batteries.

The flatter cells enable a lower, more aerodynamic vehicle height without compromising interior space. Digital simulations tested over 300,000 battery designs before the first physical prototype, reducing development time from years to a single month.

## LMR batteries promise 33 per cent higher energy density

LMR cells offer 33 per cent higher energy density than LFP while keeping similarly low production costs, using fewer expensive and critical raw materials. Production of LMR cells begins in 2028 via the GM Ultium Cells / LG Energy Solution joint venture at the Spring Hill, Tennessee plant.

## New computer architecture from 2028

A central computer in the front of the vehicle will handle all functions, from powertrain and chassis to safety. GM says this reduces wiring by 30 per cent and increases computing power by a factor of 40, enabling AI for passenger interaction and energy management. It debuts in the Cadillac Escalade IQ starting with the 2028 model year.
