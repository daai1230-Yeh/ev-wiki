# India: Energy In Motion secures order for 500 electric heavy-duty trucks

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 2026-11-20

---

Indian electric trucking solutions provider Energy In Motion (EIM), an associate company of Ravindra Energy, has bagged a major order for 500 heavy-duty trucks from Sorigin Green Mobility, a homegrown electric truck fleet operator. The duo have signed a memorandum of understanding to deploy these trucks over a period of two years.

Sorigin Green Mobility will finance, operate, and maintain the electric heavy-duty trucks it procures from EIM. It will deploy them on India's major freight corridors, such as those serving the Jawaharlal Nehru Port Authority (JNPA) in Navi Mumbai, Maharashtra. As of September, nearly 150 electric trucks supplied by EIM were already operating at the port.

EIM will use its Ashwa 55-tonne battery-swappable electric tractor for the latest fleet order and also provide charging infrastructure, battery-swapping, and energy management services along with the trucks. The company expects Sorigin Green Mobility to begin deploying the trucks immediately, starting with an first batch of 18 in the Jawaharlal Nehru Port area.

The Ashwa is based on Chinese commercial vehicle manufacturer Foton's eAuman. EIM started its sales in August 2025 and had sold 477 units by September this year. The company used to import it in a completely built-up (CBU) form and recently switched to completely knocked-down kits (CKDs). It opened a commercial vehicle plant with an annual capacity of 5,000 units in Talegaon, Maharashtra, on 14 October and started assembling the Ashwa from these kits on the 30th of the same month.

During Ravindra Energy's Q2 and H1 FY2027 earnings conference call on 5 November, Vice Chairman Narendra Murkumbi, who is also EIM's managing director, said regular production of the Ashwa will likely commence in January 2027 at a rate of 200 units per month. In addition to scaling up local assembly of the Ashwa, EIM is working on expanding its network of battery swapping-cum-charging stations.

The company has opened eight battery swapping-cum-charging stations so far and aims to grow that number to 15 by the end of this year, and then to 40 by March 2027. Commenting on the recent order from Sorigin Green Mobility, Murkumbi said, "By combining our Ashwa 55-tonne electric tractor with charging infrastructure, battery swapping solutions and energy management capabilities, we aim to address critical operational requirements such as vehicle utilisation, turnaround time and energy costs."
