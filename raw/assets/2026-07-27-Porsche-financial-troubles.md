# Things Are Getting Really, Really Bad At Porsche. That's Not Good For EV Fans

**Author**: Mack Hogan | **Date**: Jul 27, 2026

---

Porsche now plans to trim roughly 9,000 jobs through 2035, representing over 21% of its workforce, as it faces a tough market in China and a tumultuous pivot to EVs. Cuts will come largely through natural attrition, early retirement schemes, and some voluntary buy-outs. Together, the 5,000 jobs announced this round plus roughly 4,000 previously announced cuts represent 21% of the 42,066 employees Porsche had in fiscal year 2025.

The Porsche Taycan was the most sophisticated EV from a legacy automaker when it arrived in 2019, with 800-volt architecture and fast charging, and has only improved since. That early technical leadership suggested Porsche would maintain its engineering edge in the electric age — but the follow-through has struggled. The Macan went electric-only, and the Macan EV arrived relatively impressive but its high price and buyers' affinity for gasoline held it back, forcing Porsche to scramble to develop a new gas-powered Macan. The electric Boxster and Cayman were delayed repeatedly, with gas variants now planned instead. The new Cayenne Electric hit U.S. showrooms this year and impressed in InsideEVs testing (over 350 miles highway range), but isn't replacing the gas version, which continues alongside it.

This flip-flopping on electrification has cost Porsche dearly in money and sales. In China, sales fell to just under 42,000 cars last year, less than half its 95,671-unit peak in 2021.

The piece argues this is bad news for EV fans broadly: it looked briefly like the high-end market would rapidly electrify, with traditional luxury manufacturers sparing no expense to secure a tech lead. These cuts are a reminder that misjudging the timeline is costly, and that small-volume manufacturers like Porsche can't afford to constantly rejigger model plans. A capital-constrained Porsche is less likely to pursue moonshot EV programs, and instead may focus on maximizing gas-car profits while it tries to build a profitable EV business later — good for shareholders, less so for enthusiasts.
