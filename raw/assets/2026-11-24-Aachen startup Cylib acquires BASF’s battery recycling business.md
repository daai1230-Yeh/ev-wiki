# Aachen startup Cylib acquires BASF’s battery recycling business

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-11-23

---

In a major development for what is a relatively young company, Aachen-based battery recycling startup Cylib is taking over BASF's battery recycling business. The deal includes, among other things, two facilities in Schwarzheide (Brandenburg) and the North America business.

The transaction covers all of BASF’s battery recycling activities in Europe and North America, including customer and partner contracts, intellectual property, and two existing facilities at the Schwarzheide site in Brandenburg. For startup Cylib, this is a remarkable step: the RWTH Aachen spin-off, founded in 2022, is taking over an established industrial business from a major corporation while itself only just beginning to scale its own recycling technology to industrial levels.

The acquisition includes the black mass production plant and the prototype battery recycling facility in Schwarzheide. It also encompasses existing customer relationships in the USA and access to the North American market. Additionally, a network for the collection, discharge, and dismantling of end-of-life batteries – established by BASF with partners – adds strategic value to the takeover. Both parties have agreed to keep the purchase price confidential. The closing is expected in the second or third quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals. It remains unclear how many BASF employees will transition to Cylib.

## BASF’s black mass plant: operational since 2025

The scale of the deal is particularly evident in the black mass plant in Schwarzheide, which BASF commissioned in June 2025. It can process up to 15,000 tonnes of end-of-life lithium-ion batteries and production scrap annually. According to the company, this corresponds to around 40,000 electric vehicle batteries. The plant is part of a larger battery materials complex in Schwarzheide, into which BASF has invested a total of around €550 million. The project received approximately €175 million in funding from the federal and state governments. Brandenburg additionally committed around €5 million specifically for the recycling plant.

One particularly interesting thing is the previous integration of the two business areas: the recycling process was designed to recover key metals such as lithium, nickel, cobalt, and manganese, which were then to be used for cathode material production at the adjacent BASF plant. This production will remain with the chemical company. BASF intends to focus its Battery Materials portfolio exclusively on cathode materials in the future and is therefore divesting its recycling business.

For Cylib, the acquisition represents a significant leap forward. The Aachen-based startup has developed a water-based recycling technology that, according to its own statements, can recover around 90 per cent of the raw materials from lithium-ion batteries. To date, Cylib has demonstrated the entire process chain in a pilot plant in Aachen. In a facility acquired in the summer in Wernberg-Köblitz, Bavaria, a mechanical processing plant with a capacity of up to 10,000 tonnes per year is now operational. The subsequent hydrometallurgical recovery of raw materials is set to take place in a larger plant currently under construction in Dormagen. With Schwarzheide, additional industrial infrastructure, expertise, and established customer relationships are now being added.

## Cylib founder: “The startup era is over”

The acquisition comes at a time when Cylib is expanding its financing and industrial capabilities. In mid-November, the company announced a bank financing arrangement with Sparkasse Aachen for an undisclosed amount. Just a few days later, the participation of the Federal Agency for Disruptive Innovation (SPRIND) was made public. Now, the company is taking over the recycling business of an international chemical corporation. “The startup era is over for us,” co-founder Lilian Schwich told Handelsblatt.

For BASF, the sale is part of a broader portfolio realignment. The company had already adjusted its investment plans in the battery materials business in response to changing market conditions. For Cylib, the acquisition presents an opportunity to become a key player in the Western battery recycling industry. Herbert Mangesius of investor Vsquared Ventures has already described the company in Handelsblatt as a “central consolidator of the Western battery recycling industry.” However, it remains to be seen whether Cylib can successfully integrate the acquired structures with its own technology and operate them economically.

## Similar processes – and a good technological fit

Technologically, the two companies are a good fit. In Schwarzheide, BASF has not only built a commercial plant for the mechanical processing of end-of-life batteries into black mass but also a prototype refinery for the subsequent hydrometallurgical recovery of battery metals. Cylib’s water-based OLiC technology also follows a hydrometallurgical approach, with a particular focus on the early recovery of lithium and the additional recovery of graphite. With this acquisition, Cylib is gaining not only additional processing capacity but also existing infrastructure for chemical metal recovery. However, it is not yet known how the company plans to combine the two technological approaches in the future.

Meanwhile, in connection with the transaction with Cylib, BASF has also announced that its Battery Materials division is exploring strategic options for its production site in Battle Creek, Michigan, USA, as well as for its precursor cathode active material (PCAM) facility in Harjavalta, Finland. Both sites are currently idled. BASF is seeking suitable partners capable of building upon the existing infrastructure and expertise at these locations.
