# Toyota likely planning range-extender models for China

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-09-21

---

Japanese automaker Toyota apparently plans to launch an electric car with a range extender in China, featuring a small combustion engine for additional range. According to a Japanese publication, 400,000 units of the model are to be built as early as 2028.

To strengthen its position in the world's largest electric vehicle market, Toyota is reportedly planning to produce and sell at least one range-extender model in China in the future. The report comes from the well-informed news agency Nikkei Asia, citing insider sources. The move would mark Toyota's first entry into the range-extender electric vehicle (REEV) segment, as the term "range extender" refers specifically to this technology.

Such vehicles feature a battery-electric powertrain supplemented by a small internal combustion engine designed to extend the vehicle's range beyond the battery's capacity. However, range-extender combustion engines do not directly power the axles and wheels. Instead, they function as generators, supplying additional electricity to the electric powertrain and batteries.

## Toyota follows Li Auto into range extenders

Even though the charging infrastructure in Chinese cities is already considered good to very good, range-extender models remain particularly relevant for travel to rural areas. The brand Li Auto has made range extenders its trademark. Later, other manufacturers followed suit with such models, including Aito, Leapmotor, Xpeng, and most recently Xiaomi with its new sub-brand SkyNomad. Even Volkswagen has launched its first China-exclusive range-extender vehicle this year—something Li Auto has already poked fun at. At the same time, Chinese authorities have recently responded to the growing market by introducing significantly more concrete technical requirements and testing procedures for range-extender systems.

According to Japanese media, Toyota specifically plans to begin production of several hundred range-extender vehicles per month in China in April 2027. Scaling is expected to happen quickly as Toyota apparently aims to reach an annual production of around 200,000 units by the end of 2027 and increase production to approximately 400,000 units by 2028.

Toyota has been relatively cautious in entering the New Energy Vehicle (NEV) market. The Japanese manufacturer first introduced the global battery-electric SUV model bZ4X to China in 2022. This was followed by the bZ3 electric saloon, developed in collaboration with BYD and FAW as the first model designed directly in China. In 2025, Toyota launched the bZ3X electric SUV, co-developed with GAC, and the bZ5 electric SUV coupé, which is also based on BYD and FAW technology. With the 2026 launch of the bZ7 electric saloon, developed with GAC, Toyota continues to expand its China-specific model lineup.
