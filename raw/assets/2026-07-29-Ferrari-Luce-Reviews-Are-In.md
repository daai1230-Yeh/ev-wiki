# Ferrari Luce Reviews Are In And Sorry, But It Actually Seems Pretty Great

**Author**: Nico DeMattia | **Date**: Jul 29, 2026

---

Sure, it isn't most Ferrari-looking Ferrari ever but did we really think Maranello would put out anything other than a banger super sedan?

Every company's dream is to break the internet with a product launch—but sometimes that can be a bad thing. When Ferrari revealed its first-ever electric car, the Luce, it was met with more criticism than seen from a car reveal in ages, largely over its design, which admittedly doesn't look very Ferrari-like. Now that the media embargo has lifted on initial test drives of a late-stage prototype, the reviews are in and they're pretty spectacular.

The Luce may not look like a proper Ferrari but it apparently drives like one, with razor-sharp steering, slide-happy handling dynamics, and a sonorous soundtrack — even as an EV.

According to MotorTrend's European Editor Angus MacKenzie: "This might be the heaviest and biggest Ferrari ever built, 2.0 inches longer than the Purosangue, but the Luce dances down the road. It feels beguilingly natural in its responses, fluid and concise in its change of direction, and beautifully balanced through all phases of a corner, from hard braking to full acceleration. The steering is light and linear, and the feedback through the lovely thin-rimmed steering wheel is excellent."

The Luce weighs just shy of 5,000 pounds yet handles like a much lighter sports car thanks to its balance, low center of gravity, and suspension. Top Gear's Jason Barlow said: "It slides like a Ferrari. I'm amazed how good that is."

It also sounds good. Using a high-precision accelerometer attached to the rear motor inverter, Ferrari picks up sounds and vibrations from the electric motor in real time, amplifying them while filtering out unwanted noise — modified mechanical noise rather than synthesized fake sound. MacKenzie noted: "Because you're hearing the actual speed of the e-motor change in response to input and load, you can, for example, hear the instant the rear tires begin to break traction." No other EV on the market currently does that.

The paddle shifters are also notable — the left paddle adjusts regenerative braking, while the right paddle increases available torque, letting drivers "pull" for more power in a way that mimics the feel of shifting gears in a combustion car, while still allowing a max-torque mode with full power always available.

Ferrari set a goal to sell 500 Luces this year and, per the Financial Times, already hit that goal in under two months, despite a price tag north of $640,000.
