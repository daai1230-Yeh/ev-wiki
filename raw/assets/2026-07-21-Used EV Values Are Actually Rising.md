# Used EV Values Are Actually Rising. Why That's Good And Bad News.

**Author**: Tim Levin | **Date**: Jul 21, 2026

---

Used cars don't usually become more valuable over time — and especially not used electric ones. It's common wisdom that a car starts shedding value the second its tires leave the dealer's lot, especially true for EVs, which have seen brutal depreciation thanks to tax incentives, price wars, rapid tech improvements, and concerns around battery longevity.

That's what makes it strange to see used EVs actually gaining value lately. According to a new report from Recurrent, which evaluates vehicle battery health and tracks the used EV market, secondhand EV prices are up 5.1% since January. Vehicles under $20,000 have seen the biggest increase, up 9.4% from January through June. "For years, the knock on EVs was depreciation risk: buy one and watch it lose value faster than a comparable gas car," Recurrent wrote. "New EVs still do depreciate quickly. But that narrative is not accurate if you're buying a used EV. Used EVs are holding value, and at the affordable end, gaining it."

Examples: the 2023 Chevy Bolt EV now averages $21,204, up nearly 20% from $17,718 at the start of the year. 2022 Tesla Model 3s are about $3,000 pricier, now near $27,000. The 2023 Ford Mustang Mach-E is up about $4,000, nearing $35,000. Meanwhile, used EVs priced above $40,000 continued to decline in value.

The likely drivers: supply and sales of used EVs have risen thanks to heavy EV leasing in recent years, yet prices are climbing and days-to-sell is now in line with used gas cars — over 128,000 used EVs sold in Q2, eclipsing Q3 2025 when the tax-credit frenzy was still in effect. At the same time, gas prices, while below their spring peak, remain above $4 per gallon, likely pushing budget-conscious buyers toward sub-$20,000 EVs and boosting demand in the used market generally.

Recurrent frames the shift positively for existing owners: "For a vehicle category often defined by steep depreciation since 2022, that shift is a sign of a maturing market."
