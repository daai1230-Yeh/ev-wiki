# New Jersey Law Would Outlaw Tesla's Camera-Based Robotaxi Tech

**Author**: Nico DeMattia | **Date**: 2026-07-09

---

A bill is headed for a vote in New Jersey that would force self-driving cars to use two additional technologies on top of cameras.

As spotted by The Verge, a bill currently under consideration in New Jersey would prevent companies from operating fully autonomous vehicles unless they have camera-based technology and two additional forms of sensor tech, like lidar or radar. Elon Musk has been adamant that Tesla's camera-based system is enough, but it legally won't be in New Jersey if this bill becomes law.

"This is not anti-Tesla," Democratic state Sen. Andrew Zwicker, the bill's primary sponsor and also a physicist, told The Verge. "I'm pro-New Jersey safety." After spending time in a Waymo in Phoenix, Zwicker said he's into the idea of robotaxis but recognizes the limitations of a camera-only system: "At this point, I don't think the evidence is sufficient that a single sensor with software can handle situations that humans can."

As it stands, the bill requires that a fully autonomous vehicle "be equipped with crash-avoidance systems, including a camera system and two distinct sensing modalities that are capable of detecting and tracking obstacles in the event of failure of the camera system." Beyond the sensor mandate, the bill also has testing requirements: companies would need specific state authorization to operate autonomous vehicles in New Jersey, which wouldn't come until after at least 50,000 crash-free miles of supervised testing, with the testing pilot program lasting at least three years.

Philip Koopman, an autonomous vehicle safety expert at Carnegie Mellon, argues that "human brains are fundamentally more powerful than AI because we understand" — cameras can get covered in snow, mud, or insects, completely obscuring their view, making backup sensors safer even if a car's main source of info is camera-based. Most companies developing autonomous vehicles, including Zoox, Waymo, and Nuro, are going the multi-sensor route, and Rivian and Lucid have both added lidar to future vehicles for higher levels of driving automation.

Waymo, whose cars carry lidar, radar, cameras, and audio receivers, is doing over 500,000 paid driverless trips per week across at least 10 cities. Tesla's robotaxi service is still in early days, with growth far slower than promised. New York has similar legislation in the works; if more states follow New Jersey's lead, it could be another obstacle for Tesla's self-driving ambitions.
