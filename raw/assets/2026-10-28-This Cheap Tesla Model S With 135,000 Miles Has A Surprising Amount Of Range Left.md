# This Cheap Tesla Model S With 135,000 Miles Has A Surprising Amount Of Range Left

**Author**: Andrei Nedelea | **Date**: 2026-10-27

---

After 135,000 miles, Edmunds' cheap used Model S still has useful range and enough performance to make it look tempting.

- Edmunds' sub-$13,000 Model S covered 221 miles on a charge despite 135,000 miles on the odometer.
- It still hit 60 mph in five seconds, but motor whine and aging electronics hint at potential repair bills.
- Other high-mileage Model S tests still show useful range, though they can't match new EVs' charging speed.

Buying an old Model S for under $13,000 is tempting until you consider replacement battery costs and the problems of an aging luxury car. Edmunds bought one to see what you get for the money: it managed 221 miles in its EV range test and, single-motor and rear-wheel drive, hit 60 mph in five seconds with a 13.3-second quarter mile, on old mismatched tires.

Edmunds quotes an original MSRP of $69,900 (base Model S 60), but the performance and range suggest it is likely the larger-battery Model S 85 ($79,900 at launch): 85 kWh, 362 hp, 325 lb-ft, 0–60 in 5.4 s claimed, 125 mph top speed, 265-mile EPA range when new, with Supercharging standard.

Presenter Alistair Weaver was pleasantly surprised: the air suspension works, the cabin is quiet, no obvious creaks, still luxurious and enjoyable in corners. Signs of age include rear motor whine, a damaged driver's display and a tired powered tailgate.

The author's drive in a 2015 Model S P85D with ~160,000 miles on its original battery (about 86% capacity per Tessie) gave around 200 miles of real-world range. Out of Spec tested a 250,000-mile 2019 Model S bought for $9,000 including incentives; believed to have its original battery, it covered 206 miles at roughly 70 mph.

Charging showed its age: about 47 minutes for 10–80%, and two hours 41 minutes for empty to full (recalibration and cell balancing lengthened the end). Dismissing a used EV on mileage alone could mean overlooking a usable car. Edmunds will keep testing; its original 2013 Model S needed three drive-unit replacements and a battery replacement under warranty, but this time there is no safety net.
