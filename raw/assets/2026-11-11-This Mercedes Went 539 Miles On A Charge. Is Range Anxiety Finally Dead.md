# This Mercedes Went 539 Miles On A Charge. Is Range Anxiety Finally Dead?

**Author**: Kevin Williams | **Date**: 2026-11-10

---

Edmunds says the Mercedes EQS 450+ is the longest-range sedan it has ever tested, beating out the Lucid Air.

- The 2027 Mercedes-Benz EQS 450+ covered 539 miles in one charge, according to Edmunds.
- This is significantly further than its 467-mile EPA range rating.
- Edmunds also found that it's the third-fastest charging EV it has ever tested, beaten only by the Hyundai Ioniq 6 and Mercedes-Benz CLA 250+.

A 2027 Mercedes-Benz EQS sedan just traveled well over 500 miles in a real-world range test by Edmunds. It's an amazing result that shows range anxiety may finally be fading into the rearview—especially if you've got deep enough pockets.

When put through its paces, the Mercedes EQS 450+ covered 539 miles, surpassing its 467-mile EPA rating by a substantial 72 miles. That also makes it the longest-range sedan Edmunds has ever tested, beating out the Lucid Air Dream Edition by 34 miles. Edmunds tests range on a route with a 60/40 split between city and highway driving, whereas the EPA aims for a 55/45 breakdown. In Edmunds' testing, only the Cadillac Escalade IQ has bested the EQS 450+'s range, covering 558 miles.

For 2027, Mercedes-Benz updated the EQS with tweaks that helped boost its efficiency and range. The battery is now 122 kWh, up from 118 kWh in the older model. The rear motor now has a two-speed transmission, and the whole platform has been upgraded to an 800-volt design.

The updated 800-volt platform also allows it to charge far more quickly. Based on its miles-per-hour charging metric, Edmunds says it's the third-fastest charging EV it has ever tested: the EQS 450+ added 100 miles of range in just 7 minutes, bested only by the Hyundai Ioniq 6 and the new Mercedes-Benz CLA 250+, which uses similar tricks such as a two-speed transmission and an 800-volt platform.

Edmunds' test example stickered at nearly $122,000, which isn't attainable for most EV buyers. Still, the growing availability of ultra-long-range EVs shows that range anxiety is becoming a thing of the past: the BMW iX3, i3 and iX5 all have range ratings well north of 400 miles, while the Volvo EX60 and Porsche Cayenne Electric land around there too. Edmunds has now tested six EVs that sail past 500 miles, and more than 90 models have exceeded 300 miles in its testing.

Ten years ago, 300 miles seemed like a stretch goal for the mainstream; only the Model S had such a rating. Now, cars like the Chevy Equinox EV or Kia EV3 can easily hit that number at prices not far off the average gas-powered crossover.

EVs still have obstacles to overcome in the eyes of the general public, like high purchase prices, scattered charging infrastructure, and a lack of options. But increasingly, range isn't one of them.
