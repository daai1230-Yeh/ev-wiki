# Ford Tested 36,000 Mustang Mach-E Batteries. Here's How They Held Up After 100,000 Miles

**Author**: Tim Levin | **Date**: 2026-11-09

---

Battery degradation is one of the top concerns among potential EV buyers. New data from Ford shows you probably shouldn't worry much.

- Ford collected battery health data from around 36,000 Mustang Mach-E electric SUVs and shared the results.
- Even Mach-Es with 100,000 or 200,000 miles on the odometer retained most of their original range.

A huge study of Ford Mustang Mach-E SUVs further proves that modern electric cars hold up just fine over years of normal use.

The Detroit automaker collected battery-health data from 36,000 Mach-E SUVs from the 2021 model year that were sold in the U.S. and Canada. On average, the cars had traveled 55,000 miles over 5.2 years and retained 92% of their usable battery capacity. That means a top-trim 2021 Mustang Mach-E with 305 miles of EPA range would in theory be rated for around 280 miles today. All the cars in the study have nickel-cobalt-manganese (NCM) battery packs, since Ford didn't introduce an LFP battery for the Mach-E until the 2023 model year.

Ford physically tests cells in the lab and tries to predict battery degradation using computer models before a model launches, Kevin Vander Laan, the automaker's technical leader of high-voltage battery cells and systems, said. "Our report card slowly trickles in over time. So here we are with a five-year report card, and it looks great."

Putting on twice as many miles as average in the same time period barely impacted overall battery health. Ford says the over 2,000 SUVs with more than 100,000 miles on the odometer retained more than 91% of their battery capacity. The 30-plus cars in its dataset that had covered more than 200,000 miles showed 88% battery health.

These are averages. EVs in the hot American Southwest tend to age more rapidly than those in temperate climates, and batteries frequently fast-charged to 100% and drained to near-empty typically wear out faster than those kept between 20% and 80%. Still, 95% of the 36,000 cars fell between 87% and 97%.

The results are in line with other research. According to Recurrent, the average EV retains 97% of its original observed range after three years and 95% after five years. In September, Aviloo released results of 500,000 EV battery tests: after 150,000 km, most models showed 90%–94% health; the Mustang Mach-E showed 92.5%, Tesla Model 3 88.9% and Model Y 90.3%.

Ford's recommendations: manage temperature extremes (park in shade, precondition while plugged in), rely on Level 2 charging for daily driving, and use DC fast chargers sparingly. Vander Laan says the learnings feed development of Ford's next-generation EVs, like the sub-$30,000 Fathom pickup coming next year.
