# Can Xiaomi Take On Porsche On Its Home Turf? It's About To Try

**Author**: Rob Stumpf | **Date**: 2026-09-03

---

Xiaomi is officially headed to Europe, including Germany, in 2027, the company announced on Thursday. Eight dealership networks have now signed on. German luxury brands have been feeling the heat in China, and now that competition is coming to their backyard.

Xiaomi has spent the last two years proving that a company that builds cell phones can also build cars. It makes EVs that are fast, desirable, and full of tech that makes most American cars feel archaic. It's passed the test in its home market, having sold over 700,000 vehicles there to date. Now it's setting its sights on Europe.

After bringing the heat to Porsche, BMW, Mercedes-Benz, and Volkswagen within China, the country's automakers are increasingly exporting the fight to European turf. Xiaomi is just the latest disruptor, and based on its popularity in China and high praise abroad, it could be a formidable competitor.

After years of raking in healthy profits in China, European luxury brands have struggled to keep up with the country's fast-moving homegrown automakers, and their sales have dwindled as a result. Only 125 Porsche vehicles were imported into China in July, down 99% from its 2023 peak; Porsche's China sales fell 26% last year and have more than halved since 2021. Now the same tech winning over Chinese consumers is headed straight for the Germans' backyard.

The Xiaomi SU7 Ultra sedan has already done battle with Porsche on the Nürburgring, dethroning the Taycan Turbo GT. There's also the YU7 GT, a curvy crossover with 990 horsepower — about 300 more than the highest-performance Porsche Macan EV. Xiaomi hasn't said which model, or models, it plans to sell in Europe.

Beyond speed, Xiaomi is also courting tech-hungry buyers with its "Human x Car x Home" ecosystem, integrating more than 1,000 different Xiaomi devices with the car. Its vehicles are aggressively priced in the home market with premium features baked in, making a convincing value proposition against German luxury brands.

How well Xiaomi does in Germany, and Europe more broadly, may come down to price and positioning. The SU7 and YU7 could launch as better-value Porsche and BMW rivals — but pricing too close to already well-respected brands could make for a tougher sell.

Xiaomi is just one of the Chinese brands making its way to Europe. Chinese-imported cars made up 14.2% of all EVs sold across the region during the first half of 2026, meaning Western automakers across the board are already feeling the pressure. Some have even resorted to importing their own made-in-China vehicles into Europe to stay competitive. As up-market models from Xiaomi and others gain more traction, it could prove to be a tough road ahead for even Germany's most prestigious brands.
