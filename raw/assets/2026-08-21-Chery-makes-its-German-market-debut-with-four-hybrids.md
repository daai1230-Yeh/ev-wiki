# Chery makes its German market debut – with four hybrids

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 20.08.2026 - 13:15

---

Chery is the export world champion among Chinese passenger car manufacturers — but until now, the brand has lacked a presence in Germany. That's soon set to change, as Chery plans to launch four hybrid SUVs in Germany simultaneously in the first half of 2027. A pure electric car is expected to follow with a slight delay.

Chery is currently establishing its German headquarters in Gateway Gardens, a redeveloped district in Frankfurt within sight of Frankfurt Airport's Terminal 2. Showrooms on the ground floor will showcase the core Chery brand and its sub-brands, Omoda and Jaecoo. Omoda & Jaecoo Deutschland GmbH have been based there for some time — Chery initially launched its sub-brands first, with the PHEV SUV Jaecoo 7 SHS being the group's first official vehicle in Germany at the beginning of the year.

In the first half of 2027, the core Chery brand will make its German debut with its own subsidiary at the same address. The four launch models are all members of the Tiggo family, all SUVs, and all hybrids: the Tiggo 4 is a full hybrid, the Tiggo 7 is available as full hybrid or plug-in hybrid, and the seven-seater Tiggo 8 and 9 are plug-in hybrids. Chery is launching in Germany without a battery-electric vehicle.

## All-electric Chery Q to be unveiled in October

Patrick Reimers, recently appointed Chery Country Director, notes that SUVs and hybrids are currently in high demand in Germany. In less than two months, the first all-electric model for the German market will be unveiled at the Paris Motor Show: the Chery Q, presented on Chery's international website as a playful compact electric car vaguely reminiscent of the Renault 5 E-Tech.

Chery is China's most successful passenger car exporter. Eric Zheng, CEO of Chery Auto Deutschland and Vice President of Chery Automotive European Region, said that for 23 years Chery has been the largest Chinese passenger car exporter, and in July 2026 it set a new delivery record with over 275,000 vehicles (including 202,500 exports), around half of which were electrified. Chery is present in 130 countries, including 25 in the EU, and first entered Europe in 2023.

Zheng described the German market as "challenging" with demanding customers, saying it compels the company to meet the highest standards from the outset.

Within the group, Chery is positioned as a car brand for families (tagline: "Chery. For Family"). Omoda targets a progressive, urban demographic, while Jaecoo embodies an adventurous spirit. Brand presentation, management and distribution channels for Chery will be kept separate from Omoda and Jaecoo.

## Chery to kick things off with 30 sales outlets

Chery is adopting a traditional stationary retail model for Germany, aiming for 30 partner dealerships by launch and at least 100 locations by the end of 2027. Positioning emphasises technical refinement and high safety standards at moderate prices — the full hybrid Tiggo 4 starts in Austria at well under €25,000.

Reimers said competition from Leapmotor, Xpeng and Geely does not concern Chery, describing the company as "the most international car manufacturer from China" and orienting itself more towards brands like Škoda or Hyundai.

## Chery already operates a production facility in Barcelona

Since 2024, Chery has operated a production facility in Spain in collaboration with the brand Ebro — a former Nissan plant near Barcelona. Initially, models sold in Germany will be imported from China, but Chery aims to establish Spain as a European production hub in the medium term. Chery also operates two R&D and design centres in Europe, including an R&D facility in Raunheim, Hesse, since 2018, and recently announced a research centre in Bedfordshire, UK. The group has seven subsidiaries in Europe, with two more branches in Germany and France being established.

Chery has secured an aftersales cooperation with Kühne & Nagel and a breakdown assistance agreement with Allianz. Local financing partners are expected to be finalised by launch.

European registrations: 37,000 in 2024 (first full sales year), around 200,000 in 2025, and 174,000 already in the first half of 2026. Market share in Spain, Poland and the UK is now 1.5 to 2.5 per cent.

Neither hybrids nor plug-in hybrids are currently the fastest-growing powertrain type in Germany — that distinction belongs to battery-electric vehicles, which Chery is initially excluding from its launch.
