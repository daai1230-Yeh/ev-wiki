# Tata Motors opposes BEE credit sale proposal under CAFE II norms

**Author**: ETAuto Staff | **Date**: Jul 16, 2026

---

Tata Motors has urged the government to revise proposed Corporate Average Fuel Efficiency (CAFE-II) amendments, warning that allowing the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) to sell compliance credits at a fixed price could weaken the CAFE framework and distort the market.

The company's main objection is pricing: non-compliance under the Energy Conservation Act costs roughly ₹5,000 per gram of CO2/km, while the draft mechanism would let manufacturers buy BEE credits at ₹2,500 per gram — half that cost. Tata Motors argues that if the cost of achieving compliance exceeds ₹2,500/g CO2/km, manufacturers would find it cheaper to simply buy credits than to invest in cleaner technology.

Tata Motors also objects to BEE's proposed dual role as regulator, market administrator, price-setter, and seller simultaneously, arguing a credit market only works if the regulator remains neutral. It instead proposes that manufacturers exceeding targets trade credits directly with those facing compliance gaps, enabling market-based price discovery while preserving regulatory neutrality.
