# California invests nearly $1.3 billion in clean public transit and rail projects

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-11-17

---

While the bulk of the funding goes towards major rail infrastructure and preserving existing train schedules, targeted grants will deploy more than 75 battery-electric and hydrogen buses, expand microtransit, and support a battery-electric train pilot.

Governor Gavin Newsom announced the funding as a state delegation travelled to Türkiye for the COP31 climate summit. The California State Transportation Agency is distributing the money using funds from Senate Bill 1 and the state’s Cap-and-Invest program.

A significant portion of the awards funds clean, zero-emission fleets and electric rail technology. The grants will put more than 75 zero-emission buses and smaller microtransit shuttles on the road.

Governor Gavin Newsom said: “California has shown that investing in cleaner transportation can improve people’s everyday lives. These investments build on that progress, funding cleaner buses, better rail connections, and the train service families count on to get to work and school. As we take California’s experience to the world stage, we’re continuing to build a cleaner, more connected state.”

Santa Monica’s Big Blue Bus will acquire battery-electric buses to complete the conversion of its fleet to all-electric vehicles. In Northern California, the Sacramento region will add 21 hydrogen fuel cell electric buses to support increased service between Sacramento and Davis. Kings County will introduce battery-electric buses alongside modernised transit facilities to connect residents with future high-speed rail service, and Merced County will expand zero-emission microtransit options.

Additional zero-emission buses and microtransit services will roll out in Marin, Los Angeles, Ventura, and Riverside counties, as well as the city of Montebello. Outside of bus fleets, the awards fund a battery-electric train pilot for Caltrain between San Jose and Gilroy and expand e-bike sharing in Truckee.

Most of the total funding, however, goes towards rail infrastructure and preserving existing train operations. The largest award provides $750 million for The Portal, extending Caltrain roughly two miles into downtown San Francisco to connect with the Salesforce Transit Center and the future high-speed rail terminus.

In Southern California, a $20 million grant prevents Metrolink from cutting weekday service by 29 per cent and weekend service by 40 per cent, maintaining current schedules through the 2028 Olympic and Paralympic Games. Remaining grants fund tap-to-pay fares for Metrolink, maintain San Diego’s COASTER commuter rail, expand track capacity in Fremont and Newark, advance passenger rail to Salinas, modernise intercity rail cars, and upgrade signals, stations, and rail crossings statewide.

The awards come shortly after California reopened its $55 million ZESBI incentive programme to fund electric school buses and charging infrastructure across the state.
