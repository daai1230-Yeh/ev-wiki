# I Drove All The New Toyota EVs. Here's My Pick—And How To Choose

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: September 10, 2026

The Toyota bZ, C-HR, and bZ Woodland all share the same bones. I drove them all to find the best value.

---

Toyota's EV timing has been a bit counterintuitive. When electric car sales were booming and the federal tax credit sweetened the deal, the automaker crawled through the transition with just one so-so model, the bZ4x. Now that the incentive is gone and EV sales have cooled, Toyota suddenly has three new or refreshed models to sell, plus more on the way.

The strategy is working. In the first quarter of this year, the bZ was America's best-selling non-Tesla EV, thanks to big range and charging improvements. It's also no longer the automaker's sole battery-powered model, now joined by the smaller C-HR and the wagon-like bZ Woodland. The broader lineup helped push Toyota's U.S. EV sales up 225% in the second quarter.

I've tested all three, and there are some obvious and some not-so-obvious differences that set them apart. My pick of the bunch is the all-wheel-drive bZ XLE.

## Pricing & Basics

The three EVs share underlying technology but cater toward slightly different buyers. The bZ leans toward value in the hot crossover segment, competing with the Tesla Model Y and Hyundai Ioniq 5. The C-HR is compact with a sportier feel. The bZ Woodland is the biggest and most adventure-oriented.

Base pricing with destination: $36,495 for the bZ, $38,695 for the C-HR, and $46,975 for the bZ Woodland.

## Range Compared

All three are built in Japan on the same e-TNGA platform and share the same 74.7-kWh NMC battery. The bZ delivers up to 314 miles of EPA-rated range, the C-HR 287 miles, and the bZ Woodland 280 miles (260 on all-terrain tires). The entry-level bZ also offers a smaller 57.7-kWh battery good for 236 miles.

## Efficiency & Charging Speed

Model year 2026/2027 Toyota EVs get upgraded batteries and motors that make them more efficient than the old bZ4x — over 3.0 mi/kWh observed on all three in mixed driving, touching 5.0 mi/kWh in the city before settling to 3.5–4.0 mi/kWh on highways. Cold weather cuts this significantly (2.7 mi/kWh max in winter testing upstate).

All three charge 10–80% in roughly 30 minutes, versus nearly an hour for the old bZ4x. In real-world testing at an Ionna station, the C-HR went 15–80% in 26 minutes (nearly 200 miles added); the bZ Woodland went 12–80% in 28 minutes.

## How Do They Drive?

The C-HR is the most fun to drive — standard AWD, 338 hp, a claimed 0–60 in 4.9 seconds, and minimal body roll in corners. The AWD bZ (also 338 hp) is no slouch either. The bZ Woodland is the most powerful at 375 hp and accelerates ferociously, though its extra length and 4,497-lb curb weight (131 lb more than the C-HR, 264 lb more than the bZ) shows up as more body roll in corners.

Ride quality varies: the bZ is most comfort-biased with a soft suspension; the C-HR feels slightly stiff with lateral jolts over bumps; the bZ Woodland — with longer suspension travel, all-terrain tires, and 8.4 inches of clearance — was the author's favorite for ride and off-road capability, with Snow, Dirt, and Mud driving modes.

## Interior And Technology

Dashboards are practically identical across all three: a 14-inch touchscreen with wireless Android Auto/CarPlay, the same steering wheel, digital gauge cluster, and rotary gear knob. The infotainment is merely "fine" and occasionally buggy — the bZ Woodland randomly failed to connect Bluetooth/Android Auto during testing. None of the three get Toyota's newer Arene software platform (found in the RAV4 and Lexus ES); a Toyota spokesperson said they lack the hardware/software architecture to support it.

All three share a persistent issue since the 2022 bZ4x debut: the digital gauge cluster sits far back and gets blocked by the top of the steering wheel for many drivers. The bZ Woodland has the roomiest cabin and most cargo space (33.8 cu-ft); the C-HR's rear seat is cramped for taller adults.

## Verdict

None of these three EVs are class-leading, but they're good enough for the average buyer, backed by Toyota's brand reputation. The C-HR's stiffer ride and higher price than the roomier bZ make it a dealbreaker for the author. The bZ Woodland has the best ride quality and most room but costs $10,000 more than the bZ.

That makes the bZ the value pick: the XLE FWD trim with the larger battery delivers 314 miles of range for nearly the same price as the base C-HR, while going AWD adds a couple grand for 338 hp and the same off-road drive modes as the Woodland.
