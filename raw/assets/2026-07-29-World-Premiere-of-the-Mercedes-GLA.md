# World Premiere of the Mercedes GLA: Up to 320 kW of charging power and 657 km of range

**Author**: Kasimir Hupe | **Date**: 29.07.2026 (updated 30.07.2026)

---

Mercedes-Benz presents the all-new GLA in Warsaw. The compact SUV has a range of up to 657 kilometers and charges at up to 320 kW. The electric car can be ordered immediately at prices starting from 48,599 euros. However, a look at the data sheets shows that the peak values only apply to two of the three models.

With the new GLA, Mercedes has reversed its usual approach: previously, the carmaker launched the ICE model first, followed later by an electric variant. This time, it will debut three battery-electric models in November, while hybrid versions will not hit the road until early 2027 — a clear signal that electric drive has arrived at Mercedes even in the volume segment of the A-Class.

The GLA is built on the 800-volt architecture first introduced with the electric CLA and also used in the electric GLB. The entry-level GLA 200 features a 58 kWh LFP battery, while the GLA 250+ and GLA 350 4MATIC models get an 85 kWh NMC battery offering up to 657 km (WLTP). All three variants reach a top speed of 210 km/h. Starting price is €48,599, making it Mercedes' most affordable electric SUV.

**Charging**: Thanks to the 800-volt architecture, the GLA 250+ and GLA 350 4MATIC draw up to 320 kW, going from 10 to 80% in 22 minutes (around 60 kWh transferred, ~162 kW average). Ten minutes of charging adds up to 270 km of WLTP range. The base GLA 200 uses an LFP battery, charges at a max of 200 kW, and takes just over 20 minutes for a 10–80% charge despite storing less energy — LFP is cheaper, more durable, cobalt-free, but has lower energy density. Notably, the DC converter enabling charging at 400-volt stations is an optional extra; without it, the car is limited to 800-volt chargers. AC charging is 11 kW standard, 22 kW optional.

Key specs across the three trims: GLA 200 (165 kW/224 hp, 0-100 in 8.1s, 394-447 km range, €48,599), GLA 250+ (200 kW/272 hp, 7.3s, 577-657 km, €54,978), GLA 350 4MATIC (260 kW/354 hp, all-wheel drive, 5.4s, 555-643 km, €58,107, 2-tonne towing capacity). A fourth variant with a 71 kWh battery arrives in early 2027 to close the gap between the 58 and 85 kWh options.

**Efficiency and weight**: At around 15 kWh/100km, the 4.57m SUV achieves strong efficiency thanks to a 0.27 drag coefficient. Weight is less impressive at 2,145–2,235 kg, with a payload of 480 kg.

**Design**: The GLA measures 4.57m x 1.87m x 1.60m (20mm lower than predecessor), with a wheelbase grown 61mm to 2.79m. The front grille — 608 backlit polycarbonate dots, 158 micro-LEDs, and a large centrally placed star — is the first time Mercedes has brought this design to the compact segment; it's polarizing in the bright version but blends better in the Night Edition.

**Interior and tech**: An optional MBUX Superscreen spans a 10.25-inch driver display, 14-inch central display, and 14-inch passenger display. Rear legroom grows 38mm, headroom 24mm, boot capacity reaches 410L (1,400L seats down), plus a 107L frunk. Eight cameras, five radar sensors, and twelve ultrasonic sensors feed a 254 TOPS control unit for driver assistance (SAE Level 2, with autonomous lane-changing in the Plus version). The MBUX Virtual Assistant combines ChatGPT, Microsoft Bing, and Google Gemini models, and navigation is built on Google Maps enhanced by Google Cloud's Automotive AI Agent.

**Sustainability**: The electric GLA cuts CO₂ footprint by around 40% versus the current ICE model — roughly 400 kg of CO₂ savings per vehicle — with 30% less CO₂ per battery cell and about 40% of aluminum from renewable-electricity electrolysis. Recycled material content is four times higher than the predecessor, and carpets are made entirely from recycled Econyl yarn.

**Positioning**: The GLA and its larger sibling, the electric GLB, share the MMA platform, 800V architecture, and battery options. The GLB is 16cm longer with 540L boot capacity (vs 410L) and an optional third row, starting at €59,048 — about €4,070 more than the GLA 250+. Compared to the CLA 250+ Shooting Brake, the GLA 250+ is around €2,110 cheaper.
