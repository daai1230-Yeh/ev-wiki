# Tesla announces multi-billion-euro expansion of Gigafactory Grünheide

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-11-16

---

Tesla has announced plans to invest three billion euros in expanding its factory in Grünheide, Brandenburg. From 2029, the Tesla Cybercab robotaxi is set to be manufactured there. Battery cell production will also be expanded, with construction work scheduled to begin in 2027.

Tesla plans to invest three billion euros in expanding its Gigafactory in Grünheide. The US-based electric vehicle manufacturer intends to construct a new production building and various auxiliary buildings on the factory premises. An additional wastewater treatment plant is also planned. "This will lay the foundation for the most technologically advanced Tesla factory in the world," explains André Thierig, plant manager of the Gigafactory in Grünheide.

The new production hall is set to manufacture up to one million Cybercabs per year. The Cybercab is an autonomous robotaxi without a steering wheel or pedals, designed to transport passengers without a driver. In the US, selected private customers can now also pre-order the robotaxi.

## Cybercab set to roll off the assembly line in 2029

Tesla has already established a timeline for implementing the expansion in Brandenburg. Later this year, the company intends to submit the official permit application for the new building to the authorities. This application builds on a previous comprehensive submission from 2023. Construction work is scheduled to begin in the first half of 2027, with production of the first robotaxis set to start in 2029.

The state government of Brandenburg has responded very positively to these plans. Dietmar Woidke, Minister-President of Brandenburg and a member of the SPD, comments: "The investment of three billion euros and the prospect of producing up to one million Cybercabs in Grünheide in the future are a decision of enormous economic significance."

After a global sales crisis in 2024 and 2025, which led to a temporary halt to expansion plans for Grünheide, the site – operational since March 2022 – is now experiencing a noticeable upturn. Its production capacity of around 375,000 electric vehicles per year had not been fully utilised until recently. Last year, approximately 200,000 Model Y units left the factory. Weekly production currently stands at around 7,000 vehicles, approaching the current technical limit of 7,500. In-house battery cell production is also expected to reach full capacity by mid-2027.

The workforce, which had shrunk to 11,000 employees and has now grown back to 12,600, reflects this development. Thierig has also called for applications on LinkedIn, primarily "in the areas of construction and manufacturing planning" in Grünheide. Battery production still requires 1,500 additional workers, along with several hundred specialists to prepare and build the new robotaxi hall. However, the long-term staffing requirements for the new production facility have not yet been quantified.

## Site expansion to be carried out in three phases

Tesla's expansion plans are fundamentally divided into three phases, each requiring a so-called partial approval. The first partial approval was already granted in October 2024 by the Brandenburg State Office for the Environment. Tesla submitted the corresponding application in March 2023. According to an earlier report by Märkische Oderzeitung, the expansion involves 70 hectares in the northern part of the site. While the clearing of land was still covered by the current development plan, Tesla had to reapply for the expansion work on its own factory premises.

While the first phase focused on modernising the existing hall and creating additional storage and logistics areas on the Tesla site, the upcoming second phase centres on the construction of a second production hall. The third partial approval will later cover, among other things, factory equipment and the construction of a freight railway station on the factory premises. RBB noted in a former article that, although "all three phases were applied for in partial approvals in 2023, additional documents must still be submitted for phases two and three."

RBB highlights an important detail of the further planning: the design of an additional water circuit, including wastewater treatment. Water consumption has been a sensitive issue in Grünheide since the beginning. RBB states: "The new hall and additional employees would indeed consume more water overall than before – however, due to in-house treatment, no additional sanitary wastewater would be generated. Consequently, the quantities of fresh and wastewater contractually agreed with the Wasserverband Strausberg-Erkner (WSE) should also suffice for the further expansion. The expanded wastewater treatment would be an in-house effort by Tesla on the factory premises."
