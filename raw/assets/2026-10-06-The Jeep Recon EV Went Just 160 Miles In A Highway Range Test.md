# The Jeep Recon EV Went Just 160 Miles In A Highway Range Test

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-10-05

---

The Recon's boxy body and stubby wheels are made for off-roading, but drivers still need to drive on the highway to get to trails.

- The 2026 Jeep Recon is one of the most inefficient EVs in the U.S. today.
- It has an official EPA-estimated combined range of 222 miles.
- At highway speeds, that figure drops dramatically, as discovered by Car and Driver.

The Jeep Recon loves to eat electricity every time it hits the road. It was designed to go where no other electric car can, so it has a boxy body, non-aerodynamic bumpers and big, chunky wheels. Owners are trading long-range convenience for off-road capability.

Car and Driver drove a brand-new Recon on the highway at a constant 75 mph and managed just 160 miles on a full battery. That's 62 miles less than the combined EPA estimate and 36 miles shorter than the EPA highway-only estimate.

Considering the Recon's generous 94-kWh usable (100.5 kWh total) battery pack, its efficiency is among the worst of any EV on sale in the U.S. Car and Driver reported about 1.7 miles/kWh, a smidge better than the 1.4 miles/kWh of the GMC Hummer EV and Mercedes-Benz G 580 Electric.

InsideEVs tests cars at a constant 70 mph, and the Recon is expected to travel further at lower speeds. Sticking to the 10-to-80% rule of thumb, only 70% of the battery is available, which means a real-world range of 112 miles at a constant 75 mph. At lower speeds it does better — the instrument cluster showed around 2.5 miles/kWh during InsideEVs' short time with the Recon.

That means plenty of charging on the road, probably at trailheads, with Jeep claiming a 10-to-80% time of 23 minutes. That's decent, but bills will be higher than on most EVs, simply because the off-roader needs more energy.
