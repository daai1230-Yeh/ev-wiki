# GM's Next-Generation EV Platform Has 30% Less Wiring And 20% Less Battery

**Author**: Tim Levin | **Date**: 2026-10-21

---

GM announced a fourth-generation EV platform at its GM Forward event in New York. It uses lithium-manganese-rich (LMR) batteries to reduce cost, and GM says the architecture yields EVs that are 15% lighter and 20% more energy efficient.

GM gave no firm timeline or nameplates; President Mark Reuss said the launch would happen "very shortly", and a spokesperson clarified the "highest-volume" segments means crossover SUVs. The idea is to use fewer expensive batteries while delivering the right range. Reuss: "when the U.S. market is ready for EVs in large numbers, we will be there for them."

GM cites 15% less vehicle mass, 20% greater efficiency, 20% less battery capacity overall, and 25% better aerodynamics; part count inside each motor falls 40%. LMR is cheaper than high-nickel cells and 33% more energy-dense than LFP. Zach Walker, GM director of global EV architecture: "equivalent or more range for 20% less battery... changes everything." The prismatic cells are structurally integrated, allowing lower, more aerodynamic packaging; GM wouldn't say whether cell-to-pack or modules are used.

Ford's $30,000 Fathom pickup on its Universal Electric Vehicle platform is due late next year and appears farther along.

GM is also replacing dozens of control modules with a single Central Compute Unit near the front of the vehicle (perception, propulsion, safety, chassis), following Tesla and Rivian. Result: 30% less wiring, 40x more processing power, 50% faster cellular connectivity. It debuts in the 2028 Cadillac Escalade IQ, critical to GM's first eyes-off driving system, and will appear across GM's gas lineup starting with pickups and three-row SUVs.
