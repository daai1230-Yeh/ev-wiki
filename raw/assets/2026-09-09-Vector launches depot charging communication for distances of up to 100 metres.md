# Vector launches depot charging communication for distances of up to 100 metres

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-09-08 13:30

---

German supplier Vector presents vSECC.InPlug, a solution for DC charging infrastructure that enables up to 100 meters between the charging vehicle and central power electronics.

The Stuttgart-based company is expanding its portfolio for the electrification of bus and truck depots. With vSECC.InPlug, Vector presents a communication controller board integrated directly into the DC charging plug. This design allows the charging plug and central power electronics to be spatially separated by a significantly greater distance.

According to Vector, this addresses a technical limitation of conventional DC charging architectures. In these systems, the possible distance between the charging communication controller and the vehicle is limited to approximately 7.5 metres. With vSECC.InPlug, the manufacturer now supports distances of up to 100 metres between the vehicle and the central charging infrastructure. The current company announcement does not yet include details on initial customer projects or experiences from regular depot operations.

Technically, Vector relocates part of the charging communication directly into the DC plug. The board installed there handles power-line communication with the vehicle. An additional controller remains centrally located in the switchgear cabinet, continuing to manage the charging process, communication protocols, power electronics, and backend connectivity.

## Vector moves charging communication into the plug

For depot operators, this spatial separation is meant to create greater planning flexibility. Charging points could be distributed directly at the parking spaces, while the power electronics are consolidated at a central location with suitable power supply and cooling. Vector has not specified the impact of this architecture on installation effort or costs.

In collaboration with LAPP Mobility, Vector has already integrated the board into a DC charging plug, which is expected to be available through LAPP. Charging infrastructure manufacturers can also integrate vSECC.InPlug into their own plug and system concepts. Supported standards include IEC 61851, DIN SPEC 70121, ISO 15118-2, and ISO 15118-20, meaning that bidirectional charging is also possible. As our readers will know, smart and bidirectional charging protocols enable charging when electricity is cheap and support grid balancing. These capabilities are increasingly advantageous for large vehicle fleets with schedules – especially as grid operators all over the world are generally in urgent need of energy storage capacity with the increasing build-out of electrification and renewable energy.

In parallel, Vector is launching vCharM.DMS, its first depot management system for small and medium-sized bus depots. The software consolidates information such as vehicle status, parking space occupancy, maintenance details, and scheduled departures. For electric bus fleets, the software can be connected to Vector's charging and energy management system, vCharM, making it possible to incorporate departure times and required charging levels into the charging schedule. Vector has not disclosed any information about initial customers or pricing.

The launch of the new connector expands Vector's portfolio into the field of charging communication. In 2025, the company presented the MCS-compatible test system vCTS.performance for charging powers of up to 3.84 MW and started series production of a communication controller for megawatt charging systems (MCS).

Vector will showcase vSECC.InPlug and vCharM.DMS at IAA Transportation 2026 in Hanover. The booth will be located in Exhibition Hall 13, Stand E10, from 15 to 20 September.
