# First drive: Opel Corsa GSE delivers 207 kW for €39,900

**Author**: Kasimir Hupe | **Date**: 2026-09-03

---

Opel is sending Germany's best-selling small car into the race as a fully electric hot hatch. On the handling course in Pachfurth, the Opel Corsa GSE impresses with better performance than its starting price of 39,900 euros suggests. However, for everyday use, some features are missing, which slightly tarnish the otherwise positive overall impression.

Opel first announced the Corsa GSE in February, at the time still largely without concrete data. However, the car is now officially available to order from 1 September, with a starting price of €39,900.

During the first test drive on public roads east of Vienna, and on the closed handling course of the Driving Camp, the Corsa GSE revealed more about itself than a data sheet ever could. On tight right-hand bends, early on the accelerator, the full 345 Nm of torque arrives with no delay, and the front axle pulls out of corners cleanly without drama — remarkable given 207 kW (281 PS) is sent through just the front wheels, thanks largely to the Torsen limited-slip differential.

The Corsa GSE ("Grand Sport Electric") sprints to 100 km/h in 5.5 seconds, outpacing all its OPC-era predecessors, though top speed is capped at 180 km/h. Up front sits a permanent-magnet synchronous motor shared with the Mokka GSE and Mokka GSE Rally, saving development costs. The battery is the same 54 kWh gross / 51 kWh net pack as the standard Corsa Electric, with thermal management adapted for higher continuous loads.

Three driving modes are available: Sport (207 kW/281 PS), Normal (170 kW/231 PS), and Eco (140 kW/190 PS, capped at 150 km/h). The Alcon four-piston brake system delivered consistent pedal feel throughout the test day, and the active sport sound was judged tasteful and switchable.

Everyday compromises include no auto-hold function, coarse two-stage regenerative braking with no paddle adjustment or true one-pedal drive, a tight rear seat, and a boot of just 267–1,042 litres. Charging tops out at 100 kW DC (27 minutes for 20–80%), which the review calls the car's weakest point for a 2026 model.

Real-world test consumption averaged 19.8 kWh/100 km, implying a real-world range of around 260 km against Opel's WLTP claim of 354–374 km.

Compared to rivals: the mechanically identical Peugeot e-208 GTi costs more (€44,900) for the same specs; the VW ID. Polo GTI is slower (6.8s to 100 km/h) but has a longer range (424 km WLTP) at a similar price (~€39,000); the Alpine A290 GTS is the weakest performer and priciest (€44,700) but most exclusive.

**Verdict**: The Opel Corsa GSE is fast, nimble, and stable, with a highly competitive starting price and standout limited-slip differential and brakes. Its main weaknesses are middling 100 kW charging power and the lack of a one-pedal drive mode. It's positioned as a strong farewell to the current Corsa generation, ahead of an all-electric STLA Small-based successor due in 2028.
