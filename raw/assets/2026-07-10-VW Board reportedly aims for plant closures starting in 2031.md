# VW Board reportedly aims for plant closures starting in 2031

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-07-09

---

Led by Group CEO Oliver Blume, VW's executive board reportedly plans to phase out production at the Zwickau and Emden plants by 2031, with the Hanover factory set to follow in 2032, and the Neckarsulm Audi plant in 2034. This information was obtained by Spiegel ahead of the VW Supervisory Board meeting.

Until now, internal documents had suggested Volkswagen would not close production sites before 2032 at the earliest. Both scenarios remain proposals from the executive board for the time being. The supervisory board was expected to offer "massive resistance to the plan," according to Der Spiegel, and the group's powerful works council was likely to fiercely oppose the course of action.

Manager Magazin reported at the end of June that the board is pursuing a far more radical restructuring plan than previously known, citing an executive board presentation titled "Group Target Picture" for 2030 that outlines the closure of four plants and a reduction of the global workforce by 100,000 jobs.

As Der Spiegel revealed in advance, VW's CEO plans to cut 50,000 jobs by 2030, alongside the plant closures targeted from 2031 onwards. The group currently employs around 657,000 people. Planned investments are also set to be reduced from the previous €180 billion for 2027–2031 to €135 billion.

The four at-risk plants — Hanover, Zwickau, Emden, and Audi's Neckarsulm — were already flagged during an April supervisory board meeting "due to high costs." Around 40,000 employees work at these four sites, which together have an annual capacity of approximately 750,000 vehicles. Zwickau and Emden are dedicated BEV production sites: Zwickau manufactures the VW ID.3, ID.4, ID.5, Cupra Born and Audi Q4 e-tron, while Emden produces the ID.4, ID.7 and ID.7 Tourer. Hanover is the headquarters of VW Commercial Vehicles, producing the ID. Buzz and ID. Buzz Cargo series. Neckarsulm currently manufactures primarily ICE and hybrid models, though its Böllinger Höfe facility produces the electric Audi e-tron GT.

**Update 10 July 2026**

Following the meeting at Volkswagen, further details emerged: the Board of Management presented the Supervisory Board with a package of twelve initiatives aimed at restructuring the company, alongside its Vision 2030 roadmap. According to media reports, the most contentious issues have been deferred for the time being.

The plans include reducing the model portfolio by up to 50 per cent, cutting product complexity by as much as 75 per cent, lowering annual production capacity from ten million to around nine million vehicles, and streamlining administrative and development functions. According to Handelsblatt, no final decisions have been taken on potential plant closures, additional job cuts, or a fundamental reorganisation of the Group's structure.

Works council chair Daniela Cavallo reportedly reacted angrily following Thursday evening's meeting, calling on Group CEO Oliver Blume to address employees and respond unequivocally to reports about the Board's alleged restructuring plans.
