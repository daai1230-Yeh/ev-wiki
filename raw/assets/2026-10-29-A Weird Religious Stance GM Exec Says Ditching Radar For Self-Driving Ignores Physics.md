# 'A Weird Religious Stance:' GM Exec Says Ditching Radar For Self-Driving 'Ignores Physics'

**Author**: Nico DeMattia | **Date**: 2026-10-27

---

Tesla is famous for its camera-only approach to autonomous driving. GM's chief product officer thinks deleting radar "ignores physics."

General Motors plans to launch an autonomous driving feature for the highway by 2028, and it isn't thinking about paring back its hardware to a camera-only setup like Tesla uses.

"If you scale back to vision-only, physics suggests you'll be in trouble in certain situations. Radar is so cheap that dropping it doesn't make sense—dropping it is a weird religious stance that ignores physics," GM Chief Product Officer Sterling Anderson said in an interview with The Verge. GM's beefed-up version of Super Cruise will launch in the 2028 Escalade IQ and will use radar, lidar, and cameras.

Tesla has stuck with a camera-only approach for personal cars, despite that road taking far longer than Elon Musk anticipated; Musk has also heavily criticized rivals that use lidar. Most other AV developers see additional sensors as critical for safety, and Waymo said cameras "aren't enough" in a recent blog post.

Anderson, who led Tesla's Autopilot program before cofounding Aurora, said: "Lidar operates on a similar wavelength to visible light and helps co-train camera systems to get better faster. Cameras can't see through dense fog or measure instantaneous velocity like radar can."

GM's goal is an autonomous highway system that works even with the driver asleep and won't suddenly ask the driver to take over (eventually expanding to other roads). "This is not Level 3," Anderson said: the car will offer a "graceful countdown" saying the driver needs to take over in something like 10 minutes, and otherwise pull safely off the off-ramp until the driver takes over.
