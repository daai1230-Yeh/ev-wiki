# The Lucid Air Sapphire Beats Genuine Mid-Engine Supercars From 0-200-0 MPH

**Author**: Nico DeMattia | **Date**: Aug 20, 2026

---

*Big luxury sedans aren't supposed to be capable of such nutty performance but the fastest Lucid put on a show for Car and Driver.*

Fast sedans aren't anything new — the BMW M5 CS, Cadillac CT5-V Blackwing, W223 Mercedes-AMG S63, and the Lucid Air Pure have all impressed. But during Car and Driver's instrumented testing, the Lucid Air Sapphire not only surpassed 200 mph — impressive enough for an electric sedan — it did so and then came to a stop before both the Corvette ZR1X and McLaren 750S.

Just getting to 200 mph is a massively impressive feat for any car, requiring immense power and slippery aero to overcome drag. Yet the Lucid Air Sapphire, a five-seat, four-door luxury sedan, reached 200 mph seven seconds faster than the Corvette ZR1X. In the gap it took the Corvette to catch the Lucid at 200 mph, the Sapphire could have gone from 0-60 mph almost four times.

That's the same ZR1X that holds the title of the fastest car C&D has ever tested. How did the Lucid beat that 1,250-horsepower hybrid V8? The 'Vette's big wing likely had something to do with it — the ZR1X is also meant to be a track weapon, so its aero applies heavy downforce at high speed.

Even wilder: the Lucid was 13 seconds faster to 200 mph than the McLaren 750S, one of the very best supercars in existence. The McLaren uses a V8 with no hybrid all-wheel-drive trickery and 740 horsepower; its lone advantage is being about 2,300 pounds lighter than the Lucid.

After reaching 200 mph, all three cars had to hit the brakes. Both the Corvette and McLaren braked from 200-0 faster than the Lucid, but by less than a second. That means the Lucid Air Sapphire went from 0-200-0 mph in 25.7 seconds — 6.1 seconds faster than the Corvette ZR1X and 12 seconds faster than the McLaren.

Both supercars would almost certainly be faster on track, where their weight advantage would help. But the Air Sapphire is no slouch in handling either — freakishly quick and shockingly agile for a big luxury sedan.
