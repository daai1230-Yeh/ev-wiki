# EVP secures majority against binding EV quotas for large corporate fleets

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-10-27

---

The EU Commission's draft Auto Package included binding mandates for member states from 2030 to accelerate electric company cars. In the European Parliament, those binding quotas are now likely to be downgraded to voluntary targets.

Automobilwoche reports that the Conservatives (EVP), Social Democrats (S&D) and Liberals (Renew) – the "Centre Platform" – agreed that member states will not face binding EV quotas for corporate fleets. EVP MEP Jens Gieseke, Chair of the Transport Committee: "For corporate fleets, we have found a good compromise with the other groups that protects fleet operators from major burdens."

The Commission had proposed country-specific requirements for electrifying company cars from 2030, inspired by Belgium's successful company car taxation scheme, which sharply raised EV sales. Under the compromise, the binding targets become "aspirational targets": they stay voluntary and member states face no penalties for missing them.

The compromise clarifies how the Auto Package will change before the final plenary vote. Central is the relaxation of the combustion engine phase-out: majorities in the Industry and Transport Committees support softening CO₂ rules. The Centre Platform proposes that manufacturers need only cut new-car CO₂ emissions by 95% instead of 100% (vs 2021) by 2035, with a further 7.5 percentage points possible through use of European-made, climate-friendly steel – i.e. a target as low as 87.5%. According to the groups, this leaves room for new combustion and hybrid registrations after 2035.

Next, per Spiegel, the Environment Committee votes in early November, with the plenary likely in December.
