# Geely to trial solid-state batteries from 2027

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 20.08.2026 - 12:00

---

Geely has announced plans to test solid-state batteries in its first vehicles starting in 2027. The most powerful variant is expected to achieve up to 500 Wh/kg, enabling ranges of over 1,000 km.

Geely is advancing the development of its own solid-state batteries and has now provided a clear timeline for their deployment. As reported by CarNewsChina, real-world validation has been underway since this year. By 2027, the technology is set to be introduced in a pilot programme using vehicles from across the Geely Holding brand portfolio.

The group's subsidiaries include the core Geely brand, Zeekr, Lynk & Co, Volvo, Polestar, Lotus and Smart. It has not yet been disclosed which brands or models will be first to feature the solid-state batteries, nor how many vehicles will participate in the pilot.

The announced energy density is particularly ambitious. Geely has outlined several variants, with the most advanced expected to achieve up to 500 Wh/kg — a figure that, according to media reports, refers to the battery pack level, not just individual cells. For comparison, the first generation of Zeekr's mass-produced 'Golden Brick' battery achieves around 128 Wh/kg at pack level.

Geely claims EVs could achieve ranges comparable to diesel-powered cars, targeting over 1,000 km per charge, though it remains unclear how this was determined or which driving cycle it refers to. Details on capacity, charging performance and cell chemistries have not been disclosed. In materials development, Geely is collaborating with US chemical corporation Dow, which has developed an adhesive for solid-state cells that remains stable from minus 40 to plus 120 degrees Celsius.

## 2027 set to be a key year for solid-state batteries

BYD also plans to deploy solid-state batteries in demonstration vehicles from 2027. According to Chinese media reports, the automaker is also planning small-scale production of cells with a dual solid-electrolyte system. BYD confirmed in 2025 that it had produced its first solid-state cells, though broader commercialisation is not expected until the end of the decade.

CATL is also developing the technology, aiming for pilot production by 2027, but has recently tempered expectations. CEO Robin Zeng stated in June that CATL's solid-state technology is at level four of a nine-level technological readiness scale.

2027 is thus shaping up to be a year for initial pilot applications rather than large-scale series production. Geely has explicitly referred to a pilot operation at this stage and has not indicated when equipped vehicles might reach customers regularly.
