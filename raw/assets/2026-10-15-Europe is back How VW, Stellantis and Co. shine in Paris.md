# Europe is back: How VW, Stellantis and Co. shine in Paris

**Author**: Robin Engelhardt | **Date**: 2026-10-14

---

The Paris Motor Show is a magnet for the global automotive industry this week. Europe's sector is making it clear that it will not cede the electric vehicle field to China without a fight. Above all, Stellantis and VW are presenting numerous innovations.

A few years ago, the automotive industry had a quasi-sacred triangle: Geneva, Paris and Frankfurt. After the pandemic, the Geneva Motor Show disappeared entirely, and the IAA in Frankfurt has been in a kind of identity crisis ever since its move to Munich. The 2024 Paris edition was largely driven by Chinese manufacturers; two years later European automakers have significantly refreshed their lineups and there is a sense of renewed momentum.

## The retro wave now reaches Stellantis

Citroën is bringing back the iconic 2CV as a concept: under one tonne, production from 2028, around €15,000. Fiat revives the Multipla with a stand mimicking an Italian piazza. Stellantis is leveraging vehicles with character, such as the new Opel Corsa GSE (54 kWh battery, 281 PS). The partnership with Leapmotor plays a key role. Renault showed concepts such as an electric Espace, the R8 and an R4 beach buggy.

## 'True Volkswagen' takes shape

The core VW brand aligns the ID.Polo, ID.Cross and ID.Tiguan visually with their combustion siblings. The Group's 'Urban Electric Car Family' comprises the Škoda Epiq, Cupra Raval and ID.Cross/Polo, and a wider audience gets its first look at the new Audi A2 e-tron.

## Smart steals the show

The new Smart #2 was the most anticipated car. Mercedes-Benz refocuses on compact and mid-size vehicles, with the electric C-Class as an 800-volt benchmark.

## The quiet achievers

Hyundai adds the Ioniq 3 and Kia has the EV2. Porsche, BMW and almost all Japanese automakers are absent, as are Hongqi, GWM and MG.

## Conclusion

After this show, no one believes the era of motor shows is over. Europe's automotive industry has made clear it will not cede the field to China without a fight, reinforced by the French president's 'made in France' push on battery factories and reindustrialisation.
