# Waymo Plans To Have 20,000 Cars By The End Of 2027

**Author**: Rob Stumpf | **Date**: 2026-10-20

---

Waymo plans to grow its fleet of driverless cars five-fold by the end of next year, Semafor reported. That means moving from 4,000 cars to 20,000 in just 14 months.

The robotaxi race is on. While there are quite a few players in the running, the clear leader in the U.S. is Alphabet's Waymo. Waymo aims to quintuple its fleet from roughly 4,000 cars today to 20,000 by the end of 2027, Semafor reported on Monday, citing people familiar with the plans. The company reportedly wants to grow its fleet to 8,000 vehicles by close of 2026.

Last month, Waymo locked in $5 billion in debt financing which it said it will use to "accelerate the continued expansion of our fully autonomous ride-hailing service across the United States and internationally."

Expanding means more than just buying more Zeekr vans and outfitting them with computers and sensors. Waymo also needs teleoperators to remotely steer cars when they create a traffic jam, plus the infrastructure and operational might to run a taxi service — depot staff who clean and charge the cars.

Waymo has been steadily expanding its fleet and operating areas throughout 2026. As of September, it was serving 15 major cities across eight states, making it America's most significant robotaxi operator. Waymo averages upwards of half a million paid rides every week. It is also testing services in London and Tokyo, with expansion announced for Canada, Singapore, and Spain.

To grow service, Waymo will need to scale its fleet considerably with help from partners like Hyundai and Geely. Hyundai recently said it plans to make "tens of thousands" of Ioniq 5 robotaxis for Waymo, while Geely-owned Zeekr's Ojai people movers have also started rolling out in three cities.

Tesla, by contrast, only started autonomous rides last year and isn't close to matching Waymo's current scale. If Waymo does quintuple its driverless fleet in roughly the next year, the gap between it and the rest of the pack could grow even greater.
