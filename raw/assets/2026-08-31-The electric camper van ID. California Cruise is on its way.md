# The electric camper van ID. California Cruise is on its way!

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-08-28

---

Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles is presenting the ID. California Cruise at the Caravan Salon 2026 – the first representative of a new all-electric California family. The recreational vehicle is based on the ID. Buzz Pro. Prices for the electric camper in the Bulli format start at 60,761 euros.

Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles is currently turning heads at the trade fair in Düsseldorf with the ID. California Cruise, Volkswagen's long-awaited fully electric camper van, featuring specially developed camping equipment. Based on the rear-wheel-drive ID. Buzz Pro, the recreational electric vehicle is available with either a short or long wheelbase. The first deliveries of the fully electric ID. California Cruise are planned for early 2027, with additional California models set to follow by 2028.

The California brand has its roots in 1988, when the German carmaking giant first introduced a motorhome based on the VW Bus, the VW California. Several generations of camper vans followed. These iconic vans have become known as the Eurovan in North America, or a transporter or camper van in Europe and the UK, while other territories call it a combi van. These different names illustrate the popularity of these vehicles, named to describe a lifestyle as much as the vehicle. The official Volkswagen title "California" evolved from a model name into an independent lifestyle and motorhome brand within Volkswagen.

The vehicle on display in Düsseldorf is the first California based on an electric platform. It is designed so "the new vehicle brings the freedom, flexibility and everyday practicality of the California into the age of electric mobility," as described by its maker, Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles (VWN). The starting price is set at €60,761 for the short-wheelbase ID. California Cruise. The price for the long-wheelbase version has not yet been announced, but it is clear that the extended equipment is reflected in the cost. The camping variant always includes the Interior Style Package, the Comfort Package, and the PowerSocket as a discharge adapter for the vehicle-to-load function as standard. These packages are optional extras for the standard ID. Buzz.

Technically, the electric camper is based on the ID. Buzz Pro with a 210 kW rear-wheel-drive powertrain. The short-wheelbase version features a 79 kWh battery integrated into the underbody, while the long-wheelbase variant houses an 86 kWh battery. The range for the camping version has not yet been disclosed, but the ID. Buzz Pro achieves 460 or 498 km (WLTP), depending on the battery size. Particularly useful for camping is the new vehicle-to-load function in the ID. Buzz can supply power to external devices with a continuous output of 2 kW and a peak output of 3.6 kW. Visually, the ID. California Cruise stands out with exclusive seat covers, California lettering on the rear, and special "Cruise" decals on the vehicle sides.

The camping equipment includes a sleeping solution for two people, with a lying surface measuring 2.03 metres in length and 1.20 metres in width. The mattress is a newly developed, nine-centimetre-thick folding model, which VWN claims ensures optimal comfort. Additional features include ventilation grilles for the front side windows, blackout elements for all windows, two camping chairs, and a camping table. Optional extras include a panoramic glass roof, which also comes with matching blackout functionality.

VWN has also announced a new California Mode, specifically designed for camping. This mode allows users to adjust lighting and comfort functions during trips and ensures a continuous power supply to the USB ports. The air conditioning can heat or cool the interior, providing a comfortably tempered sleeping environment for up to 48 hours continuously, depending on the battery charge level and the temperature difference, as the manufacturer explains.

Notably, Volkswagen's official statement does not mention the different weights of the new camper models. As our colleagues at the German edition of InsideEVs have reported, weight has long been a challenge in developing a camping version of the ID. Buzz. Just a few years ago, VWN's headquarters in Hannover stated that a camper based on this electric van would become too heavy. It is not yet clear exactly how the famous carmakers have been able to balance the tare weight and payload.
