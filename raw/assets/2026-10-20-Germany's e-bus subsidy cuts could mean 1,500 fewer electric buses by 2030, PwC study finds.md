# Germany's e-bus subsidy cuts could mean 1,500 fewer electric buses by 2030, PwC study finds

**Author**: Editorial Staff | **Date**: 2026-10-15

---

Germany's draft federal budget for 2027 envisages reducing electric bus subsidies from €403 million in 2026 to €130 million, a 68% cut that could put around 1,000 jobs at risk, according to a new PwC study commissioned by the German public transport association VDV and Klima-Allianz Deutschland.

The study, published on 15 October 2026, estimates that the German urban bus sector generates €1.6–2 billion in annual economic value added, supporting 10,300–12,600 jobs across vehicle manufacturing, suppliers and infrastructure. VDV says the proposed €273 million reduction would affect electric bus procurement and the domestic supply chain. A coalition of industry, business, civil society and trade unions has called on the Bundestag to reverse the cuts. Germany halted its e-bus funding programme in early 2024 before launching a new call.

**Market status**: Germany had about 4,750 zero-emission buses at end-2025 (battery-electric, fuel cell and trolleybuses), growing about 48% per year. In 2025, 1,397 of about 2,900 new city buses were electric (48% share). About 70% of the electric fleet had received federal support. VDV says more than 6,000 locally zero-emission buses were in service as of 1 July 2026 (about 12% of scheduled-service buses). Daimler Buses, MAN and Iveco account for about 72% of electric buses; European manufacturers hold more than 85%.

**Cost gap**: a standard battery-electric bus costs about €580,000 versus €310,000 for diesel (+€270,000); articulated €780,000 versus €410,000 (+€370,000), excluding charging infrastructure, grid connections and depot conversion.

**Regulatory drivers**: the Clean Vehicles Directive requires 65% clean vehicles in new urban bus procurement, half zero-emission (about 950–1,900 e-buses a year). Germany's climate programme targets 50% electric urban buses by 2030 (about 2,550 purchases a year), and EU CO2 standards require 90% zero-emission new urban buses from 2030 (about 2,900 a year).

**Three funding scenarios 2027–2030** (from 5,750 e-buses in 2026):
- Draft budget (€130m in 2027): about 10,250 e-buses by 2030, about 1,100 added per year.
- Transformation (€403m kept): 11,800 by 2030, about 1,500 per year.
- Lead market (about €511m a year, +25%): 17,500 by 2030, about 2,900 per year.

Under the draft budget, about 6,000 new diesel buses would be bought in 2027–2030: roughly 7,200 fewer e-buses (about 60%) than Lead market and about 1,500 fewer than Transformation. PwC's modelling assumes operators extend diesel use, buy new diesel buses, source cheaper non-EU e-buses or use municipal funds. Compared with Transformation, the draft would mean about €150 million less annual gross value added and around 1,000 jobs affected.

VDV Vice-President Paul Hemkentokrax said that by 2030 at least 65% of newly procured buses must be zero-emission, which cannot be achieved without federal and state subsidies; a cut of more than two-thirds would slow procurement and push many operators back to diesel.

*Source study: PwC "E-Bus-Förderung: Investitionen in den Wirtschaftsstandort, Beschäftigung und Klimaschutz"*
