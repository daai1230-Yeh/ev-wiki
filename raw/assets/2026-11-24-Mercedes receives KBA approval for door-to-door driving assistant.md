# Mercedes receives KBA approval for door-to-door driving assistant

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-11-23

---

Mercedes-Benz has become the first automaker in Germany to receive regulatory approval for a point-to-point driving assistant. The German Federal Motor Transport Authority (KBA) approved the MB.Drive Assist Pro system based on UN Regulation R171. This enables automated door-to-door driving at SAE Level 2, including complex urban traffic situations.

With active navigation, the system can initiate lane changes, position the vehicle in the correct lane, turn at intersections, and stop and restart at traffic lights. It can also handle merging onto and exiting highways. A total of 27 sensors – including ten cameras, five radar sensors, and twelve ultrasonic sensors – monitor the vehicle’s surroundings. A high-performance computer processes the data with up to 254 TOPS. One of the system’s unique features is that, even when MB.Drive Assist Pro is active, the driver can still make steering adjustments without the system automatically deactivating.

“Innovation is about making a real difference to our customers. MB.DRIVE ASSIST PRO does exactly that. From busy urban traffic to country roads and motorways, our intelligent assistance makes driving more comfortable and less demanding. Its approval in Germany underlines our ambition to remain at the forefront of automated driving,” said Mercedes Chief Technology Officer Jörg Burzer.

## Assisted – but not autonomous – driving

Despite its expanded functionality, MB.Drive Assist Pro remains an Advanced Driver Assistance System (ADAS) at SAE Level 2. This should not be confused with autonomous driving (from SAE Level 4). In other words: with MB.Drive Assist Pro, the driver retains responsibility for the driving task. While they are allowed to take their hands off the steering wheel, they must still pay close attention to the traffic and be ready to intervene at any time (“hands-off, eyes-on”).

Unlike the already introduced Mercedes Drive Pilot, this is not about Level 3 automated driving, where the driver can temporarily hand over the driving task under certain conditions and even look away from the road (“hands-off, eyes-off”). However, that system has significant limitations, such as only allowing highway driving at restricted speeds. MB.Drive Assist Pro, on the other hand, is designed for everyday use and can also be used in urban traffic and on rural roads.

## BMW and Tesla pursue similar approaches

Mercedes is not the first manufacturer to receive DCAS approval in Germany. BMW already obtained approval under UN R171 for its highway assistant in the Neue Klasse in November 2025. This enables hands-free driving on highways up to 130 km/h but is limited to highway scenarios. Navigation-guided assistance from start to destination, including urban traffic, is planned by BMW from 2027. According to BMW, the required real-world tests have already been completed. However, regulatory confirmation is still pending and is expected in early 2027.

Tesla is also working on a more advanced assistance system. The system previously known as Full Self-Driving (Supervised), which has been marketed in the EU as Tesla Assisted Driving since October due to political pressure, received a provisional type approval from the Dutch authority RDW in April 2026. Several EU countries have nationally recognised this approval, but Germany has not yet done so. The Federal Ministry of Transport supports a timely Europe-wide approval. A decision at the EU level is still pending. Most recently, Tesla received a provisional national approval in Italy on 20 November.

## Market launch planned for 2027

The legal basis for the door-to-door functionality of MB.Drive Assist Pro is, in any case, UN Regulation R171 for Driver Control Assistance Systems (DCAS). The harmonised legal framework is expected to enter into force in January 2027 and will pave the way for introduction in further markets. Mercedes plans – as previously announced – to launch an early-access programme in Stuttgart and Munich starting in December 2026. The regular market launch in Germany is scheduled for the first half of 2027. For vehicles with suitable hardware, existing models may also receive the functions via an over-the-air update. It is not yet known which vehicles will initially support the system. However, the all-electric Mercedes CLA is likely to be among them, as Mercedes has already demonstrated the solution in the USA in January.
