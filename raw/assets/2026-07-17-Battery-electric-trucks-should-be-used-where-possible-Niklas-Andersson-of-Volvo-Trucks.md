# "Battery-electric trucks should be used where possible" – Niklas Andersson of Volvo Trucks

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-07-16

---

Volvo Trucks enters a new era of electric trucking. A few weeks ago the manufacturer unveiled the FH Aero Electric in Gothenburg, promising a range of up to 700 kilometres. In this interview, Niklas Andersson, Director Electric Solutions at Volvo Trucks, explains what customers can expect from the new generation of battery-electric trucks. Andersson is responsible for electric truck sales across Scandinavia, the Benelux, Germany, Austria and Switzerland.

Volvo Trucks aims to be the first manufacturer to enable a battery-electric truck to complete a full day of long-haul operation without recharging, equipping its upcoming flagship with around 100 kWh more usable battery capacity than most competitors. On 15 June, Volvo opened order books for its second-generation battery-electric trucks; series production starts in early September on flexible lines alongside combustion models in Ghent (Belgium) and Tuve (Sweden), as the first-generation FH, FM and FMX Electric are phased out.

**On competitiveness:** "We're taking a significant step forward. With a range of up to 700 kilometers, we're leading the industry," alongside proven flexibility and a wide variety of vehicle variants. Range is "the central issue when people consider switching from diesel to electric trucks."

**Key lesson from the first generation:** "You can achieve much more with an electric truck than you initially think." Customers who "dare to simply give it a try" make the fastest progress — testing a first truck across applications, then expanding their fleet.

**Testing:** Most is internal, using Volvo's own proving grounds, winter testing in northern Sweden and summer testing in Spain, plus field trials with selected operators running pre-series vehicles.

**Strategy:** As a global company in 130 countries, Volvo Trucks will continue to rely on internal combustion engines, fuel cells and battery-electric drivetrains depending on local conditions. But "wherever it is possible to operate an electric vehicle, we should do so," since across the entire energy chain a battery-electric vehicle "is by far the most energy-efficient solution."

**On collaboration:** "Partnership is the new leadership" — manufacturers, transport companies, logistics customers and public authorities all have a role. Asked when battery-electric trucks will start contributing to profitability, Andersson replied: "They already are."
