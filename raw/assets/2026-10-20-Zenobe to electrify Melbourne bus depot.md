# Zenobe to electrify Melbourne bus depot

**Author**: Ciaran Daly | **Date**: 2026-10-19

---

CDC Victoria, a subsidiary of Australian firm ComfortDelGro, has appointed the fleet electrification company Zenobē to electrify its Truganina bus depot in western Melbourne. The site will feature charging infrastructure for over 130 electric buses in order to support the introduction of the first electric bus fleet to the city's western corridor.

According to the partners, Zenobē will design and install the depot's charging infrastructure, including any associated civil and electrical works. The planned installation comprises 65 dual-lead DC chargers, with provision for a further 20 charging points to accommodate future fleet growth. Zenobē has reportedly already modelled the depot's power requirements and charging demand to help size the grid connection.

The project will support the expansion of bus services in western Melbourne; CDC routes 170, 180, 190 and 192 will see an additional 470 weekly trips, alongside increased frequencies and longer operating hours. The state aims to transition its public bus fleet to zero-emission operations by 2035.

Jeff Wilson, CDC Victoria CEO, said the electrification of the Truganina depot is a significant milestone that will support the rollout of electric buses. Gareth Ridge, Zenobē Australia New Zealand Country Director, said Truganina has been designed around both how CDC's fleet will operate today and how it will grow, and noted Zenobē has delivered similar infrastructure alongside Metroline in the UK.

The project forms part of CDC Victoria's delivery of the Victorian Government's 'Metropolitan Zero Emission Bus' franchises. In 2024, the state awarded three franchises to CDC Victoria, Kinetic, and Dysons, with the programme aiming to replace 600 diesel buses with battery-electric models by 2035.
