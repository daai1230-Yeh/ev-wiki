# Germany: hydrogen truck mobility 'alliance' moves forward

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-09-03

---

An alliance is forming in Germany to accelerate the commercialisation of heavy hydrogen (H2) trucks and associated infrastructure. Daimler Truck, the Volvo Group, and Toyota are joining forces, supported by Bosch, Air Liquide, TotalEnergies, TEAL Mobility, and MB Energy — a bid by domestic players to establish a hydrogen ecosystem before China, which is already ahead on H2 truck rollout, extends its lead into Europe.

The alliance's goals will be presented in detail on 15 September at IAA Transportation in Hanover. The manufacturer trio's inclusion is unsurprising: Daimler Truck and Volvo Group have cooperated for years on fuel cell technology through their joint venture Cellcentric, which Toyota joined as a third equal partner this year.

Daimler Truck said the alliance wants to "accelerate the commercialization of hydrogen-powered mobility and build the ecosystem needed to scale it along the whole value chain," in parallel with continued battery-electric truck rollout. Planned activities include hydrogen refuelling stations "along key strategic corridors in Europe" and ensuring hydrogen supply at competitive prices. The stated aim is for Germany to bring together "the full ecosystem needed to scale the deployment of hydrogen trucks by 2030," combining supportive policy, a strong OEM offering, and integrated supply chain and infrastructure.

The German government is separately building an initial hydrogen truck network, having received over 500 applications for its first funding call, with awards due in the second half of the year. Currently €220 million in subsidies is available in Germany for hydrogen mobility infrastructure, in line with the EU's Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation (AFIR); the funding also covers hydrogen internal combustion engines.

The article notes that Daimler Truck and Volvo Trucks bet on hydrogen early alongside their BEV roadmaps but underestimated the pace required: Cellcentric's large-scale production facility in Weilheim an der Teck has faced multiple delays, Daimler's Next GenH2 Truck is entering customer testing (e.g. with Dachser) but series production has slipped to the 2030s, and Volvo Trucks — quiet on fuel cell trucks recently — will showcase a unit at IAA Transportation.
