# Renault 5 gains more range and new entry-level LFP version

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 25.08.2026

---

Renault only announced lower prices for the electric Renault 5 a few days ago. Now, the French carmaker has revealed technical updates that increase its range. A new entry-level version with an LFP battery is also joining the line-up, although it will not be available in Germany for the time being.

Renault has updated one of its bestselling models, the battery-electric Renault 5. Both the Urban Range version with a 40 kWh battery and the Comfort Range model with 52 kWh have received technical and aerodynamic improvements. As a result, their WLTP ranges increase to up to 315 and 430 kilometres, respectively. The smaller battery version gains three kilometres, while the larger battery adds 20 kilometres.

A more significant change in some European markets is the introduction of a new entry-level version of the electric compact car. Renault is combining an 80 kW motor with a 36.5 kWh LFP battery, providing a range of up to 305 kilometres. The two existing powertrains deliver 90 and 110 kW and use 40 and 52 kWh batteries, respectively, both with nickel-based NMC chemistry. Reports that Renault could introduce LFP batteries for the R5 first emerged in British media over the summer, and Renault has now confirmed those plans.

The new entry-level powertrain will be offered with the Five trim. In markets such as France, where the Five has so far combined the 90 kW motor with the 40 kWh battery, the new 80 kW/LFP configuration replaces that version. The new Five also gains DC fast-charging capability at up to 80 kW, which was previously unavailable. Renault Deutschland announced the Five trim in 2024 with a starting price of €24,900, but the version has yet to appear in the German configurator — the new LFP Five is therefore unlikely to be offered in Germany for the time being.

Renault has also made changes across the wider R5 range: efficiency improvements, additional range, new colours and greater versatility. A lower ride height and redesigned exterior mirrors improve aerodynamics on existing versions, alongside optimised battery electronics. The R5 with the 40 kWh battery improves slightly from 312 to 315 kilometres, while the 52 kWh version goes from 410 to 430 kilometres.

The new LFP version uses the 80 kW "Gen 2 Evo" motor from the Twingo E-Tech Electric (limited to 60 kW in that A-segment model). The 36.5 kWh LFP battery uses a cell-to-pack design, eliminating the module level and integrating the battery as a structural element of the underbody. Renault says the 0–50 km/h sprint takes just 3.5 seconds, with charging up to 6.6 kW AC and 80 kW DC.

Renault will also expand the R5's colour and material choices in the coming weeks, adding DeZir Red and Dolomite Grey, new gold-finish 18-inch alloy wheels, and new roof/door decals. Inside, the car gains a ventilated inductive smartphone charging tray (Techno trim and up) and a 4G-compatible e-call system, plus an automatic driving-mode switch that removes the manual Multi-Sense steering wheel button (manual changes remain available via the OpenR Link screen or voice command).

Previously optional safety systems — blind-spot warning, exit assist, cross-traffic alert, and Active Driver Assist with emergency stop assist — are now standard on the top Iconic Five and Roland Garros trims. Renault will also introduce Google's Gemini assistant via an over-the-air update, replacing Google Assistant.

Renault will present the updated model range at the 2026 Paris Motor Show (12–18 October). The new model year is already available to order in all European markets where the model is sold. Renault announced German pricing for the new model year last week, cutting prices across all variants by €1,710 — the R5 now starts at €26,290 for the Evolution 120 Urban Range in Germany:
