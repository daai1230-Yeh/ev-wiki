# This Tiny New EV Motor Cuts Weight And Size For Impressive Efficiency

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-07-17

---

China's Geely has unveiled a new electric drive system, "Thunder," that integrates just about all the hardware and software functions into a single unit. The setup has 30% less high-voltage wiring and 15% less low-voltage wiring compared to conventional systems. It weighs just 165 pounds but makes 328 horsepower, and is extremely efficient.

With a claimed efficiency of 93.8%, Geely reckons this is a production-level record in the industry, thanks to the tight integration of multiple components that, in conventional drive systems, live in separate enclosures and require additional wiring to work correctly.

The "Thunder" has 12 components sitting inside the same enclosure, including the motor, motor controller, DC-to-DC converter, on-board charger, vehicle control unit, active pre-charge controller, low-voltage and high-voltage battery management systems, and power domain gateway. Four AI-powered software features are also baked into the system.

Geely claims it has eliminated over 180 components by going down this route, resulting in a power density of 11.8 kilowatts/kilogram. The unit's magnesium housing is just 12.8 inches (325 mm) tall, freeing up to one cubic foot (28 liters) of trunk capacity compared to a conventional drive unit. The compact packaging reduces high-voltage wiring by 30% and low-voltage wiring by 15%, leading to a total weight of just 165 pounds (75 kg) — 15% less than a comparable mainstream drive unit.

The first car powered by "Thunder" is the Geely Galaxy TT, an electric sports sedan making 328 horsepower in single-motor form and 570 hp in the range-topping dual-motor all-wheel-drive variant. The EV is based on an 800-volt architecture and can be specced with a 52.4-, 63.8-, or 75.2-kWh battery, with a maximum CLTC range rating of 450 miles (725 km).
