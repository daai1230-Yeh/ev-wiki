# Dacia Spring, born in China, now made in Europe

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-09-08 11:11

---

Dacia has unveiled its second Spring generation and is bringing the former China model to Slovenia. There, the A-class electric car will roll off the production line alongside the Twingo E-Tech, which has the same technical platform. The entire range of the Dacia Spring is priced below €20,000, starting at €17,900 ($20,800).

With subdued colours, robust styling, and most accessories available only as extras, Renault's Romanian subsidiary Dacia stays true to its approach with the Spring, offering an entry-level model for electric mobility. This electric car suits buyers who will compromise on extras for affordability or for a second car.

Although the small A-segment car is now moving to the Renault Group's new RG-EV platform and could theoretically match the technical level of the Twingo E-Tech Electric, corporate strategists think in terms of target groups and positioning, so even in its second generation, the Spring remains an EV that demands tolerance from its drivers — with optional DC charging and a base variant without navigation — while attracting price-sensitive customers.

How the Renault Group aims to prevent cannibalisation can be read, quite literally, in the car's colour palette, as recent Renault models are almost all eye-catching and vibrant. The new Dacia Spring, by contrast, offers just four paint options: Agave, Slate Grey, Diamond Black, and Glacier White. It doesn't get much more understated. While the E-Twingo arrives as a hip, playful retro EV, the Spring exudes its charm as an unpretentious budget model with SUV styling.

## Two trims – same technology

The most important change is that the Spring will now be produced at Renault's Novo Mesto plant in Slovenia, rather than in Wuhan, China, where the first generation was built by its partner, Dongfeng. For the powertrain, Dacia is adopting a one-size-fits-all strategy. While there are two trim levels called 'Expression' and 'Journey', the underlying technology remains identical, based on a front-mounted 60 kW motor paired with a 27.5 kWh LFP battery. Both components are already familiar from the Twingo E-Tech. The LFP battery, featuring cell-to-pack architecture, is said to deliver a WLTP range of up to 250 km—25 km more than before, though slightly less than the electric Twingo, likely due to less favourable aerodynamics.

The base motorisation of the Spring now accelerates from 0 to 100 km/h in 12.5 seconds — compared to nearly 20 seconds at its initial market launch. Not that this matters much in urban driving. The model remains a city car through and through. Nevertheless, this evolution highlights the progress Dacia's EV has made, even if the sprint performance is slightly lower than that of the currently available 75 kW motor.

What's far more important for daily use is that the new Spring still only supports AC charging as standard. The built-in 6.6 kW onboard charger allows charging from 15 to 80 per cent in 2 hours and 55 minutes. An optional 11 kW AC charger is also available, which includes V2L capability, so the vehicle can recharge other devices or equipment, while camping, for example. But for those venturing on long trips with their Dacia, there's no alternative but to add the DC charging option with a maximum of 50 kW DC. This is an improvement over the previous Spring's 40 kW, but still modest. On the bright side, the small battery means charging from 10 to 80 per cent takes "only" 30 minutes. This doesn't change the fact that with a 250 km WLTP range, any long journey becomes a segmented, multi-stop affair. Even the improved efficiency, now rated at 12.7 kWh/100 km according to WLTP, won't help much.

## Dacia Spring grows 18 cm in width

In terms of dimensions, the Spring now measures 3.85 metres in length and 1.74 metres in width (excluding mirrors). This makes it 15 cm longer and a full 18 cm wider than its predecessor. Dacia thus moves to the upper end of the A-segment, promising customers significantly improved interior space. The boot capacity increases to up to 337 litres, or 1,283 litres with the rear seats folded down. Additionally, an 18-litre frunk is available as an option under the bonnet.

According to Dacia, the new Spring is the first Dacia model to benefit from the Renault Group's new development programmes. The carmaker says these aim to accelerate the vehicle development cycle, reduce development time to under two years, and simultaneously lower development costs. The Group has achieved this with substantial support from China, as the Renault Group's dedicated EV research and development centre in Shanghai, named ACDC, contributed the entire engineering for the critical powertrain. This is meant to deliver the kind of manufacturing speed China is famous for. Interestingly, this is happening even though Renault and Dacia are not active in China with their brands. Renault recently emphasised that the Shanghai facility has advanced projects "by working closely with local partners and new industrial companies" — namely CATL for battery design and Shanghai e-drive for the powertrain.

## Dacia Spring weighs just 1.2 tonnes

To keep costs in check, numerous decisions were made to simplify the vehicle. As mentioned, the new Spring is available in just one technical configuration and only in the four colours already named. In the base version, Dacia not only omits DC charging and navigation but also a central display and a rear-view camera. Overall, the Spring in its stripped-down form weighs just over 1.2 tonnes.

This brings us to the equipment levels. The new Spring Expression standard has 15-inch steel wheels, central locking with remote control, electric front windows, and electrically folding mirrors. It also includes manual air conditioning, automatic headlights, rear parking sensors, and an electric handbrake. Dacia also mentions the Media Control system, which uses steering wheel controls to display media information and phone calls on a digital instrument cluster. The Journey trim adds a rear-view camera, a 10.1-inch central display with wireless smartphone mirroring via Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, navigation, and connected services. In this case, the Spring also rides on 16-inch steel wheels.

## The Dacia Spring is a no-nonsense commuter car

Dacia reiterates that the Spring is designed as a no-nonsense commuter car—simple, clear, and functional. The design retains almost vertical front and rear ends typical of SUV design, combined with the familiar black plastic cladding. A new feature is the Dacia lettering at the rear, now integrated directly into the trim strip. Inside, the dashboard houses the aforementioned 7-inch digital instrument cluster, while the higher trim level includes a centrally mounted 10.1-inch multimedia touchscreen. Notably, Dacia does not mention sliding rear seats or a fold-flat passenger seat, which are features available in the nearly identical Twingo E-Tech. It is likely that neither option will be offered for the Spring.

Dacia sets the market launch of the second generation for September 15; the model will be available for order starting then. The first deliveries are scheduled to take place shortly before the end of the year. According to the company, the Spring has sold around 210,000 units across Europe since its introduction in 2021, including approximately 50,000 in Germany. Most recently, in autumn 2025, the model received a technical update, replacing its previous electric motors (33 kW and 48 kW) with new units rated at 52 kW and 75 kW. Additionally, the Spring became the first vehicle in the Renault Group to receive an LFP battery last year.

Customers have not had the full range of options for the current Spring for some time. Dacia recently removed the entry-level 'Essential' trim from its lineup, citing high demand. At the beginning of the year, the base model was temporarily available in Europe for as little as €11,900 thanks to a €5,000 discount offered directly by Dacia. Currently, the range starts with the 'Expression' trim at a list price of €18,700, while the manufacturer discount has been reduced to up to €3,000. However, this discount can now be combined with government incentives.

Regarding the pricing of the new Spring, Dacia promises that the entire model range of the new Spring will be priced under €20,000 (excluding government incentives), starting at €17,900. The Spring will not remain the only EV in Dacia's lineup for long. By 2030, the Renault subsidiary plans to introduce three more fully electric vehicles. The French brand's cooperation with China will likely play a key role in this as well.
