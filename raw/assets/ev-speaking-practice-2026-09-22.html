1. Warm-Up3 min　暖身

When you think about what makes an electric car expensive, what parts come to mind first?
當你想到電動車為什麼貴，最先想到的是哪些零件？
Have you ever bought something where the most important component was made by a company you had never heard of?
你有沒有買過某樣東西，其中最關鍵的零件是出自一家你從沒聽過的公司？
If one country supplied almost all of a material your industry depended on, would that worry you, or would you see it as simply efficient?
如果某一個國家幾乎供應了你所處產業所依賴的全部某種材料，這會讓你擔心，還是你覺得這只是有效率而已？

2. Read Aloud6 min　朗讀短文

Inside every lithium battery there is a thin plastic sheet called a separator. It sits between the positive and negative electrodes and stops them from touching. If it fails, the cell short-circuits and can catch fire. Nobody advertises it, and almost no driver has ever heard of it, yet without it there is no electric vehicle.
每一顆鋰電池裡面都有一層薄薄的塑膠膜，叫做隔離膜。它夾在正極與負極之間，防止兩者接觸。一旦失效，電芯就會短路，甚至可能起火。沒有人會拿它做廣告，幾乎沒有駕駛人聽過它，但少了它就沒有電動車。
Demand for this invisible layer is growing fast. According to SNE Research, batteries registered worldwide between January and July of 2026 contained roughly 11.7 billion square metres of separator film, an increase of 24.9 percent compared with the same period a year earlier.
這層看不見的材料需求正在快速成長。根據市調機構 SNE Research，2026 年 1 月到 7 月間全球登記的電池，共使用了約 117 億平方公尺的隔離膜，比前一年同期成長 24.9%。
What makes the figures interesting is where the growth ended up. Markets outside China grew even faster, by 43.2 percent. But Chinese suppliers captured most of that growth anyway. In the second quarter of 2026 their global market share reached 91.5 percent, while Korean and Japanese producers actually lost ground.
這些數字有趣的地方在於：成長最後落到了誰手上。中國以外的市場成長得更快，達 43.2%。但這些成長的果實大多仍被中國供應商拿走。2026 年第二季，中國業者的全球市佔率達到 91.5%，而南韓與日本廠商反而是衰退的。
The squeeze is pushing rivals to reposition rather than compete head-on. South Korea's SK Innovation is absorbing its separator subsidiary and shrinking its Chinese operations. Another Korean firm, WCP, is shifting toward separators for energy storage systems. Japanese materials groups such as Toray and Asahi Kasei are building capacity in North America instead. The logic is the same in each case: stop fighting on price, move to products where margins survive.
這股壓力促使競爭對手選擇重新定位，而不是正面對決。南韓 SK Innovation 正在吸收合併旗下的隔離膜子公司，並縮減中國業務。另一家韓廠 WCP 則轉向儲能系統用的隔離膜。東麗、旭化成等日本材料集團則改為在北美擴建產能。背後邏輯都一樣：不要再打價格戰，轉進還能保住利潤的產品。
Not every corner of the market is commoditised, though. Ultra-thin separators of around five micrometres, and films with high heat-resistant coatings, are still in short supply, and buyers cannot always secure the volumes they want. Chinese wet-process prices have already rebounded from their lows, and industry utilisation has climbed back above 80 percent.
不過，並不是市場的每個角落都淪為大宗商品。約 5 微米的超薄隔離膜，以及具高耐熱塗層的膜材，供給仍然吃緊，買方不一定能拿到想要的量。中國濕法隔離膜的價格已從低點反彈，產業產能利用率也回升到 80% 以上。
A similar dependence is taking shape one generation ahead, in solid-state batteries. This month the American developer Factorial Energy signed a joint development agreement with Mitsui Kinzoku of Japan, one of the very few firms that has mastered sulphide-based solid electrolytes. Mitsui has been refining that material since 2016 and already supplies an estimated 90 percent of the ultra-thin copper foil used by the semiconductor industry.
類似的依賴關係，正在下一個世代的固態電池上成形。本月美國電池開發商 Factorial Energy 與日本三井金屬簽下共同開發協議；三井金屬是全球極少數掌握硫化物固態電解質的公司之一。它自 2016 年起就在打磨這項材料，並且已供應半導體業界約九成的超薄銅箔。
Factorial's chief executive put the point bluntly: battery innovation cannot be done by one single company. Her firm is backed by Mercedes-Benz, Stellantis and Hyundai-Kia, and its Solstice platform promises energy density of up to 450 watt-hours per kilogram. Even so, it still needs a Japanese chemicals company to make the electrolyte work at scale. The cell may carry a carmaker's badge, but the hard part often happens several layers upstream.
Factorial 的執行長把話說得很直白：電池創新無法由單一一家公司完成。該公司背後有賓士、Stellantis 與現代起亞的支持，其 Solstice 平台宣稱能量密度上看每公斤 450 瓦時。即便如此，它仍然需要一家日本化學公司才能讓電解質量產可行。電芯上頭印的也許是車廠的標誌，但真正困難的部分，往往發生在供應鏈上游好幾層的地方。

5. Useful Expressions3 min　實用句型

A accounts for X percent of B. — Chinese suppliers account for more than ninety percent of the separator market.
用來表達「佔比」。比 "the share is..." 更自然、更口語，開會報告時很好用。
The growth did not translate into ... — Faster demand growth outside China did not translate into higher sales for Korean firms.
「成長並沒有轉化成……」。談數據漂亮但結果不如預期時，這個句型非常精準。
Rather than competing head-on, they ... — Rather than competing head-on, they shifted to energy storage customers.
「與其正面對決，他們選擇……」。描述策略轉向時使用，聽起來成熟老練。
The bottleneck sits ... — The bottleneck sits upstream, in materials, not in vehicle assembly.
「瓶頸在於……」。指出問題真正所在，討論供應鏈時很常用。
On paper ..., but in practice ... — On paper anyone can build a battery plant, but in practice the materials come from very few suppliers.
「理論上……，但實際上……」。用來對比理想與現實，是很好的轉折句型。

6. Discussion5 min　討論題

  1. Chinese suppliers reached 91.5 percent of the separator market without much public attention. Why do you think dominance in materials attracts less political reaction than dominance in finished cars?
    中國業者在隔離膜市場拿下 91.5%，卻沒引起太多關注。你覺得為什麼材料端的壟斷，比整車壟斷引來的政治反應少得多？
  2. Korean and Japanese firms are retreating to energy storage and North America. Is that a smart specialisation, or is it surrendering the main market?
    韓、日廠商退守儲能與北美市場。這是聰明的分工專業化，還是等於放棄主戰場？
  3. Some would argue that concentrated supply is simply the market rewarding the most efficient producer. Others call it a strategic risk. Which argument do you find stronger, and why?
    有人主張供應集中只是市場在獎勵最有效率的生產者；也有人稱之為戰略風險。你覺得哪個論點比較有說服力？為什麼？
  4. Factorial's CEO said innovation "can't be done by one single company." Do you agree, or do you think that is something companies say when they need partners?
    Factorial 執行長說創新「無法由單一公司完成」。你同意嗎？還是覺得這只是需要夥伴時才會說的話？
  5. Taiwan has strong materials and precision manufacturing. Where in this battery supply chain could Taiwanese companies realistically compete?
    台灣在材料與精密製造上有實力。在這條電池供應鏈中，台灣廠商實際上可以切進哪些環節？
  6. If you were advising a carmaker, would you pay a premium to keep a second, non-Chinese supplier alive, even if you never needed it?
    如果你是車廠顧問，你會願意付出較高成本去維持一家非中國的備用供應商嗎——即使你可能永遠用不到？

7. Wrap-Up2 min　總結

In sixty seconds, summarise the article in your own words. Cover three things: what a separator does, how concentrated the market has become, and why solid-state batteries may repeat the same pattern.
用 60 秒、以自己的話摘要這篇文章。涵蓋三件事：隔離膜的作用、市場集中到什麼程度，以及固態電池為何可能重演同樣的模式。
Then state your own position using this frame: "What strikes me most about this is ..., because ... . If I had to choose, I would ..."
接著用這個句型表達你的立場：「這件事最讓我印象深刻的是……，因為……。如果一定要選，我會……」
For example: What strikes me most about this is how quietly the concentration happened, because nobody was watching a plastic film. If I had to choose, I would accept slightly higher costs in exchange for a second source.
例如：這件事最讓我印象深刻的，是這種集中發生得如此悄無聲息，因為沒有人在盯著一層塑膠膜。如果一定要選，我會接受略高的成本，換取第二供應來源。
