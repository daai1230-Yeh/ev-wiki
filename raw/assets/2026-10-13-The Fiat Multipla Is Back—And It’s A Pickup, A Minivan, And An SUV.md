# The Fiat Multipla Is Back—And It’s A Pickup, A Minivan, And An SUV

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-10-12

---

The Multiplay concept has a sliding rear section that transforms the body from SUV to truck in no time.

- Fiat is bringing back the Multipla’s heritage with a new concept.
- The Multiplay uses a sliding rear section that turns the family car from a ruggedized minivan into a pickup truck.
- There’s also a dedicated seat for pets and an integrated bike rack.

The first-generation Multipla (1950s) seated six in a body under 3.5 m long. The second generation arrived in 1998 as a family car with divisive styling. The Multiplay adds another layer of versatility with a modular body.

The rear part slides in and out, turning the SUV-like minivan into a pickup. In minivan form it has a 6+1 seating configuration, with six places plus a modular “Petsenger seat” that works as a dedicated pet space or child-seat mount; its turquoise base has a detachable outer section usable as a leash, and a folding bowl sits in a door compartment.

In pickup form, one rear backrest slides forward to back the “Petsenger” base, making a four-seater. When parked, the front passenger seat can swivel rearward and the dashboard becomes the backrest for a lounge-like chair. A panoramic glass roof and big side windows make the cabin airy. With the roof slid forward there is room for camping gear or two bikes, with the integrated roof spoiler acting as a bike rack.

The paint scheme is watermelon-inspired: bright green exterior, red-and-pink interior. The horizontal dash strip is inspired by the Mediterranean horizon, with a hidden display invisible when the car is off. Outside, a horizontal pixelated LED light bar sits under the windshield, with rounded daytime running lights in the large bumper.

Fiat did not say what powers it, but short overhangs and a long wheelbase suggest an EV platform.

Citroën tried something similar: the 2003 C3 Pluriel was a small two-door usable as hatchback, convertible and roadster pickup, but its intricate roof suffered reliability and water-ingress problems.
