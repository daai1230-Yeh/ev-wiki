# NHTSA opens probe into 1.2 million Teslas

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-08-03

---

The US traffic safety authority NHTSA has launched a preliminary investigation into around 1.2 million Tesla Model 3 and Model Y vehicles. Earlier model years may have suspension defects that could lead to a loss of steering control.

The NHTSA has opened an investigation into around 1.2 million Tesla Model 3 vehicles from the 2018–2020 model years and Model Y vehicles from the 2021–2023 model years. The probe follows 156 owner complaints alleging that the front lower lateral link — a key suspension component connecting the steering knuckle to the vehicle's subframe — can detach unexpectedly while driving, which can severely impair steering and directional stability.

The failure typically occurs without warning; while some owners reported unusual noises beforehand, most incidents had no prior visual warning messages, and in many cases the vehicle became undriveable and required towing.

NHTSA said it has not received any reports of crashes, injuries or fatalities related to the issue. Its Office of Defects Investigation (ODI) is examining the complaints to determine the scope of the problem and identify the underlying technical cause.

Tesla has previously recalled vehicles over lower lateral link issues — around 2,800 vehicles in 2021 and a further 422 Model 3 cars in 2023 — but the complaints under the current investigation appear to extend beyond those earlier recalls and may point to a different technical cause.

This preliminary evaluation is the first stage of NHTSA's defect investigation process. If the agency concludes the issue stems from a safety-related design or material defect, it could lead to a formal recall affecting a much larger number of vehicles.
