# India: Delhivery to add up to 2,000 electric light commercial vehicles

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 2026-11-17

---

Indian logistics service provider Delhivery has announced plans to deploy between 1,500 and 2,000 electric small trucks over the next 12 to 18 months. The company will use the new EVs for its intra-city logistics operations.

Delhivery plans to acquire the new electric commercial vehicles from Tata Motors, Euler Motors and Montra Electric and has signed memoranda of understanding with all three manufacturers. From Tata Motors, the country’s largest commercial vehicle manufacturer, it will source the Intra EV Pickup, which offers a maximum payload capacity of 1,750 kg.

The Intra EV Pickup measures 4,734/4,745 mm in length, 1,750 mm in width and 2,010 mm in height, with a 2,600-mm wheelbase. Its cargo bed is 2,960 mm long, 1,750 mm wide and 460 mm high. It has a single motor producing 72 kW and 230 Nm of torque, giving it a top speed of 80 km/h and a maximum gradeability of 23 per cent. A 28.2 kWh LFP battery pack powers the motor and delivers a range of 211 kilometres (certified) or 125 to 140 kilometres (real-world estimate).

Euler Motors will supply its Turbo City Plus, which could be a derivative of the Turbo EV 1000 City small truck, which offers a maximum payload capacity of one tonne. For reference, the latter is 3,950 mm long, 1,535 mm wide and 2,440 mm high, with a 2,080-mm wheelbase. It has a single motor producing 16 kW and 120 Nm of torque, giving it a top speed of 65 km/h. Its 14.7 kWh battery pack delivers a range of 197 kilometres (certified) or 135 kilometres (real-world estimate).

From Montra Electric, Delhivery will acquire the Eviator, which costs 1.644 million rupees (approximately €15,900) in its base configuration and offers a maximum payload capacity of 1,707 kg. The company offers it with two cargo bed options, both measuring 3,140 mm long and 1,740 mm wide, with one bed measuring 415 mm high and the other 491 mm high. The Eviator features a single motor that generates 80 kW and 300 Nm of torque. Customers can choose from 32 kWh, 40 kWh and 50 kWh battery packs, which are claimed to deliver real-world ranges of over 140 kilometres, over 170 kilometres and over 200 kilometres, respectively.

The planned rollout of 1,500 to 2,000 EVs in partnership with Tata Motors, Euler Motors and Montra Electric is the first step towards Delhivery’s goal of deploying nearly 5,000 electric commercial vehicles within three years. “Scaling fleet electrification means finding solutions that work across the diverse needs of our network without compromising operational efficiency,” said Prashant Gazipur, Chief Operating Officer, In City Operations, Delhivery.

Delhivery has converted about 12 to 13 per cent of its intra-city fleet to EVs and aims to increase that figure to more than 50 per cent in the next two to three years. The company aims to fully transition to EVs on routes shorter than 200 kilometres within four to five years. It is also piloting charging facilities at its sites, starting with Delhi-NCR.
