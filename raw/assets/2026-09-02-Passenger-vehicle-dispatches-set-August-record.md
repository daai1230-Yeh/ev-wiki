# 450,000 and counting: Passenger vehicle dispatches set August record

**Author**: Sohini Das | **Date**: 2026-09-01

---

Passenger vehicle (PV) dispatches to dealers are estimated to have touched a record for August at around 450,000 units, as India's leading carmakers reported strong double-digit growth ahead of the festival season — only around 1.7% below July's 457,810 units, the highest-ever July for the industry. The near-40% year-on-year jump comes on an unusually weak base: PV wholesales had fallen 8.8% to 321,840 units in August 2025 after manufacturers recalibrated dispatches ahead of the mid-2025 GST rate rationalisation.

Maruti Suzuki India (MSIL) estimates August industry wholesales at around 450,000 units, plus or minus 2,000–3,000 units. Growth was strong across the largest manufacturers: Tata Motors led with domestic PV wholesales up 59% YoY to 65,253 units; Mahindra & Mahindra's SUV sales rose 50% to 59,257 units; MSIL's domestic PV dispatches rose 35% to 176,971 units; and Hyundai Motor India's sales rose 23.6% to a record 54,396 units for August. Together these four accounted for nearly 79% of the estimated PV market for the month.

Manufacturers are preparing for the festival selling period: MSIL has around 180,000 pending bookings and dealer inventory of only about 16 days. "Whatever we are producing, we are able to just dispatch it to our channel partners," said Partho Banerjee, senior executive officer, marketing and sales, MSIL, adding there were virtually no vehicles sitting at factories. MSIL is producing around 8,000 vehicles a day and is adding 250,000 units of capacity each at Kharkhoda (Haryana) and Hansalpur (Gujarat) during FY27. The new Brezza has received more than 55,000 bookings in about 35 days.

Shailesh Chandra, MD and CEO of Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles, expects full-year industry growth to exceed 10%, though second-half growth could moderate below 10% due to the higher base. MSIL continues to expect the PV industry to grow by at least 10% in FY27: "We have given an outlook that this year industry should grow by 10 per cent. And we stand by that," Banerjee said, noting first-half growth rates were unlikely to persist in the second half because of the base effect.
