# Hybrids And Gas Cars Don't Make People As Happy As EVs Do: Study

**Author**: Tim Levin | **Date**: Jul 27, 2026 (updated)

---

New-vehicle owners are happier overall with their cars than last year, but EV buyers have the biggest smiles, according to JD Power's 2026 Automotive Performance, Execution, and Layout (APEAL) study, which gauges "owners' emotional attachment and level of excitement with their new vehicle." JD Power found that "electric cars outperform internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles in nearly every category" — EVs averaged 910 on the study's 1,000-point scale versus 850 for combustion cars (gas, diesel, and hybrids combined).

The biggest gap came in powertrain satisfaction, where EVs outscored ICE cars by 109 points — unsurprising given EVs are punchy, smooth, and quiet. Respondents also favored EVs across other categories: setup and starting, getting in/out, driving feel, driving comfort, infotainment, interior, and fuel economy.

EVs also posted the biggest year-over-year improvement of any powertrain type, up 22 points, reflecting incremental gains like more convenient NACS charging, range upgrades, and heat pumps. Notably, even as everyday buyers (not just early adopters) make up a growing share of EV drivers, overall enthusiasm hasn't waned — a contrast to public charging satisfaction surveys, where broader adoption brought more critical buyers and lower overall scores.

For this year's study, JD Power surveyed 78,514 owners of 2026 model-year vehicles about their first 90 days of ownership, covering 37 attributes. The findings echo other research: EV owners rarely go back to gas. A recent CDK study found 90% of EV owners plan to buy another EV, and 73% say their household will always own at least one EV.
