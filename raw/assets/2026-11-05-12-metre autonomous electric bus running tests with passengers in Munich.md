# 12-metre autonomous electric bus running tests with passengers in Munich

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-11-04

---

The MINGA project ("Munich's automated public transport with ridepooling, solo buses, and bus platoons") is about to reach a decisive milestone: starting November 17, test users will be able to ride in an autonomous electric MAN bus. Over 2,000 people have pre-registered for a test ride.

MINGA has been running in Munich since 2023, managed by the City of Munich's Mobility Department and funded with approximately €13 million from the German Federal Ministry of Transport. It includes autonomous on-demand vehicles based on the VW ID. Buzz, tested on public roads without passengers, and an autonomous electric bus based on the MAN Lion's City E, which began trial operations in public traffic at the beginning of September. Equipped by partner Adastec with five LiDAR sensors, six radars, eight cameras and satellite positioning, the Lion's City 12 E runs on route 178 between Petuelring and Freimanner Hölzl, so far without passengers.

## Test users on board from 17 November

Route information was collected during initial test drives by MAN, MVG and SWM. From 17 November, passengers can ride the bus between Petuelring and Freimanner Hölzl, Monday to Friday. Test operations with users continue until June 2027, and a safety driver will always be on board.

Dr Frederik Zohm of MAN Truck & Bus said autonomous vehicles need people's trust, and that experience from MINGA will feed into future vehicle generations.

## New opportunities for public transport

Munich Deputy Mayor Mona Fuchs said autonomous buses will open new opportunities for local transport amid staff shortages and tight budgets, and stressed accessibility and passengers' sense of safety. The trials are monitored by the Technical University of Munich and the University of Stuttgart.

## Scaling autonomous buses soon

Sinaida Cordes of SWM and MVG said the key question is whether and how autonomous buses can be scaled up and integrated cost-effectively into scheduled services; the 12-metre bus is the first vehicle size trialled with potential for high-capacity regular operations.
