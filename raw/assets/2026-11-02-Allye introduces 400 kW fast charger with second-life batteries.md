# Allye introduces 400 kW fast charger with second-life batteries

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-10-30

---

British company Allye Energy unveiled the Mobile Energy Vault (MEV), a battery-buffered depot fast charger built from complete second-life EV battery packs. It delivers up to 400 kW without needing a more powerful grid connection.

- Variants: MEV200 (155 kWh) and MEV300 (232 kWh); both charge two vehicles at once. Based on the 800 V Max300 storage system, supporting 400 V and 800 V vehicles; "PackFlex" allows packs of different sizes, ages and chemistries in one system.
- Allye estimates 15–24 tonnes CO₂e saved per unit vs. new batteries (manufacturer's calculation).
- Concept: decouple grid and charging power — charge slowly from a weak connection, discharge at high power. Example: a waste-management depot (J&M Murdoch, Glasgow) avoided a ~£300,000 grid upgrade by using a Max300 on an existing 22 kW connection to supply ~200 kWh per day to two EVs. Limitation: it doesn't replace the energy volume; daily vehicles served still depend on how much can be recharged over the connection.
- UK depot grid connections can take 18–36 months; the MEV can be commissioned within hours and redeployed.
- Safety: cable cut monitoring, forensic markings, quick-release cables, cameras, pyrotechnic fuses, gas detection, fire barriers between packs.
- Pricing from £70,000, or "EV Charging as a Service" from £1,900/month (MEV200). First deliveries planned for Q2 2027.
