# Cylib acquires Bavarian EV battery recycling facility

**Author**: Carla Westerheide | **Date**: 21.07.2026

---

German battery recycler Cylib has acquired a mechanical processing facility in Bavaria with capacity for up to 10,000 tonnes of end-of-life EV batteries per year. The site will supply black mass to the company's hydrometallurgical recycling plant under construction in Dormagen.

Cylib is thus expanding its operations to cover the entire battery recycling chain from end-of-life batteries to recovered raw materials. The newly purchased facility has an annual processing capacity of up to 10,000 metric tonnes of end-of-life EV batteries and is expected to begin operations in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to approval under Germany's Federal Immission Control Act (BImSchG).

Cylib does not name the insolvent company, but it is likely to be Roth International, which had planned a recycling facility in Wernberg-Köblitz and recently filed for insolvency.

The Bavarian plant will mechanically process end-of-life EV batteries into black mass, which will then be supplied to Cylib's hydrometallurgical recycling facility currently under construction in Dormagen, North Rhine-Westphalia, where the material will be refined into battery-grade raw materials including lithium, graphite, nickel, cobalt and manganese.

"Cylib is built on a clear conviction: battery recycling has to be end-to-end, from the end-of-life battery to the high-quality critical raw material," said Dr Lilian Schwich, Co-CEO and Co-Founder of Cylib. "In our pilot plant we have demonstrated those end-to-end capabilities. In Dormagen we are building the high-quality material recovery end. With this acquisition we are now adding the end-of-life processing capability at large scale."

The acquisition was completed through insolvency proceedings and includes the processing line, building and property. According to the company, the site gives Cylib direct access to Germany's automotive manufacturing region and battery supply chains in southern Germany and Central Europe.

"Building large-scale processing capacity allows us to offer our customers and partners, including OEMs, tier-one suppliers, and battery manufacturers, even greater value and a more comprehensive service," said Dr Gideon Schwich, Co-CEO and Co-Founder of Cylib.
