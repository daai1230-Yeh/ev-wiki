# Volkswagen's EVs Are Selling Like Crazy. That's Actually Hurting Its Profits

**Author**: Nico DeMattia | **Date**: 2026-10-29

---

Volkswagen is heavily investing in EVs, has a new family of them coming to market, and customers are buying them in large numbers. But profits are hurting, because its EVs just aren't very profitable. VW sold 209,100 EVs globally last quarter, up 19.6% year over year, yet that does not help much.

"The market success of our electric vehicles is putting additional pressure on our earnings, as we currently earn less from electric vehicles than from combustion-engine vehicles," said Arno Antlitz, VW Group's CFO and COO. "After nine months, our operating return on sales before special items stands at 3.8 percent. That is too low to continue investing strongly in our future."

EVs should be simpler and cheaper to manufacture than ICE vehicles, but they are expensive to develop and make at the moment. Costs will fall as batteries get cheaper and production scales, and Tesla and some Chinese automakers prove EVs can be profitable, but for now legacy automakers struggle with the economics.

VW Group reported $6.6 billion in operating losses in Q3, its biggest loss since Dieselgate in 2015. Thin EV margins are not entirely to blame: a rapidly shrinking Chinese market is probably the biggest issue, with massive U.S. tariffs second. It also wrote down its stake in Porsche and took close to $9 billion in one-off charges. The disclosure of poor EV margins comes as VW pushes for looser CO2 regulations in the EU.

"We cannot afford to lose any time and must act quickly and decisively," Antlitz said.
