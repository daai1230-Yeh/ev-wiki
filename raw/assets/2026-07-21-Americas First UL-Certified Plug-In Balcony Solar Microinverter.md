# America's First UL-Certified Plug-In Balcony Solar Microinverter Is Here

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: Jul 21, 2026

---

Solar panels are great, especially in areas where the sun shines all year round. However, not everybody lives in a house or can afford costly setups — that's where plug-in systems come in. Now there's a legal and more affordable way to get some solar power flowing through an apartment or rental home.

The Hoymiles HiFlow Pro is the first solar microinverter in the United States certified to meet UL 3700, North America's new safety standard for plug-in solar panels, sometimes called balcony solar panels. It plugs directly into a 120-volt outlet and complies with the new standard.

The microinverter takes power from a mobile solar panel and funnels it directly into a regular household socket, minimizing grid usage and lowering energy bills. Built-in safeguards automatically cut power during a grid outage to protect line workers, and the microinverter cuts power to the exposed cable pins to avoid electrical shock risk. Hoymiles claims output voltage drops to less than 34V within 1 second after disconnection. The enclosure is NEMA Type 6 and IP67-rated, allowing installation outdoors from -40°F to 149°F.

One microinverter is rated for 360 watts, with 95.5% dynamic efficiency and 99.8% static efficiency. Up to four can be used in parallel for a maximum input of 1,200W. The company says units can be set up in about a minute via Bluetooth and Wi-Fi connectivity, with total annual energy production up to 2,403 kWh in ideal conditions and a two-year return on investment. A 180W kit with one solar panel retails for $499; a 360W kit is $700.

Eight states — Colorado, Connecticut, Maryland, Maine, New Hampshire, Utah, Vermont, and Virginia — currently have legislation allowing plug-in solar panels, with over two dozen more having introduced legislation. This type of system is hugely popular in Europe, especially Germany, and that trend is now moving toward the U.S. Because balcony solar doesn't require permanent installation, owners can pack up the equipment and take it with them when they move.
