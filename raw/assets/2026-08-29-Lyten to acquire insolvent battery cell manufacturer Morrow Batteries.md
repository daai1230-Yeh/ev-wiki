# Lyten to acquire insolvent battery cell manufacturer Morrow Batteries

**Author**: electrive | **Date**: 2026-08-27

---

US battery developer Lyten is now seeking to acquire the insolvent Norwegian battery cell manufacturer Morrow Batteries, after acquiring Swedish battery maker Northvolt. If the deal goes through, Morrow's battery technology activities could continue to be based at the company's site in Arendal.

Lyten has signed a letter of intent to acquire Morrow Batteries, in collaboration with Innovation Norway — Norway's state-owned economic development and investment bank — and Morrow's insolvency administrators. The Norwegian battery specialist initiated insolvency proceedings earlier this year for Morrow Batteries ASA, Morrow Technologies AS, and Morrow Industrialisation Center AS, and declared insolvency in early summer. Morrow opened a smaller industrial plant for LFP battery cells in 2024, and stated in June this year that it aimed to salvage at least part of its operations. These prospects now appear promising with the recently signed letter of intent from US battery developer Lyten.

According to their statements, the insolvency administrators and Innovation Norway had been negotiating with several bidders and finally selected Lyten as the preferred bidder. The three partners have agreed to keep the details of the letter of intent confidential.

Leon Bakkebo, Executive Vice President at Innovation Norway and responsible for financing and banking, stated: "As a secured creditor, our priority throughout the process has been to maximise value recovery and reduce execution risk." He said they support the trustee's decision to proceed with Lyten "because their proposal provides the strongest overall combination of financial recovery, transaction certainty and industrial credibility." The executive further noted that, if completed, "the transaction could also create a foundation for future battery-related activity in Arendal."

Lyten is already familiar with the acquisition of insolvent Nordic battery makers from its acquisition of the remaining assets of Swedish battery manufacturer Northvolt. Christian Bohne, the lawyer administering Morrow's insolvency estate, explained that the goal is to preserve value and seek a solution for the company's continued existence. He pointed out that employees have been severely affected by the insolvency. "Under demanding circumstances, they have made an important and highly valued contribution to the work to continue battery production in the Arendal region. Their commitment has been instrumental in enabling us to enter into this agreement."

In the short term, Bohne said the process secures around 70 jobs until autumn. In the long term, several hundred jobs at Morrow and orders for local material and service providers could be safeguarded. However, the insolvency administrator also warns that there are circumstances and conditions beyond their control that must be met before the transaction can be completed. Nevertheless, Bohne said, "Although we do not yet know whether those circumstances can be met, I am optimistic about Lyten's ability to execute."

Dan Cook, CEO and co-founder of Lyten, sees significant potential in Europe. He pointed out, "Europe has entered a sustained period of infrastructure investment, and batteries are now an important part of this critical infrastructure." Lyten aims to build a financially robust and scalable production base for batteries and energy storage in Europe, coupling technologies, supply chains, and production sites in the USA and Europe. "The Morrow team has built a business aligned with this strategy, making the assets a strong strategic fit for Lyten in Europe," said Cook.

According to Lyten, the company is evaluating the expansion of the Morrow site into a potential "Lyten Industrial Hub." The US company said it should be able to integrate battery production, upstream and downstream activities in the energy storage value chain, and industrial activities to support other critical sectors—including AI data centres, mobility, and defence. Lyten also plans a similar hub in Skelleftea at the former Northvolt site.

Northvolt and Morrow Batteries were long regarded as two of the few companies capable of producing battery cells in Europe but were unable to sustain activities. While Northvolt struggled with production quality during its ramp-up phase, Morrow Batteries fell victim to its small size in a consolidating market. The plant opened in summer 2024 at its headquarters in Arendal for the production of prismatic LFP cells with an annual capacity of just 1 GWh—far too small to supply a major car manufacturer. This capacity is sufficient for only around 25,000 small cars with a 40-kWh battery. For comparison, Volkswagen's subsidiary PowerCo states that its new cell factory in Salzgitter has an annual capacity of 20 GWh, enough for around 250,000 small cars.

Morrow Batteries was also targeting the construction of such a gigafactory. The Norwegian company had planned to expand the Arendal site in four stages to eventually reach 43 GWh. But this never materialised due to a lack of investors. Even the ramp-up of the existing 1-GWh plant proved to be a significant challenge, and the cell factory never achieved series production. As recently as January 2026—nearly a year and a half after the plant's opening—Morrow Batteries stated that it was now ready for series production but was adopting a "partnership-based approach," seeking long-term investments and strategic alliances to accelerate technology validation and secure offtake agreements. Morrow Batteries' management commented on the insolvency in June, stating that the background was complex. "The company has been operating in an early and capital-intensive industrialisation phase, while at the same time the global battery market has become more competitive with oversupply and resulting price pressure. In addition, increased capital costs, delays in the industrialisation process, and a more restrained investment market have made it significantly more difficult to secure financing."
