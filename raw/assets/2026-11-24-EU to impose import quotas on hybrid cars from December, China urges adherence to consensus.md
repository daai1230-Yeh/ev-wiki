# EU to impose import quotas on hybrid cars from December, China urges adherence to consensus

**Author:** Phate Zhang　｜　**Date:** 2026-11-24

**KEY TAKEAWAYS**
- The European Commission will impose provisional safeguard measures for up to 200 days, setting quotas at a little over 15% above 2025 import levels.
- China's Ministry of Commerce called on the EU to uphold the principle of non-discrimination and said it reserves the right to pursue dispute settlement under the WTO framework.

The European Commission announced it will impose provisional safeguard measures on imported hybrid passenger vehicles starting December 1, prompting China to urge the bloc to abide by their previously reached trade consensus.

In a statement released Tuesday, the European Commission said the provisional measures will take the form of import quotas aimed at protecting the EU auto industry from a sharp recent increase in imports.

While the measures implement the understanding reached at the second meeting of the EU-China Trade and Investment Consultations on October 9 to moderate China's hybrid exports to the EU, they will apply to imported vehicles from all origins, the European Commission said.

According to the announcement, the EU will allocate country-specific quotas to exporting countries that accounted for more than 2% of its imports in 2025, set at a little over 15% above each country's 2025 export volume to the bloc.

All other origins will share a residual quota, and the European Commission explicitly said that hybrid vehicles exceeding these quotas will not be permitted for import.

The provisional safeguard measures cover full hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs), plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs), and mild hybrid electric vehicles (MHEVs), and will remain in place for a maximum of 200 days.

China received an import quota of 191,780 vehicles — the highest among all countries — valid through June 18, 2027. Japan, in second place, received a quota of 170,889 vehicles.

However, compared with the rapid rise in China's hybrid vehicle imports over the past year, this quota falls significantly short of current import trends.

The EU initiated the global safeguard investigation on October 30, and definitive measures may be imposed within 9 months of the launch, a timeline that can be extended by up to 2 months.

Driven by global production overcapacity and increased trade-restrictive measures in other major markets, EU imports of hybrid vehicles have surged since 2023, leaving local automakers facing declining market share and an imminent threat of serious injury, the European Commission said.

China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) responded Tuesday that it noted the EU's decision to take provisional measures less than a month after launching the probe, expressing hope that the bloc will strictly follow World Trade Organization (WTO) rules, uphold the principle of non-discrimination, and conduct the investigation fairly and impartially.

MOFCOM said China will closely monitor the progress of the subsequent investigation and reserves all rights under the WTO framework, including pursuing dispute settlement and requesting trade compensation, to safeguard the legitimate rights and interests of Chinese industries.

Previously, on October 12, MOFCOM cited Eurostat data showing that EU imports of hybrid vehicles from China rose from 107,000 units in 2024 to 302,000 units in 2025, adding that both sides had reached an upward arrangement based on the 2025 baseline.

Hybrid vehicles have become a new focal point in EU-China auto trade, after the EU imposed definitive countervailing duties on China-made battery electric vehicles (BEVs) in October 2024, which did not cover hybrids.
