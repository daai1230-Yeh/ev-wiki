# The A2 E-Tron Is Audi's Cheapest EV Yet. But It Doesn't Skimp On Range

**Author**: Stefan Wagner (translated by Iulian Dnistran) | **Date**: 2026-09-07 7:51am ET

---

The new A2 follows in its predecessor's footsteps, with impressive efficiency and quirky styling.

The original Audi A2 was a bit of an oddity when it debuted back in 1999, but today it sits comfortably among the icons of the four-ring brand. Nearly 21 years after the aluminum-bodied efficiency special ended production, Audi is giving the idea of a distinctive, highly technical small car another chance.

The name is familiar, but the concept behind it is new: The new A2 e-tron will arrive at the end of 2026 as Audi's cheapest electric car, set to be built in Ingolstadt.

## Dimensions and design

At 170.1 inches (4.32 meters) long, the A2 e-tron sits squarely in the traditional compact segment. Thanks to the same underpinnings as the Volkswagen ID.3 Neo and Cupra Born, Audi's new entry-level EV boasts a rather impressive 108.8-inch (2,764 mm) wheelbase, the same as the larger Q4 e-tron. This leads to more interior room, despite having a smaller body overall.

Audi is also taking an efficiency-focused approach to aerodynamics. In its most economical configuration, the A2 e-tron has a drag coefficient of 0.24. To achieve that, engineers developed air curtains at the front, so-called gap reducers around the wheel arches, and an active cooling-air intake. The latter remains closed during normal driving and opens only when cooling is needed. The underbody is also completely flat.

According to Audi, the aerodynamic tweaks can reduce WLTP energy consumption in the 188-horsepower model with the efficiency package by up to 1.4 kWh per 100 miles (0.9 kWh/100 km), bringing it down to 20.6 kWh/100 miles (12.8 kWh/100 km). The efficiency pack is offered only for the 188 hp version and includes the sleeker S line exterior package, the sport suspension, and special 19-inch aero wheels with lower-rolling-resistance tires. The A2 rides on wheels ranging from 18 to 20 inches.

The doors are completely flush, with small grab handles, similar to those fitted to the Ford Mustang Mach-E. They operate electrically and include a secondary safety layer in case the vehicle electronics fail after damage. All power levels are expected to be able to tow up to 3,527 pounds (1,600 kilograms).

## Interior, cargo space, and infotainment

For infotainment, Audi uses its now-familiar panoramic display with an 11.9-inch digital instrument cluster and a 12.8-inch infotainment screen. An optional augmented-reality head-up display projects speed, navigation instructions, and media information into the driver's field of view.

Climate controls are handled through the central screen, though Audi has at least put some functions on the steering wheel, which features proper buttons and rollers. Cloud-based AI models support more natural voice interaction, and a route planner with charging stops is integrated.

Audi is among the first automakers to bring the Fidlock system into one of its vehicles. Fidlock combines strong magnets with a mechanical latch, allowing items such as a cupholder, a charging-cable bag, or a cargo-area strap to be attached easily and positioned flexibly. Three modular Fidlock adapters in the center console and a matching cupholder are standard.

Another new feature is optional Audi Connected Work. The system can link to a Microsoft 365 business account and, for example, summarize the day's appointments, read emails aloud, or bring a Teams call onto the screen.

Wireless smartphone charging is standard. The gear selector moves to the steering column, freeing up space for two charging trays that secure phones magnetically and charge at up to 25 watts.

Cargo volume measures 14.0 to 47.2 cubic feet (396 to 1,336 liters). There is also a frunk with a modest 0.5 cubic foot (13 liters) of volume.

## Powertrains and batteries

Technically, the A2 e-tron is based on a version of the Volkswagen Group's MEB 31 architecture, the platform also used by the ID.3 Neo and Cupra Born. Product manager Herman Verbeek noted that "MEB Plus" is a press term that does not officially exist: there is Variant 2 for the Audi Q4 e-tron and VW ID.4, and Variant 1, which the new A2 e-tron uses. The VW ID. Polo and Cupra Raval will use MEB 21, the smaller front-wheel-drive version.

There will be no A2 e-tron with electric quattro all-wheel drive — expected volumes in the compact segment were deemed too low.

Four output levels are planned at launch: 168, 188, 228, and 322 horsepower. All versions are rear-wheel drive, and the most powerful variant accelerates from 0 to 62 mph in 5.7 seconds, topping out at 124 mph. A more powerful version is in development — possibly an S2 e-tron in the 375-402 hp range.

Three battery sizes: 52, 61, and 84 kilowatt-hours. Audi uses LFP cells for the two smaller batteries (cell-to-pack design), while the 84-kWh NMC battery is module-based and reserved for the 228- and 322-hp versions.

Bidirectional charging is included. V2L allows external devices to be powered at up to 3.6 kW via a 230-volt outlet in the cargo area. V2H can turn the vehicle into an energy storage unit for the house (a compatible DC wallbox is required); at launch V2H is planned for Germany, Austria, and Switzerland.

## Price and equipment

The Audi A2 e-tron will be available to order in Germany starting in fall 2026, with deliveries beginning in December. The German-market starting price is €38,200 (about $44,400). A comparable Volkswagen ID.3 Neo starts at €33,995.

Standard equipment on the entry-level A2 e-tron is extensive: the large curved display, navigation, a rearview camera, Level 2 driver-assistance functions, wireless smartphone charging, and bidirectional charging. Three equipment packages — Tech, Tech Plus, and Tech Pro — will be offered. The heat pump costs extra.

The 61-kWh version starts at €41,900. In 228-hp form with the 84-kWh battery, the A2 e-tron costs €48,900. The top-spec 322-hp version is priced at €51,200. There are currently no plans to sell the A2 e-tron in the United States.

## The bottom line

Technically, the new Audi A2 e-tron is a polished VW ID.3 Neo tuned for maximum efficiency. There are certainly worse starting points, but the designers have managed to give it a character of its own and, surprisingly, quite a bit of presence. The first impression is promising, including the perceived quality and thoughtful packaging of the interior.
