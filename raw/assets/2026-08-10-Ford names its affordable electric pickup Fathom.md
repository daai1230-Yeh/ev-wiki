# Ford names its affordable electric pickup Fathom

**Author**: Carla Westerheide | **Date**: 2026-08-07

---

Ford has revealed the name of the first production model based on its Universal EV Platform. The midsize electric pickup, called Fathom, will be available for pre-order in early 2027 with a starting price of $29,945, including the US destination fee.

Unlike previous announcements centred on the platform's engineering, battery technology and production strategy, the latest update focuses on the production model's identity. According to Ford, the target customer differs from traditional pickup buyers, influencing decisions ranging from the vehicle's packaging to its price point.

"The blueprint for Ford's new midsize electric truck — from the engineering to its name — came from one place: a deep understanding of the person who would drive it," said Kay Hart, General Manager of Ford's Model e division.

Ford says the name thus needed to represent more than capability and devoted considerable attention to the naming process itself. Rather than following the alphanumeric naming convention common in the automotive industry, Ford wanted the new pickup to have what it describes as "personality" and "a soul."

According to Hart, the word "Fathom" appealed because of its multiple meanings. Historically, a fathom was the span of a person's outstretched arms, and later became a nautical unit equal to six feet (1.83 metres) for measuring water depth. Over time, the verb "to fathom" also came to mean understanding something completely.

"A name about measuring what you can't see, that grew into a word about understanding. For a truck that started with years of trying to understand a specific customer, that was the whole brief in six letters," she explained.

**Technical details remain scarce**

Ford has set a base price of $28,350 for the standard-range version. Including the destination fee of $1,595, which is customary in the US, the minimum price comes to $29,945 before taxes and registration costs. This is particularly noteworthy because it means Ford remains just below the originally announced $30,000 price point even including the destination fee.

The manufacturer also confirmed several features already planned for the production model. The Fathom will seat five adults and is projected to offer more passenger volume than a Toyota RAV4 despite remaining in the midsize pickup segment. It combines a front trunk with a conventional cargo bed to increase load flexibility and will feature a large, high-resolution central touchscreen, digital key functionality and bidirectional power capability.

Every Fathom will also be BlueCruise-capable. Ford says the hands-free driving system will integrate with Apple Maps to provide "a more seamless on-ramp to off-ramp experience." The pickup will additionally support Apple CarPlay and Android Auto.

While the latest news contains few new technical details, the announcement provides another piece of the puzzle for Ford's next-generation EV strategy. The Universal EV Platform is intended to underpin multiple future models beyond the Fathom.

When the US carmaker unveiled it in August 2025, it described the architecture as a "Model T moment" for electric cars. It presented it as a clean-sheet platform designed to dramatically reduce costs and support a range of affordable electric vehicles.

In February this year, Ford followed up with additional technical details, revealing that the first vehicle would be a midsize pickup engineered with a strong focus on efficiency, simplicity and low manufacturing costs. The new platform and production system are designed to reduce the number of parts per vehicle by 20% and fasteners by 25%. With 40% fewer workstations required in assembly, production time is reduced by 15%. The Fathom will become the first production vehicle from that programme when pre-orders begin in early 2027.
