# Rolls-Royce Phantom VI swaps V8 for electric powertrain

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-17

---

Electrogenic has converted a 1973 Rolls-Royce Phantom VI to electric drive. The official representative limousine of the City of London now has 120 kW of power, a 62 kWh battery and charging capacity of up to 100 kW.

A Rolls-Royce Phantom VI does not simply drive up – it arrives. Six metres of automobile in white tie demands its space. Built as a sturdy Brit for state occasions, red carpets and people who rarely need to open a door themselves, this 1973 veteran once did so with a roaring V8. Now, it glides electrically through London’s streets.

The Phantom VI is part of the official representative fleet of the City of London and has been converted to an electric powertrain by Electrogenic. Instead of the original 6.2-litre V8 and four-speed automatic transmission, a 120 kW electric motor with 310 Nm of torque and a 62 kWh battery now power the vehicle.

Its first official appearance after the conversion was at the inauguration of the new Lord Mayor of London. The Lord Mayor, however, is not the mayor of the entire city of London but rather the representative of the historic City of London – that small district where an astonishing amount of money moves during the week and surprisingly few people are seen at weekends.

According to Electrogenic, the Phantom VI offers enough space to potentially double the battery capacity in the future. For its current use in central London, however, the existing range should suffice. Two driving modes are available for different scenarios. In normal mode, the electric powertrain delivers full power and enables one-pedal driving. The so-called Ceremonial Mode reduces power and torque and adjusts the accelerator pedal response.

## Battery could be doubled in size in the future

This also suits the character of the ageing statesman. While other cars accelerate, the Phantom VI proceeds with dignity. And when processional speed is required, there is now even a dedicated driving mode for it.

The interior has also been carefully adapted. The original column-mounted gear selector now serves to select D, N and R. Information about the electric powertrain appears on a display hidden in the glove compartment. Additionally, the rear passenger area has been fitted with a modern heating system.

The technical heart of the conversion is Electrogenic’s proprietary control system, EG-OS. This includes vehicle control software, electronics for the controls and displays, and integration of the battery system. The battery is located under the bonnet and, according to Electrogenic, offers a real-world range of approximately 160 to 190 kilometres. Charging is via CCS at up to 100 kW.

According to the company, no cutting, drilling or welding was performed on the vehicle during the conversion. The electrification is said to be fully reversible, preserving the historic integrity of the Phantom VI. Rolls-Royce built only 374 examples of the Phantom VI. Introduced in 1968, the model belongs to an era when such limousines were seen less as means of transport and more as social institutions.

Oxford-based Electrogenic specialises in the electrification of classic vehicles. Previous projects include a 1929 Rolls-Royce Phantom II, classic Land Rovers and an Aston Martin DB6.

So, the old state carriage is far from retired. It has simply changed its powertrain. Externally, everything remains the same – white tie and chrome for the grand entrance. And perhaps this is even the most elegant form of transport transition: not everything old needs to disappear. Sometimes, it just needs a CCS port.
