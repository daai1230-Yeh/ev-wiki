# Zero-emission trucking corridor from Canada to Mexico

**Author**: Carrie Hampel | **Date**: 2026-08-31

---

State governments from Canada, the USA and Mexico along the North American West Coast have launched an initiative to collaborate on an intra-continental truck charging and hydrogen refuelling (ZEV - zero-emission vehicle) corridor. Megawatt chargers and hydrogen filling stations are planned across the 2,222-km route spanning three countries to form the British Columbia to Baja California ZEV Corridor (BC2BC).

The British Columbia to Baja California ZEV Corridor (BC2BC) will enable electric trucks to charge and refuel from Vancouver, in British Columbia, Canada, through Washington, Oregon and California, USA, to Baja California in Mexico.

The planned electric truck charging and refuelling corridor runs along the I-5 highway along the North American West Coast. According to the project partners, nearly 10,000 trucks travel the corridor on an average day. The initial buildout involves 20 electric charging stations and three hydrogen stations.

California Transportation Secretary Toks Omishakin noted, "With global fuel prices driving up the cost of moving goods, partnerships like this are more critical than ever to strengthen our economies and advance Governor Newsom's zero-emission transportation goals."

The initiative was launched at the Port of Long Beach with representatives from the collaborating governments as well as Volvo Group North America, Southern California Edison, Tesla, EV Realty and King Fio Trucking. Zero-emission trucks from IMC Logistics, Volvo, Tesla and ZM Trucks were on display at the launch.

"Freight corridors are where electrification can deliver real operational value at scale," said Aravind Kailas, advanced technology policy director at Volvo Group North America.

"By aligning charging infrastructure, technical standards, and planning across jurisdictions, the BC2BC initiative can give fleets greater confidence to plan routes, maintain uptime, and make long-term investment decisions," he said.

The five involved governments announced their commitment to coordinating technical standards, infrastructure planning, and other requirements. The partners aim to ensure zero-emission trucks can use charging and fueling infrastructure across jurisdictions along the trucking corridor that binds the largest freight ports on the continent, supplying essential goods to millions of people.

At the launch, representatives from the five jurisdictions confirmed they will hold technical meetings to coordinate standards, guidance and infrastructure planning.

The previous federal US administration had its eye on freight corridors across the USA, announced in 2024. California has already made significant efforts to develop zero-emission truck charging and fueling infrastructure along West Coast freight routes dating back to 2024. Now, that coordination is expanding across the Canadian and Mexican borders.

The partners plan for their efforts to feed into the next edition of Caltrans' California-Baja California Border Master Plan, which will include plans for zero-emission vehicles and supporting infrastructure.

Freight corridors along essential intra-continental routes will begin to operate more independently of the increasingly volatile oil market. The newly launched BC2BC is part of a broader initiative, called Global Green Road Corridors, which is working to develop lower-emission freight corridors connecting ports, logistics hubs and cities. According to the BC2BC partners, similar corridor projects are under development in Africa, Europe, the Americas and Asia.
