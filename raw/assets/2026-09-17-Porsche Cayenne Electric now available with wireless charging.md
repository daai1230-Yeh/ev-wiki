# Porsche Cayenne Electric now available with wireless charging

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-09-16 18:00

---

The Porsche Cayenne Electric is now available with inductive charging technology. The so-called 'Wireless Charging' system operates via a charging pad embedded in the vehicle floor and a base plate, with the charging current transmitted via a magnetic field.

Porsche first unveiled its inductive charging system in the run-up to the IAA last September. Since then, it has become clear that Porsche Wireless Charging operates with a maximum charging power of up to 11 kW, matching the output of many cable-based AC wallbox chargers. At the time, Porsche stated that the energy transfer efficiency from the grid to the battery was "up to 90 per cent" – though it did not rule out lower deviations.

Even so, Porsche teased a 2026 launch for Europe and, during the world premiere of the Porsche Cayenne Electric in November 2025, officially confirmed that the electric SUV would be the first model to support Wireless Charging. However, the inductive charging system was not yet available when orders eventually opened.

That changes now: Porsche Wireless Charging is now available for the Cayenne Electric, which has been in series production at the VW plant in Bratislava, Slovakia, since February. The Stuttgart-based sports car manufacturer describes it as a "particularly convenient form of charging" designed to simplify daily life with an EV. Instead of plugging in a cable, drivers need only position the Porsche Cayenne Electric over a permanently installed floor pad – such as in a home garage or carport. Assisted parking, integrated into Park Assist, ensures precise alignment, and the charging process begins automatically via inductive coupling, much like smartphone wireless charging pads.

## Porsche Cayenne Electric: wireless charging comes at a price

However, this "particularly convenient form of charging" comes at a price, as it is a paid optional extra. According to the German configurator, the vehicle-mounted charging pad, including pre-installation, costs €1,844.50, while the floor pad (three-phase) is priced at €5,116.99. This brings the total to €6,961.49. That said, Porsche is known to cater to a clientele with deep pockets rather than bargain hunters – and those who can afford the Cayenne Electric, even in its base configuration at €105,200, are unlikely to be deterred by the cost.

Alternatively, customers can initially opt for just the pre-installation at a cost of €226.10. This may be useful for those unsure whether they need inductive charging or for those looking to increase the vehicle's resale value, as the feature or option may appeal to a future buyer. Without this pre-installation, retrofitting Porsche Wireless Charging at a later date is not possible.

All components of Porsche Wireless Charging are now available to order for the Porsche Cayenne Electric in Germany and other European markets. The system will be gradually introduced worldwide in all Porsche Connect markets except China. It remains unclear whether and when Porsche will offer the system in additional vehicle models. The brand's all-electric portfolio currently includes the Porsche Taycan and the Porsche Macan. Additionally, the all-electric Porsche 718 (Boxster/Cayman) is expected next year.
