# Illinois opens new EV rebate round from 1 August

**Author**: Carla Westerheide | **Date**: 22.07.2026

---

Illinois will reopen its electric vehicle rebate programme on 1 August, offering up to $4,000 towards the purchase of a new or used battery-electric car. The latest funding round includes $14 million and continues to prioritise lower-income households.

For the current fiscal year, which runs until 30 June 2027, the Illinois General Assembly has allocated $14 million to the programme. However, the actual number of rebates issued will depend on the balance of the state's Electric Vehicle Rebate Fund. The programme will be open from 1 August until 31 December 2026, supporting new and used battery-electric vehicles and motorcycles while funding lasts.

The programme continues with several changes introduced during the previous application cycle. Buyers of eligible new or used battery-electric vehicles can receive a rebate of $2,000. Applicants classified as low income qualify for an additional $2,000, bringing the total incentive to $4,000. Eligible electric motorcycles remain entitled to a rebate of $1,500.

The programme is open to vehicles with a purchase price of up to $80,000. Applicants must submit their rebate request within 180 days of purchasing the vehicle, provided the application falls within the official application window. Applications received before 1 August or after 31 December will not be accepted.

Eligibility is restricted to Illinois residents whose household income does not exceed 500 per cent of the federal poverty level. Applicants must also purchase the vehicle from a dealership licensed by the Illinois Secretary of State rather than lease it.

The previous application cycle, which ran from 28 October 2025 to 31 May 2026, attracted 3,499 applications. The Illinois EPA approved 3,001 rebates, including 1,658 awarded to low-income applicants. The agency has published updated application forms and guidance documents ahead of the new application period.

"Illinois continues to see an increase in electric vehicles registered within the state, and many of those have been recipients of an Illinois EPA EV Rebate," said Illinois EPA Director James Jennings. "This incentive programme continues to encourage residents to adopt zero-emission transportation for the benefit of Illinois' environment."

Sources: epa.illinois.gov (statement), epa.illinois.gov (programme)
