# New Volkswagen ID.3 GTI: The First RWD GTI Is Here With More Power Than Ever

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: Sep 16, 2026, 8:25am ET

---

Based on the recently revealed ID.3 Neo, the new electric hot hatch is the most powerful GTI in the nameplate's history.

Volkswagen has revealed the first-ever ID.3 GTI. The Golf-like electric hot hatch is the first rear-wheel-drive GTI in history and also the most powerful production GTI ever made.

This is the new Volkswagen ID.3 GTI, the car that proves the German automaker has finally let go of the decades-old idea that the GTI badge should only live on a combustion-powered hatchback.

Sure, the "I" in GTI stands for "Injection," but during the nameplate's 50-year history, its meaning has shifted to represent a fun, performance-oriented car that doesn't break the bank. And now Volkswagen is banking on that with its second GTI-branded EV, after the ID. Polo GTI.

It's pretty big news, as the ID.3 has always been regarded as the electric alternative to the ubiquitous Golf, which has a special place in the hearts of enthusiasts. But despite this, the ID.3 GTI's launch is a marketing exercise more than anything else. That's because the car itself isn't actually all that new. Under all the red and black accents and GTI badges lies the same powertrain as in the now-discontinued VW ID.3 GTX, which debuted a little over two years ago: a rear-mounted electric motor that makes 322 horsepower and 402 pound-feet of torque (545 Nm), enough to make it the most powerful GTI model in history, overtaking the gas-powered Golf GTI Edition 50 by just one horsepower.

The 79-kWh battery powers the hot EV, with the ID.3 GTI able to go up to 373 miles (601 km) on a full charge on the WLTP cycle — up to six miles (10 km) more than the outgoing GTX. Maximum charging power has gone up from 175 kW to 183 kW, but going from 10% to 80% now takes 29 minutes, versus 26 minutes for the discontinued GTX.

It's also the first rear-wheel-drive GTI ever, because it's based on the MEB platform, which is RWD-biased, whereas the newer MEB+ architecture underpinning the ID. Polo is predominantly front-wheel drive. Just like the ID.3 GTX Performance it replaces, the new GTI can sprint to 62 mph (100 km/h) in 5.6 seconds, with top speed limited to 124 mph (200 km/h).

The new ID.3 GTI also gets all the goodies introduced by the revamped ID.3 Neo: a slightly redesigned exterior and an improved interior with more physical buttons and larger screens, including a 10.25-inch digital instrument cluster and a 12.9-inch central touchscreen running VW's latest software, plus a new dual-spoke steering wheel and proper buttons for each of the four electric windows.

Because this is a GTI, a pair of sports seats welcomes the front occupants, accompanied by red accents throughout the interior, along with a dedicated GTI driving mode that unlocks launch control and activates a digital engine sound to mimic the Golf GTI experience.

Outside, the new ID.3 GTI comes standard with a set of 20-inch wheels shod in 225-millimeter-wide tires, with 235 rubber available for more money. It goes on sale in Europe in the spring of 2027.
