# I Saw Scout's New Trucks Up Close. They Could Be Worth The Wait

**Author**: Maddox Kay | **Date**: Jul 9, 2026

---

I caught Scout's electric Traveler and Terra in the wild. They have a lot to offer, but the company's road ahead is far from smooth.

Scout Motors plans to launch its extended-range electric SUV, the Traveler, in 2028. The company hosted a brand event to showcase its prototypes and connect with potential customers at Rolling Stone's Stateside Fest, where the author got an up-close look at the vehicles — a Traveler SUV and a Terra pickup, with Scout CEO Scott Keogh on hand.

The Traveler, in a red-white-and-blue striped livery, pulls off a patriotic vibe effortlessly; its width-to-height-and-length ratio is said to be among the highest of any SUV on the road. The wide stance and blunt front ends lend Scout's trucks a rugged yet friendly vibe in person. These aren't Jeep Wrangler- or Ford Bronco-sized vehicles — the Scout Traveler is pretty much the same size as a Rivian R1S, while the Terra is a full foot longer than the R1T.

Inside, the body-on-frame SUV boasts sumptuous leather, denim trim on the seat backs, headrests, dashboard and door cards, and chunky metal switchgear throughout the center console and steering wheel. The team's goal was to reduce the need for screen taps by making each core function accessible through a button or toggle. The Terra, with its 37" BFGoodrich K03 tires, forest green paint, roof rack, and light bar, gave it a rugged aesthetic.

Scout's future models will offer a gasoline-powered range extender — called Harvester — something neither Rivian nor any other U.S. automaker currently matches. With the Harvester, a Scout's total claimed range is over 500 miles, and more than eight out of every 10 reservation holders have opted for the range extender so far.

Customers can expect delivery in 2028, following the start of initial production in 2027. The Traveler SUV represents around 70% of reservations so far, and Scout will prioritize building extended-range Harvester models first.

The broader VW Group picture is bleaker: the company is reportedly considering cutting up to 100,000 jobs and closing four German factories, with rumors it's weighing potential sales of Ducati and Lamborghini. A VW spokesperson said the company's "business model no longer works" for developing and producing cars for global markets in Europe. Scout has tried to distance its brand image from VW, but it's still a wholly-owned subsidiary operating off a VW-owned trademark and helmed by a veteran VW Group executive — bad news for VW is bad news for Scout, which is trying to launch an ambitious new platform not shared with any other VW Group vehicle. Scout is also working to bypass dealerships and sell directly to consumers, which has invited a legal storm from Volkswagen dealers across the country.

Building a new EV brand from scratch is tough in any environment — see the junkyard of failed EV startups: Fisker, Canoo, Nikola, Lordstown, Fisker again. It's an especially tough time to sell a new EV, given slowing EV sales and the premature end of the EV tax credit. On the other hand, Scout's novel EREV powertrain and 2028 timing may let it skip over the worst of the current EV-hostile regulatory environment; BloombergNEF projects EV and plug-in hybrid market share to dip before recovering to 2025 levels in 2028.

If Scout can start delivering cars in 2028 at the promised $60,000 price point, the throwback design, body-on-frame chassis, and range extender could set it apart from the competition — though Rivian has a clear head start with the R2 on sale today around $60,000.
