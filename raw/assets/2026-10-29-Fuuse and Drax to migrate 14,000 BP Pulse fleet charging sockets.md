# Fuuse and Drax to migrate 14,000 BP Pulse fleet charging sockets

**Author**: Nicole Storch | **Date**: 2026-10-28

---

Fuuse and Drax Electric Vehicles will support almost 1,000 fleet customers as BP Pulse exits workplace charging. The partnership aims to keep existing chargers operational, preserve hardware warranties and avoid costly replacements.

Fuuse and Drax Electric Vehicles have been appointed to support the migration of almost 1,000 former BP Pulse fleet charging customers in the UK, covering more than 14,000 charging sockets. Fuuse will lead the migration to its charging management platform, while Drax Electric Vehicles will provide nationwide maintenance and service support, including continuity of existing hardware warranties.

BP Pulse announced its withdrawal from the workplace charging market in July 2025, choosing to focus on charging hubs and its rapid charging network. In May 2026, the company was still seeking a migration partner after some fleet operators criticised a lack of support following the market exit. The appointment follows more than a year of collaboration, hardware testing and platform development between BP's EV charging business and Fuuse.

"Being selected as the exclusive migration partner is a huge endorsement of both our platform and our people," said James Chapman, strategic sales director at Fuuse.

Adam Hall, energy services director at Drax Electric Vehicles, said: "With nationwide field service capability and continuity of existing hardware warranty support, we can help ensure charging infrastructure remains maintained, supported and fully operational."

Affected customers can register for migration with a link in the notification email sent by BP Pulse. Fuuse's onboarding team will then validate their infrastructure requirements, confirm service arrangements and guide them through the process.
