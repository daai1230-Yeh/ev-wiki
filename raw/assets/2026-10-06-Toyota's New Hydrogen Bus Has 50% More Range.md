# Toyota's New Hydrogen Bus Has 50% More Range

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-10-05

---

That's despite having less hydrogen on board than its predecessor.

- Toyota and Isuzu have unveiled a new hydrogen fuel cell bus.
- The second-generation Sora bus needs less hydrogen to travel further.
- Its three tanks can be refueled in as little as ten minutes, while the range is quoted at over 186 miles.

Toyota is still keeping the hydrogen dream alive, though at a scale that's not even close to the automaker's hybrid vehicle might. Now there's a new kid on the block: the second-generation Toyota Sora urban and regional bus, powered by the company's latest hydrogen fuel cell stack. A roof-mounted fuel cell stack generates electric power, which is sent to an ordinary electric powertrain built by Isuzu.

The whole vehicle — except for the roof-mounted hydrogen powertrain — is a rebadged Isuzu ERGA EV, which debuted in 2024, and Isuzu is now launching its own ERGA FCV variant.

Under the skin, a pair of 110-kilowatt electric motors make a combined 295 horsepower and 715 pound-feet of torque. They draw power from an 88-kWh lithium-ion battery that enables a driving range of over 186 miles (300 km), roughly 50% more than the first-generation Sora bus that debuted in 2018. The fuel cell stack can deliver up to 113 kW.

The 471-liter roof tanks can be refilled in about ten minutes, and Toyota claims future filling stations will be able to cut that in half. Like the first-gen Sora, the bus can export power from its high-voltage battery as an emergency power source during disasters or long grid outages.

Inside, the bus has a fully flat floor and an optional one-touch securement system for wheelchair users. Four versions are available, two for urban and two for suburban use, with different seat layouts. It comes with the Emergency Driver Stop System (EDSS) and Driver Status Monitor (DSM), which can bring the bus to a complete stop if the driver is unable to drive.

The second-generation Sora is expected to go into production in the fiscal year running April 2026 to March 2027, built by J-Bus (a 50:50 Isuzu–Hino joint venture) at the Utsunomiya Plant in Tochigi Prefecture, Japan.
