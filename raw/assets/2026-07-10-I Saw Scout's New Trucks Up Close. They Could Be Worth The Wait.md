# I Saw Scout's New Trucks Up Close. They Could Be Worth The Wait

**Author**: Maddox Kay | **Date**: 2026-07-09

---

I caught Scout's electric Traveler and Terra in the wild. They have a lot to offer, but the company's road ahead is far from smooth.

Scout Motors plans to launch its extended-range electric SUV, the Traveler, in 2028. The company hosted a brand event to showcase its prototypes and connect with potential customers at Rolling Stone's Stateside Fest, where the author got an up-close look at the vehicles — a Traveler SUV and a Terra pickup, both prototypes, with Scout CEO Scott Keogh on hand.

Scout's VP of interior design, Dwayne Jackson, gave a tour of the Traveler. Its proportions are striking in person — the ratio of its width compared to height and length is among the highest of any SUV on the road. The Scout Traveler is roughly the same size as a Rivian R1S, while the Terra is a full foot longer than the R1T. Inside, the body-on-frame SUV features leather, denim trim, and chunky metal switchgear, with the team's goal being to reduce screen taps by making core functions accessible through physical buttons.

The Terra's smaller wheels, 37" BFGoodrich K03 tires, and forest green paint gave it a rugged aesthetic. Scout's future models will offer a gasoline-powered range extender called Harvester — something neither Rivian nor any other U.S. automaker currently matches — giving a total claimed range of over 500 miles. More than eight in ten reservation holders have opted for the range extender so far. Customers can expect delivery in 2028, following initial production starting in 2027; the Traveler SUV represents around 70% of reservations so far.

Scout is a wholly-owned VW Group subsidiary, and the parent company is in dire financial straits, reportedly considering cutting up to 100,000 jobs and closing four German factories, with rumors of potential sales of Ducati and Lamborghini. A VW spokesperson said the company's "business model no longer works" for developing and producing cars for global markets. Scout has tried to distance its brand image from VW, but bad news for VW is bad news for Scout. Scout is also working to sell vehicles directly to consumers rather than through dealerships, which has invited a legal storm from Volkswagen dealers.

Building a new EV brand from scratch is tough in any environment — see Rivian and Lucid, which still lose money after years of selling vehicles, or the list of failed startups (Fisker, Canoo, Nikola, Lordstown). It's an especially tough time to sell a new EV given slowing EV sales and the end of the EV tax credit. On the other hand, Scout's EREV powertrain and 2028 timing may let it skip the worst of the current EV-hostile regulatory environment; BloombergNEF projects EV and plug-in hybrid market share dipping before recovering to 2025 levels by 2028.

If Scout can deliver cars in 2028 at the promised $60,000 price point, its throwback design, body-on-frame chassis, and range extender could set it apart from competitors like Rivian, though Rivian already has a head start with the R2 on sale today around the same price.
