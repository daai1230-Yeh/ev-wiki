# E.ON and BMW expand V2G offering for Neue Klasse models

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-11-18

---

In addition to the pioneering BMW iX3, further models from the Neue Klasse series can now use V2G functions. The technical prerequisite is the sixth generation BMW eDrive technology, which debuted with the iX3.

Following the commercial Vehicle-to-Grid package launched exclusively for the BMW iX3 in February 2026, the technology is now also available for the BMW i3, iX4 and iX5 — four vehicles across different segments.

The solution combines the bidirectional BMW Wallbox Professional with the E.ON ÖkoStrom Home & Drive V2G tariff. Unlike Vehicle-to-Home, V2G feeds electricity back into the public grid. An algorithm jointly developed by BMW and E.ON automatically controls charging and feed-in from the high-voltage battery.

Via the My BMW app, users set their minimum charge level and planned departure time, while the battery operates within lifespan-optimised parameters.

Filip Thon, CEO of E.ON Deutschland: "The energy transition requires flexibility, and electric cars can play a key role in this. With Vehicle-to-Grid, we are utilising their batteries as flexible energy storage for the energy system whilst simultaneously creating a tangible financial benefit for our customers."

Marcus Krieg, VP New Business Models at BMW Group, noted the project won the Bavarian Energy Prize this summer and that the V2G offering is now growing to four models.

## E.ON pays €0.42 per kWh fed into the grid

Customers receive a bonus of 24 cents per hour that the vehicle is connected with V2G activated — up to €60 per month or €720 annually, theoretically up to 16,000 km of free driving for the new i3. E.ON also pays 42 cents for every kWh actually fed back into the grid.

For older or non-V2G-capable BMW and Mini plug-ins, 'Remote Charging Control' with a suitable E.ON tariff and a conventional AC wallbox shifts charging to cheaper periods, saving up to €240 per year plus a €50 new-customer bonus.
