# NYC selects provider for 700-point kerbside charging network

**Author**: Ciaran Daly | **Date**: 2026-09-16 17:30

---

New York City authorities have chosen US charging provider 'it's electric' to build a permanent street-level public EV charging network across all five city boroughs. The company will replace the city's existing pilot infrastructure and install hundreds of new charging points over the next three years.

The expansion is set to bring the number of public, Level 2 street charging points in NYC up from 88 to nearly 700. The charging company said it will focus on areas with high concentrations of taxi and rideshare drivers that face limited off-street parking, an issue it calls "the biggest obstacle keeping drivers from switching to electric vehicles". This is against the backdrop of a looming deadline in the city which will require all Uber and Lyft trips to be accessible or electric by 2030.

According to a joint statement, the chargers will be manufactured in Queens by Boyce Technologies, with engineering and construction services provided by Maverick Corporation and backend services supplied by SWTCH Energy. Brooklyn-based 'it's electric' stated that "infrastructure for the city's clean transportation future will be entirely made in New York" as a result.

"This initiative lets New Yorkers across the city participate in the energy transition, improves air quality in our most densely populated neighbourhoods, and positions the city to lead the next generation of climate innovation," said Nathan King, co-founder and CEO of it's electric. "Every charger powering electric transportation in cities we work in across North America will be built right here in Queens. That investment won't just improve the health of New Yorkers – it'll improve the health of city-people everywhere."

The announcement follows plans – reported by electrive in August – to add 600 kerbside charging points across the five New York boroughs. At the time, the city stated that the first phase will focus on neighbourhoods such as Soundview and Unionport in the Bronx, Rego Park, Forest Hills and Far Rockaway in Queens, Red Hook, Carroll Gardens and Bedford Stuyvesant in Brooklyn, the Upper West Side and Washington Heights in Manhattan, and Stapleton on Staten Island. Whether or not these neighbourhoods will be prioritised first under the 'it's electric' project remains to be seen, as specific build locations are yet to be provided.

Josh Gold, Senior Director of Public Policy at Uber, said of the 'it's electric' tender: "More accessible charging is critical to helping Uber drivers make the switch to electric. Rideshare drivers cover significantly more miles than the average motorist, so expanding charging where they actually need it can deliver outsized benefits for air quality while strengthening the city's charging network for everyone. We'll continue working with our partners and the City to help build an all-electric future for New York."

In August, we also reported how the plans built on a kerbside charging pilot launched in 2021 by the city. This saw 88 Level 2 charging points enter permanent operation in 2025, often with utilisation rates of above 70 per cent. It's now been confirmed that the new chargers installed by 'it's electric' will replace this pilot infrastructure.
