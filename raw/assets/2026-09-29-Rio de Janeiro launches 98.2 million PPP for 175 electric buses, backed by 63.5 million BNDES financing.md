# Rio de Janeiro launches $98.2 million PPP for 175 electric buses, backed by $63.5 million BNDES financing

**Author**: Editorial Staff | **Date**: 2026-09-28

---

Rio de Janeiro has announced a R$509 million ($98.2 million) project for 175 battery-electric buses and a new charging depot, backed by R$329 million ($63.5 million) in financing from Brazilian development bank BNDES.

The financing was announced by the City of Rio de Janeiro on September 25 at Terminal Gentileza and will be provided through BNDES's Climate Fund (Fundo Clima). According to the municipality, the project will establish Brazil's first public-private partnership (PPP) dedicated to electric buses.

The 175 vehicles are scheduled to begin joining the Sistema RIO public transport network in the second half of 2027. The project also includes the construction of a publicly owned depot in Maré, equipped with charging infrastructure. The new electric buses will electrify the entire bus fleet operating from Terminal Gentileza.

The Rio project comes amid other electric bus investments in Brazil. In 2025, Brazil joined the Global MoU on Zero-Emission Medium- and Heavy-Duty Vehicles (100% ZE new sales by 2040, at least 30% by 2030). Also in 2025, an €80 million financing facility for zero-emission buses in Brazil was launched, aiming to support more than 1,700 vehicles by 2030. BYD has outlined plans for a new bus factory in Brazil, targeting annual capacity of up to 7,000 units from around 2,000. Rio's Sustainable Development Plan targets 20% zero-emission share of the municipal bus fleet by 2030 (approx. 1,000 buses).

## Rio electric bus PPP: R$509 million investment for 175 vehicles

Total investment is estimated at R$509 million (~$98.2 million): R$329 million ($63.5 million) from BNDES, R$141.2 million ($27.3 million) from the concessionaire and R$38.8 million ($7.5 million) from the municipality. The BNDES financing is allocated to the acquisition of the battery-electric buses.

It is an administrative PPP with a 16-year contract (one year implementation, 15 years operation). The concessionaire is responsible for partial acquisition of the vehicles, charging infrastructure, depot construction work, and operation and maintenance.

According to the ZEBRA-backed E-Bus Radar, Latin America counts 10,628 electric buses, with Brazil at 2,216 (about 21%): 1,686 standard 12-15 m e-buses, 333 trolleybuses, 129 battery-electric articulated, 46 battery bi-articulated and 22 midi e-buses.

## New electric bus depot planned in Maré

All 175 buses will be concentrated at a new public depot on Rua Novo Horizonte da Vila do João in Maré, opposite Fundação Oswaldo Cruz on Avenida Brasil. The site covers about 41,000 m², of which around 23,000 m² is allocated to the electric bus depot, with parking, maintenance, administrative and storage areas plus charging infrastructure. Charging will take place mainly overnight.

## Rio estimates 13,300 tonnes of CO2 savings per year

Over the 16-year contract the municipality estimates about 199,400 tonnes of CO2e avoided, plus lower local pollutants and noise. The first electric depot is expected to involve around 490 drivers, 35 inspectors and 10 dispatchers, plus maintenance and administrative staff; around 100 jobs are estimated for the implementation phase.

Mayor Eduardo Cavaliere said the 175 buses are another step in transforming the city's bus transport, combining electrification and biofuels. BNDES director Luciana Costa stressed fewer emissions and better quality of life. Municipal Transport Secretary Jorge Arraes called it the beginning of what they call "Elétrico Carioca".
