# The Ford Fathom's Home Backup System Will Be Way Cheaper Than The F-150 Lightning's

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-10-27

---

Thanks to an onboard DC-to-AC inverter, you won't need expensive hardware to back up your home with Ford's $30,000 EV truck.

Electricity costs have continued to soar in America this year, and using an EV's battery to power your home or send electricity back to the grid can ease that burden. Bidirectional charging will come to the upcoming Ford Fathom, the $30,000 electric truck set to go on sale in the U.S. next year. Ford has now shared more detail on how the system differs from the now-discontinued F-150 Lightning's: cheaper and easier to set up.

The Fathom can supply more than 10 kW of AC power to a home without an external DC-to-AC inverter. The inverter is built into the vehicle's E-Box, one of its five main electrical modules, making the system simpler and more affordable than the Lightning's. The Tesla Cybertruck also has a built-in inverter.

An external bidirectional charger is still needed: Ford says the hardware will consist of its Home Power Station and Home Backup Panel. The Home Power Station will cost half the price of the first-generation Charge Station Pro, which debuted at $1,310 in 2022. The Lightning's entire backup system (bidirectional charger plus Home Integration System) cost $5,205; Ford says the new hardware will be about 40% of that, roughly $2,000.

There is a catch: depending on a home's electrical setup, additional conduit, trenching or panel upgrades may be needed, so total installation cost could differ significantly from Ford's advertised price. The feature could prove useful in hot summers when demand strains the grid, letting a compatible EV keep essentials running during outages without a polluting generator.
