# Huge Tesla recalls in China while European authorities remain cautious

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 27.08.2026

---

Tesla is subject to the largest recall campaign in China's history: due to potential issues with the mechanical emergency door unlocking system, Tesla and other manufacturers must rectify several million vehicles. European authorities remain hesitant so far.

Tesla is recalling nearly 2.98 million Model 3, Model Y, Model S and Model X vehicles in China, with the recall beginning on 25 September. Other manufacturers affected include Xiaomi, Leapmotor, Xpeng and Zeekr, as announced by China's market regulator SAMR last week — making a total of 4.3 million vehicles.

Most of the recalls involve the mechanical emergency door releases inside the vehicles. According to SAMR, these releases may be difficult for occupants to locate or operate in an emergency. In the worst case — such as a fire — this could turn the affected vehicles into death traps. As part of the recall in China, Tesla and other manufacturers plan to make improvements: the emergency releases will be better labelled, and windows will automatically open in the event of an accident.

## KBA investigates Tesla door opening mechanism

As these electric vehicles are used worldwide, the question is whether similar recall actions will follow in Europe. The answer, for now, is no. The German Federal Ministry of Transport told Reuters that an investigation by the Kraftfahrt-Bundesamt (KBA) into the safety concerns is still ongoing.

The KBA investigation began in April 2026, when the public prosecutor's office requested an assessment following an accident in Schwerte, Germany, in which three Tesla occupants were fatally injured, to determine why the door opening mechanism had apparently failed.

As in April, the current statement reiterates that the KBA is not actually responsible for such investigations. Instead, the competent type-approval authority — in this case the Dutch organisation RDW — holds jurisdiction and can order a recall in the EU.

"There is currently no recall in Europe for the affected electric vehicles," the RDW told Reuters on Wednesday, adding that it is monitoring the situation. The Dutch authority also stated that it believes vehicles must be capable of being opened under all circumstances — including when electrical systems fail — in an intuitive way, without tools, from both inside and outside. RDW has not ruled out a recall at this stage.

## Debate encompasses both door handles and emergency releases

The recent debate about the safety of modern door opening systems has become somewhat conflated in media reporting, as it involves two distinct issues.

First, the external electrically retractable door handles, such as those on the Tesla Model S. These provide no physical point of access for first responders or firefighters in the event of a vehicle power failure, and will therefore be completely banned in China from next year.

Second — and the focus of the current recall actions in China — is the issue of the emergency releases inside the vehicles. While these are often mechanically present, they can be difficult for occupants to locate and operate intuitively in panic situations or when smoke is present. Such emergency releases are also found in the Tesla Model 3 and Model Y, which feature significantly safer flush-folding mechanical handles on the outside.
