# Something Doesn't Add Up About The Rivian R2's EPA Efficiency Rating

**Author**: Andrei Nedelea | **Date**: Jul 27, 2026

---

On paper, the Rivian R2 matches the Tesla Model Y's EPA efficiency numbers — remarkable for the taller, boxier, heavier R2 — but the numbers don't hold up in real-world tests. A head-to-head test by Out of Spec Reviews and RivianTrackr lined up the most powerful Performance versions of both models. Officially, both have the same 105 MPGe EPA rating (32 kWh/100 miles, or 3.125 miles/kWh).

The testers drove the R2 Performance alongside a Model Y Performance at 50, 60, 70 and 80 mph, with matched tire pressure, occupants, and climate settings (R2 in Conserve mode, Tesla in Chill mode). At 50 mph, the R2 consumed 18.4% more energy than the Model Y; at 60 mph the gap grew to roughly 26%; at 70 mph it used around 20% more (though traffic may have given it an aerodynamic assist); and at 80 mph, over a 30-mile route, the R2 used 26.5% more energy, as the Model Y's better aerodynamics and lower weight showed through. The R2 also used about 25% more during a low-speed stop-and-go simulation.

This doesn't make the R2 an inefficient EV — it was designed with more off-road focus than the Model Y, with more ground clearance. The R2 can mechanically disconnect its front motor while cruising to operate as a more efficient rear-wheel-drive EV, something the Tesla can't do.

Questions remain about why two cars with identical EPA ratings performed so differently in the real world. One explanation: the ratings may have used different EPA test procedures and correction factors. The hosts call it an "apples-to-oranges" comparison — the R2 underwent the full five-cycle test, while the Model Y's figure appears derived from a shorter set of tests with additional submitted data and an EPA correction applied. The comparison has limits: testers relied on car-reported energy consumption rather than charger-measured electricity, and most routes were relatively short.
