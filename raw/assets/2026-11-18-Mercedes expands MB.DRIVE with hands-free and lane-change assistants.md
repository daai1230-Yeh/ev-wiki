# Mercedes expands MB.DRIVE with hands-free and lane-change assistants

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-11-17

---

Mercedes-Benz will introduce two new motorway assistants in Germany by the end of 2026. Drivers will be allowed to take their hands off the steering wheel at speeds of up to 130 km/h while the car autonomously overtakes or positions itself in the correct lane. Point-to-point driving in the city is expected to become possible from 2027.

Mercedes-Benz is introducing two new motorway functions for its MB.DRIVE driver assistance system in the German market: the Hands-Free Assistant and the proactive Lane-Change Assistant. As the Stuttgart-based manufacturer has announced, both systems will be rolled out in Germany by the end of 2026, with a nationwide launch in the US following in 2027.

With the Hands-Free Assistant, drivers on motorways can take their hands off the steering wheel at speeds of up to 130 km/h. Previously, partially automated driving at this speed required regular contact with the steering wheel, while hands-free driving was reserved for Mercedes-Benz’s highly automated Drive Pilot system in traffic jams.

The new system remains continuously active under suitable conditions and adapts to the flow of traffic. However, responsibility and the duty of supervision remain with the driver, who must always be ready to take control.

The function is complemented by the proactive Lane-Change Assistant. This system independently initiates and executes overtaking manoeuvres or automatically positions the vehicle in the correct lane. Prior confirmation from the driver, such as via the turn signal lever, is no longer required.

Plans for the previously announced MB.DRIVE ASSIST PRO system, which offers assisted driving from start to destination, including in urban traffic, are now taking shape. The Early Access Programme for selected customers in Stuttgart, Munich and California will begin in December 2026. The official market launch of the point-to-point system is planned for the first half of 2027 in Germany and the US.

According to the manufacturer, the new assistance systems will be gradually rolled out across the entire car and van range and all powertrain variants. Owners of suitably equipped existing models will be able to unlock the functions via an over-the-air update after the wider market launch. Prices and specific model assignments will be announced by Mercedes-Benz shortly before the market launch.

Technically, MB.DRIVE ASSIST PRO is based on the in-house MB.OS operating system, a sensor array comprising a total of 27 components (ten cameras, five radar sensors and twelve ultrasonic sensors), and a central computer with a performance of up to 254 TOPS. For software development, the company relies on Nvidia for Western markets and Momenta for China. In the future, Mercedes-Benz also plans to integrate the British company Wayve for point-to-point assistance in upcoming models.
