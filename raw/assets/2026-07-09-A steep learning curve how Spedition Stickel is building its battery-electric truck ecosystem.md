# A steep learning curve: how Spedition Stickel is building its battery-electric truck ecosystem

**Author**: Nicole de Jong | **Date**: 2026-07-09

---

The German logistics company Stickel is putting more of its freight on electric trucks, with a fleet of ten now in operation. Electrive spoke with managing director Christian Stickel about investment willingness, initial complexity, and the confidence needed to embrace a new energy future.

A few years ago, when Christian Stickel's daughter asked him what he was doing for the environment, the company boss was at a loss for an answer, which fundamentally changed his perspective on sustainability. Today, Baden-Württemberg-based Spedition Stickel is one of the pioneers in the use of battery-electric trucks among Germany's medium-sized haulage firms, and is participating in a real-world laboratory for grid-supportive electric truck charging run by NetzeBW. Stickel now operates ten battery-electric vehicles daily, though procurement required significant lead time — initial discussions with energy provider EnBW took place as early as 2020, when the technology was still considered too expensive for a company with around 100 employees, a fleet of 69 trucks, and €15 million in annual turnover.

For the installation of charging infrastructure, Stickel received grants totalling around €240,000, with the state also funding the purchase of the electric trucks at €75,000–€85,000 per vehicle. The company deliberately opted against a cautious single-vehicle start, wanting "to implement a large-scale solution to assess organisational processes, driving profiles, and economic viability more quickly."

At the end of 2024, the first six electric Mercedes eActros 600 trucks entered service, followed by four MAN eTGX models. Behind a dedicated 1,000kW transformer, there are currently four fast-charging stations with eight charging points: two Alpitronic chargers up to 400kW each, and an expandable Kempower system, all managed by an intelligent load management system.

Customer requirements played a key role in the purchase decision — the haulage firm works for automotive manufacturers and suppliers such as Mercedes-Benz, Porsche, and Bosch, and for one client the planned use of battery-electric trucks became a prerequisite for collaboration. Stickel now deploys vehicles in both regional and longer-distance routes, including Düsseldorf, Bremen, and Bayreuth, charging en route at fast-charging hubs up to 400kW, often combined with legally mandated driver breaks.

Battery-electric trucks remain significantly more expensive than diesel, but Stickel expects Chinese manufacturers entering the €180,000–€220,000 price segment to increase competitive pressure in Europe, potentially pushing prices toward €150,000 long-term. He also highlights maintenance and service-life advantages: while diesel trucks typically run 600,000–800,000km economically, manufacturer specs for battery-electric trucks cite up to 1.2 million km or around 1,800 charging cycles.

Driver acceptance was a key success factor — "once someone has driven an electric vehicle, they don't want to switch back to a combustion engine," with drivers appreciating the quieter, more relaxed driving experience and high torque for overtaking on hills. Demand is already high for Stickel's eleventh battery-electric truck.

At its premises, Stickel already relies on photovoltaics and is expanding further — a new logistics hall opening at the end of February 2027 will include a roughly 650 kWp photovoltaic system and a 4 MWh battery storage system, with plans to eventually open its charging hub (up to 1.6 MW capacity) to third parties.

Stickel emphasises that electrification involves far more than buying new vehicles — charging infrastructure, IT systems for load and energy management, and integration of photovoltaics, battery storage, and charging planning are all "true pioneering work." Clients must support the transformation process and be willing to pay more, he says. His advice to other companies: prepare thoroughly, consider charging infrastructure, energy supply, and IT from the outset, and involve drivers, dispatch teams, and customers early. "For us, there is no turning back. We are continuing down this path—consistently and decisively."
