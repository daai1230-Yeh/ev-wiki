# Denmark plans social EV subsidies from 2028

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-11-16

---

Denmark is earmarking DKK 3.9 billion (around €521.7 million) for a climate package aimed at making the transition to sustainable transport socially equitable. From 2028, incentives will help low-income households switch to electric cars, including through subsidised leasing. Purchase subsidies are also being considered.

The Danish government and opposition parties have agreed on a climate package worth around DKK 3.9 billion (approximately €521.7 million) as part of the 2027 budget agreement. A key element is financial support for battery-electric vehicles. Denmark aims to reduce its dependence on fossil fuels while making the transition to electric mobility more affordable for low-income households.

Franciska Rosenkilde, political leader of the Green party Alternativet, said: "The green transition should not be a privilege for those with the largest savings or the highest salary. Everyone should have the opportunity to participate."

Between 2028 and 2030, the government plans to allocate DKK 150 million (around €20 million) annually, or DKK 450 million (approximately €60.2 million) in total, to EV incentives. The focus is on supporting low-income households in replacing older combustion-engine cars with battery-electric models.

In rural areas, where many commuters depend on cars due to a lack of alternative transport options, the scheme is intended to provide tangible financial relief. Pelle Dragsted of the socialist party Enhedslisten said: "So we are actually killing three birds with one stone: More money in the pockets of citizens, less dependence on oil and gas, and a new direction for the green transition that is more socially just."

The exact details of the scheme have yet to be decided. According to the agreement dated 14 November 2026, support for leasing electric cars is being considered, alongside possible purchase subsidies. The funding levels and eligibility criteria, including income and location, will be developed with reference to experience in other countries, such as Sweden and France. The scheme will also take into account recommendations from an expert commission reviewing Denmark's vehicle taxation system. Its findings are due to be presented to the parties involved by mid-2027.

The agreement also includes measures for the heating sector. Existing funding programmes for climate-friendly heating systems will be extended, while a long-term green heating scheme for low-income homeowners is planned from 2028. The agreement also provides for an assessment of electric island ferries and a planned ban on installing new oil and gas heating systems.

The package will be financed through general budget reserves and potentially through funding from the EU's Social Climate Fund. If EU funding becomes available, it could increase the budget for EV incentives. Further revenue is expected from raising the DKK 1,200 threshold for VAT refunds to tourists from outside the EU and introducing VAT on short-term rentals through platforms such as Airbnb. In addition, funding for biogas will be reduced, while DKK 90 million will be reallocated annually from an EU project for AI gigafactories.
