# BAIC Motor plans to sell battery swap assets as parent expands partnership with CATL

**Author:** Phate Zhang　｜　**Date:** 2026-11-18

**KEY TAKEAWAYS**
- BAIC Motor aims to free up capital through the sale, while its parent is working with CATL to bring more models onto the battery maker's swap network.
- The assets have an appraised value of about 278 million yuan ($41.4 million).

BAIC Motor (HKEX: 1958) plans to sell assets tied to 112 battery swap stations through a public tender to free up capital tied up in non-core assets.

Its wholly owned subsidiary, BAIC Powertrain, plans to submit a notice of the proposed sale to the China Beijing Equity Exchange (CBEX) by the end of November, BAIC Motor said in a Hong Kong stock exchange filing on Tuesday.

BAIC Powertrain plans to sell its entire interest in the assets, which include inventory, fixed assets, construction in progress and intangible assets used in station operations.

A September 30 appraisal using the cost approach valued the assets at about 278 million yuan ($41.4 million), excluding tax, about 1.54% above their unaudited book value.

The listing price will be at least as high as the appraised value ultimately filed with the authorities. The winning bid will determine the sale price. At the time of the filing, BAIC Powertrain had yet to identify a buyer or sign a sale agreement.

BAIC Motor estimates that a sale at the appraised value in 2026 would lift its consolidated profit for the year by about 3.52 million yuan after transaction costs. BAIC Powertrain plans to use the proceeds to fund its operations and development.

The proposed sale comes as BAIC Motor's parent, Beijing Automotive Group (BAIC Group), expands its battery swap partnership with CATL (HKEX: 3750).

BAIC Group said in October that it would bring compatible models from several of its brands onto CATL's Choco swap network and make dealership sites available for new swap stations. The partners aim to serve 1 million vehicles.

On November 13, BAIC Group's Arcfox brand launched a LiDAR-equipped battery swap version of the Beta S3 and a battery swap version of the Beta T1. Both use CATL's battery swap system for passenger vehicles.

Arcfox said it would offer a battery swap version of every model in its lineup next year.

Battery swap stations are expensive for automakers to build and maintain, BAIC Group chairman Zhang Jianyong said at the launch. Battery makers can benefit from economies of scale in both construction and operation, he said.

BAIC began researching battery swap technology in 2011, making it one of the first automakers in China to invest in the field, Zhang said.

He said BAIC Group and CATL would draw on their respective strengths to build China's first battery swap network capable of serving about 1 million vehicles.
