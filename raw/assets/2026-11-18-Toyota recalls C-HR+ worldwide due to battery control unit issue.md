# Toyota recalls C-HR+ worldwide due to battery control unit issue

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-11-17

---

Toyota must recall around 19,000 units of its electric C-HR+ worldwide, including 1,096 vehicles in Germany. The reason is a fault in the battery management system which, in the worst case, could cause the electric drivetrain to shut down.

According to the database of the German Federal Motor Transport Authority (KBA), the recall affects vehicles produced between 17 June 2025 and 9 July 2026. The KBA states that, in the affected electric vehicles, the programming of the control unit in regenerative mode may cause the battery to overcharge at high states of charge, triggering warning messages and potentially shutting down the powertrain.

So far, the KBA is not aware of any incidents involving property damage or personal injury resulting from this defect. Nevertheless, the affected Toyota C-HR+ units must be taken to a workshop, where the EV-ECU (Electric Vehicle Electronic Control Unit) will be reprogrammed. This control unit manages, monitors and coordinates the electrical and mechanical processes in an electric vehicle. Toyota has also recalled around 10,000 C-HR EVs in North America over the same software issue. The campaign affects certain 2026 model-year vehicles, including 1,310 units in Canada.

The Toyota C-HR+, which has been available to order in Germany since December 2025, is a fully electric SUV coupé that closely resembles the regular Toyota C-HR in terms of design. However, it does not share the hybrid platform of its counterpart; instead, it is based on the all-electric e-TNGA platform, which is also used by the Toyota bZ4X.

The 4.52-metre-long model is available with two battery sizes: the smaller version offers a gross capacity of 57.7 kWh (54 kWh net) and provides a WLTP range of up to 458 kilometres. The larger battery offers 77 kWh gross (72 kWh net) and a range of up to 607 kilometres. Depending on the variant, the electric motor on the front axle delivers 123 kW or 165 kW. There is also an all-wheel-drive version with two electric motors and a system output of 252 kW. DC fast chargers support charging at up to 150 kW.
