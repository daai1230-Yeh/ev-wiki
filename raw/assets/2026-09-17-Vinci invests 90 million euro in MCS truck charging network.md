# Vinci invests €90 million in MCS truck charging network

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-09-16 18:15

---

Vinci Concessions, together with Épopée Gestion and ADEME Investissement, will invest nearly €90 million in a fast-charging network for electric trucks. Around 20 locations are planned for the first phase - most of them in France. Later, the network will expand to Germany and Spain through Voltix.

Voltix, a subsidiary of Vinci Concessions specialising in public charging for heavy electric vehicles, will oversee construction and operation of the charging infrastructure. Starting with around 20 sites, the partners aim to establish the foundation for a European fast-charging network along key freight transport corridors. The project will begin in France, where most of the first sites are planned. The first charging park is set to open in autumn 2026 in Vierzon, in the Cher département. The city is located at a major motorway junction in central France. Additional stations are primarily planned at strategic road intersections and freight transport hubs.

The charging parks will use a megawatt system as standard. According to Vinci, electric trucks will be able to charge enough energy there in less than 45 minutes for an additional 4.5 hours of driving. The company aims to align the charging process with the mandatory rest periods for drivers. Vinci has not yet provided details on the number of charging points per site or their specific charging capacity.

## Voltix also involved in Germany's truck charging network

The planned Voltix network is also set to expand beyond France, with Vinci naming Germany and Spain as the next markets. In the long term, the sites are intended to form a cross-border charging corridor for electric freight transport.

In Germany, Voltix is already involved in the construction of public charging infrastructure for heavy-duty vehicles. Together with Eliso, the company – under the Eliso Voltix Truck Charging bidding consortium – has been awarded a contract for 25 sites in the federal government's planned truck fast-charging network. These are part of the first expansion phase at 124 unmanaged motorway service areas. A total of 836 charging points are planned there, including 447 with MCS. Operators are currently beginning site-specific planning. In the long term, the German truck fast-charging network is expected to comprise around 350 sites on federally owned motorway areas with approximately 4,200 charging points. These will include around 1,800 MCS and 2,400 CCS charging points.

Nearly €90 million is being jointly provided by Vinci Concessions, Épopée Gestion, and ADEME Investissement for the new Voltix network. Additional funding comes from the EU's CEF-AFIF programme. The distribution of the investment sum among the three partners is not specified in the information provided.

Vinci Concessions also highlights its experience with charging infrastructure in the French motorway network. However, heavy-duty vehicles require significantly higher connection capacities and larger parking areas compared to passenger car charging. The sites must also be adapted to the processes of long-distance transport as well as the driving and rest times of drivers.
