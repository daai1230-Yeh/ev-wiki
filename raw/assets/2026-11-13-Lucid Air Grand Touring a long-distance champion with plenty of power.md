# Lucid Air Grand Touring: a long-distance champion with plenty of power

**Author**: Kasimir Hupe | **Date**: 2026-11-12

---

For two weeks, we travelled with this luxury sedan from California. The Lucid Air impresses with its long-distance capability, an extremely quiet interior, and intuitive controls. However, it also has a few weaknesses that shouldn't exist at this price point.

**Facts:** Battery 117 kWh · Drivetrain 611 kW · Range (WLTP) 960 km · DC charging 300 kW · Base price €130,900

Some cars you reluctantly return after two weeks. Others leave your notebook overflowing with observations. The Lucid Air Grand Touring managed to do both, which aligns perfectly with what the brand has represented over the years. Technically, Lucid outpaces the established premium class in many areas. Yet, in some respects, it's clear this is not the work of a corporation with a century of experience.

I tested the Lucid Air during a turbulent phase. Lucid is in the midst of a restructuring that new CEO Silvio Napoli himself describes as an operational restart. The company aims to save $1.4 billion this year; around 18 per cent of the workforce has been let go, and the launch of the more affordable mid-range models has been postponed by Lucid to the second half of 2027.

In the third quarter, 3,806 vehicles were delivered to customers. At the same time, Lucid is exploring new business areas, such as a robotaxi service for Europe, which the company is planning together with mobility provider Bolt. It is set to be based on the upcoming mid-range platform. Until then, the Air and the Gravity SUV will carry the business alone.

My test car was a Lucid Air Grand Touring in 'Infinite Black Metallic' with the 'Stealth Pack', 19-inch Aero Range Stealth wheels, a glass roof, Mojave Extended Leather, and comfort seats with massage and ventilation. The total price for the test vehicle was a hefty €144,500. I drove it for two weeks in everyday conditions: on the motorway, in the city, and on country roads.

## Sleek instead of showy

At 4.97 metres, the Lucid Air is shorter than its competitors. A Mercedes EQS measures 5.23 metres, while a BMW i7 is even longer at 5.40 metres. The Lucid Air is also only 1.41 metres tall and 1.94 metres wide, with a wheelbase of 2.96 metres. On its 19-inch wheels, Lucid states an impressive drag coefficient of 0.197 – particularly important for EVs, as above approximately 100 km/h most of the propulsion energy is consumed by air resistance.

In the 'Stealth Pack' the Lucid Air is barely noticeable, and that suits it well. Only on second glance do you notice the flat headlights made of tiny lenses, the narrow light strip across the front, and the continuous rear light.

## Plenty of space for all kinds of cargo

Lucid develops its own powertrain units, and they are remarkably compact: motor, inverter and transmission together weigh just 74 kg. The 'frunk' offers 283 litres and opens and closes electrically. At the rear, the boot holds 627 litres; total with frunk is 910 litres, or 1,835 litres with the rear seats folded. Up front there's 115 cm of legroom, 93 cm at the rear. A tow hitch is not available.

## An intuitive menu

In front of the driver stretches the 34-inch Glass Cockpit; below it the 'Pilot Panel' controls climate, seats and settings and can be raised electrically to reveal a storage compartment. The menu structure is highly intuitive and route guidance reliably plans charging stops, filterable by charging power and operator.

However, Apple CarPlay and Android Auto only run in the smaller right section of the Glass Cockpit. Lucid has been rolling out Air UX 3.0 since the end of September to fix this. A head-up display is not even available as an option – a real oversight at this price. The steering-wheel rotary controls click in harshly, and the key is cumbersome; the smartphone app is better.

## Massages and silence

What I remember most is the quietness inside the cabin, even at high motorway speeds. The massage function is firm but audible. Standard equipment includes four climate zones, heated seats front and rear, soft-close doors, and the 21-speaker Surreal Sound Pro system. 'DreamDrive Premium' includes ACC and lane-keeping; Auto Park is sluggish. The LiDAR-equipped DreamDrive Pro (€4,200) is required for future hands-free driving and cannot be retrofitted.

## 611 kW that you rarely need

Two permanently excited synchronous motors deliver 611 kW and 1,200 Nm. 0–100 km/h in 3.2 s, top speed 270 km/h. Three drive modes: Smooth, Swift and Sprint. Regeneration allows one-pedal driving; six-piston callipers on 380 mm discs at the front.

## Confident on the motorway, less so on corners

Double wishbones front, multi-link rear, steel springs and adaptive dampers adjusting 500 times per second deliver excellent ride comfort. But at 2,435 kg (EU norm) and nearly five metres, it's no corner carver. No rear-wheel steering and a 12-metre turning circle make tight car parks cumbersome.

## Consumption and range

With a full battery the on-board computer showed 935 km. In the city: 14–15 kWh/100 km; mixed: 19–23 kWh; motorway at and above the limit: 22–25 kWh. A brisk 500 km stretch used 25.8 kWh/100 km. Resulting real ranges: city ~780–835 km, mixed ~510–615 km, fast motorway ~470–555 km. In the NAF winter test (January 2026) it managed 520 km at -31°C.

## 22 kW AC charging as standard

The 117 kWh battery uses a 900-volt architecture. DC charging up to 300 kW via CCS; in the test 10–80% took about 28 minutes, peaking at 305 kW. 22 kW AC is standard; the on-board charger is bidirectional. At older 400-volt stations a converter kicks in, slowing charging.

## Price

From €130,900 for the Grand Touring; the range spans from €85,900 (Air Pure) to €250,000 (Sapphire). A certified used programme started in October. Sales via studios in Munich, Frankfurt, Düsseldorf and Hamburg and online; service partners are being expanded (Wackenhut, Autohaus Possögel, Cloppenburg Automobil from January 2027).

## Competitors

Mercedes EQS 580 4MATIC (€134,732, 430 kW, 122 kWh, up to 869 km, 350 kW charging, HUD, rear-wheel steering, towing). BMW i7 60 xDrive (€140,100, 400 kW, 112.5 kWh, 727 km, 400 V, 250 kW). Volvo ES90 Twin Motor Performance (€94,490, 500 kW, 102 kWh, 686 km, 350 kW, 2,000 kg towing, 180 km/h limit). The Lucid's strengths are performance, space and efficiency; it is also the lightest.

## Conclusion

This luxury saloon is one of the best long-distance electric cars currently available: excellent suspension, an exceptionally quiet interior, an immense but understated powertrain, self-explanatory menus and 910 litres of storage. Weaknesses: no HUD, CarPlay/Android Auto confined to a small window, notchy controls, a cumbersome key, and limited agility. Keep an eye on Lucid's restructuring – the car itself isn't the issue.
