# Dr. Tim Meyer: "The electric car is a gift from the powertrain transition to the energy system"

**Author**: Peter Schwierz | **Date**: 05.09.2026

---

Interview with energy expert Dr. Tim Meyer ahead of the CharIN Testival & Conference Europe 2026. Meyer argues Germany's energy and transport transitions are treated as separate issues when EVs could actually link them — as flexible consumers, mobile storage, and market participants — but regulation, distribution-grid infrastructure, and energy-industry processes are lagging behind the technology.

He calls the electric car a "sleeping giant" for the grid: even a modest share of the ~50 million EU passenger cars charging flexibly would represent storage capacity far exceeding today's pumped-hydro storage, and a cited Fraunhofer study estimates bidirectional charging (V2G) could relieve the energy system by over €8 billion/year. He says V2G's main bottleneck isn't vehicle technology but the energy industry's lack of standardized, time-resolved metering and settlement processes, plus regulatory incentives that still favor building more grid infrastructure over smarter, flexible use. He calls for dynamic, grid-aware price signals rather than blanket limits (referencing Germany's planned 50% PV feed-in cap), stronger cross-operator standardization, and a dedicated German bidirectional-charging strategy. He's also optimistic that consumer and corporate adoption of EVs is outpacing political consensus.
