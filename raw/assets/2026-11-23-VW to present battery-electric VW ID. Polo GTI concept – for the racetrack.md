# VW to present battery-electric VW ID. Polo GTI concept – for the racetrack

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-11-21

---

A 240 km/h top speed and 0 to 100 km/h in 4.7 seconds: with the ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept, Volkswagen is demonstrating how the GTI idea can evolve in the electric age. The sprint-strong study delivers 270 kW - that's nearly 370 hp in old currency.

With the ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept, Volkswagen is treating itself to a gift in the GTI's 50th anniversary year: a track-ready ID. Polo GTI study. The one-off study will be presented to the public next week at the Essen Motor Show (27 November to 6 December 2026). Visually, the striking two-door silhouette dominates the design, flanked by flared wheel arches, a large front splitter, diffuser and rear wing.

The foundation of the ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept remains the advanced Modular Electric Drive Matrix (MEB+) with front-wheel drive. However, by installing the APP550 drive system, Volkswagen pushes the platform into new performance realms. This creates "the most dynamic interpretation of a front-wheel-drive ID. model to date," as the Wolfsburg-based manufacturer emphasises.

Conceptually, the new ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept is based on the production model unveiled in May: namely, the 166 kW ID. Polo GTI. In both models, power is delivered to the front axle, but the study adds a significant boost with 270 kW. Moreover, in the ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept, this power delivery is controlled so that the model accelerates from a standstill to 100 km/h in just 4.7 seconds via Launch Control – in part made possible thanks to an electronically regulated front-axle cross-locking differential (VAQ).

The battery is also specifically tuned to the high performance of the ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept. According to VW, the 52 kWh high-voltage battery in the underbody can deliver up to 290 kW in short bursts, enabling a top speed of 240 km/h. "The DCC (adaptive chassis control) has also been tuned for use on the racetrack and ensures precise handling and high traction in conjunction with the front differential lock," the manufacturer stated. Additionally, a new ceramic brake system within the One-Box braking system is another key component in delivering the high performance.

Kai Grünitz, Member of the Board of Management for Technical Development at Volkswagen and Brand Group Core, commented: "The GTI has been one of Volkswagen's great success stories for 50 years. This year, we launched our GTI as an electric vehicle in series production for the first time. With the ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept, we are showing how much more potential there is in the electric GTI world. Our platform strategy and MEB+ give us the technological freedom to make even extreme concepts possible."

It is particularly interesting how the developers managed to integrate the APP550 drive system into the study in the first place. According to VW, this motor was originally designed for the rear of larger models like the ID. Tiguan or ID.7. "It therefore had to be fitted into the smaller ID. Polo as front-wheel drive and additionally combined with an electronic front differential lock," the company explains.

Externally, the ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept follows the design of the GTI production model, enriched with the aforementioned motorsport attributes. The most striking feature is the two-door silhouette instead of the four-door design, which is reflected in the interior with a two-seat layout. The cockpit is tailored for the racetrack and only loosely based on the production model's interior. For example, all infotainment systems have been omitted. Instead, Recaro racing bucket seats with red five-point harnesses await the driver and passenger. And in place of the rear seats and boot, a roll cage extends above and behind the front seats.

Whether aspects of the study will make it into series production – and whether we might eventually see the APP550 drive system in the electric small-car segment – remains an open question. For now, VW will first launch two electric GTI models. The new ID.3 GTI is set to go on sale in early 2027. The ID. Polo GTI has been available for order since October.

Thomas Schmall, Board Member for Technology and CEO of Volkswagen Group Components, emphasised: "The ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept shows just how emotive electric mobility can be: This is made possible by our APP550, one of the most efficient electric drives in its class. Manufactured over one million times at the Group Components plant in Kassel, this drive was actually developed for larger vehicle classes. Integrating it into the Polo required millimetre precision and is a powerful example of engineering skill and teamwork at Volkswagen, from Development and Design through to our trainees who worked on the project."
