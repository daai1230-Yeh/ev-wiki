# Infineon introduces power module for commercial electric vehicles

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 24.11.2026 - 14:32

---

Semiconductor manufacturer Infineon is expanding its portfolio with a power module for traction inverters in electric commercial vehicles. The new HybridPACK HD is based on silicon carbide technology and is designed for a service life of more than 1.2 million kilometers.

The power electronics of electric commercial vehicles face special demands, including high mechanical stresses from vibrations, intensive load cycling and operating lifetimes exceeding 80,000 hours. Infineon’s new power module is designed to cover a broad range of applications, from heavy-duty electric trucks in long-haul transport to urban delivery vehicles and electric buses.

According to Infineon, the HybridPACK HD offers an effective output current of 500 to 1,200 amperes and is based on silicon carbide MOSFETs (SiC) from the CoolSiC family, with a blocking voltage of 1,400 volts. Combined with the company’s proprietary .XT interconnect technology, the module is said to achieve a long service life and enable compact inverter designs without the need to connect multiple power modules in parallel.

The traction inverter converts the direct current from the vehicle battery into alternating current for the electric motor. In heavy commercial vehicles that require high drive power over extended periods, efficiency, thermal robustness and the lifespan of the power electronics play a critical role.

Infineon states that the module has been specifically tailored to the requirements of silicon carbide semiconductors. This includes a low-inductance design and thermal management optimised for high switching currents. Direct water cooling and laser-welded connections during system integration are said to further enhance robustness under demanding operating conditions.

Infineon expects these features to reduce the total cost of ownership for vehicle manufacturers and fleet operators. However, the company has not provided specific details on potential cost savings or efficiency improvements compared to previous power modules.

Additionally, the HybridPACK HD housing is already designed for battery voltages of up to 1,500 volts DC, according to Infineon. This is intended to lay the groundwork for future high-voltage vehicle architectures, which are gaining importance in connection with megawatt charging. The development effort required to transition to higher system voltages is expected to be reduced as a result.

In parallel with the development of new power modules, Infineon is also scaling up its silicon carbide production. At the beginning of November, the semiconductor manufacturer announced that it had produced the first functional SiC power devices on 300-millimetre wafers at its Austrian plant in Villach. Compared to the previous 200-millimetre technology, this reportedly allows approximately 2.3 times as many chips to be obtained per wafer. In the long term, this is expected to enable more cost-effective production of larger volumes. The company has not yet specified a timeline for ramping up production.

Infineon is also working on more efficient processes for manufacturing SiC wafers themselves. At its Dresden site, the company has successfully tested the so-called cold-split technology for 300-millimetre SiC. This method separates the wafers from a SiC crystal with less material loss and shorter processing times compared to conventional wire sawing.

These developments are part of Infineon’s overarching 300-millimetre strategy. At the end of October, the company announced that it would invest around €1.1 billion in the Villach site by 2034. High-volume production of gallium nitride power semiconductors on 300-millimetre wafers is also set to begin there at the end of 2027.
