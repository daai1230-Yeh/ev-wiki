# Nissan Kicks coming to Europe as a series hybrid

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-09-16 12:00

---

Nissan has announced a new model for the European market, which will be built at its plant in Sunderland, UK. However, unlike the Leaf - which is also manufactured in Sunderland - the new Nissan Kicks is not an electric model but a full series hybrid.

Those familiar with the Nissan Kicks may have already spotted the car while travelling: the compact B-segment crossover was launched by Nissan outside Europe as early as 2016 as part of its activities as a sponsor of the Rio de Janeiro Olympic Games. Now available in 70 countries, the second generation Nissan Kicks is currently produced in Japan, Mexico, and Brazil – with 1.8 million units sold to date.

Despite this, the model has so far been absent from Europe – until now. A decade after the car's global debut, the company plans to invest £170 million pounds (~€198 million) in its Sunderland plant in the UK, where the Nissan Kicks will be produced alongside the all-electric Nissan Leaf, the Nissan Juke, which will also be all-electric from 2027, and the hybrid Nissan Qashqai, whose electric version has apparently been put on ice.

## New BEV Nissan Pixo also on the way

The all-electric Nissan Pixo – closely related to the Renault Twingo Electric and the second generation of the Dacia Spring – will also be unveiled next week. However, it will not be built at Nissan's Sunderland plant but at Renault's factory in Novo Mesto, Slovenia. At the same time, the decision to introduce a European version of the Nissan Kicks shows that Nissan is not yet ready to fully focus on BEV models for Europe, as evidenced by the delays in the planned electric Nissan Qashqai.

The Nissan e-Power system, which will also be used for the European Nissan Kicks, is a series hybrid system and differs from full hybrids by other manufacturers: the wheels are always driven by an electric motor, while the internal combustion engine serves solely as a power generator. There is no mechanical connection between the combustion engine and the wheels. This makes the concept similar to an electric car with a range extender – except that the battery in the e-Power system is not charged externally. In contrast, with classic full hybrids, such as those from Toyota, the combustion engine can also directly transmit driving power to the wheels depending on the driving situation.
