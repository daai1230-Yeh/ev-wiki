# World’s largest electric ship enters commercial operation

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-10-26

---

The world's largest electric ship has begun commercial operations in South America. The 130-meter-long 'China Zorrilla' can transport up to 2,100 passengers and 225 vehicles. The electric ferry was built by Australian company Incat Tasmania for the ferry operator Buquebus.

The electric ship, which was still known as Hull 096 during its construction and testing phase, has now completed its first commercial voyage between Buenos Aires in Argentina and Colonia in Uruguay. It was named after the popular Uruguayan actress and director Concepción "China" Zorrilla, a figure beloved in both countries.

The vessel was built by Incat Tasmania for the South American ferry operator Buquebus. The all-electric ferry can transport up to 2,100 passengers and 225 vehicles across the Río de la Plata. Its energy storage system has a capacity of more than 40 MWh, with the batteries weighing a total of over 250 tonnes. Eight electrically powered waterjet propulsion units provide the thrust.

## Historic day for the maritime industry

Incat describes the start of operations as an important milestone for the electrification of maritime transport. CEO Robert Clifford states: "This is a truly historic day for Incat, for Tasmania, and for the global maritime industry. Over the years, hundreds of talented people at our shipyard in Hobart have worked tirelessly to make this extraordinary vessel a reality."

In Incat's view, the China Zorrilla demonstrates that even large commercial vessels with high transport capacity can be fully electrically powered. The battery-electric propulsion is expected to give passengers a quieter crossing, free from engine noise and vibrations, with zero direct exhaust emissions on board.

## Originally an LNG-powered vessel

Incat received the order in summer 2023. Initially the ferry was designed with LNG propulsion, but the concept was later changed to fully battery-electric. The ship is an aluminium catamaran.

Even during construction, Incat secured an order for additional electric ships of similar scale: for Danish operator Molslinjen, the Hobart shipyard is building three 129-metre electric ferries for the Kattegat route.
