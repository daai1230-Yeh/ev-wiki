# Inside the Smart #2: Timo Weil explains key vehicle features

**Author**: Peter Schwierz | **Date**: 2026-07-02 18:00

---

The Smart #2 aims to be more than a nostalgic revival of the brand's original city car. Conceived as an ultra-compact electric vehicle with a range of around 300 kilometres, fast-charging capability and an exceptionally small turning circle, it is designed specifically for urban mobility. Smart is equipping the model with a dedicated ECA platform, a turning circle of less than seven metres, a 35.7 kWh battery and a targeted range of around 300 kilometres, with charging from 10 to 80 per cent in under 20 minutes. The vehicle is expected to start at less than €22,500.

In an interview conducted at a press event in Rome, Timo Weil — the lead vehicle development engineer who worked closely with colleagues from Mercedes' partner Geely in China — explained the technical foundations of the new electric city car. The new platform replaces the old De Dion solid rear axle with a state-of-the-art five-link rear axle, a first in the A-segment, giving engineers new possibilities for tuning comfort while maintaining stability. The steering has also been redesigned to be more direct, and the battery capacity is roughly double that of previous models.

Weil confirmed that the Tridion safety cell — a hallmark of the original Smart fortwo — will return in the Smart #2, underlining that passive safety remains a top priority despite the car's compact size. He declined to give exact range or thermal-management details, noting homologation is still underway, but said the car "will be setting the benchmark for range in the A-segment." Asked what would most intrigue a former fortwo driver, Weil pointed to the turning circle, and said he hopes to see the new #2 complementing rather than replacing the old Smarts on the streets of Rome within five years.
