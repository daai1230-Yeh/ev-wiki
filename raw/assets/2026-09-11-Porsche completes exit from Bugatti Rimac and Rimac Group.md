# Porsche completes exit from Bugatti Rimac and Rimac Group

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-09-10

---

The sale of all Porsche shares in Croatian automaker Rimac Group and the Bugatti Rimac hypercar joint venture, announced in April, has now been completed. Porsche generated approximately €1 billion from the transaction — and Bugatti President Christophe Piochon is stepping down.

Tensions between Porsche and Rimac have been public knowledge since April 2025, when rumours emerged that Rimac wanted to push the German sports car manufacturer out of the partnership. In April 2026, both companies announced that a consortium led by financial investor HOF Capital — backed by the Egyptian billionaire Sawiris family — intended to purchase Porsche's stakes in Bugatti Rimac and the Rimac Group.

These transactions have now been completed: following regulatory approval, the Stuttgart-based sports car manufacturer finalised the sale of its stakes in Bugatti Rimac and the Rimac Group on 9 September 2026. Porsche AG will receive proceeds of around €1 billion, of which the company will allocate €250 million to further fund its pension obligations.

Rimac founder Mate Rimac said: "This is an important step for Bugatti Rimac and the entire Rimac Group. I am delighted that HOF Capital and its consortium are becoming our partners in the next phase of our Group's development."

**Bugatti CEO Christophe Piochon departs.** The new shareholder structure brings a reshuffle in Bugatti Rimac's top management: Christophe Piochon, who became head of the Bugatti brand during the VW era under Ferdinand Piëch, is stepping down as President of Bugatti Automobiles and Chief Operating Officer of Bugatti Rimac. Mate Rimac will now take on the role of President of Bugatti Automobiles himself, while Marko Brkljačić — former Chief Operating Officer of Rimac Technology — joins Bugatti Rimac's leadership team as Chief Operating Officer.

Mate Rimac, a Croatian who grew up in Germany, founded his company in 2009 after converting an old BMW 3 Series into an electric car in a garage. His startup went on to develop the fully electric hypercar Rimac Nevera, in limited production since 2022, with a top speed of 412 km/h.

**Rimac and Porsche linked since 2018.** Porsche first invested in Rimac in 2018 and gradually increased its stake to 21%. In 2021, Porsche brought its hypercar brand Bugatti into a joint venture with Rimac called Bugatti Rimac, with the Rimac Group holding a 55% majority. Today Rimac spans four divisions: Bugatti Rimac (hypercars, electric and conventional), Rimac Technology (Tier-1 supplier, including battery systems for the BMW i7), Verne (robotaxi service), and Rimac Energy (stationary battery storage).
