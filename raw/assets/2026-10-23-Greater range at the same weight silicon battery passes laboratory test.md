# Greater range at the same weight: silicon battery passes laboratory test

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-10-22

---

A critical breakthrough in battery research saw film cells with silicon-rich anodes endure over 1,000 cycles in testing without dropping below 80 per cent capacity.

Researchers at Fraunhofer ISE operated a self-manufactured 25-Ah pouch cell with silicon-rich anodes for over 1,000 full cycles in the 'StrOboBatt' project. After testing, the cell retained more than 83 per cent of its capacity; 1,000 cycles at 80%+ is considered the minimum for commercial use.

## Silicon: high energy density, but extreme expansion

Silicon's volume expands by up to 300 per cent during charging (graphite: about 10–12 per cent), causing loss of electrical contact and faster electrolyte decomposition. Fraunhofer used a silicon-carbon composite (51 per cent of the anode's active material) with a nickel-rich cathode, produced on an industry-scale pilot line.

## Pressure and charging dynamics are key factors

With axial pressure of 1,000 kPa (about 10 bar), electrode contact stayed stable; at 300 kPa (about 3 bar), usable capacity dropped much faster. Capacity also declines more rapidly at high power levels than in gentle test cycles, which battery management systems must account for.

The cells reached 280 Wh/kg; optimised mass production could reach about 330 Wh/kg — roughly 20–35 per cent above today's NMC/NCA cells and almost double LFP.

## European raw materials for greater range

An EV could carry nearly 100 kWh instead of about 75 kWh at the same battery weight, raising a mid-sized car's realistic range from about 450 km to well over 600 km. Because graphite is imported mainly from outside Europe, synthetic silicon composites could shift part of the raw-material value chain to Europe.
