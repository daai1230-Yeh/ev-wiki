# Tesla to train Optimus with employees at Grünheide plant

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 22.07.2026

---

Tesla will use employees at its Grünheide vehicle plant to help train its humanoid robot, Optimus, by recording human movement patterns during assembly work. At the same time, the company is expanding its robotics activities in Reutlingen, where it is developing components and production processes for the large-scale manufacture of the robot.

Selected employees at Tesla's Gigafactory in Grünheide will soon wear backpack-mounted cameras while working. According to Handelsblatt, citing an internal Tesla communication, Tesla will use the captured movement data to train its humanoid robot, Optimus.

The cameras will record how employees grip tools, handle components and perform individual work steps. Tesla will use the data to train Optimus to carry out these tasks independently. The company already follows this approach in the US, where so-called Data Collection Operators perform specific movement sequences to train the humanoid robot.

It remains unclear what role Optimus could play at the Grünheide plant in the future. The report also does not specify the conditions under which employees will participate in the data collection. According to Handelsblatt, the initiative has not yet been discussed with the works council, even though technical systems capable of monitoring employee behaviour or performance may be subject to co-determination under German labour law. Tesla did not respond to the newspaper's enquiry.

While Grünheide is set to provide movement data, Tesla's robotics business is taking shape at another German site: Reutlingen. There, the company is recruiting specialists to develop and manufacture components such as sensors, gearboxes and actuators, as well as design production equipment and manufacturing processes for future mass production. Tesla acquired a significant part of the site from the insolvent machinery manufacturer Manz in 2025.

It remains unclear how close Optimus is to mass production. Tesla currently builds the humanoid robot on a pilot production line in Fremont, California, and does not disclose production volumes. The company's long-term ambitions are far more expansive, with plans for a factory capable of producing up to one million robots per year.

The plans in Grünheide are set against an interesting backdrop. Tesla aims to significantly ramp up production of the Model Y and is hiring additional staff for this purpose. In parallel, battery cell production is scheduled to commence in 2027. Grünheide plant manager André Thierig has previously indicated that additional automation could eventually reduce the number of employees required to produce the same volume of vehicles.
