# Hydrovolt to recycle Iceland's EV batteries in Future

**Author**: Kasimir Hupe | **Date**: 2026-10-20

---

Norwegian battery recycler Hydrovolt has signed a cooperation agreement with the Icelandic Recycling Fund (IRF). Under this agreement, Hydrovolt will collect end-of-life batteries from electric vehicles and industrial applications in Iceland, transport them to Norway, and recycle them at its own facility in Fredrikstad. The company classifies this step as part of its Nordic strategy: no battery in the region should remain unused.

The IRF implements extended producer responsibility in Iceland. Anyone who places products such as batteries on the market is also responsible for ensuring they are properly collected and recycled at the end of their life cycle. Traction batteries from electric vehicles are explicitly included.

Demand is growing. According to a press release, 17 per cent of Iceland's passenger car fleet now consists of battery-electric vehicles and plug-in hybrids. For new cars, Hydrovolt reports an electrified share of nearly 80 per cent – though this figure includes hybrids. In the future, end-of-life batteries will be recycled in Norway. "As battery volumes continue to grow, it is important that appropriate recycling solutions are in place," said Íris Gunnarsdóttir, who is responsible for e-waste and batteries at the IRF.

## From battery packs to black mass

Hydrovolt offers battery importers a complete package: assessing whether a battery can have a second life, collection, transport, and recycling. End-of-life batteries will be transported across the North Atlantic to Fredrikstad, south of Oslo. "Through our logistics network and recycling process, we will ensure the recovery of critical raw materials from the batteries at high recovery rates," said Hans Bjerkaas, Chief Commercial and Development Officer at Hydrovolt.

The Fredrikstad plant has been operational since May 2022 and is the largest battery recycling facility in the Nordic region. It is designed to process 12,000 tonnes of battery packs per year, equivalent to the batteries from 25,000 to 30,000 cars.

Hydrovolt uses a mechanical and dry process: the packs are discharged, disassembled, and shredded. Aluminium, copper, and plastics can be separated directly. What remains is the so-called black mass, containing lithium, nickel, cobalt, and manganese, which are extracted in a second, chemical step with partners (e.g. Fortum in Harjavalta, Finland). Hydrovolt states that the overall recycling rate is nearly 100 per cent.

## Beyond Northvolt

Hydrovolt is authorised as a treatment facility in Norway, Sweden, and Denmark. It entered Sweden in February 2026 and, together with partner KABoB, Denmark in September 2026. Iceland is the next step. In 2024, the company also decided on an expansion to Hordain in northern France.

Hydrovolt was founded in 2020 as a joint venture between Norsk Hydro and Northvolt. Northvolt withdrew from financing in November 2024; in January 2025 Hydro agreed to acquire the remaining shares for NOK 78 million (approx. €6.7 million), increasing its stake from 72 to 100 per cent. Northvolt itself filed for bankruptcy in Sweden in March 2025.
