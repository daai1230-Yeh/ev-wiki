# Critical Materials: Tesla Cybercabs are coming

**Author**: Rob Stumpf & Tim Levin | **Date**: 2026-08-28 | **Source**: InsideEVs *Critical Materials* weekly newsletter

---

## Plug In: Tesla's Big Cybercab Launch

Tesla's steering wheel-free, scissor door-having Cybercab is apparently ready for primetime.

The two-seat robotaxi is getting an official launch event next Thursday in Austin, the carmaker announced this week. How quickly Cybercabs will actually hit streets—and whether you'll be able to buy one for $30,000 anytime soon—are just some of the questions hanging over this rollout.

A big one centers around software. Specifically: Is Tesla's good enough? Famously, Elon Musk is betting that he can make Tesla an autonomous-vehicle juggernaut using cameras alone. The Cybercab was built from the ground up around that gamble. But how reliable Tesla's system is at scale is still an open question. Many experts argue that sensor redundancy through lidar and radar is key for safety. Industry leader Waymo took a not-so-subtle shot at Tesla's strategy this week by saying in a blog post that cameras are "not enough" for safe autonomous driving at scale.

To Tesla's credit, its Robotaxi service does now offer driverless Model Y rides in Austin and some other markets, and that part of the business seems to be growing. On X this week, Tesla said its "unsupervised fleet is a lot bigger" and that it had extended its operating hours. Ethan McKanna of the crowdsourced Robotaxi Tracker website has detected a significant uptick in unsupervised rides, The Verge reported.

But the Robotaxi rollout has also been far more sluggish than Musk once promised. Last year, it was supposed to be "hyper exponential" and cover half the U.S. population by the end of 2025. On recent earnings calls, Musk has struck an uncharacteristically reserved tone, noting that the company is moving slowly to ensure safety. Tesla's robotic taxi business remains far behind Waymo, which has thousands of vehicles in U.S. cities and is doing more than 500,000 autonomous rides per week. To date, the Alphabet subsidiary has logged over 200 million driverless miles, while Tesla has done in the neighborhood of 380,000, per its last earnings call.

The Cybercab rollout may not actually be that fast, either. Data is part of the reason. "Because it is a new vehicle chassis," Musk said on Tesla's latest earnings call, "we need to accumulate driving data that is specific to the Cybercab before we can put a lot of them on the road." Hence why human-driven Cybercabs have been spotted all over the country, with retrofitted steering wheels and brakes. There's another reason for caution: The flashy, instantly recognizable cars will put any missteps by Tesla's autonomous system on full display.

Also open: what's going on with the wireless chargers. Tesla has said the cabs would charge wirelessly, much like a phone does, presumably so they could operate with as little human intervention (and cost) as possible. But lately little has been heard about the infrastructure or logistics required to operate Cybercabs. Cybercabs have also been spotted testing with a conventional charging port, so perhaps the plans have changed.

Operating an Uber-like robotaxi network has always been just part of the Tesla pitch. Musk's core promise—the reason Tesla's market cap is over 10 times that of General Motors—is that this is the company that will make your car drive itself. In 2024, Musk said anyone would be able to buy a Cybercab for $30,000 and add it to the Tesla network to earn side income. Right now, there is no vehicle you can buy with Level 4 capability.

There's also the question of legally selling or deploying a vehicle without features typically required by federal motor vehicle safety standards, like brakes, side mirrors, and a steering wheel. Amazon-owned Zoox recently got an exemption from the U.S. government to commercially deploy up to 5,000 of its toaster-shaped pods, which also lack such accoutrement, over two years.

## Get Fully Charged: This Week's Biggest EV News

- **BMW hits 2 million EVs sold.** Its biggest winner of the year is the new iX3, which is carrying the brand's electrified vehicle sales growth. Half of all X3 nameplate orders in Europe are for the iX3.
- **The Chevy Bolt is not long for this world.** GM's affordable Ultium-based EV was pitched as a major win for BEV adoption, but changes are coming for anyone who wants to pick one up before it's gone.
- **The new Hyundai Santa Fe will be electric — but keeps the gas tank.** It's an EREV with over 600 miles of range, arriving in 2027 as part of an EREV boom.
- **Polestar feels burned.** Per a Wall Street Journal report, the Chinese/Swedish EV brand still has no idea why it was kicked out of the U.S. despite sister company Volvo cutting a deal to keep selling cars. Both are owned by Geely. One New Jersey dealer is suing Polestar for $25 million.
- **Rivian's CFO is departing** at the end of October.
- **Tesla Cybertruck sales are struggling**, and a $5,000 price bump for the base model made its cheapest version 25% more expensive since February.

## Battery & Charging News

- **BYD launched its 10,000th Flash Charging station**, doubling its charging network in five months and still averaging about 80 new stations each day.
- **An automaker stress-tested an EV battery** by fast-charging it 350 times over 9 days to address concerns about ultra-fast charging and long-term battery reliability.
- **Chinese researchers hit a battery density breakthrough**, uncovering a way to break the 600 Wh/kg barrier — more than double both Tesla's 2170 and 4680 cells.
- **Hyundai is entering smart charging** via a partnership to help UK EV drivers automatically charge when electricity rates are lower, laying groundwork for future vehicle-to-grid tech.
- **Voltpost**, the lamppost-charger startup, announced ceiling-mounted units for parking garages, plus a product for walls and posts.

## Autonomy & Tech News

- **Tesla's door handles are proving a big headache.** It must recall 3 million cars in China to meet new safety standards, and a European country is now weighing whether to act.
- **Drone delivery for auto parts:** one dealership in Detroit is launching an air-to-ground delivery service for car parts.
- **Waymo took an indirect swipe at Tesla's self-driving strategy.** Waymo VP of onboard software Srikanth Thirumalai said cameras alone "aren't enough" for autonomous driving, that improving a Level 2 driver-assist system until it reaches full autonomy is a "false summit," and that end-to-end AI self-driving systems "run the risk of black box failures."
- **Waymo is coming to Europe**, targeting a 2027 launch in Germany.
