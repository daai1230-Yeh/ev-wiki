# This 400 kW EV Charger Uses Old EV Batteries To Avoid Costly Grid Upgrades

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-10-28

---

Allye's new DC fast charger can use a simple 16A grid connection, energizing depots and temporary sites in hours instead of months.

A new charging station typically needs extremely costly grid connection upgrades, and paperwork can take months. Great Britain's Allye Energy is now selling a battery-buffered EV fast charger that can go online in hours and only needs a modest grid connection. The new MEV is billed as the world's first dedicated EV charger with built-in battery storage made from repurposed electric vehicle packs.

It can deliver up to 400 kW to two EVs simultaneously through integrated cables, or feed multiple charging satellites. The second-life packs, from different manufacturers and of varying ages, offer 155 or 232 kWh of storage. The minimum grid connection is a three-phase 16 A hookup, with up to 125 A supported. The batteries top up when the ports are idle and deliver energy when an EV plugs in.

Allye sees depots with industrial electrical connections as the best use case; the all-in-one unit can be placed on the ground, hooked up by industrial outlet or hardwiring, and later lifted onto a truck and relocated. It works with 800 V and 400 V EVs and uses 95% British-sourced components, with batteries from UK-registered cars. Safety and anti-theft features include cable-cut detection, forensic marking, quick-release terminals, a pyro fuse per pack, off-gas detection and fire barriers between packs.

At J&M Murdoch's refuse depot in Glasgow, where a grid upgrade was quoted at £300,000 (about $400,000), a battery-buffered Allye dispenser was running within a day and now supplies around 200 kWh daily.
