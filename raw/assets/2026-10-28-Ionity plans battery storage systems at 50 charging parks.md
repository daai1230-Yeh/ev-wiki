# Ionity plans battery storage systems at 50 charging parks

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-10-27

---

Ionity has integrated a battery storage system at one of its sites for the first time, enabling the number of charging points at a charging park in Baden-Württemberg to be increased. But this is just the beginning, as Ionity now plans to install 50 battery storage systems at its charging parks next year.

The use of battery storage systems is a key part of Ionity's future expansion strategy. The charge point operator intends to deploy battery energy storage systems at selected locations to provide additional charging points and utilise the available energy more efficiently. At sites with photovoltaic systems, the batteries will also store locally generated solar power and make it available later for charging operations.

The first charging park with an integrated battery storage system has been operational for a few days in Wertheim, Baden-Württemberg. The existing park, between an A3 motorway exit, a McDonald's and the Wertheim Village outlet centre, previously had four charging points; Ionity has now expanded to six, all Alpitronic HYC400 stations. Two additional charging points with an ABB 350 kW station are to follow soon.

## BESS bridges long wait for grid connection upgrade

In Wertheim, the primary purpose of the battery storage system is to bridge the time until the medium-voltage grid connection is upgraded, which can take up to three years. The system is supplied by Chinese manufacturer Sungrow and consists of four modular units with a total capacity of 1,028 kWh and maximum power of 500 kW, housed in switchgear cabinets on site.

An intelligent energy management system allocates available power between grid connection, battery storage and charging points. During high utilisation the storage can provide additional charging power while reducing peak loads.

## Twelve charging points, PV system, and battery storage

Another project is under development in Hemsbach, Baden-Württemberg: a completely new fast-charging park with a photovoltaic canopy (more than 223 kWp) and a BESS of over 2 MWh, serving twelve fast-charging points. Locally generated solar power will be stored and later used for charging.

In the long term, Ionity aims to use battery storage for grid services and potentially energy trading, and is further developing its energy management system to coordinate grid connection, storage, solar and charging points.

## More than 20 MWh of BESS capacity by 2027

Scaling in 2027 involves around 50 battery storage systems across Europe with a total capacity of more than 20 MWh. Christoph Strecker, Head of Rollout at Ionity: "For us, it's not just about building more charging points, but about creating the right charging infrastructure in the right locations and continuously improving the charging experience for our customers." He adds that storage enables new locations, more efficient electricity use and an active role in the energy system, and that "Wertheim is just the beginning."

So far Ionity has only used external BESS systems, which offer more flexibility as they can supply multiple charging points independent of the hardware. The company remains open to charging systems with integrated storage and is evaluating them.
