# 'Unburdensome': The Slate Truck Is Easier Than A Tesla To Live With, James May Says

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-10-28

---

Jeff Bezos-backed Slate Auto is expected to begin deliveries of its affordable electric pickup in December. With that date approaching, the startup is handing the truck to more journalists, with former Top Gear host James May offering his take in his latest video.

May found the Slate truck surprisingly easy to live with despite its stripped-back cabin and limited standard equipment. "This is an incredibly easy car to live with, even easier than my Tesla to be honest," he said. "This is a welcome return to the pickup philosophy. It's straightforward and basic and cheap as it should be."

He was impressed by the driving characteristics: regenerative braking under one-pedal driving was pronounced but easy to get used to, and "the ride is actually pretty good for something short and springy on big, fat tires." The cabin was reasonably quiet; he called the plasticky interior "perfectly okay," though he criticized the large blind spot over his left shoulder.

Starting at $24,950 before a $1,450 destination charge, the two-seat, rear-wheel-drive pickup does away with modern software, touchscreen, conventional stereo and elaborate trim. Buyers get air conditioning, cruise control and physical controls, with manual windows and mirrors.

It uses a single electric motor and an LFP battery pack with a projected EPA range of 205 miles. The five-foot bed offers 35.1 cubic feet; payload is estimated at 1,550 pounds and towing about 2,000 pounds.

Its most unique feature is customizability: accessories, exterior wraps, and conversion kits that turn the pickup into an SUV or fastback. "You can think of this a bit like buying a bagel. You buy the bagel and then you put the filling in," May said.

The upcoming Ford Fathom will be the opposite, with a $30,000 starting price but a fully software-defined vehicle with a central touchscreen and powered windows.
