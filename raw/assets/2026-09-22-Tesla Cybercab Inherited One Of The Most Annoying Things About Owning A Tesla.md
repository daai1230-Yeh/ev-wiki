# Tesla Cybercab Inherited One Of The Most Annoying Things About Owning A Tesla

**Author**: Rob Stumpf | **Date**: 2026-09-21

---

Riders report that Tesla's robotaxis are taking strange routes, something that Tesla owners have been highlighting for years.

- The Tesla Cybercab seems to have inherited a problem that owners have complained about for years: navigation.
- One rider says that a simple 10-minute drive took them over an hour to complete.
- Navigation issues have plagued Tesla owners for some time, but the robotaxi's issues may be compounded by tighter safety constraints.

Tesla owners have complained about their cars' navigation systems for years, and now, the cars operating on Tesla's robotaxi network seem to be experiencing similar problems.

Normally, this is just something that's annoying. The navigation points you right when you should go left, only for you to figure out that it just put you on a 5-mile detour for seemingly no good reason. If you catch it early enough, just flip a U-turn, and you're golden. Unfortunately, Tesla's driverless cars don't have that option.

One rider in Austin, Texas recently experienced this firsthand when they hopped in a Cybercab expecting a ten-minute trip to their destination. The Cybercab had other plans: a massive south detour through East Austin turned the trip into a 70-minute ride, according to a Reddit post.

One issue likely responsible for this rider's hour-long trip in the robotaxi is the restriction on highway travel. Tesla's fleet sticks to surface-level, low-speed roads. Another, as one report from 2025 points out, could be at-grade railroad crossings across Austin. Put the two together, and now the Cybercab has to do some routing magic to make the trip possible. The user said that upon booking the ride, the Robotaxi app didn't indicate how long the trip would take.

Other Tesla Robotaxi riders have reported odd routes. An Axios reporter said the Cybercab dropped him off at the business next door to where he wanted to go. It's unclear how frequently these problems occur across the service as a whole.

It's common for robotaxis to have restrictions on which kinds of roads they can handle. Waymo operated in the San Francisco Bay Area for well over a year before its cars began venturing onto highways with paying passengers. Tesla did not respond to questions about whether this detour had to do with a mapping error, operational design domain constraints, or both.

However, it's hard to ignore that Tesla has had an ongoing mapping problem for ages. Users have been complaining that Tesla's navigation has made illogical decisions for years, including on Full Self-Driving (Supervised). The only difference in this case is that there's no driver behind the wheel to nudge the car in the right direction if that occurs.

If the fleet's restrictions on highway use or railroad crossings change in the future, it could help to cut down on some of the issues that early robotaxi riders are seeing today. However, CEO Elon Musk did say that Tesla was going to be "super paranoid" about safety in its rollout. So the expansion to more types of road conditions may happen gradually.
