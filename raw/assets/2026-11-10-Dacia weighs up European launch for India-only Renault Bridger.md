# Dacia weighs up European launch for India-only Renault Bridger

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-11-09

---

Dacia could be set to expand its range of electric cars in Europe with another model. According to reports, the Renault subsidiary is considering bringing the Renault Bridger, developed for the Indian market, to Europe as an electric car - for which it would need to be adapted accordingly.

The Renault Bridger was unveiled in March 2026 as part of the new Renault futuREady strategy plan, but has so far received little attention in Europe due to its focus on the Indian market. The sub-four-metre SUV features a boxy, rugged design with 20 centimetres of ground clearance and a spare wheel mounted externally on the tailgate. Despite its compact dimensions, Renault promises a spacious interior, offering 20 centimetres of rear knee room and a 400-litre boot.

## Bridger could complete Dacia's electric lineup

The production version is set to be based on the new modular RGMP small platform and will be available with internal combustion, hybrid, and battery-electric powertrains, depending on the market. Renault plans to launch the model in India by the end of 2027, with a gradual rollout to other international markets to follow. Europe was not originally part of the plan.

According to British trade publication Autocar, this could now change, as Dacia needs to electrify its model range more quickly and reduce the brand's CO₂ emissions. Dacia has already announced plans to introduce a total of four electric vehicles by 2030. These include the second-generation Spring, which has just been launched, as well as electric versions of the Sandero and Sandero Stepway.

Originally, an electric version of the new Striker crossover was apparently intended as the fourth model. However, according to the report, Dacia has since abandoned these plans. The Bridger could fill this gap. On the European market, it could compete directly with similarly conceived models such as the Citroën ë-C3 and the Fiat Grande Panda Electric.

## 'Super-credits' for Bridger possible under EU rules

Proposed European incentive regulations could also play a role. The Industrial Accelerator Act, proposed by the European Commission, includes additional super-credits for electric vehicles produced in Europe. Models under 4.20 metres in length, belonging to the planned M1E vehicle class, could particularly benefit. At less than four metres long, the Bridger would fall below this threshold. However, a European version would need to be adapted accordingly and ideally produced locally.

Renault Group CEO François Provost already hinted at the possibility of a European launch at the Paris Motor Show in October. Although the Bridger was developed for India, he said it could one day enter the group's markets, according to Autocar. No official decision has yet been made regarding its introduction as a Dacia model.
