# Canada's EV import quota for China-made cars mostly benefits Tesla

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-09-08 11:34

---

In January, Canada and China announced an agreement enabling the import of 49,000 electric vehicles from China to Canada at a significantly reduced tariff rate of 6.1 %. Now it turns out that Tesla is benefiting the most from this deal and is once again shipping electric cars from its Shanghai factory to Canada.

The agreement is initially limited to one year and divided into two time windows. Originally, 24,500 electric vehicles were to be imported into Canada at the reduced tariff rate from March to August 2027 and from September 2027 to February 2028 in exchange for lower tariffs on other goods. The regulation allows automakers to import their electric vehicles manufactured in China at a tariff rate of just 6.1 per cent instead of the usual 106.1 per cent. Importantly, the emphasis here is on "manufactured in China" rather than "Chinese manufacturers"—more on this later.

The reduced tariff rate operates on a first-come, first-served basis, as the responsible authority, Global Affairs Canada, announced at the time. However, a genuine "race" for the discounted import of EVs from China to Canada did not materialise in the first six months. By 31 August 2026, only 15,603 electric vehicles were imported from China; not the approved 24,500.

Perhaps the discounted tariff rate for Chinese EV manufacturers simply came too soon. For instance, market leader BYD is not expected to launch in Canada until the end of 2026, and its vehicles are not yet available there at all. Similarly, brands such as Chery (Jaecoo/Omoda), Geely, Leapmotor, MG Motor, Nio, Xpeng, or Zeekr are not yet active in Canada.

## Tesla is the real winner

So how did the figure of 15,603 EV imports from China come about? The answer may come as a surprise at first: it's Tesla. Currently, the US manufacturer is the biggest beneficiary of the tariff discount for EVs from China because, according to media reports, Tesla began importing electric vehicles manufactured at its Gigafactory in Shanghai to Canada in May. Tesla had previously shipped vehicles from Giga Shanghai to Canada because production costs there are significantly lower than at its factories in the US or Germany. However, in 2024, Canada followed the US example and imposed additional import tariffs of 100% on electric vehicles from China, after which Tesla halted imports from China.

Now, however, the reduced tariff rate does not apply explicitly only to Chinese manufacturers but to EVs manufactured in China, from which Tesla and its customers benefit. Additionally, a few vehicles from Lotus, Polestar, and GM's Lincoln brand are said to have been imported into Canada under this programme, although Canadian authorities have not yet published official figures.

The unused quota of 8,897 electric vehicles from the period between February and August has now been transferred to the second window, according to Global Affairs Canada. Therefore, between 1 September 2026 and 28 February 2027, a total of 33,397 (instead of the original 24,500) electric vehicles from China can be imported into Canada at the reduced tariff rate of 6.1%. At least towards the end of this window, Tesla may face serious competition from Chinese brands like BYD for the favourable tariff quotas.
