# Vulcan advances Ludwig lithium project

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 04.09.2026

---

Vulcan Energy published a pre-feasibility study for "Ludwig," its second lithium-and-geothermal project in Germany's Upper Rhine Valley (Ludwigshafen), following its more advanced Lionheart project near Landau. Unlike Lionheart (which splits extraction and processing between Landau and Frankfurt-Höchst), Ludwig would extract and process lithium-rich geothermal brine at one central plant using Vulcan's proprietary direct lithium extraction technology, targeting 21,100 tonnes/year of battery-grade lithium carbonate plus up to 3,125 GWh/year of renewable heat. Combined with Lionheart's planned output, Vulcan estimates enough lithium for roughly one million EV batteries annually — though all figures remain study-stage projections.

Estimated investment is €1.26 billion (about 15% below Lionheart's), with projected operating costs of €4,101/tonne LCE that Vulcan claims would place it in the lowest cost quartile globally. Lionheart is much further along, with €2.2 billion in financing secured, permits obtained, offtake deals with LG Energy Solution, Umicore, Stellantis, and Glencore, and Siemens as a strategic investor; production there is due in 2028. Ludwig still needs 3D seismic surveys and exploration drilling, with a final investment decision not expected until after Lionheart's production starts. The project is framed as part of a broader push to bring more battery raw-material value creation to Europe, where a recent Deloitte study found the region lags in extraction and processing (50–60% of a battery's value).
