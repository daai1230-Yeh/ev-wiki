# Scenic E-Tech Electric gets new batteries and faster charging

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-09-01

---

Renault has comprehensively revised its Scenic E-Tech Electric, introducing new battery options, faster DC charging, and a range of updates to its design, equipment, and driver assistance systems. The more powerful variant of the electric mid-size SUV will receive a new 89 kWh NMC battery, while a 67 kWh LFP option will follow at a later date.

First launched in spring 2024, the electric Scenic received its first minor update in April 2025 which included one-pedal driving, among other improvements. Under the new model year, Renault is now presenting a comprehensively revised Scenic E-Tech Electric – following recent updates to the Mégane E-Tech Electric and the smaller electric Renault 5.

The new batteries are reserved for the more powerful Scenic E-Tech Electric 220 Long Range version with a 160 kW drivetrain. This variant previously used an 87 kWh NMC battery. In the future, Renault will offer an 89 kWh NMC battery, providing slightly more range and improved fast-charging capability. An LFP battery with 67 kWh will also be available as an alternative "at a later date". For the lower-powered version, Renault has announced no battery changes, retaining the 125 kW drivetrain with the 60 kWh battery.

Previously, the electric Scenic achieved up to 625 km of range with the 160 kW drivetrain while offering average DC charging power of 150 kW, with charging from 15 to 80 per cent taking 37 minutes. With the new 89 kWh NMC battery, range increases slightly by 18 km to 642 km, and charging time is expected to be reduced to 28 minutes (15–80% SoC), though peak charging power remains at 150 kW. The smaller 67 kWh LFP battery offers a WLTP range of up to 467 km, with DC charging time of 24 minutes (15–80% SoC) and peak charging power of 165 kW — the same LFP battery used in the recently facelifted Mégane.

"LFP technology complements the NMC offering with a robust, affordable and versatile alternative," Renault states. It uses the cell-to-pack (C2P) architecture introduced on the New Mégane E-Tech Electric: 232 closely packed pouch cells enabling packing efficiency of 53%.

The vehicle remains based on the AmpR Medium platform (renamed RG Medium 1.0). The battery sits under the floor for better weight distribution and a lower centre of gravity. The electric Scenic remains relatively light (<1,900 kg) and compact (4.47 m).

As part of the facelift, Renault is also revamping the design, digital in-car experience, driver assistance systems, and accessories list. Exterior updates include seven body colours (two new: Satin Blue Slate and Exo Galaxy Grey), glossy black bumper splitters, and new 19-inch/20-inch wheel designs. Interior updates include a new PET fabric for 'Techno' trim seats, a Carbon Black dashboard, a 'spectral titanium' look for 'Esprit Alpine' trim, and an expanded accessories list.

Driver assistance improvements include enhanced adaptive cruise control and 'Active Driver Assist', a new 'Multi Target' function that tracks multiple vehicles simultaneously, and an improved emergency brake assistant. The model also automatically switches between driving modes (Eco, Comfort, Sport) based on driving style, and comes standard with an innovative driver monitoring system across all trim levels.

The car continues to use the 'openR' link system with Google integrations, including new Google Maps route customisation and Gemini as an alternative to Google Assistant, plus a magnetic, ventilated Qi2/MagSafe-compatible wireless smartphone charger.

The Scenic E-Tech Electric is manufactured at Renault's French plant in Douai. It is currently listed starting at €43,000 (60 kWh battery) or €48,000 (87 kWh battery) in Germany, not yet repriced for the facelift. In France, the more powerful motor with the 89 kWh battery starts at €38,750, and the upcoming LFP variant starts at €31,750. The revised model will be available to order in France from autumn 2026.
