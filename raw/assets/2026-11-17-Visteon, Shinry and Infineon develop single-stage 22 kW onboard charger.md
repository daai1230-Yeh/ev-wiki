# Visteon, Shinry and Infineon develop single-stage 22 kW onboard charger

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-16

---

Visteon-Shinry Power Electronics and Infineon are jointly developing a single-stage onboard charger for electric vehicles. The new architecture supports AC charging at up to 22 kW and uses bidirectional GaN switches to achieve a more compact, less complex design than conventional two-stage systems.

Visteon, Shinry and Infineon announced their collaboration at the electronica trade fair in Munich. The new onboard charger combines the converter topology and control strategy developed by Visteon-Shinry Power Electronics (VSPE) with monolithic bidirectional gallium nitride (GaN) switches from Infineon.

The concept centres on a single-stage architecture. Conventional onboard chargers typically convert power in several stages, whereas VSPE aims to combine several of these functions into a single stage. This eliminates the need for large DC-link capacitors, which are used in traditional two-stage OBCs. According to the partners, this reduces the system's size, complexity and cost while improving reliability.

The planned onboard charger supports three-phase AC charging at up to 22 kW and single-phase charging at up to 19.2 kW. The solution is also designed to support bidirectional vehicle-to-everything (V2X) applications.

This means the vehicle can not only draw electricity from the grid but also feed it back. Depending on the vehicle and infrastructure integration, this could enable applications such as vehicle-to-grid (V2G) and vehicle-to-home (V2H).

## Up to 22 kW AC charging and bidirectional power flow

Infineon's bidirectional GaN switches play a central role in the design. According to the semiconductor manufacturer, these GaN BDS devices can block voltage in both directions and conduct current bidirectionally. This makes it possible to implement bidirectional switching functions with fewer semiconductor components. In addition, gallium nitride's high switching frequency allows for smaller magnetic components, increasing power density and further reducing the overall system size.

Infineon is already involved in another project developing compact 22 kW onboard chargers with bidirectional GaN switches. In the EU HiPower 5.0 project, a consortium led by Fraunhofer IZM is developing an OBC designed to reduce the typical volume from around 12 litres to just four litres. Here, too, monolithically integrated bidirectional GaN switches are intended to reduce the number of components, power losses and overall size.

The OBC is being developed by the joint venture Visteon-Shinry Power Electronics LLC. Visteon contributes its expertise in automotive electronics, while Shinry brings experience in onboard chargers and DC/DC converters.

Infineon supplies the GaN power semiconductors. The company considers bidirectional GaN switches particularly suitable for new power conversion architectures that require high switching frequencies, low losses and a compact design.

Development is currently still in the validation phase. The companies have not yet disclosed when the single-stage onboard charger is expected to enter production or which carmaker might adopt the technology.
