# Even Russia Is Beating The U.S. In EV and PHEV Market Share

**Author**: Andrei Nedelea | **Date**: 2026-10-06

---

Gasoline shortages are pushing Russian buyers toward electric cars and plug-in hybrids. Most of them come from China.

- EVs and PHEVs have captured a larger share of the Russian market as the country reels from a fuel crisis.
- Gasoline shortages following Ukrainian refinery strikes are pushing Russian buyers toward Chinese plug-in cars.

Russia is an unlikely place for an electric car sales breakthrough. It has enormous oil resources, long driving distances, middling infrastructure, and winters that wreak havoc on EV range. Yet it now has a higher share of plug-in car sales than the United States, according to new research from Autostat, as cited by The New York Times.

Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries have disrupted fuel supplies, leaving motorists searching for gasoline or waiting hours at stations. That has given some buyers a pressing reason to choose a car that can run without it.

Citing Autostat, the NYT reports that electric vehicles and plug-in hybrids reached a combined 11.5% of Russia's new car market at the end of September, up from about 5% at the beginning of the year. That share is slightly higher than America's. The statistic includes plug-in hybrids, so it doesn't establish that Russia has overtaken the U.S. in fully electric cars alone.

Autostat chief Sergei Tselikov bought a GWM Ora 03 GT electric hatchback in July after visiting five gas stations, none of which had gasoline. The model's price rose by roughly $2,000 within days as demand increased.

Reuters reported that Russian sales of new pure electric cars and plug-in hybrids reached 26,543 during June through August, more than double the 12,187 sold in the same period a year earlier. Separately, NEWS.ru cited Tselikov reporting 2,216 new electric-car sales in August, up 105%, with the main growth coming from models priced at 2 million–3 million rubles ($23,500–$35,300).

America, meanwhile, faces a different disruption. EV sales spiked just before September 30, 2025, when the federal tax credit ended, then immediately slumped, although they have shown some signs of recovering. Cox Automotive estimates that Americans bought 78,895 new battery-electric vehicles this August, accounting for 5.7% of new-vehicle sales. Volume rose 2.5% from July but fell 46.9% against the unusually strong August 2025 comparison. That is a BEV-only figure, so it should not be compared directly with Russia's combined 11.5%.

The EIA, citing Omdia, says BEVs represented 6% of new light-duty sales in the second quarter, with PHEVs adding 1.4%, while conventional hybrids reached 16%.

The European Union is much further along. ACEA reports 1.64 million BEV registrations during January through August, representing 21.7% of new cars. Another 758,082 PHEVs brought the combined plug-in share to 31.7%.

China operates on another scale again. According to CPCA figures, August retail sales included 698,000 BEVs and 307,000 plug-in hybrids (including range extenders). That is roughly 1 million plug-in cars capturing over 65% of the country's passenger car market. Fully electric models alone accounted for approximately 45.3%.

The numbers aren't perfectly apples-to-apples. But in a world of rising gas prices and growing EV demand, America looks further and further behind the pack.
