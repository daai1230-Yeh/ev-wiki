# Envoy and LACI launch EV car-sharing initiative in New Jersey

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-11-17

---

The partners will deploy up to 30 shared EVs in Hudson and Bergen counties. An $8.5 million (around €7.8 million) state grant funds the project to expand electric mobility in underserved communities.

Envoy and the Los Angeles Cleantech Incubator (LACI) announced the rollout on 9 November 2026. The New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) awarded the $8.5 million (around €7.8 million) grant to LACI through its eMobility Grant Program. LACI subsequently contracted Envoy to develop and operate the car-sharing service.

The companies stated in their announcement that initial activities would begin within weeks. Envoy’s dedicated project website lists 15 December 2026 as the submission deadline for interested property owners, with full vehicle launches planned for spring 2027. Envoy and LACI are currently evaluating affordable housing developments and market-rate residential properties as potential host sites, and have invited hotels, workplaces, and mobility investors across New Jersey to nominate additional locations.

Residents will book the EVs by the hour or day via the Envoy mobile app. Envoy manages vehicle maintenance, insurance, and daily fleet operations. Participating properties also receive free installation of the charging infrastructure. The companies did not disclose the specific EV models or charging infrastructure suppliers for the rollout.

“By expanding access to EVs where people live, work, and spend their time, we can help provide more convenient transportation options while supporting New Jersey’s commitment to a cleaner, more sustainable transportation future,” said Drew Hopkins, President of Envoy.

Matt Petersen, President and CEO of LACI, said the initiative brings the organisation’s City Climate Innovation Challenge to New Jersey cities to expand “reliable, shared electric mobility” and charging in frontline communities.

Robin Heston-Murphy, Chief of the Bureau of Mobile Services at DEP, emphasised the infrastructure gap: “This initiative meets people where they are, bringing light-duty urban charging infrastructure into communities that have too often been left out of the transition to cleaner mobility.”

The New Jersey rollout marks the first expansion outside California for the Envoy-LACI partnership. The Los Angeles-based non-profit and Envoy, a subsidiary of Nasdaq-listed Dot Ai, previously operated similar community car-sharing pilots in Los Angeles, where LACI stated resident usage proved sufficient to cover ongoing operational costs.
