# Delhi EV policy comes into effect: What it covers and what it does not

**Author**: Rimjhim Singh | **Date**: 2026-07-01

---

The Delhi government's new Electric Vehicle (EV) Policy came into effect on Wednesday, July 1. The government says the policy aims to speed up the shift to electric mobility, cut air pollution and encourage investment in the sector.

Chief Minister Rekha Gupta said the government expects the policy to attract nearly ₹15,000 crore in investment over the next four years. The broader target is to move Delhi towards cleaner transport and reduce vehicle-related pollution by March 31, 2030.

**What the Delhi EV policy includes**

Under the new policy, electric cars registered in Delhi and priced up to ₹30 lakh (ex-showroom) will not have to pay road tax or registration charges. Electric cars priced above ₹30 lakh will not be eligible for this benefit.

People buying electric two-wheelers will receive incentives that reduce each year: ₹10,000 per kWh (max ₹30,000) in year one, ₹6,600 per kWh (max ₹20,000) in year two, and ₹3,300 per kWh (max ₹10,000) in year three. The subsidy applies only to vehicles with an ex-factory price up to ₹2.25 lakh.

Buyers of electric auto-rickshaws will receive incentives in phases: ₹50,000 in the first year, ₹40,000 in the second year and ₹30,000 in the third year, applying to both new vehicles and replacements of existing CNG autos. For N1-category electric goods vehicles (light commercial e-trucks), incentives reach up to ₹1 lakh in year one, ₹75,000 in year two and ₹50,000 in year three.

The policy also introduces scrapping incentives: owners of BS-IV and older cars who buy an electric car worth up to ₹30 lakh after scrapping their old vehicle will receive a ₹1 lakh incentive, provided the new vehicle is purchased within six months of receiving a scrapping certificate.

EV-only registrations will be phased in: from January 1, 2027, only electric auto-rickshaws can register in Delhi; from April 1, 2028, registration of new petrol and CNG two-wheelers will be phased out in favour of electric-only.

To support the transition, Delhi Transco Limited will act as the nodal agency for planning and implementing charging infrastructure across the city. The policy proposes a single-window approval system for charging station operators, and vehicle manufacturers operating in Delhi will be required to ensure public charging facilities are available at dealerships.

Schools will have to gradually increase electric buses in their fleets, reaching a 30 per cent share by March 31, 2030. Fleet operators and delivery companies will face limits on adding new petrol and diesel vehicles in certain categories, and all newly hired or leased vehicles for official government use will be electric, except for exempt categories.

Vehicles purchased using government incentives cannot be transferred and registered in another state for the first three years, aimed at ensuring subsidies benefit Delhi users rather than quick resale outside the city.

**What the policy does not cover**

The final policy does not provide any subsidy, road tax exemption or registration relief for hybrid vehicles — a change from the draft, which had proposed a 50 per cent exemption for strong hybrids priced up to ₹30 lakh. Buyers of premium electric cars above ₹30 lakh will not receive tax benefits. The policy also does not include a dedicated framework for second-hand EV sales, battery health certification for used vehicles, or measures to support the EV resale market.
