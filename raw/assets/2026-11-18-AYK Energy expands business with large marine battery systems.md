# AYK Energy expands business with large marine battery systems

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-17

---

AYK Energy is supplying battery systems with a total capacity of 165 MWh for three new all-electric fast ferries operated by Danish shipping company Molslinjen. Each of the catamaran ferries, being built by Incat Tasmania, will receive a 55 MWh system.

The three new catamarans are currently under construction at Incat Tasmania and are set to operate on routes between Jutland and Zealand in Denmark. Each ferry will be equipped with a DNV-certified Polaris battery system from AYK, with a capacity of 55 MWh. Across all three vessels, this results in a total installed storage capacity of 165 MWh.

AYK Energy has signed the contract with system integrator Wärtsilä. Wärtsilä is supplying the integrated electric propulsion system and waterjet drives for the vessels. The new fast ferries are expected to achieve a top speed of up to 40 knots. At the same time, the battery systems are designed to be charged at high power in just 30 minutes.

The vessels are designed to accommodate up to 1,500 passengers and 500 cars. According to the parties involved, once commissioned, they will be the largest electrically powered ferries in Europe. The first of the three ferries is scheduled to arrive in Denmark at the end of 2027 and begin operations in the first quarter of 2028. The other two vessels are set to follow in 2028 and 2029, respectively.

A decisive factor in awarding the contract, according to AYK and Molslinjen, was the weight of the battery systems. When the shipping company began procurement around two years ago, AYK would have offered a battery system weighing more than 700 tonnes for the required capacity.

## AYK reduces battery weight to under 400 tonnes

By the time the first system is delivered to the shipyard in Tasmania, its weight is expected to drop to less than 400 tonnes. AYK states that it has fundamentally revised the battery design to achieve this.

The new system incorporates advanced battery cells and a lighter aluminium structure. Additionally, AYK has partially adopted manufacturing processes from the automotive industry. The goal is to store more energy per kilogram while providing higher power output. Weight plays a central role, especially in fast aluminium catamarans. Molslinjen CEO Carsten Jensen therefore cited the weight reduction as a decisive factor in selecting the supplier.

AYK’s Polaris system uses LFP cells. According to the company, the technology has been specifically adapted to meet the demands of maritime applications. This includes solutions for fire suppression and limiting potential overheating within the battery modules.

AYK has significantly expanded its marine battery business in recent years. At the end of 2025, the company received an order for a 3 MWh battery system for a new Greenpeace ship. In addition, two 12 MWh systems were supplied for Brittany Ferries’ hybrid ferries, Guillaume de Normandie and Saint-Malo. Both are among the largest marine battery systems AYK has implemented to date. With the new 55 MWh systems for Molslinjen, the manufacturer is reaching a significantly larger scale.

Furthermore, AYK has secured an order for the new German research vessel Polarstern. For Svitzer, the company also delivered a 6 MWh battery system for a methanol tugboat with battery-electric support.

AYK Energy was founded in 2018 by Chris Kruger and focuses on battery systems for maritime applications. Since 2023, the company has also operated its own production facility in China.
