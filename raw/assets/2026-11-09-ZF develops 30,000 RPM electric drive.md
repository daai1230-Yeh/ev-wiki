# ZF develops 30,000 RPM electric drive

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-06

---

ZF has developed the High Revolution Drive, a particularly compact electric drive with up to 30,000 RPM. The supplier promises reduced installation space and weight, as well as a significantly lower magnet requirement.

At the same power output, increasing rotational speed reduces the torque requirement of the electric motor, allowing a more compact, lighter machine. To still deliver torque at the wheel, ZF pairs the motor with a two-stage spur gear transmission with a ratio of approximately 18:1.

The electric machine, inverter and transmission are integrated into a 3-in-1 system with a peak power of 200 kW and peak wheel torque of 3,500 Nm. Compared with a SELECT drive of the same 200 kW and max 18,000 rpm, it needs about 25% less installation space and is about 25% lighter. ZF aims to cut magnet requirement by 50%, reducing cost and dependence on specific raw materials. Efficiency stays at the level of the comparison drive, though no figures are given.

Noise and vibration are the main challenge at high speeds; ZF co-developed the machine, gearing, bearings and tooth geometry. It calls 30,000 rpm a technical and economic "sweet spot".

The focus is the volume segment of roughly 150–200 kW, with an architecture scalable for other power levels and supporting 400 V and 800 V systems. It builds on ZF's modular SELECT platform.

The drive is in the pre-series phase. ZF is in talks with several automakers and aims for series production by 2030, with no customers named yet. This year ZF reorganised its e-mobility business, deciding in spring to keep developing and producing motors and inverters in-house, while the e-drive division is under cost pressure from lower utilisation than expected.
