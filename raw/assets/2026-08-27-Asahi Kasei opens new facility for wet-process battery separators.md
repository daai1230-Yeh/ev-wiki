# Asahi Kasei opens new facility for wet-process battery separators

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 26.08.2026

---

Japanese chemical company Asahi Kasei has opened a new coating line for Hipore wet-process battery separators in Charlotte, North Carolina, USA. The facility marks the first step in establishing a regional supply structure for wet-process plants.

The new facility has been constructed on the existing Celgard plant premises and is scheduled to begin commercial production in the second half of fiscal 2026. This investment coincides with the 40th anniversary of the Celgard site, where dry-process separators have primarily been manufactured to date.

Through this new facility and a concurrently developed separator factory in Canada, Asahi Kasei aims to significantly expand its local supply chain in North America. This expansion will enable the company to respond flexibly and directly to rising demand for components used in battery-electric vehicles and stationary energy storage systems (ESS) in the region.

"Opening the Charlotte coating line is a key milestone in establishing our North American supply platform for battery separators," said Ryu Taniguchi, President of Asahi Kasei Battery Separator Corporation. "By leveraging Celgard's experienced workforce and established operations, we are expanding our North American product portfolio to include both Celgard dry-process and Hipore wet-process technologies. Together with our planned separator facility in Canada, this investment will bring production capabilities closer to the region's battery market and position Asahi Kasei to swiftly respond as demand grows across electric vehicles and energy storage systems."

A battery separator is an extremely thin, microporous plastic membrane placed inside a lithium-ion battery cell as an essential insulating layer between the positive electrode (cathode) and the negative electrode (anode). Its primary function is to prevent physical short circuits – thus avoiding uncontrolled heating or destruction of the cell – while simultaneously allowing lithium ions to flow through its tiny pores. This ion flow, facilitated by the liquid electrolyte, is critical for power generation.
