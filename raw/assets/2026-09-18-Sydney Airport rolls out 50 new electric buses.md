# Sydney Airport rolls out 50 new electric buses

**Author**: Ciaran Daly | **Date**: 2026-09-17 15:00

---

Sydney Airport has begun rolling out a fleet of 50 electric buses as part of its plans to reach net zero Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 2030. They're set to replace diesel buses currently used to transport passengers and staff in and around the airport.

According to the airport, its new fleet comprises 25 dual-control airfield apron buses and 25 Citirider electric shuttle buses – all supplied by Australian EV manufacturer Bus & Coach International (BCI).

While exact specs remain thin, airport officials said the buses are fully powered by renewable energy through its 'power purchase' agreement. Onboard, the buses will feature digital information displays giving passengers live estimates of time-to-destination as well as wait times – with these displays also being present at bus stops around the airport. The airport also highlighted that the buses are wrapped in artwork commissioned from indigenous artist Shane Youngberry.

Karen Tompkins, Sydney Airport's group executive of government relations, sustainability and legal, said: "The new fleet meets the airport's commitment to Net Zero for Scope 1 and 2 by 2030, while celebrating the cultural significance of the lands on which it operates."

"The 50 new electric buses reduce our scope 1 emissions from our operations, with the bus being powered by 100 percent renewable through our power purchase agreement. Importantly, they also improve the passenger experience by providing quieter, cleaner and more comfortable transport across the airport precinct."

The rollout comes amid a broader push by the New South Wales government to accelerate the electrification of its wider bus fleet. In June, authorities announced a AU$6.5bn allocation within the state budget to purchase 1,700 electric buses and build 17 charging depots over ten years.
