# Vehicle to life: When the Kia EV5 becomes a camping battery

**Author**: Peter Schwierz | **Date**: 27.08.2026

---

**Facts** — Power 160 kW / 218 PS · Battery 81.4 kWh · Range 520 km WLTP · DC Charging Power 150 kW · Braked Towing Capacity 1,200 kg · V2L up to 3.6 kW · Base Price (Earth) €48,990 · Test Vehicle Price €54,250

An electric SUV, a small caravan, and a journey to the Polish Baltic coast: The Kia EV5 had to prove itself not just as a tow vehicle. Thanks to Vehicle-to-Load, its 81.4-kWh battery also became a mobile power source at the campsite. And the consumption with a trailer turned out to be a pleasant surprise.

For the front-wheel-drive Kia EV5 Earth with 19-inch wheels, the manufacturer states a WLTP range of 520 kilometres and a consumption of 17.1 kWh/100 km. The summer test vehicle in Ivory Silver Metallic, including optional equipment, costs €54,250. During a stay at a small campsite near the Polish Baltic coast, it became clear that one feature proved to be one of its greatest strengths on this trip — a feature that would typically merit only a few lines in a standard road test: vehicle-to-load.

This is less a conventional test than a travelogue: nearly three weeks of real-world use. First Berlin and Brandenburg, then a small caravan in tow, and off via the Feldberger Seenlandschaft up to Usedom and West Pomerania to near Łeba on the Polish Baltic coast.

The companion in tow is a Mink Camper 2.0 — a small teardrop caravan with a tare weight of 490 kg and a maximum permissible weight of 750 kg.

## Big in Berlin, just right in Brandenburg

The EV5 initially feels large — objectively at 4.61 m long and 1.875 m wide, and subjectively from the driver's seat. The bonnet stretches imposingly forward and manoeuvring into Berlin's tight parking spaces takes getting used to. The upright front, largely enclosed grille and striking light signature make it appear imposing.

Out in Brandenburg it's a different story. With 167 mm of ground clearance, a high seating position and comfortable suspension, narrow roads and farm tracks pose no problem. The suspension is clearly tuned for comfort — a bit soft, with noticeable body roll. It's not sporty, but it does the job.

## Now the Mink gets hitched

The front-wheel-drive model is approved to tow braked trailers up to 1,200 kg — more than enough for the Mink. Kia's FWD model delivers 160 kW (218 PS) and a maximum of 295 Nm to the front axle. Combined with the lightweight camper, that's more than sufficient.

The beauty of electric drive shows in the first kilometres: no gear shifts, no revving, no build-up. The compact camper remains stable behind the Kia and sits neatly in its slipstream. Only on dusty or sandy surfaces does the FWD model remind you that 295 Nm must go solely through the front wheels — wheel spin can occur under heavy acceleration. For regular wet grass, gravel or rough tracks, the all-wheel-drive model might be worth considering.

## 81.4 kWh for kettles and hairdryers

From the Earth trim upwards, the EV5 comes with vehicle-to-load (V2L) as standard, supplying external devices with up to 3.6 kW via a 230-volt socket in the boot and an adapter for the charging port.

At a small waterside rest area at Kamp am Peenestrom, and later for four days at a campsite near Smołdziński Las, the Mink was never parked next to a power socket — and it didn't matter. Plug in the V2L adapter, connect an extension lead to the camper, and the electric car becomes a generously sized camping power bank. Fill the kettle, charge the laptop, use the hairdryer.

The kettle in particular became a favourite appliance: preheat water electrically, then finish briefly on the gas stove. A small electric hotplate is the next planned step, with the gas stove as backup. When camping, V2L simply means you can do without a traditional grid connection.

## And what does the caravan do to the range?

Caravans and electric cars aren't natural allies — additional weight matters, but aerodynamics are the main issue. The Mink has an unfair advantage: it's low, compact, and partially disappears behind the EV5's large frontal area. Towing also encourages a more moderate travelling speed.

The big range shock never materialised. On the first well-documented long motorway leg in Poland, the onboard computer showed 24.4 kWh/100 km after 192 km. That was followed by 38 km of brisk country road at 26.0 kWh/100 km. On another 174-km leg back towards Germany, at around 22°C with a slight headwind, consumption was 24.1 kWh/100 km.

Over these 404 km, average consumption was around 24.4 kWh/100 km — remarkably low, especially since Kia itself states a WLTP motorway consumption of 23.3 kWh/100 km for the EV5 Earth with 19-inch wheels.

At 24.4 kWh/100 km, the nominal 81.4 kWh battery would theoretically allow over 330 km. Realistic stages were more in the range of 250 to 300 km — roughly the point where driver, passenger or camper could use a break anyway.

## Charging in Poland: The Kia EV5 isn't the problem

The EV5's 400-volt architecture achieves a maximum of 150 kW, with Kia stating 30 minutes for 10 to 80 per cent. In an era of 800-volt systems above 250 or even 300 kW that doesn't sound impressive, but on this trip it was surprisingly unproblematic.

The bigger issue was individual charging stations. In Płoty, the first fast charger was under maintenance and the one next to it occupied. At a shopping centre, an older station refused to cooperate via ad-hoc payment or its card terminal — in the end a German charging card via roaming did the trick. Later in Łeba, the situation was similar: individual charging points instead of large charging parks, and ad-hoc payment systems with a mind of their own.

One detail proves exceptionally clever for towing: the charging port is at the front right, behind the front wheel. With a camper in tow, you simply drive forward, charge, and reverse out — no need to unhitch the trailer. A charging socket at the rear would be far less practical.

## The Kia knows there's something hitched

As soon as the Mink is connected, the EV5 switches to trailer mode and factors the trailer into its range prediction; you can also specify a light, medium or heavy trailer. The displayed remaining range quickly adjusts to the higher consumption.

Curiously, detailed consumption values during the journey aren't easy to find. There's a detailed view in the central 12.3-inch display behind the steering wheel, and after a longer search on the central touchscreen, a bar chart sorted by days and trips.

The cockpit is an odd mix of digital and very analogue. The right side of the central touchscreen is barely reachable without leaning forward, and part of the climate control is hidden behind the steering wheel. At the same time, almost everything still has a button or lever — boot, charging flap, parking brake, gear selection.

## An assistant keeps trying to send me to bed

The attention warning system sometimes wants to send the driver for a break just after setting off, and is surprisingly persistent. The lane-keeping assist on Poland's narrow roads orients strongly towards the right-hand markings and tries to maintain distance from them, even with oncoming traffic — you end up feeling like you're steering against the car.

On the motorway the situation improves: the EV5 drives calmly, quietly and unflustered, even with a camper in tow. The EV5 is a travel car — one that prefers to glide rather than carve through corners.

## Boot space: Large – but not huge

The EV5 has five fully usable seats and an official boot capacity of 566 litres, rising to 1,650 litres with the rear seats folded. There's also a frunk. The double loading floor takes up quite a bit of height, but small items, cables and camping accessories disappear underneath.

## Conclusion: When camping, what matters is what comes out of the battery

It's not the EV5's charging performance that sticks in the mind, nor its acceleration. It's the combination of three rather unspectacular features: a large battery, a reasonable towing capacity, and vehicle-to-load.

The EV5 effortlessly tows a small camper, offers realistic travel stages of 250 to 300 km despite the trailer, and consumes around 24 to 26 kWh/100 km on the most meaningful routes with the Mink. The small, aerodynamically favourable Mink helps — with a family caravan standing two and a half metres tall, the story might look very different.

The electric drive opens up possibilities for camping that a combustion-engine SUV can't offer. The 81.4-kWh battery isn't just an energy store for driving; it can serve as a power socket for life beside the car. Vehicle-to-life instead of vehicle-to-load.
