# Tesla opens first public 1.2 MW Megacharger for Semi in California

**Author**: Carla Westerheide | **Date**: 22.07.2026

---

Tesla has opened its first full-scale public Megacharger station for the Semi in California. The new site in Bloomington features six charging stalls delivering up to 1.2 MW each and supports the latest-generation Tesla Semi with the MC2 charging connector.

As usual, Tesla's communication was brief. In a post on social media, it states "New Tesla Megacharger: Bloomington, CA (6 stalls)," followed by a link to the company's charging map. Located at 18434 Valley Boulevard in Bloomington, the site offers six Megacharger stalls, each capable of delivering up to 1.2 MW of charging power.

What is interesting is that the map no longer shows an earlier site, which was located nearby. That site had initially been regarded as Tesla's first public Megacharger site, featuring two stalls and the first-generation MC1 connector, delivering a maximum charging power of 750 kW.

But the previous site in Ontario already seems forgotten. Zach Lawson, Business Development at Tesla Charging, stated on LinkedIn that Tesla has "opened the first public Tesla Megacharger in the world in Bloomington, CA. Very soon these two incredible products, the Tesla Semi and Megacharger, will be ubiquitous."

With its higher charging capacity and expanded number of charging points, the new Bloomington site represents Tesla's first public Megacharger station built around its latest charging hardware. MC2 refers to Megacharger 2, Tesla's second-generation high-power charging interface for the production Tesla Semi, which aligns with the industry's Megawatt Charging System (MCS) standard rather than Tesla's proprietary connector.

The Tesla Semi, which recently entered series production, can recharge up to 60% of its battery in 30 minutes at a Megacharger. This allows drivers to add several hundred kilometres of range during mandatory rest breaks, making the Megacharger ideal for en-route charging or quick top-ups while cargo is being loaded or unloaded.

Tesla has announced plans to establish a significantly larger Megacharger network across North America. According to previous company plans, the network is expected to comprise at least 64 Megacharger locations across the United States, with a possible expansion into Canada at a later stage. Until now, the company has mainly operated private charging facilities serving customer fleets and its own operations in California, Texas and Nevada.
