# Hungary threatens clampdown on EV battery industry after pollution case

**Author**: Reuters (via Autonews) | **Date**: 2026-07-02

---

Hungarian Environment Minister Laszlo Gajdos has threatened to close factories in the EV battery industry that fail to abide by environmental regulations, marking a major policy shift.

From 2021, PM Orban bet big on EV batteries, attracting foreign investment worth around €26 billion ($29.7 billion), mainly from South Korean and Chinese manufacturers, making Hungary a significant hub in Europe. However, environmental, health, and safety concerns around the plants surfaced as a key issue ahead of the election, where centre-right rival Peter Magyar — who pledged a tougher stance on the sector — defeated Orban in a landslide.

The immediate trigger was Debrecen mayor Laszlo Papp (a member of Orban's Fidesz party) calling on Chinese battery parts maker Semcorp to leave the city after environmental pollution was found, following the regional government office suspending Semcorp's production licence in late June after authorities found large-scale aluminium pollution in water samples taken from monitoring wells around the plant.

Hungary will create a new top authority to monitor and sanction polluting industries, potentially launching in September and applying "extremely stringent" standards. Reforms are also planned to set pollution fines as a percentage of turnover instead of the current fixed amounts, to act as a stronger deterrent.
