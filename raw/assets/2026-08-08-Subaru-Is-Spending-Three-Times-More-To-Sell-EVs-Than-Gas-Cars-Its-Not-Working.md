# Subaru Is Spending Three Times More To Sell EVs Than Gas Cars. It's Not Working

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: Aug 7, 2026

---

Subaru is spending big money to incentivize its new EVs, but it's not really converting into more sales. The company boosted marketing expenses in the U.S. by 40% last quarter, with the lion's share going to the Solterra, Uncharted, and Trailseeker. For each Solterra sold, Subaru reportedly spent $9,650 on incentives.

Subaru now has three EVs in its U.S. lineup, triple compared to last year. But during the April-June quarter, Subaru spent three times the money to promote the Solterra, Uncharted, and Trailseeker compared to its gas car portfolio, and sales have so far failed to live up to expectations.

Specifically, the automaker spent $9,650 on incentives for each Solterra sold from April through June, according to Motor Intelligence data quoted by Automotive News. Additionally, $9,155 was spent for each Uncharted sale and $8,982 for each Trailseeker. By comparison, incentive spend for the Outback, the brand's third-best-selling model in the U.S., averaged $3,036 per unit sold.

The result is grim: the expenses contributed to a 44% drop in the company's operating profit in the first fiscal quarter ended June 30, from $472 million last year to $263.2 million this year. Meanwhile, U.S. sales of the Solterra dropped 21% in the first half of the year, while the Uncharted and Trailseeker, which went on sale recently, amassed roughly 2,500 sales each by the end of June.

Overall, Subaru sold 10,064 EVs stateside from January through June, a drop in the bucket compared to the brand's total sales of 307,340 units. The best-selling Forester crossover reached over 100,000 new customers, followed by the Crosstrek with 87,623 units sold. Subaru's overall U.S. sales dropped 4.5% in the first half of the year compared to the same period in 2025.

Spending so much money on anything is not typical for Subaru, which has traditionally been very disciplined — but the extra spending was likely necessary to keep sales relatively stable after the $7,500 federal tax credit was canceled last year. Back in May, the company said it was taking a $362 million impairment charge related to slowing EV demand, and it has delayed production of its independently developed EV in Japan, originally scheduled for its new Oizumi plant.
