# ChargePoint's New Charger Can Hit A Whopping 750 kW. That's Not Even Its Best Trick.

**Author**: Andrei Nedelea | **Date**: 2026-10-27

---

The Express Solo promises cheaper installation and can shift power between plugged-in cars to make better use of what's available.

- Two paired Express Solo stations can deliver up to 750 kW to one port, sharing power across connected EVs.
- The all-in-one design simplifies installation and lets operators add plugs without another complete charger.
- Solar and battery storage integration could lower operating costs and reduce reliance on expensive grid power.

ChargePoint announced on Tuesday that its latest EV charger can send 750 kW to one plug. Developed with Eaton, the Express Solo combines the charger and power conversion equipment in a single enclosure, eliminating the large separate power cabinet and reducing wiring and groundwork. ChargePoint calls it the most powerful standalone charger in the U.S.

It comes in 200-, 400- and 600-kW configurations delivering up to 800 amps. Reaching 750 kW at one port requires at least two connected stations, which share power across up to four vehicles, so not all four get 750 kW at once. A vehicle that can accept 750 kW would be something like an electric big rig such as the Tesla Semi, not a passenger car. The system distributes power so that if one car accepts only a modest rate, more output goes to another that can use it.

ChargePoint and Eaton's earlier microgrid announcement described chargers drawing from on-site batteries and solar, reducing conversion losses and reliance on expensive grid power.

Driver-facing features include an Omni Port system of built-in adapters offering both CCS and NACS, cables on moving arms to reach any plug location, cut-resistant cables, and an alarm that uses the station's speakers, screen and lights to deter vandalism. A more powerful charger won't shorten a stop if the EV can't take the power, but shifting power between plugs could be a big convenience upgrade.
