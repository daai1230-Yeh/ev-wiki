# Volvo begins deliveries of first EX60 models

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-07-16

---

Volvo Cars has begun deliveries of the new Volvo EX60 to European customers. The electric mid-size SUV has been produced at the Torslanda plant since April and is available across Europe, and has recently become available for order in the USA. Volvo plans to significantly increase deliveries in the second half of the year.

"This is a huge moment for us and our customers," said Erik Severinson, Chief Commercial Officer at Volvo Cars. The EX60 is the first fully electric car to be designed, developed and manufactured in Sweden. Its predecessor, the XC60 (still in production as a hybrid), remains among Sweden's most important export products, and Volvo expects the EX60 to provide an economic boost to Gothenburg and Sweden.

The EX60 is the first group model based on Volvo's SPA3 electric platform with an 800-volt architecture. At launch it comes in three variants: an entry model with a 275 kW RWD powertrain and 80 kWh battery; an AWD version with 375 kW and a 91 kWh net battery; and the P12 AWD, which Volvo calls "the most powerful production Volvo ever," delivering 500 kW with a 112 kWh battery and up to 810 km range. With a peak charging capacity of 370 kW, the AWD variants charge 10–80% in 18–19 minutes, positioning the EX60 against the new BMW iX3, Mercedes-Benz EQC and Chinese 800-volt SUVs.

Volvo invested around 10 billion Swedish kronor (~€930 million) in the Torslanda plant, adding a modernised paint shop, upgraded final assembly, megacasting and on-site battery assembly.

**Specifications** — P6: RWD, 275 kW, 480 Nm, 5.9 s, 620 km WLTP, 83/80 kWh, 320 kW DC, 18 min, €62,990. P10 AWD: 375 kW, 710 Nm, 4.6 s, 660 km, 95/91 kWh, 370 kW, 18 min, €65,990. P12 AWD: 500 kW, 790 Nm, 3.9 s, 810 km, 117/112 kWh, 370 kW, 19 min, €71,990. Top speed 180 km/h across the range.

Volvo has also opened order books in North America, where the EX60 starts at $58,400 before delivery charges. It is the first Volvo in the region with a North American Charging Standard (NACS) connector as standard, giving drivers direct access to Tesla's Supercharger network without an adapter.
