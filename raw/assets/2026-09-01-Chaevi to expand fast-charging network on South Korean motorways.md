# Chaevi to expand fast-charging network on South Korean motorways

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-08-31

---

South Korean charging infrastructure provider Chaevi is set to install 131 fast chargers at 29 motorway service areas. Most of the new charging points will be equipped with NACS connectors, allowing Tesla drivers in particular to charge without a CCS1 adapter.

Chaevi has been selected as the preferred bidder for an expansion project by the state-owned Korea Expressway Corporation. According to the Korea Times, 131 new chargers are to be installed at 29 service areas along major motorway corridors as part of the 2026 programme. The plans include 118 200 kW fast chargers and 13 so-called multichargers.

According to Chaevi, 82 of the new chargers are to feature the NACS standard established in North America. Including Chaevi's expansion projects from 2025 and 2026, the new installations will bring the total number of NACS chargers installed at the company's South Korean motorway service areas to 167. The company says this represents around 62 per cent of the chargers installed as part of the two projects.

The 200 kW chargers equipped with NACS will provide currents of up to 250 amps. According to Chaevi, this allows Tesla vehicles to make better use of their supported charging capacity. The company also offers Plug&Charge, allowing vehicle identification, charging initiation, and payment to be handled automatically once the vehicle is connected.

Chaevi also points to high utilisation of its existing infrastructure. At 109 charging bays across 23 sites from the 2025 expansion programme, an average of around 2.5 times as many charging sessions per day were recorded per bay in July as at conventional charging stations. However, the company has not disclosed absolute utilisation figures or further details about the comparison group.

Chaevi operates around 6,000 of its own fast-charging points in South Korea. Including infrastructure it manages on behalf of other companies and institutions, the provider has around 10,000 fast-charging points. Chaevi is vertically integrated, developing and manufacturing charging stations as well as installing and operating them.

The expansion comes at an important time for the company. Chaevi listed on the KOSDAQ technology exchange in Seoul at the end of April, raising around €64 million. However, demand from institutional investors was weaker than initially hoped, partly due to concerns about growth prospects and profitability in the charging business. Chaevi had announced plans to achieve positive EBITDA in the fourth quarter of 2026 and an operating profit in 2027. Winning the contract for additional motorway sites could therefore also be important for scaling and improving utilisation of its charging network.
