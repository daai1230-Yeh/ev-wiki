# Why America Built Fewer EV Fast-Charging Stations In Q2

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-07-14

---

The U.S. added thousands of new public fast-charging ports in the second quarter, but the rate of growth slowed. The all-time market share of Tesla Superchargers fell below 50% for the first time, while utilization rates remained steady as drivers absorb newly added capacity.

The buildout of America's public fast-charging stations slowed in Q2 as charging networks shifted focus toward profitability and reliability, charging data platform Paren said. Companies added 4,382 new ports across 806 new stations in Q2, down from 4,865 ports and 891 stations in the same period last year — a 10% year-over-year decline, and well short of Q4's record of 5,966 new ports and 937 new stations.

Loren McDonald, chief analyst at Chargeonomics, attributed the slowdown to operators prioritizing profits and charging-experience quality over sheer expansion. "A two-quarter year-over-year decline is not definitive proof of a slowdown," he said, "[but] combined with recent CPO layoffs and pullbacks, it reinforces the industry's new mantra: operations, customer experience, and profitability."

Charging companies are adding fewer stations with more high-powered ports each, and emphasizing amenities — restrooms, cafes, WiFi, and in Ionna's case, air-conditioned lounges. Quarter-over-quarter growth remained strong: just 3,521 new ports went in during Q1, meaning Q2 installs rose 24% quarter-over-quarter.

Tesla still led the buildout but its all-time market share dropped below 50% for the first time, as rival networks picked up pace — Tesla accounted for just 27% of new deployments in Q2 (1,185 new ports), followed by Walmart (368), ChargePoint (333), Red E (315) and Electrify America (202).

Average utilization held steady at 15.8%, suggesting new capacity is being absorbed at roughly the same rate stations are built. Growth was heavily regional: 40% of new ports landed in just five states, led by California (120 new stations), then Texas, Florida, Illinois, and New York. North Dakota added zero new public fast-charging ports in Q2; Montana, Wyoming, and South Dakota each added just one station.

Even as U.S. EV sales have swung with shifting policy, the charging industry has kept building at a strong clip, betting demand will rebound — a bet that looks reasonable given second-quarter EV sales grew at their fastest pace since the federal tax credit expired, aided by rising gas prices tied to the Iran conflict.
