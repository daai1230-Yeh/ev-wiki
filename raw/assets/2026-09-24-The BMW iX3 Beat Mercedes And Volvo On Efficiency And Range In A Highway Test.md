# The BMW iX3 Beat Mercedes And Volvo On Efficiency And Range In A Highway Test

**Author**: Andrei Nedelea | **Date**: 2026-09-23

---

*The iX3 used less energy than the GLC and EX60 on the same route, blending lower consumption with a bigger battery.*

- The BMW iX3 used less energy than the Mercedes GLC Electric and Volvo EX60 in the same highway test.
- Better efficiency and a larger battery gave the BMW a significant calculated range advantage.
- The Volvo was the car the drivers liked most, showing that range alone doesn't decide the best highway companion.

A bigger battery increases range, but the real trick is using energy more efficiently. In this highway range comparison against the new Mercedes-Benz GLC Electric and the Volvo EX60, the BMW iX3 combined both.

These are three of the most important new arrivals in the premium electric SUV market, and their specs make for a close contest on paper. On the same highway on the same day, though, the BMW distances itself with superior economy.

Kris Rifa and friends drove the cars on a motorway route north of Oslo, with a target speed of 120 km/h (around 75 mph). Temperatures ranged from 14°C to 17°C, with mostly dry conditions.

Each car started at 90% charge and ran in its most efficient driving mode. The drivers rotated cars and the lead position, and returning along the same route helped reduce elevation and wind effects. A larger Xpeng G9 also joined the test, though it's one size class above the others.

The iX3 finished with the lowest consumption of 19.4 kWh/100 km (around 3.2 miles/kWh). The GLC returned 20.3 kWh/100 km (3.06 miles/kWh), while the EX60 used 21.1 kWh/100 km (2.94 miles/kWh). The BMW consumed roughly 4% less energy than the Mercedes and 8% less than the Volvo.

With the biggest battery in the test, 108.7 kWh usable, the iX3 had a considerable range advantage: 338 miles (544 km) versus 279 miles (449 km) for the GLC and 260 miles (419 km) for the Volvo – after subtracting 3% for heat and discharging losses.

The Volvo EX60 P10 in the comparison wasn't the top version. The P12 has a 112 kWh battery versus the P10's 91 kWh pack, and Volvo rates it as marginally more efficient (by 0.1 kWh/100 km). Using the same efficiency, a P12 would have achieved a theoretical range of 320 miles (515 km) – still 18 miles (29 km) less than the iX3.

One aspect that likely affected the Volvo's efficiency was its larger wheels: it was the only vehicle on 22-inch wheels, while the BMW and Mercedes had 20-inch wheels.

The Volvo also topped the drivers' subjective highway comfort rankings, with praise for its quiet cabin and smooth ride. The Mercedes' seats, steering, and smoothness were also liked. The iX3 has shown impressive efficiency elsewhere too; BMW took it 626 miles on a single charge in a hypermiling experiment, with 3% remaining.
