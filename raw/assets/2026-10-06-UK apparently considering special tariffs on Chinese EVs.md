# UK apparently considering special tariffs on Chinese EVs

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-10-05

---

Just days after a report that the EU is allegedly pushing for higher tariffs on Chinese electric cars, the British government is now apparently drawing up a corresponding regulation. This is presumably the only way the UK can be eligible for the EU's upcoming "Made in Europe" initiative.

The UK is reportedly considering additional tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles, which could align it with EU rules imposing anti-subsidy tariffs of between 7.8 and 35.3 per cent on battery-electric cars from China (in force since end of October 2024, on top of a regular 10 per cent import duty).

According to The Times, Economic Secretary Jonathan Reynolds is developing a package of potential tariffs on Chinese vehicle imports, amid concern that Chinese manufacturers are bringing subsidised vehicles to the UK at dumping prices (per Reuters). Chinese manufacturers currently hold a 16 per cent share of UK new registrations across all powertrain types.

No new tariffs have been imposed yet. A government spokesperson told Reuters it continues to engage closely with industry so its approach reflects the sector's and UK's national interests.

The background is likely EU political pressure: the EU is working on its Industrial Accelerator Act, which places a stronger focus on European-made products in funding, public procurement and other industrial policy, with local content and "Made in Europe" designations. This matters for UK automakers: SMMT published an Oxford Economics analysis showing UK car production supports €24 billion in economic activity, 250,000 jobs and €1.6 billion in tax revenues in the EU annually. UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said in September he would work to ensure the UK is treated as a "trusted partner" under the planned regulations. The Financial Times reported that the EU is pressuring the UK to raise tariffs on Chinese EVs so it can become a partner under the Made in Europe criteria.
