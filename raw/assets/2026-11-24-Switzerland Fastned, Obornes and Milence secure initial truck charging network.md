# Switzerland: Fastned, Obornes and Milence secure initial truck charging network

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 24.11.2026 - 15:30

---

The Swiss Federal Roads Office (FEDRO) has awarded the 32 locations tendered in January 2026 for the construction of a total of 82 truck charging stations along national roads to three companies. Lot 1 goes to Fastned, Lot 2 to Obornes, and Lot 3 to Milence.

Almost a year ago, ASTRA published a tender divided into three lots – aimed at establishing Switzerland’s initial truck charging network. According to the authorities, eleven applications were submitted within four months, more than in previous tenders, as ASTRA noted. The initiative is part of Switzerland’s Electromobility Roadmap 2030. In the past, fast-charging parks for electric passenger cars along national roads have already been tendered multiple times.

Describing the finalised selection process, ASTRA said: “The contracts for the three lots were awarded to the companies whose submissions were rated highest, particularly in terms of construction quality and energy efficiency.” Lot 1 was awarded to Fastned Switzerland AG, based in Zug. Lot 2 was won by Obornes SA, based in Aix-en-Provence (France), and Lot 3 was secured by Milence, based in Amsterdam (Netherlands).

The three selected companies now have two years to plan their projects and apply for building permits. They are responsible for financing, constructing and operating the truck fast chargers themselves. ASTRA, for its part, ensures the power connections and covers the pre-financing of the associated costs. “These costs will subsequently be reimbursed by the operating companies in the form of an annual compensation,” the Federal Office explained. It expects the first truck fast-charging stations from this project to enter operation in 2030, with all 82 stations along the national roads likely to be available by 2035.

The dedicated truck fast-charging network is to be built at rest areas and heavy traffic control centres along the aforementioned national roads. Lots 1 and 3 each comprise eleven fixed locations, while Lot 2 covers ten locations. ASTRA has already determined the number of required truck charging points for all 32 sites. The equipment varies significantly: for example, the number ranges from just two CCS charging points (e.g., in Campagnola) to two MCS charging points and ten CCS charging points at the SVKZ Rothenburg. In total, seven of the 32 sites will also have at least one MCS charging point. For the CCS charging points, a minimum power of 350 kW must be maintained, with Fastned already stating its intention to provide at least 400 kW per charging point.

The project is reminiscent of Germany’s initial truck charging network, which is being driven forward by the Federal Ministry of Transport and Autobahn GmbH. This aims to create 350 truck charging parks along Germany’s motorways. The second tender round has just begun (DE).

In Switzerland, the expansion is even more urgent, as the country already has a relatively high number of electric trucks: the small Alpine nation is a pioneer in Europe when it comes to electrifying heavy-duty transport. According to a recent analysis by auto-schweiz, 847 heavy commercial vehicles over 3.5 tonnes with purely electric propulsion were newly registered between January and September. This corresponds to a market share of 27.4 per cent. Focusing solely on heavy trucks, Switzerland also has a disproportionately high share of electric vehicles in this segment.

For Fastned, the award represents a stronger commitment to the truck sector. So far, the Dutch company has primarily invested in charging parks for passenger cars. However, since 2025, it has been implementing and operating special charging areas for trucks at several locations in Belgium and the Netherlands, according to its own statements. “We are very pleased to have been awarded Lot 1,” said Christine Zingg-Graf, Country Director Switzerland at Fastned. “Drawing on our experience in developing and operating fast-charging stations for electric trucks in other European markets, we can build on a proven concept. Switzerland is already demonstrating the potential of heavy-duty transport electrification and is considered a European pioneer in this field. At the same time, it is clear that charging infrastructure needs to keep pace with market developments.”

The second lot winner, Obornes, is a subsidiary of the Ortec Group, which, under the Oreve brand, builds and operates public high-power charging stations. To date, the company has been primarily active in France and follows a business model in which Obornes constructs turnkey charging stations on land owned by property owners or fleet operators and subsequently takes over their operation and maintenance.

The third lot winner is Milence, a well-known name. The joint venture between Daimler Truck, Traton and the Volvo Group is independently building a European fast-charging network for trucks, which now includes 42 charging parks across nine European markets. Milence has not yet been active in Switzerland, but this is set to change following the award. Milence CEO Anja van Niersen comments: “Switzerland is a central hub for European freight transport. With these new locations, we are strengthening our charging network along key international transport corridors and making it easier for battery-electric trucks to operate across borders. To unlock the full potential of electric heavy-duty transport, Europe must significantly accelerate the rollout of charging infrastructure. The award by FEDRO demonstrates how public and private stakeholders can work together to drive the transition towards zero-emission road freight transport.”
