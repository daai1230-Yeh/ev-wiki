# "Battery-electric trucks should be used where possible" – Niklas Andersson of Volvo Trucks

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-07-16

---

Volvo Trucks is entering a new era of electric trucking. Weeks after unveiling the FH Aero Electric in Gothenburg — promising up to 700 km of range — electrive interviewed Niklas Andersson, Director Electric Solutions at Volvo Trucks, about what customers can expect from the new generation of battery-electric trucks.

Volvo Trucks aims to be the first manufacturer to let a battery-electric truck complete a full day of long-haul operation without recharging, equipping its upcoming electric flagship with roughly 100 kWh more usable battery capacity than most competitors — a choice that involves technical trade-offs. Order books for the second-generation battery-electric trucks officially opened on June 15. Series production starts in early September on flexible lines alongside combustion models in Ghent, Belgium, and Tuve, Sweden, while Volvo phases out its first-generation FH Electric, FM Electric, and FMX Electric.

Asked how Volvo's competitiveness is evolving, Andersson said the 700 km range "leads the industry" and builds on the proven flexibility of Volvo's vehicle variants. Range remains the central concern for fleets considering the switch from diesel, he said: "It's the first thing that comes to mind."

The key lesson from the first generation, according to Andersson, is that electric trucks can handle far more transport tasks than operators initially expect — customers who buy an early unit, test it across applications, and put new drivers behind the wheel tend to progress fastest and expand their electric fleets quickest. Testing for the second generation happened mostly internally, using Volvo's own proving grounds (winter testing in northern Sweden, summer testing in Spain) plus field trials where select operators ran pre-series vehicles under real conditions.

Ahead of launch, Volvo is running journalist demo events, training its sales network, and delivering vehicles to national sales companies so customers can test-drive the new trucks directly.

On strategy, Andersson noted Volvo Trucks operates in 130 countries and will keep developing combustion, fuel-cell, and battery-electric drivetrains to serve different markets — the right technology depends on local electricity/hydrogen/HVO availability and regulation. But he added: "wherever it is possible to operate an electric vehicle, we should do so," since across the full energy chain a battery-electric truck has the lowest energy losses and is the most efficient use of available energy.

He emphasized cross-industry collaboration — quoting Volvo's CEO: "Partnership is the new leadership" — arguing that manufacturers, transport companies, their customers, and public authorities all need to work together to drive the transition. Asked when battery-electric trucks will start contributing to a company's profitability, Andersson's answer was simply: "They already are."
