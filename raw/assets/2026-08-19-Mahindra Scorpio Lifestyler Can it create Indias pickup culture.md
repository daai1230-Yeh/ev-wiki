# Mahindra Scorpio Lifestyler: Can it create India's pickup culture?

**Author**: Sarthak Bhadane | **Date**: 17 August 2026

---

I feel the Scorpio Lifestyler could just ignite the lifestyle pickup culture in India. While the Hilux has always been aspirational, it is expensive (before discounts).

After showcasing the concept in 2023, Mahindra finally unveiled its new pickup truck, the Scorpio Lifestyler. A global pickup, it will be seen in multiple international markets as well. But the biggest highlight is that the pickup truck will launch in India in April 2027. Not only that, Mahindra even gave a price teaser saying that it will start from under Rs. 19.79 lakhs.

Currently, the small pickup space is ruled by the Toyota Hilux. Isuzu V-Cross numbers are less in comparison, and dealerships don't even have a test drive vehicle.

Forum member "Dippy" had the chance to check out the Scorpio Lifestyler at the unveil event, and noted it has a lot of promise: "Mahindra is also sure to load it with tech and features. With the current price teaser, I expect the fully loaded 4x4 variant to touch approx. the Rs.28-30 lakh mark on-road." The Thar did something similar back in 2020 — no one, not even Mahindra, expected a 2-door SWB 4x4 SUV to sell in big numbers, but the Thar has since become hugely successful.

Another member, "Asoon", argued that if the pickup segment is going to succeed in India, only Mahindra's Lifestyler can make it happen: "It has the right mix of road presence, a powerful engine, scorpio legacy and the backing of Mahindra's marketing team, which has done wonders over the last five years across product categories (Thar, XUV700, the BE twins, and Scorpio-N). If the legendary Hilux failed to create an impact, then it's not a product problem; it's a positioning and marketing / category creation problem."

A third member, "GTO", was more skeptical: "The Lifestyler Pickup will be popular, but by pickup standards. It won't fly anywhere close to SUVs like the Thar Roxx, Scorpio-N & gang. The pickup is more or less useless in India and 99% of owners will be carrying around the cargo bed without using it once in their entire lives... We'll see an initial spurt in sales numbers due to the excitement, but it will fall down soon thereafter."
