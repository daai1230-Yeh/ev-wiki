# Toyota launches new generation Sora fuel cell bus, based on Isuzu bus platform

**Author**: Editorial Staff | **Date**: 2026-10-07

---

Toyota has launched the new-generation Sora fuel cell route bus, offering a stated range of more than 300 km and using a platform jointly developed with Isuzu.

The new Sora combines Isuzu's battery-electric full-flat route bus platform with Toyota's fuel cell system. Toyota announced the launch on 5 October 2026, eight years after introducing the first-generation Sora in 2018.

The new Sora follows the fuel cell bus cooperation between Toyota and Isuzu announced in September 2025, when the companies said production of their jointly developed model would start in FY2026 at J-Bus's Utsunomiya plant. The cooperation followed an earlier initiative involving Isuzu, Hino and Toyota announced in 2022.

## 113 kW commercial-vehicle fuel cell stack

The model combines a revised fuel cell system with a large-capacity lithium-ion battery. Total hydrogen tank capacity has been reduced by approximately 20% versus the first-generation Sora, while cruising range has increased by around 100 km to more than 300 km (a reference value that varies with temperature, traffic, passengers and air-conditioning use).

It uses a Toyota PEM fuel cell stack for commercial vehicles with a maximum output of 113 kW. Propulsion is by two AC induction motors, each 110 kW and 485 Nm, for 220 kW and 970 Nm combined.

Hydrogen is stored in three 70 MPa tanks with a combined volume of 471 litres. The fuel cell system is installed on the roof to ease maintenance. The urban version measures 10,545 mm x 2,485 mm x 3,440 mm and carries 70 people (19 seated, 50 standing, 1 crew).

## In brief

- Range: more than 300 km, about 100 km more than the first-generation Sora.
- Refuelling: about 10 minutes; the new high-speed filling standard could halve that at compatible stations.
- Toyota says more than 3,500 fuel cell systems had been supplied to over 100 customers worldwide by end of September 2026.

## Layout, options and safety

Full-flat step-free floor; four variants (two urban, two suburban). An optional one-touch wheelchair securement system is planned, orderable from around April 2027. The bus can act as an emergency power source via an external power supply unit.

It has an Emergency Driver Stop System (EDSS) linked to a Driver Status Monitor, which decelerates and stops the bus and applies the parking brake if the driver has a problem, plus a front blind-spot monitor for pedestrians and bicycles.
