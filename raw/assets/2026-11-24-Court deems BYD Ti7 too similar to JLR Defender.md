# Court deems BYD Ti7 too similar to JLR Defender

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-11-23

---

A Dutch court has ordered ten BYD subsidiaries in interim proceedings to refrain from building and selling the Ti7 in the EU. According to the judges, the model looks too similar to the JLR Defender. However, the main trial is still pending.

The BYD Ti7 may not be sold in the EU for some time. According to a court in The Hague, the design of the large SUV infringes on the intellectual property of manufacturer JLR (Jaguar Land Rover). As reported by a number of outlets, the judges ruled in an interim injunction on 20 November that, based on a preliminary assessment, the vehicle bears too close a resemblance to JLR’s Defender. This decision was made despite BYD presenting a long list of differences in its defence.

The BYD Ti7 has been available in China under the Fang Cheng Bao brand since September 2025 and was recently among the top 10 best-selling cars in the country. Its angular body, upright front, and square wheel arches do indeed evoke the Defender. The seven-seater SUV is set to premiere in British showrooms this month, with first deliveries to customers scheduled for January 2027. Since the UK is not part of the EU, these plans are not affected by the new order (although JLR intends to take legal action there as well). However, the subsequent market launch in the European Union is now in jeopardy.

The court in The Hague has specifically prohibited BYD from importing, manufacturing, offering, selling, storing, or delivering the Ti7 in the EU. The injunction applies to ten European BYD entities, including the two German GmbHs, as well as BYD Auto Hungary – the subsidiary intended to operate the planned plant in Szeged, Hungary. It is likely that the Ti7 for the EU market was also planned to be produced there.

One thing is clear: BYD intends to fight the decision. The main proceedings are still pending, and JLR must initiate them within six months; otherwise, BYD can seek to have the interim injunction expire under Dutch law. According to media reports, BYD has stated that it “strongly disagrees” with the ruling. The company maintains that the Ti7 is the result of its own design work and that it will pursue all legal avenues, including an appeal.

The dispute over design rights between JLR and BYD has already spilled over into side battles. In August, bailiffs seized digital documents from two BYD offices in the Netherlands. However, this action was later deemed disproportionate, and the destruction of the data was ordered. The background was a suspicion that BYD might have dismantled a Defender and examined individual technologies through reverse engineering to use them in the development of the BYD Ti7.
