# Mercedes-Benz reportedly halts CLA L production in China

**Author**: Carla Westerheide | **Date**: 21.07.2026

---

Mercedes-Benz has reportedly paused production of the electric CLA L at the Beijing Benz Automotive Co., Ltd. (BBAC) joint venture in China. However, the carmaker has not confirmed the reports.

The development was first reported by the German website mb passion, citing industry sources. Mercedes-Benz has not officially confirmed the production halt or provided a reason for the reported decision.

The CLA L was developed specifically for the Chinese market, where long-wheelbase passenger cars are particularly popular. However, demand appears to have fallen well short of expectations. According to the report, disappointing sales of the long-wheelbase electric sedan are considered the most likely cause. Industry sources cited by mb passion indicate that only 627 units of the CLA L were sold during the first half of 2026. Registration figures also suggest limited market traction throughout the year.

The model recorded 35 new registrations in January and 21 in February. Registrations temporarily increased to 358 units in March before falling back to 52 in April. A further 161 vehicles were registered in May, while no new registrations were recorded in June.

The CLA L is the long-wheelbase version of Mercedes-Benz's new electric CLA and is produced by BBAC, the manufacturer's joint venture with Beijing Automotive Group (BAIC). The model was developed exclusively for the Chinese market, and unveiled to the Chinese public at an event in Beijing in November 2025.

The model is four centimetres longer than its European counterpart, bringing its overall length to 4.76 metres. It features an 89 kWh battery that delivers a CLTC range of up to 866 kilometres, placing it among the longest-range electric vehicles on the Chinese market. A key factor behind this figure is its efficient drivetrain: the 200 kW (272 hp) rear-mounted motor is said to consume just 10.9 kWh per 100 kilometres.

Thanks to its 800-volt architecture and a peak charging capacity of 325 kW, the Chinese CLA also delivers strong charging performance. Under ideal conditions, it can recover enough energy for up to 370 kilometres of range in just ten minutes.

If confirmed, the production pause would underline the challenges facing premium international manufacturers in China's fast-changing EV market, where domestic brands continue to intensify competition across both volume and premium segments.
