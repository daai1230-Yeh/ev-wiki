# Volkswagen strengthens ties with Gotion with three joint ventures

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-09-28

---

Volkswagen is moving closer to battery partner Gotion High-tech in Europe: the battery cell factory under construction by VW subsidiary PowerCo in Sagunto, Spain, will become a joint venture project. In return, Gotion opens the door for Volkswagen on battery projects in Slovakia and Morocco. The deal still depends on the relevant authorities.

Gotion's stock exchange announcement outlines three joint ventures with a combined €3.22 billion investment: €2.26 billion for Sagunto and about €480 million each for Slovakia and Morocco. The total is shared almost equally (Gotion ~€1.6 billion; PowerCo ~€1.62 billion), financed in stages with no timeline given.

## Ownership

- Sagunto (Valencia): Volkswagen/PowerCo keeps control, 51:49.
- Slovakia (Šurany, via Gotion InoBat Batteries) and Morocco (LFP cathode materials plant): reversed, 51% Gotion / 49% Volkswagen.

## Capacity

- Spain and Slovakia cell plants: combined 37.5 GWh a year, primarily for Volkswagen. Gotion now cites 29.1 GWh for Spain (PowerCo previously cited 40 GWh) and 8.4 GWh for Šurany (2025 announcement: 20 GWh initially, 40 GWh long term) — the discrepancies remain unexplained.
- Morocco: 100,000 tonnes of LFP cathode material a year, mainly for the two new cell JVs. The African Development Bank granted a €100 million loan for the plant in July.
- Construction period for each project expected to be no longer than five years.

## Background

Volkswagen holds about 25% of Gotion, its largest shareholder, a top-five global cell maker. Gotion supplies unified cells for Spanish-built models such as the ID. Polo and Cupra Raval from Hefei, China, until Sagunto is operational. Sagunto is one of three PowerCo sites, alongside Salzgitter and St. Thomas in Canada (earliest opening now expected 2029). Gotion continues its separate Valladolid plans (cathode and battery recycling plant).

Gotion's board approved the proposal on 28 September, but Chinese media report the investment agreement has not yet been signed; shareholder and regulatory approvals are still required. With Volkswagen in crisis and seeking to rein in PowerCo's investments, the partnership is expected to bring financing advantages over the medium to long term.
