# Estonia plans 5 MW charger for new battery-electric ferry

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 27.08.2026

---

High-performance charging infrastructure is to be installed for electric ferries operating on one of Estonia's most important ferry routes. PSW Power & Automation is supplying its Megawatt Charging System with up to five MW of charging power. A new electric ferry is set to serve the route starting at the end of 2028.

The Norwegian company PSW Power & Automation has won the contract to equip the ferry route between Virtsu and Kuivastu with a Megawatt Charging System (MCS). This route connects the Estonian mainland with the island of Muhu and serves as the primary link to the island of Saaremaa. Two charging points will be installed on each side of the crossing, each offering up to five megawatts.

Technically, PSW is using a 1,500-volt architecture, which enables high charging capacities and efficiency while providing headroom for future electric vessel requirements. The company is leveraging its experience from several MCS projects in Norway; according to PSW it has already installed over 250 MW of shore power capacity and more than 100 MWh of integrated battery storage in the Nordic region.

Jens Hjorteset, Director of Business Development at PSW Power & Automation, said the contract shows growing international demand for reliable, scalable and future-ready charging infrastructure in the maritime industry, and that the company is proud that technology developed in Norway is now contributing to the decarbonisation of ports and vessels beyond Scandinavia.

For the Virtsu–Kuivastu route, the Estonian state is currently building a new ferry, scheduled for completion by the end of 2028. It will accommodate up to 380 passengers and 110 cars or eight lorries, making it smaller than the ferries Piret and Toll currently in service. The contract for the ferry is worth 50 million euros, around eight million euros above the originally planned budget. The new ferry will have a battery of around 3 MWh capacity.

By combining a battery-electric ferry with megawatt charging infrastructure, Estonia is adopting a concept ideally suited to short, regularly served ferry routes. High charging capacities allow the ferry's battery to recharge during port stops. The infrastructure will not be exclusively reserved for the new ferry; according to PSW, the MCS system can also be used by other suitably equipped vessels.

Battery technology for electric vessels continues to advance. Xing Mobility recently announced two battery systems specifically designed for maritime applications, one of which is expected to achieve double the energy density of conventional ship batteries thanks to a cell-to-pack architecture, while also enabling continuous discharge at 3C.
