# My First Walmart EV Charging Experience Was A Disaster

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-09-03

---

There's perhaps no American retailer better positioned to make electric vehicle charging more ubiquitous than Walmart. It has thousands of stores, enormous parking lots, and millions of customers who already shop there. Now it's taking full advantage, rapidly filling parking lots with fast chargers, even as EV sales are sluggish.

The strategy is gathering serious momentum. Walmart added 368 public fast-charging ports to its stores in the second quarter, more than any network in the U.S. apart from Tesla. Recently, it opened up its 100th owned-and-operated fast-charging station, expanding its footprint to 20 states.

So the author went to one in New Jersey to see what it's actually like to plug in with one of the charging industry's hottest up-and-coming players. The plan was simple: drive a Toyota bZ Woodland press car to Walmart, run the usual 10-80% charging test, hit the grocery store, and grab a snack for the drive back to Brooklyn.

What actually happened: 40 minutes at the Walmart charging station, no electrons added to the press car, and no groceries bought because the poor charging experience killed the mood.

The charging station, at a Supercenter in Freehold Township, NJ, had four Alpitronic HYC400 units painted Walmart blue, delivering up to 400 kW across eight stalls split between NACS and CCS. The bZ Woodland has a Tesla-style NACS port, so no adapter was needed — but the Walmart smartphone app proved to be the real obstacle, since most Walmart stations don't yet have credit card readers.

The app's QR-code scanner initially failed to detect the code on the charger. After several tries, the app moved to the payment screen but then threw a "something went wrong" message. Repeated attempts, reinstalling the app, and even a call to Walmart customer service didn't fix it — cycling through "we're having technical issues" errors for roughly 40 minutes and five charging attempts, all in the summer sun.

A backup plan saved the day: an Ionna Rechargery station four miles away at a nearby Wawa, using the exact same Alpitronic HYC400 hardware. There, simply tapping a phone on the card reader started charging in under a minute. The Ionna station was busy; the Walmart station sat empty the whole time.

Walmart told the author it didn't share specifics on what went wrong but that more payment options were on the way: "We want charging at Walmart to be simple and reliable... Customers may currently pay for charging through the Walmart app, and we continue to explore additional payment options."

Walmart is planning to add swipe- and dip-to-pay terminals to its charging stations, which should offer an alternative when the app fails, though the rollout timeline is unclear. PlugShare check-ins show many drivers have charged successfully at this same station, but others have reported similar failed sessions and a confusing app. The author's takeaway: until card readers show up, better to plug in elsewhere than risk it again.
