# The bus industry's smartphone moment

**Author**: Editorial Staff | **Date**: 2026-10-01

*(Sponsored content by Autonomous Systems. Subtitle: "The next city bus is a smartbus. European manufacturers are best placed to define it.")*

---

**Premise**: Europe invented the bus (the first motor bus line opened on 18 March 1895 between Siegen, Netphen and Deuz) by starting where demands were smallest and learning step by step. The piece argues Europe needs that habit again, with the "smartbus" (a self-driving bus) as the next category.

**Market data**: in H1 2026 Europe registered 8,141 battery-electric buses (+49% year on year, almost 69% of the full 2025 total); BEVs are 61.2% of the European city bus market (101 units in all of 2015). Iveco is second; Solaris registered more e-buses in H1 2026 than in all of 2025 and leads European hydrogen. Yutong is the largest e-bus supplier to Europe for the second year (14.0% in 2024, 15.5% in 2025, roughly one in five in H1 2026). The EU-manufacturer share of electric city bus registrations in continental Europe fell from 80% (2018) to 64% (2025), while the Chinese share rose from 20% to 31%.

**Nokia analogy**: Nokia held about 40% of the phone market and Europe wrote GSM, yet when the iPhone (2007) changed the category, volume and cost leadership didn't help; Nokia sold its handset business to Microsoft in 2013 for €5.44 billion, about 2% of its peak value. The author maps this on to Yutong (49,518 buses delivered worldwide in 2025): Europe won't out-produce it on a standard product, but can change what a city bus is.

**Why the smartbus differs**: electrification changed parts (commoditised); the smartbus changes the system, so what is sold is proof, i.e. evidence and an accountable party. Existing rules apply: inside private depots, the Machinery Regulation (2023/1230) without public-road type approval; on roads, Regulation (EU) 2018/858, the General Safety Regulation 2019/2144 and Implementing Regulation 2022/1426; ISO 22737 for low-speed automated driving. Four questions for suppliers: who is accountable, where the data lives, who can stop the vehicle and how fast, and whether it can be shown to an auditor.

**Depot-first autonomy**: on public streets at up to 50 km/h a safety operator stays aboard ("a bus with a safety driver … is simply a more expensive bus"); MAN's MINGA project in Munich (16 partners, automated Lion's City 12 E, MVG pilot this autumn with a safety driver, Level 4 series bus within the decade). In a depot (10 km/h, no passengers, fenced) the problem changes. Manual shunting takes 10 to over 35 minutes per vehicle per day; at MZA Warsaw 35 minutes, valued at over €88,000 per vehicle over twelve years. In a 100-bus depot, 25 minutes per vehicle is about 42 driver-hours daily, roughly 15,000 hours a year or nine to eleven full-time drivers. Over 40% of European bus and coach drivers are over 55 and fewer than 3% are under 25. Assessments were completed with MZA Warsaw, TMB Barcelona and Bernmobil; PKM Gliwice is in technical validation. Bernmobil's Eigerplatz depot was rated "Smartbus Ready": payback about 4.9 years on 100 buses, over 9,000 driver hours (more than five FTEs) recovered per year.

**OEM implications**: the smartbus changes what is sold (vehicle plus certified, supervised, updated system), the margin, and tender criteria (drive-by-wire capability, defined interfaces, safety evidence, EU data storage). Two EU proposals may help: the Industrial Accelerator Act (March 2026; EU assembly and at least 70% EU-origin non-battery content for publicly procured EVs) and the Public Procurement Act (adopted by the Commission 9 September 2026; best price-quality ratio as default). Solaris, HESS and VDL issued a joint white paper in September calling for European content rules and quality-based awards.

**Competition**: Yutong already demonstrates unmanned terminal operations (Zhengzhou; Jinan robotic charging in about 40 seconds), but evidence gathered in China does not transfer to a European depot, so Europe's opening is a regulatory and accountability advantage.

*Sources cited: HESS/Solaris/VDL white paper (Sept 2026); DVV Media Group European Bus Data; MAN press release (23 April 2026); The State of Smart Depots 2026 (Autonomous Systems); IRU 2023 report; Xinhua; The Driven.*
