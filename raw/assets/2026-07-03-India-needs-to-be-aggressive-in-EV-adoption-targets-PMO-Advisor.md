# EV adoption even more relevant after West Asia crisis: PM's Advisor

**Author**: Press Trust of India | **Date**: 2026-07-02

---

Tarun Kapoor, Advisor to the Prime Minister of India, said adoption of electric mobility has become the most essential task for India, as the West Asia crisis exposed how vulnerable the country is to external energy shocks. Speaking at an Assocham conference on "Building India an electric mobility hub for Viksit Bharat," Kapoor said reducing crude oil imports would strengthen the economy and help the rupee, and that even a 5 per cent cut in petroleum use — achievable only through faster EV adoption — "would make a huge difference."

Kapoor noted that two- and three-wheeler segments have seen strong traction but "in four-wheelers we still have a long way to go," and that two-wheelers alone account for 60 per cent of India's petrol consumption, making their electrification a priority (he cited Delhi's new EV policy as a step in the right direction). He also stressed the need to cut India's dependence on China for critical EV components and minerals, saying domestic production capacity must be built up so the industry isn't disrupted if China restricts exports again, with government support promised to help the industry reach self-sufficiency.

On targets, Kapoor said India's approach must be "a very fast targeted approach," with capacity and production needing to grow to "several times" current levels: "Within the next five years, we should see that on the roads the visibility of electric vehicles becomes so significant that the world notices that at least in India something great has happened."
