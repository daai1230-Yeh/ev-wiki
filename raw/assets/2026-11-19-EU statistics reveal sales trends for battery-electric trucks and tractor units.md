# EU statistics reveal sales trends for battery-electric trucks and tractor units

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-11-18

---

Sales of electric commercial vehicles vary by vehicle structure: conventional trucks already account for a higher share of electric vehicles than tractors, according to an analysis by Germany's Federal Statistical Office (Destatis) drawing on Eurostat data.

The share of classic battery-electric trucks over 3.5 tonnes (fixed cab-and-cargo units) rose in the EU from 4.1% in 2024 to 7.7% in 2025. Across all powertrains, Destatis recorded 126,600 new trucks last year, including around 9,800 pure electric.

For tractor units, the electric share climbed from 0.7% in 2024 to 1.6% in 2025. Around 157,500 tractor units were newly registered in the EU, approximately 2,500 of them pure electric.

Diesel remains dominant: "In 2025, 89.0 per cent of newly registered trucks over 3.5 tonnes in the EU were equipped with pure diesel engines. For tractor units, the figure was 94.7 per cent," reports Destatis.

Germany's share of battery-electric trucks over 3.5 t stood at 11.7%, above the EU average, with the highest absolute number (~4,200). The highest shares were in the Netherlands (45.6%), Denmark (27.1%) and Sweden (16.7%).

For tractor units, Greece led with 11.2%, followed by Sweden (9.5%), the Netherlands (7.3%) and Denmark (6.0%). Germany's share was 3.2% but it had the highest absolute number (926), ahead of the Netherlands (502) and France (331).

Combined, Destatis' figures give 12,300 electric units above 3.5 t in 2025; ACEA reported 12,858. The difference may stem from scope — ACEA groups all "externally chargeable" vehicles including PHEVs — or late reporting by individual countries.

According to ACEA, sales of battery-electric trucks and tractor units above 3.5 t grew from 7,508 in 2024 to 12,858 in 2025 (+71%), with the registration share rising from 2.3% to 4.2%, driven by medium-duty vehicles between 3.5 and 16 tonnes.
