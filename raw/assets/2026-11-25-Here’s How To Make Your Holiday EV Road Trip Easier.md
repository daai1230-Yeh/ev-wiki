# Here’s How To Make Your Holiday EV Road Trip Easier

**Author**: Kevin Williams | **Date**: Nov 24, 2026

---

Lots of drivers will be on the roads and at charging stations. It may take some strategizing to make the commute home as pain-free as possible.

- Thanksgiving week is one of the most-traveled times of the year. Expect longer-than-usual queues at charging stations.
- Charging the night before your journey, or using a route planner, could help alleviate those concerns.
- Bring NACS or CCS adapters to ensure your car can plug into every DC fast charger.

EVs have come a long way, so range anxiety shouldn’t be much of an issue these days. Still, with so many drivers on the roads, there are bound to be annoyances on long EV road trips.

## Try Preparing The Night Before

DC fast charging takes longer than stopping for gas, so bottlenecks occur on well-traveled routes with limited fast-charging stops. Most people stop for 20-30 minutes; multiplied by the many drivers heading home, queues can balloon. With home charging, plug in and make sure the car is full for the next day. Without it, use a DC fast charger the night before, when everyone isn’t rushing to leave. Some charging companies also give off-peak discounts.

## Use A Route Planner

EVs consume more energy at higher speeds and uphill, so a flatter or slower route can maximize range. Route planning apps (A Better Route Planner, Plugshare, Google Maps) account for this, may suggest detours, and tell you when and where to stop based on efficiency, destination, desired arrival state of charge and road conditions.

## Always Have A Backup: Get Those Apps And Adapters Ready

The Sunday after Thanksgiving is typically the single busiest travel day of the year. Pay attention to the charging brands and ports along your route; highway stops often cluster competing networks (Tesla Supercharger, Ionna, EVgo, Circle K, Shell). Pre-install their apps and set up payment rather than fumbling at the station. Bring an adapter that lets your car use both NACS and CCS ports.

## Practice Good Charging Etiquette

Keep the queue moving and try not to charge beyond 80%. Charging slows substantially past 80%: 15-20 minutes to reach 80%, another 20 or more for 100%. It’s courteous, and probably quicker, to unplug and charge again closer to your destination.

## Go Ahead And Take The Break, You Probably Need It

A 15-30 minute charging stop is helpful for safe driving. The author has been doing EV road trips since 2021 and notes that range, infrastructure and apps have improved enormously, but it still pays to know what you’re doing.
