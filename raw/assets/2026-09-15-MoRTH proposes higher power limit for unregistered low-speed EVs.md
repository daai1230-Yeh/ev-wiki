# MoRTH proposes higher power limit for unregistered low-speed EVs

**Author**: RetailIntel, sourced from The Hindu BusinessLine | **Date**: 13 September 2026

---

Note: ETAuto's original article ("MoRTH proposal to drop low-speed 2W weight cap risks Chinese dumping: Yulu") linked from the newsletter used a tracking URL that could not be accessed directly. This clipping uses a secondary source covering the same underlying story.

A draft proposal would raise the motor-power threshold for registration-exempt electric two-wheelers from 250W to 600W while retaining the 25 kmph speed cap and removing the 60 kg weight limit. Yulu flags misuse risks; BGauss and other OEMs see scope for personal mobility and last-mile delivery growth.

**Key facts**

Low-speed EV motor-power limit proposed to increase from below 250W (0.25kW) to below 600W (0.6kW). Maximum speed remains below 25 kmph. The existing 60 kg unladen-weight ceiling is proposed for removal. FY26 low-speed electric two-wheeler sales are estimated at 2.3-2.6 lakh units versus 1.8-2.1 lakh in FY25. FY27 volumes are projected at 3.2-3.6 lakh units, potential FY27 growth of 30-35%. MoRTH previously warned in 2022 about vehicles capable of 40-55 kmph despite being sold as low-speed exempt models.

**Why this matters**

Retailers and mobility platforms are assessing partnerships with low-speed EV manufacturers and fleet providers as the rule could broaden economical personal-mobility and delivery use cases. Yulu, in particular, has raised road-safety concerns about removing the weight limit, arguing that a physical weight restriction provides a different kind of control compared with an electronically limited speed — a concern connected to worries about cheaper, heavier imports (including from China) entering the exempt category once the weight cap is lifted.

**What to watch**

Publication of the final MoRTH rule and implementation date; whether the 600W threshold, 25 kmph cap and removal of the 60 kg limit are retained unchanged; requirements for type approval, battery certification, speed-controller tamper resistance, insurance and vehicle identification; state-level enforcement actions against modified or non-compliant low-speed EVs; and OEM launches of sub-25-kmph models with 400W-600W motors.
