# AEM unveils new electric motor for heavy-duty commercial vehicles

**Author**: electrive | **Date**: 2026-08-27

---

British startup Advanced Electric Machines (AEM), which specialises in electric motors without rare earth materials, will unveil the 3D model of its largest and highest-torque motor at the upcoming IAA Transportation in Germany. The new electric motor set to be available as a sample for heavy-duty applications next year.

AEM has announced that it will unveil the next stage in the development of its heavy-duty electric motor technology at the IAA Transportation trade fair in Hannover, Germany, from 15 to 20 September 2026. To date, the British company has offered the HDRM150 and HDRM300C motors under its HDRM motor family for commercial vehicles and industrial applications. The new electric motor, now branded as Titan, is described by AEM as "the largest and highest-torque motor in the HDRM family to date". The manufacturer has not yet disclosed further details such as the motor's power and torque figures ahead of the trade fair.

The public premiere at the Hannover trade fair will be in a live-digital format. AEM has announced a detailed 3D model that visitors can use to explore the new motor to gain an initial insight into AEM's next-generation heavy-duty platform. The first samples of the motor are expected to be available in the third quarter of 2027.

In addition to the Titan premiere, AEM will showcase its integrated, air-cooled electric drive system, the HDRM150, at IAA Transportation. This easily installable drop-in solution is an auxiliary motor with up to 700 volts and a peak output of around 30 kW. The drive combines the HDRM150 with an air-cooled inverter and operates without rare earth elements or liquid cooling. According to AEM, it is particularly suited for demanding auxiliary applications in the off-highway sector. At the IAA, the auxiliary motor will also be demonstrated at the SAF-HOLLAND stand, where the HDRM150 is integrated into the company's e-axle as a generator for regenerative energy recovery. AEM can be found at Stand B40 in Hall 12.

James Widmer, CEO of Advanced Electric Machines, states: "Titan represents a major step forward for heavy-duty electrification, building on a platform that our customers already know and trust. Combined with our integrated air-cooled drivetrain system, we're continuing to develop industry-leading technologies that simplify electrification and secure volatile supply chains by removing reliance on rare earth elements."

AEM originated from Newcastle University in 2017 and has specialised primarily in magnet-free electric motors that eliminate the need for rare earth elements. While the drivetrains of many electric vehicles contain critical raw materials such as neodymium or dysprosium, AEM's electric units are designed to be manufactured exclusively from safe, recyclable, and readily available materials like steel and aluminium. The company has not disclosed the operating principle behind its electric motors. While asynchronous motors and externally excited synchronous motors (such as those used by BMW) are also free from permanent magnets and rare earth elements, they still rely on copper—something AEM claims it also avoids.

Recently, AEM announced two new development contracts from the passenger car industry. These are particularly significant, as AEM had been confronted with declining revenues. In 2022, its turnover amounted to GBP 5 million, but this dropped to GBP 2.2 million (approximately EUR 2.5 million) the following year. In 2024, turnover rose slightly to GBP 2.9 million (around EUR 3.3 million), though the company reported a significantly higher loss of GBP 8.3 million (about EUR 9.6 million). This was partly due to the loss of one of its main customers, Tevva Electric Trucks, and the need to write off inventory.
