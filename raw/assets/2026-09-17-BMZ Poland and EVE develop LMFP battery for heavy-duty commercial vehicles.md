# BMZ Poland and EVE develop LMFP battery for heavy-duty commercial vehicles

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-09-16 15:30

---

BMZ Poland and Chinese cell manufacturer EVE Energy are jointly developing an LMFP battery for electric commercial vehicles. The Versora LMFP, presented at IAA Transportation, is said to offer advantages over LFP, including reduced weight, higher charging power, and better cold-weather performance. Series production is planned for mid-2028.

BMZ Poland and EVE Energy have agreed on a long-term strategic partnership. As their first joint project, the partners are expanding BMZ's modular Versora battery platform to include lithium manganese iron phosphate cells (LMFP) from EVE. The focus is not only on electric trucks and buses but also on construction and agricultural machinery, special vehicles, and ships.

With LMFP, the companies position the new variant between the established cell chemistries LFP and NMC. LFP is known primarily for its low cost, safety, and longevity but has a lower energy density. NMC, on the other hand, offers higher energy density and strong performance across a wider temperature range but is more expensive.

For heavy-duty commercial vehicles, BMZ and EVE see LMFP as offering significant advantages in weight and charging speed. According to the companies, a corresponding battery system in an electric long-haul truck can be up to ten per cent lighter than a comparable LFP solution while meeting the same energy requirements. It should also support charging power of up to one megawatt or charging rates of 2.2C. This would allow the battery to charge from ten to 80 per cent in less than 25 minutes. For a comparable LFP solution, BMZ Poland cites around 35 minutes.

BMZ Poland also provides concrete figures for lifespan and cold-weather performance. The LMFP battery is expected to achieve more than 4,500 full charge cycles while maintaining 80 per cent State of Health. The next generation is targeted to exceed 5,500 cycles. At minus 20 degrees Celsius, 85 per cent of the capacity should remain available, compared to around 75 per cent for the LFP technology used by BMZ Poland for comparison.

## Same platform for NMC and LMFP

Versora is designed as a modular battery platform. The Polish team uses two module formats that can be installed in the vehicle floor, body, or roof and combined according to energy and space requirements. This approach particularly targets manufacturers with different vehicle variants and body configurations.

A key advantage is the use of different cell chemistries within the same architecture. The NMC and LMFP versions are intended to have the same dimensions and weight, as well as identical electrical interfaces and voltage classes. Vehicle manufacturers could thus offer different battery variants without modifying the underlying vehicle architecture.

BMZ Poland is already active as a battery partner in the commercial vehicle sector. Among other projects, the company in Gliwice assembles the new NMC4 battery generation for the Mercedes-Benz eCitaro, which is set to be used in all variants of the electric bus from 2026. The Polish BMZ subsidiary was not affected by the insolvency of BMZ Germany GmbH and BMZ Holding GmbH at the end of 2025. Significant parts of the group were subsequently taken over by SKion, which had previously been involved as a minority shareholder. In the current company presentation, BMZ lists the investment company founded by Susanne Klatten as the owner of the group since 2025.

It will still be some time before the new LMFP version enters series production. The first customer samples are planned for 2027, with series deliveries set to begin in mid-2028. BMZ is further along with the NMC version of Versora: selected customers are already receiving the first samples, and series production is scheduled to start in 2027.
