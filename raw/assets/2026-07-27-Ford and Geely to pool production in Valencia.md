# Ford and Geely to pool production in Valencia

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 24.07.2026

---

Ford and Geely plan to establish a joint production company for the European market. From 2028, the partners intend to manufacture new models from both brands at Ford's Valencia plant in Spain.

Ford will hold a 66 per cent stake in the joint venture, while Geely will own the remaining 34 per cent. Subject to regulatory approvals, the new company is expected to start operations in the first half of 2027, with production of the first new models scheduled to begin in 2028. Ford will continue to build the Kuga at its Valencia plant during the transition.

The partners aim to optimise use of the Valencia plant's existing capacity and lower production costs through the collaboration. According to Ford, the site has an annual production capacity of around 500,000 vehicles. However, neither company has disclosed planned output volumes for the future models.

The joint venture will initially produce four new models. Ford plans to manufacture a compact Bronco derivative and a new multi-energy crossover in Valencia. Ford will design the crossover and develop it jointly with Geely. The companies have not yet disclosed which powertrains the model will use or the platform on which it will be based. The crossover forms part of Ford's European product offensive, which includes the launch of five new multi-energy models by 2029.

Geely plans to manufacture two battery-electric SUVs in Valencia, with production also scheduled to start in 2028. The company has not yet disclosed details about the vehicles or their technical specifications. For Geely, the move marks another step towards localising its European operations.

The partnership extends beyond sharing production capacity. Ford and Geely also plan to collaborate on development, engineering and manufacturing, pooling expertise across these areas. However, the companies have not yet disclosed how far this technology transfer will extend.

Geely has continued to expand its electric powertrain portfolio. Most recently, the company unveiled a highly integrated '16-in-1' e-drive system that combines key electric drivetrain components into a single unit. The technology will debut in the new Geely Galaxy TT saloon. However, it remains unclear whether this or any other Geely technologies will feature in the jointly developed models or other vehicles to be built in Valencia.

The joint venture supports different strategic goals for each partner. Ford will be able to increase utilisation of its existing production capacity while sharing the development and manufacturing costs of new models. Geely, meanwhile, gains access to an established European production site and advances the localisation of its European business. The Geely brand is now also available in Germany with the battery-electric E5 SUV and the Starray EM-i plug-in hybrid.

The two companies are not entering entirely new territory. Ford sold Volvo Cars to Geely in 2010. However, the planned joint venture in Valencia marks a significant expansion of their relationship. The jointly developed Ford crossover is expected to show how closely the partners will cooperate on technology and development.

For Geely, the two announced battery-electric SUVs will mark the start of European production for part of its EV portfolio. From 2028, the Chinese carmaker plans to manufacture these models for the European market at the established automotive plant in Valencia.
