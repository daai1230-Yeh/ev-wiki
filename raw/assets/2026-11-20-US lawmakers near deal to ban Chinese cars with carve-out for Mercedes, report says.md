# US lawmakers near deal to ban Chinese cars with carve-out for Mercedes, report says

**Author:** Phate Zhang　｜　**Date:** 2026-11-20

- Lawmakers in Washington are aiming to send the bill to Trump by the end of the year, according to Bloomberg.

- An earlier bill advanced by a Senate panel would have barred automakers more than 15% owned by Chinese entities from selling vehicles with data-transmission capabilities in the US.

Lawmakers in Washington are closing in on a deal to ban Chinese cars from the US market, following last-minute negotiations aimed in part at sparing Mercedes-Benz Group AG, according to a Bloomberg report on Friday.

Republicans and Democrats from auto-producing states are behind the legislation and aim to send a bill to US President Donald Trump's desk by the end of the year, the report said, citing people familiar with the matter.

If enacted, the US auto market would be formally walled off to Chinese automakers such as BYD (HKEX: 1211) and Geely Auto (HKEX: 0175), whose low-cost, technology-packed electric vehicles have rapidly gained market share in Europe and South America, the report noted.

Chinese cars are already effectively shut out of the US market due to steep tariffs and restrictions on Chinese vehicle technology imposed by the US Commerce Department.

The US government raised import tariffs on Chinese EVs from 25% to 100% in 2024 and finalized rules in January 2025 banning Chinese connected-vehicle software and hardware, with the software and hardware restrictions taking effect for the 2027 and 2030 model years, respectively.

Enshrining a ban into law would take those restrictions a step further to address growing US national and economic security concerns, Bloomberg noted.

Republican Senator Bernie Moreno on Wednesday briefed automaker representatives on proposed changes to earlier legislation, including provisions that would shield Mercedes from an outright ban, the report said, citing people familiar with the matter.

Automaker representatives were broadly supportive of the effort Moreno described, though a Mercedes official voiced reservations and negotiations remain underway. Proponents are working to pass the measure before the next Congress begins in January.

Mercedes said in a statement that it continues to meet with all stakeholders who have a shared interest in ensuring this important legislation becomes law in this Congress.

Earlier this year, a Senate panel advanced a bill that would have prohibited automakers more than 15% owned by Chinese entities from selling cars equipped with technology that can transmit data in the US.

Mercedes has lobbied for changes to that bill. Chinese state-owned automaker BAIC Motor Corp holds nearly 10% of Mercedes, and Geely founder Eric Li also holds nearly 10%, putting the company above the 15% threshold set by the earlier bill.

Lawmakers are in talks with multiple parties, including Mercedes, and have expressed optimism that an agreement could be reached as early as this week, according to a person close to the talks.

Provisions under discussion include looser rules for companies with passive ownership stakes that lack voting power, as well as how such an approach would be structured and verified.

A blanket ban on Chinese cars would run counter to a more permissive approach taken by Europe and Canada. In January, Canada reached an agreement with China allowing up to 49,000 Chinese EVs per year to enter its market at a 6.1% tariff rate.

In Europe, the rapid expansion of Chinese automakers has sparked intense competition with local brands. As Chinese brands quickly expanded their market share, the European Union has started to tighten its stance, reaching an agreement with China last month to curb imports of hybrid vehicles into the bloc.
