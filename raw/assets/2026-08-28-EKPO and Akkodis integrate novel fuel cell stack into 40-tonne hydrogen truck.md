# EKPO and Akkodis integrate novel fuel cell stack into 40-tonne hydrogen truck

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 27.08.2026

---

German company EKPO Fuel Cell and Akkodis, a Swiss specialist in digital engineering, will soon integrate and test a fuel cell stack from EKPO in a hydrogen truck that achieves a system output of up to 360 kW in the demonstration vehicle. The partners plan to use the data collected for developing future fuel cell systems that can also be applied to other sectors, such as stationary energy supply, shipping or off-highway machinery.

EKPO Fuel Cell Technologies and Akkodis have announced plans to test the NM20 fuel cell stack in real-world driving conditions. Akkodis, a digital engineering consultancy and part of the Swiss Adecco Group, is responsible for developing and assembling the fuel cell-powered powertrain and integrating it into a 40-tonne hydrogen truck. During its subsequent deployment with a logistics company, the vehicle is expected to deliver critical operational data and demonstrate the technology's performance under real-world conditions.

The truck equipped with the NM20 serves as a mobile development and validation platform. Alongside ongoing test bench trials, real-world driving is intended to provide insights into how fuel cell technology performs under varying load profiles, environmental conditions and operational demands. For EKPO, the project is a key component of its system engineering approach: the fuel cell stack is deployed and validated at the system level during an early development phase, allowing interactions between the stack, balance of plant, control systems, thermal management and high-voltage architecture to be identified and optimised early. EKPO says the data obtained will directly feed into further development of the NM20 and support the transition of the technology into series production.

EKPO developed the NM20 specifically to meet the demands of heavy-duty applications requiring high power output and efficiency. Managing Director Dr Stefan Dwenger anticipates that data gathered from real-world operation will provide valuable insights for further development and make a significant contribution to the industrialisation of hydrogen-powered drivetrains.

Dr Thomas Klukas, CEO of Akkodis Germany, added that integrating a fuel cell into real-world logistics operations is a complex undertaking, and that Akkodis combines technology, system architecture, hardware and software into a fully functioning system — creating new opportunities to deploy hydrogen technology in heavy-duty transportation and beyond.

The NM20 is currently the most powerful fuel cell stack in EKPO's portfolio and is based on the company's PEM fuel cell technology. It is rated at up to 400 kW and designed for a service life of over 25,000 operating hours, making it particularly suitable for cost-effective fuel cell solutions in long-haul transport.

A one-stack-one-system approach is applied in the demonstration vehicle: the hydrogen truck operates with just one NM20 stack, achieving a system output of up to 360 kW with efficiencies of up to 61%. The partners say the concept demonstrates that high-performance fuel cell systems can be developed without complex multistack architectures, while the NM20's current-voltage characteristics enable efficient integration into modern high-voltage systems and create opportunities for megawatt-scale applications beyond road transportation.

The project aims to go beyond testing a single vehicle. The partners plan to use the collected data on system architecture, operating strategies and key system components as a foundation for future fuel cell systems, creating a reference platform for system integrators and vehicle manufacturers. The findings are not limited to road freight but can also be applied to stationary energy supply, shipping or off-highway machinery.

Development and commercialisation of the NM20 stack are supported under the European IPCEI funding programme (Important Project of Common European Interest). EKPO positions itself as a full-service provider of fuel cell stacks and components for passenger cars, light commercial vehicles, trucks, buses, and rail and marine applications. EKPO is a joint venture between automotive suppliers ElringKlinger and OPmobility.
