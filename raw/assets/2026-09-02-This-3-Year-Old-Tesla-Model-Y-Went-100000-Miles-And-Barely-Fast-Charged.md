# This 3-Year-Old Tesla Model Y Went 100,000 Miles And Barely Fast-Charged. Here's How The Battery Held Up

**Author**: Nico DeMattia | **Date**: 2026-08-31

---

Common EV wisdom says that DC fast charging, while convenient, can increase battery degradation over time. But YouTube host Branden Flasch recently bought a used Tesla Model Y with over 110,000 miles — and it shows a surprising amount of battery degradation despite almost never being fast-charged.

The 2023 Model Y Long Range All-Wheel Drive had only about 2 megawatt-hours of DC fast-charging, but over 40 MWh of AC charging, per Tesla's battery health test — it even had more charging from regen braking than from DC fast-charging. With 111,703 miles put on in around three years, the previous owner drove a ton, but rather than being a rideshare-style heavy DC user, Sentry Mode footage showed a 105-mile-each-way commute.

After driving the car cross-country using Superchargers, Flasch saw 77% battery health via Tesla's own in-car test — a 23% reduction in three years, on the aggressive side. The third-party Tessie app showed 80.95%, about four points better than Tesla's own figure.

The result isn't hugely surprising, since the common wisdom that excessive fast-charging ruins EV batteries isn't well grounded in reality. Battery-health startup Recurrent studied 13,000 Teslas and found "fast charging is not having the negative effect we expected." Davide Giacobbe, cofounder of EV health company Voltest, has told InsideEVs that the proportion of fast charging an EV has experienced isn't actually a good predictor of battery health — while fast charging can heat a battery and contribute to capacity loss, modern EV thermal management systems are well equipped to handle it, and other variables have a far greater impact on longevity.

Possible explanations for this car's degradation: the previous owner may have frequently charged to 100% and discharged to near-empty overnight before the long commute, and/or extreme heat — the car originally came from the Palm Springs, California area, which regularly sees triple-digit daytime temperatures.

The takeaway: there's far more to battery degradation than fast charging or the number on the odometer. A growing body of evidence shows modern EV batteries can generally go the distance, and fast-charging specifically doesn't appear to be as big a factor as other variables.
