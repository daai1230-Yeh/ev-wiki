# Waymo And Uber's Robotaxi Partnership Is Officially Ending: Report

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-11-04

---

- Waymo is planning to serve riders via its own app in Austin and Atlanta from 2028 onwards.
- The company said the Uber partnership was a little bit of a "crapshoot."
- Both companies still have grand ambitions when it comes to autonomy.

Today, riders in Austin or Atlanta can only take a driverless Waymo via the Uber app, thanks to an exclusive partnership. That will change because Waymo Co-CEO Tekedra Mawakana told Bloomberg that the company will exit the partnership in both cities starting in January 2028. Her reason: the Uber app doesn't guarantee a driverless ride. Riders can enable autonomous rides in settings, but whether one shows up depends on how many Waymos are nearby; otherwise they are matched with human drivers. Uber says it offered to add a dedicated robotaxi ride option, which Waymo reportedly declined.

"In the early days, we would partner because we didn't have the scale," Mawakana said, adding that partnering "was a little bit of a crapshoot."

Customers now demand a more consistent experience, she said, so from January 2028 Waymos in Austin and Atlanta will operate via Waymo's own app, as in over two dozen other US cities. Uber will become a direct rival in those cities.

Waymo is a fully owned subsidiary of Alphabet. It currently conducts over 500,000 paid driverless trips each week, operates in 15 US cities, and plans to add over a dozen more in coming months. It still partners with Lyft in Nashville (non-exclusive; Waymo also serves Nashville through its own app).

Uber plans to have 700 robotaxis online by the end of this year and 2,000 by the end of 2027, and has deals with Rivian, Lucid, Wayve and others to offer their driverless vehicles on its app.
