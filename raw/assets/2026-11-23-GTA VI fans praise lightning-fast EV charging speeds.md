# GTA VI fans praise lightning-fast EV charging speeds

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-11-20

---

The hype around Grand Theft Auto VI is enormous - and the automotive industry is getting heavily involved. BMW, Daimler Truck, and Sixt are using the game's aesthetic for their social media presence. But electromobility also has its place in the game itself: in Vice City, charging EVs happens much faster than in real life.

After more than 13 years of waiting, Grand Theft Auto VI is finally here. Expectations for the latest instalment in the successful video game series are enormous. The game world, set in the fictional state of Leonida and the metropolis of Vice City – inspired by Florida and Miami respectively – offers not only countless opportunities for virtual car chases but also a golden opportunity for the marketing departments of numerous companies.

The unmistakable GTA aesthetic, with its palm trees, sunsets, vibrant colours and distinctive visual style, has long since become a social media trend. The automotive industry has also jumped on the bandwagon.

BMW, for example, references the GTA hype and the long wait since the predecessor in an Instagram post. Daimler Truck has also published an Instagram carousel featuring vehicle designs in the style of the game. Mercedes-Benz USA, meanwhile, leverages the GTA visual language, including the nod "Mission Passed. Respect +63" alongside images of the all-electric AMG GT 4-Door – a nod to the typical success message received after completing a mission and a play on the AMG model designation.

## Sixt greets players with "Welcome to Miami Nice"

Sixt has been particularly active: the car rental company has published several posts on its Instagram profile. One of them reads "Welcome to Miami Nice!" – a clear reference to Vice City.

Other manufacturers such as Toyota, Hyundai, BYD, Xpeng and Volvo have also released their own GTA-inspired content. Xpeng, for instance, pokes fun at a particularly law-abiding car stopping at a red light – in Vice City, this is hardly a promising strategy for achieving a five-star wanted level. BYD, on the other hand, interprets the iconic visual style with its own 'Build Your Dreams' slogan.

Interestingly, hardly any of the companies have an official partnership with GTA VI publisher Rockstar Games. Instead, they are leveraging the game's immense popularity for their own brand communication – and Rockstar Games is well aware of the viral effect, even allowing the creation of images and videos using AI systems like ChatGPT or Gemini.

## GTA VI features EV charging stations for the first time

But what about electromobility within the game world itself? Fortunately, electric cars are not just decorative elements. Players driving electrically in Leonida must actually keep an eye on their battery level. The same applies to the fuel levels of internal combustion engine vehicles which also require regular refuelling.

As reported by automotive magazine Motor1, the game features both petrol stations and dedicated charging stations. If the energy level of an EV's traction battery drops, the player must drive to a charging point. However, the actual charging process is a remarkably convenient affair: it takes just a few seconds. Realistic charging power or charging curves are, of course, not the goal here. Anyone who has ever waited at a fast-charging station for the final push from 80 to 100 per cent in real life might still regard this virtual charging speed with a touch of envy.

Range also cannot be completely ignored. Gaming portal Kotaku tested various vehicles: an older pick-up ran out of fuel after 29 minutes and eight seconds of continuous driving, a sports car after just 18 minutes and 31 seconds. An electric car modelled after the Tesla Model 3 lasted 20 minutes and 23 seconds.

At least charging stations are marked on the GTA VI map. Unlike in real life, no one has to search for a charging card, download a charging app or deal with a malfunctioning charging station.

## Electrify America welcomes its virtual competitor

When it comes to charging stations, the circle even closes back to viral marketing: Electrify America, a Volkswagen subsidiary and one of the largest charging providers in the US, has jumped on the GTA bandwagon – and found a particularly clever hook. In the game world, there is a fictional charging network for electric cars called 'ReVolt America'. Electrify America has seized on this discovery by players in its own PR campaign, playfully welcoming the new competitor in Vice City. However, the company states it will continue to focus on charging electric vehicles in the real world. After all, five-star wanted levels and stolen cars are not exactly part of its customer journey.

With a dedicated website at revoltamerica.com, the company takes the joke even further: "Looking for ReVolt America? Wrong Universe. For Hyper-Fast charging in this one, find Electrify America."

## CDU celebrates the end of the combustion engine ban – as a GTA mission

The CDU also wanted to capitalise on the GTA hype and published an Instagram carousel featuring Friedrich Merz, styled like the video game. Under the motto 'Achievements Before Grand Theft Auto VI', Merz is depicted with sunglasses, as a police officer and as a fighter jet pilot, among other roles.

The party accompanies the images with a list of supposed government achievements. Particularly notable for electromobility is the final slide: it shows Merz behind the wheel of a car, with the message "Ende vom Verbrenner-Verbot" ("End of the Combustion Engine Ban").

The fact that the Union chose to deliver this political message in a video game meme is, from an electromobility perspective, naturally less than ideal. Greenpeace responded to the post with a remarkably apt satire, reconstructing the alleged prompt behind the carousel:

"We found the prompt: 'Create 7 images for an Instagram carousel: Friedrich Merz's achievements before GTA 6. If there aren't 7, just make some up. Make him look cool. Sunglasses, as a pilot in a fighter jet, that sort of thing. Put him in a car. Please leave out the topic of climate protection – he wasn't at COP in Antalya, after all. Also come up with a caption suggestion. Something about Germany.'"

One might add that the 'Climate Protection' mission apparently hasn't been unlocked yet.
