# I Used To Hate Giant EVs Like The Cadillac Escalade IQL. Then I Drove One

**Author**: Mack Hogan | **Date**: 2026-07-16

---

The Escalade IQL weighs 9,000 pounds and has 750 horsepower. Nobody needs that — but Americans want it. When GM announced the Hummer EV, the author argued a 9,000-pound monster EV proved the industry was repeating the same mistakes, valuing overkill over reason. Four years later he stands by that, but concludes that if you're going to go oversized and over-the-top, you might as well go electric — a massive electric truck with over 400 miles of range and every feature imaginable. A week testing the Cadillac Escalade IQL proved that: it ferried six adults, covered hundreds of miles, and ran a 38-speaker stereo without needing a recharge.

## Specs & features

The $156,315 Escalade IQL Premium Luxury tester came with heated, cooled and massaging front seats, 24-inch polished wheels, and 38 speakers with Dolby Atmos. Highlights: a 55-inch dashboard-spanning display (segmented into driver, central and passenger touchscreens), an infrared night-vision camera, Super Cruise hands-free driving, V2L, four-wheel steering (including diagonal "crab" movement), and power doors. Rear passengers get their own screens, HDMI inputs and 5G hotspot.

**Key specs** — As-tested price $156,315 (base $133,300 w/destination); 205 kWh battery; AWD; 460 miles EV range; 350 kW DC fast charging; 750 hp / 785 lb-ft; 24.2 cu ft cargo behind third row (75.4 max), 12.2 cu ft frunk. 0–60 mph in 4.7 seconds. Adding 100 miles takes ~10 minutes, though an Out Of Spec test recorded a 48-minute 10–80% charge due to thermal issues; the huge battery makes fast charging expensive. Real-world efficiency was 2.1 miles/kWh over ~250 miles of mostly city driving.

## Driving experience

The IQL is big, fast and comfy — floaty and unbothered, though the 24-inch wheels transmit sharper impacts. The cabin is hushed and the all-glass roof pleasant. All three rows have space, though the third row isn't as cavernous as the exterior implies (a Toyota Sienna beats it on third-row legroom despite being 25 inches shorter). Four-wheel steering closes some of the practicality gap, but width and limited sightlines make tight parking stressful.

## Why the Vistiq is a better option

Cadillac's other three-row EV, the Vistiq, starts at $79,390 — nearly $50,000 cheaper than the non-L Escalade IQ. A loaded Vistiq Premium Luxury tester was $95,415 with Super Cruise, AKG audio, four-wheel steering and 615 hp. It has less space and 300 rather than 460 miles of range, but rides better, looks better, handles better, costs far less, and uses less than half the battery materials.

## Conclusion

Talking with Midwesterners in their sixties, the author found the Escalade got a better reception than almost any other EV he's tested — the range was more than enough, the size made their gas SUVs look inadequate, and the cabin was cooler than anything in a gas car. It's built for a nation with a preferred serving size of "too much." He loves it, but as a critic he'd rather have the Vistiq — and he's glad both exist.
