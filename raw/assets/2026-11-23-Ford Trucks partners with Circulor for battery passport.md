# Ford Trucks partners with Circulor for battery passport

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-20

---

Ford is working with technology company Circulor to prepare the its electric truck batteries for the requirements of the European Battery Passport. The focus is on the traceability of battery materials and the collection of supply chain data.

Ford Trucks is preparing for the introduction of the digital EU battery passport. To this end, the Turkish commercial vehicle manufacturer Ford Otosan – a joint venture between Ford Motor and Turkey's Koç Holding – is collaborating with Circulor, a company specialising in supply chain transparency. Circulor's platform is intended to create the data foundation needed to transparently document the origin, processing and supply routes of materials used in traction batteries.

The project begins with a systematic recording of the battery supply chain based on supplier information. This involves documenting the companies and production sites involved, as well as the origin and processing of the materials used. For materials and supply chains with higher risks, additional evidence and independent audits may be required.

Using Circulor's platform, Ford Otosan can request, collect and manage the relevant documentation. The scope of verification is to be aligned with the specific risks associated with individual materials, production sites and supply relationships. The collected information will then form the basis for creating and maintaining digital battery passports.

## EU battery passport mandatory from 2027

The EU Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 requires a digital battery passport for traction batteries in electric vehicles and certain other battery categories starting 18 February 2027. This passport will be linked to the respective battery via a QR code and will provide information on technical characteristics, performance, durability, sustainability and recycling, among other details. Access rights to the data will vary depending on the type of information.

For manufacturers of electric commercial vehicles, this means that information from various stages of the supply chain must be consolidated and managed throughout the battery's lifecycle. The collaboration with Circulor is intended to help Ford Otosan establish the necessary digital processes for this purpose.

With the F-Line E, Ford Trucks has unveiled its first series-ready electric truck. Developed and produced by Ford Otosan, the model was presented at Solutrans in Lyon in November 2025 and is expected to enter the German market in 2026. The battery-electric truck will be available in two weight classes: as a 19-tonne model with a battery capacity of 294 kWh and a range of up to 250 km, and as a 26-tonne model with 392 kWh gross and a range of up to 300 km. Both variants use NMC batteries.

Circulor already has experience with digital battery passports for electric vehicles. The British company, together with Volvo, developed a battery passport for the electric EX90, which was introduced in 2024. The system enables the traceability of battery materials along the supply chain and provides selected information via a QR code. According to the company, Circulor's platform now supports the traceability of materials for more than 1.4 million vehicles.

With this new cooperation, Ford Otosan aims to create a comparable data foundation for its electric commercial vehicles and prepare for the EU's future documentation requirements.
