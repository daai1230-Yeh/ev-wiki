# VW and Elli launch €1,995 V2G BiDi wallbox

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 24.11.2026 - 17:00

---

Volkswagen and its energy subsidiary Elli will introduce their first Vehicle-to-Grid package to the German market in December 2026. The requisite DC wallbox will cost €1,995 euros. In addition to VW, VW Commercial Vehicles, and Cupra, Skoda will also be included in the launch. Details about the electricity tariff have also been announced.

At the ‘V2G Leaders Europe’ industry conference in Brussels, Elli CEO Giovanni Palazzo announced that Volkswagen Group will launch its bidirectional charging offering in December 2026. Following VW and Elli’s initial presentation at the Power2Drive trade fair in June, the concrete terms, tariff structure, and ordering process of the vehicle-to-grid package have now been finalised. In order to manage the aggregated battery capacities in the energy market, Elli is collaborating with Munich-based specialist The Mobility House.

Contrary to the announcement made this summer, Škoda will also participate in the German market launch alongside Volkswagen, Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles, and Cupra. The requisite ‘Elli BiDi Charger’ costs €1,995, including VAT and shipping, and can be ordered directly from Elli. According to company statements, a different procurement channel is planned for VWN customers, though no specific details have been provided yet. Beyond Germany, Elli intends to gradually expand the offering to other European countries, though no timeline has been specified.

The hardware is a compact DC wallbox with up to 11 kW charging and discharging power, designed for indoor or outdoor installation. It is compatible with vehicles based on 400-volt and 800-volt architectures and supports the ISO 15118-20 communication standard, enabling potential future use with electric vehicles from other manufacturers.

Interested parties can now register their interest on Elli’s website but must complete several steps before purchasing. A compatible smart meter is mandatory. As its installation often requires lead time, Elli recommends arranging this promptly with the local metering point operator or its partner company, Inexogy.

Once the offering launches in December, the technical prerequisites for installation can be verified via a digital pre-check on Elli’s website. Only after passing this digital pre-check and receiving an individual installation quote from a referred specialist company can the wallbox actually be ordered.

For the financial incentive, the initial model remains based on pure plug-in time: Elli credits €0.24 per hour connected, provided each V2G session lasts at least three hours. Customers who reach 250 hours per month can achieve the maximum of €60 monthly or €720 in the first contract year.

At a later date, users will also receive payment for the electricity actually fed back into the grid. Elli has indicated a rate of €0.40 cents p/ kWh but excludes any guarantee of minimum offtake and has not yet announced a start date for this remuneration tier.

Contractually, Elli structures the system through two combined tariffs under the ‘Volkswagen Naturstrom’ series, each running for twelve months: ‘V2G Flow’ supplies the household and charges the vehicle, while ‘V2G Return’ handles the feed-in billing. Control is managed via the ‘Elli BiDi V2G App’, where users must enter their departure time and desired minimum charge level.

According to Volkswagen, more than one million MEB-based vehicles in Europe are technically prepared for bidirectional charging, including around 360,000 in Germany. However, the number of vehicles that can actually use the new functions depends on the approval for each specific model and its software version.

While V2G is focused on stabilising the public power grid, Elli plans to expand its portfolio in 2027 with Vehicle-to-Home (V2H). This will allow the electric vehicle’s battery to be used as a buffer storage system, for example, for a private PV system. The foundation for this was laid by Elli, which tested various charging solutions in 20 German households for a year following the IAA Mobility 2025. The trials accumulated around 85,000 operating hours and more than 6,500 charging sessions, the findings of which are now being incorporated into the planned mass-market product.

“With the market launch of Vehicle-to-Grid, we are bringing a new technology to the mass market – at a competitive price, across Group brands, and with tangible economic benefits for customers,” said Elli CEO Giovanni Palazzo in Brussels. “We are starting in Germany, with Vehicle-to-Home and further European markets to follow.”
