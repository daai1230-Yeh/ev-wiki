# What To Know About California's $3,500 EV Rebate—And Which Teslas Qualify

**Author**: Tim Levin | **Date**: 06.08.2026

---

With the federal $7,500 EV tax credit gone, first-time EV buyers in California can now get thousands off an EV purchase through the state's MyFirstEV program, which just started rolling out details on how it works and which cars qualify.

The program gives an up-front $3,500 discount on new EVs (MSRP $50,000 or less) and $1,750 on used EVs (under $25,000), split in cost between the state and participating manufacturers. Price caps don't apply to California-headquartered EV makers, so Lucid and Rivian are exempt (their products already start above the cap). Buyers must attest they've never owned an EV before.

**Participation rollout by manufacturer**: Chevrolet, Ford, Hyundai, Kia, Lucid and Tesla — August 2026; Honda, Lexus, Subaru, Toyota — September 2026; Mitsubishi — November 2026; Nissan, Rivian, Volvo — "coming soon."

Hydrogen fuel-cell vehicles qualify; plug-in hybrids don't. Carmakers decide which specific models and trims are eligible — so not every EV under the price cap automatically qualifies at every dealership. Tesla's MyFirstEV discount currently applies only to existing inventory, not custom orders, and that inventory has shrunk to mostly Model Y Performance crossovers. Since eligibility is based on the base model's MSRP (well under $50,000 for the Model Y), a loaded higher trim can still qualify.

**Used EVs**: Must be at least two model years older than the purchase year (so today, 2024 or older) and sold as certified pre-owned through franchised dealerships — used EVs from independent dealers or EV specialty stores don't count.

California says the $135.5 million program (matched by manufacturers) could incentivize over 73,000 clean vehicle sales, but funding is split equally among participating manufacturers and is limited — Tesla's site already shows 50% of its allocated funds used.
