# Audi A4 e-tron: SSP model 'delayed until 2029'

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-09-17 13:30

---

Audi has seemingly postponed production of the all-electric Audi A4 e-tron to the first half of 2029. The reason for this once again appears to be issues around the introduction of the Volkswagen Group's new SSP electric architecture, which uses technology from US partner Rivian.

First introduced in 1994, the model history of the Audi A4 has confused some customers in recent years: Audi decided to change its naming convention, rebranding the latest-generation A4 as the A5 two years ago – at the expense of the discontinued coupé of the same name. The reasoning: combustion-engine models were to bear odd numbers, while electric models were to use even numbers. However, this distinction has already been abandoned in newer models, as seen with the current Audi A6 and Q6, both of which are available with various drivetrain types from the all-electric e-tron to hybrids and combustion engines.

At the same time, both customers and the industry are eagerly awaiting the introduction of the next A4, which will mean the A5 name for the combustion-engine version of the mid-size classic will once again be retired. Above all else, there will finally be an all-electric Audi A4 e-tron – for which Audi's Head of Technical Development, Rouven Mohr, confirmed a 2028 market launch as recently as summer 2026.

## Audi A4 e-tron: an absent competitor to the BMW i3

However, these plans have now apparently been revised. Handelsblatt reports that production of the Audi A4 e-tron will not begin until at least March 2029. Insiders quoted in the report expect an even later start, possibly not until late April or early May 2029. This means Audi will lack an all-electric competitor to the upcoming BMW i3 and the new all-electric Mercedes-Benz C-Class for up to three years.

The reason for the delay appears to be new issues with the introduction of the Volkswagen Group's new electric vehicle architecture, the Scalable Systems Platform (SSP), while Audi currently still uses the MEB platform for the Audi Q4 e-tron and the PPE platform for the Audi A6 e-tron and Q6 e-tron.

## SSP delayed by around three years

Volkswagen first mentioned the development of the SSP in July 2021, under then-CEO Herbert Diess, who was later succeeded by Oliver Blume. At the time, it was stated that the first SSP-based model was to hit the market as early as 2026. If the Audi A4 e-tron does indeed become the first vehicle based on SSP, the platform's launch will have been delayed by around three years.

The SSP is designed to be highly versatile: According to a tech update for VW investors from September 2025, the electric modular system will, for example, support a system voltage of 400 volts for front-wheel-drive configurations and 800 volts for rear-wheel-drive configurations – with all-wheel drive also possible. In addition to all-electric drivetrains, the focus will be on LFP batteries and vehicles with range extenders also possible.

Another key innovation of the SSP is its zonal E/E architecture. Instead of deploying a multitude of individual control units, functions will be more centrally organised via zone-based computers. VW explicitly links SSP with a unified software architecture and the development of software-defined vehicles. This is also the point at which SSP and the technology of US partner Rivian converge.

## Delays, technical issues and lengthy coordination processes

Returning to the debut of the Audi A4 e-tron, which will be available as a saloon and an estate ('Avant'): according to Handelsblatt, the start of production has been delayed by technical issues, lengthy coordination processes within the Group, as well as cost-cutting pressure at Volkswagen, which is forcing the company to extend the use of existing vehicle platforms.

Internally, there has reportedly been a months-long debate about whether the Group needs a new Chief Technology Officer to set the direction for the new SSP architecture and other technical issues. Without such a role, the Group risks once again becoming bogged down in whether the Wolfsburg HQ or the individual brands should get the final say. Rumour has it that Audi CEO Gernot Döllner is positioning himself for the CTO role, partly to establish Audi as the Group's technology pacesetter. However, this could potentially create a conflict of interest. VW brand chief Thomas Schäfer is also considered a possible candidate for the position.

## Döllner pushing for a 48-volt electrical system

During the development of the SSP, Döllner is said to have already acted as a de facto CTO – pushing for the Audi A4 e-tron and others to adopt a 48-volt electrical system instead of the 12-volt standard previously used across the Group. The higher voltage enables the connection of more powerful computers but could pose a challenge for European suppliers, who have so far primarily worked with 12-volt systems.

According to the report, the SSP will now be significantly less complex than originally planned. Instead of the initially envisaged eight variants, the electric modular system will feature just four. At the same time, the proportion of shared components is expected to increase significantly – with orders already being placed with suppliers.
