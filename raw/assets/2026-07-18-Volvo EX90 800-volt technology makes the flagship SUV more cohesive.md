# Volvo EX90: 800-volt technology makes the flagship SUV more cohesive

**Author**: Daniel Bönnighausen | **Date**: 2026-07-16

---

With the switch from a 400-volt to an 800-volt architecture, Volvo has done more than a routine model update to the EX90. The flagship SUV now charges significantly faster, addressing the biggest weakness of the original version not long after its delayed 2024 launch. Electrive's test drive covers the Single Motor Plus seven-seater.

**Background:** The EX90 launched on the SPA2 platform in 2022 with 400 volts and reached customers only in mid-2024 after software delays. Shortly after, then-CEO Jim Rowan announced a shift to 800 volts for the next model year — before Volvo replaced him with returning former CEO Håkan Samuelsson, citing "rapid technological change" and competitive pressure. The overhaul followed: the charging gap is closed and the computing platform upgraded, but the roof-mounted lidar has been removed.

**Powertrain and comfort:** The Single Motor variant's rear-axle motor now delivers 245 kW (up 40 kW), cutting 0–100 km/h to 6.8 seconds from 8.4. Kerb weight dropped slightly to 2,585 kg as a seven-seater. The Twin Motor AWD now makes 335 kW and the Performance version 500 kW (not yet configurable); top speed stays capped at 180 km/h. Despite riding on steel springs rather than the Twin Motor's air suspension, the Single Motor absorbed rough pavement with little harshness. Steering remains light and comfort-oriented rather than sharp.

**Efficiency and range:** The tested Single Motor is homologated for 559–572 km WLTP. Over a 222 km mostly-motorway route at 125–140 km/h, the onboard computer showed 21 kWh/100 km, translating to roughly 430 km of real-world range; gentler mixed driving got as low as 18 kWh/100 km (~500 km range). Notably, Volvo shrank the usable battery from 101 kWh to 90 kWh net, but calculated energy-per-range actually improved slightly (15.7 kWh/100 km vs. ~16.2–16.3 kWh/100 km previously) — Volvo traded a bit of capacity for efficiency and much faster charging.

**Charging:** The Single Motor can draw up to 310 kW DC, with a 10–80% charge rated at 22 minutes versus 29 for the predecessor. In practice, 8–80% took about 21 minutes with a preconditioned battery, hitting a peak of 325–328 kW between 12–20% state of charge. That's now competitive with the Kia EV9 and Hyundai Ioniq 9 (~24 minutes each). AC charging remains capped at 11 kW three-phase (~9 hours full charge) — still no 22 kW option, a carryover criticism. The EX90 is said to be hardware-ready for bidirectional charging.

**Tech and safety:** The EX90 gets more computing power on Volvo's Superset Tech Stack, enabling new features: Connected Safety hazard warnings between networked cars, automatic emergency-service calls after an emergency stop, dark-condition automatic emergency steering, and Park Pilot Assist parallel parking. The processor upgrade is available free to existing owners at a workshop visit; the 800-volt hardware itself cannot be retrofitted.

**The vanished lidar:** The Luminar lidar hump is gone. Volvo cites supply-chain risk reduction rather than technical reasons; Luminar disputes this and is seeking compensation, and has since filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. Volvo says the EX90 and ES90 still meet its safety standards using radar, cameras, ultrasonic sensors, and new Nvidia processors instead.

**Interior:** Nearly everything is controlled via the 14.5-inch portrait touchscreen with Google services, with few physical buttons. Second-row comfort is strong; the third row suits children better than tall adults. Boot space rose to 697 litres (from 669) plus a 49-litre frunk, and braked towing capacity for the Single Motor increased to 1.5 tonnes (from 1.2).

**Pricing:** The EX90 starts at €84,190 for the Single Motor Core (€89,590 for the tested Plus trim), rising to €106,990 for the Twin Motor Performance Ultra — positioning it against the BMW iX and Mercedes EQS SUV, though the 800-volt Kia EV9 and Hyundai Ioniq 9 are cheaper.

**Verdict:** The 800-volt update doesn't reinvent the EX90 — it remains a large, quiet, comfortable battery-electric SUV — but it now charges as fast as buyers would expect from a flagship in this class, and feels notably more cohesive than the original version, even if details like the 11 kW AC charger remain unresolved.
