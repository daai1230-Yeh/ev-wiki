# I Drove All The New Toyota EVs. Here's My Pick—And How To Choose

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-09-10

---

The Toyota bZ, C-HR, and bZ Woodland all share the same bones. I drove them all to find the best value.

Toyota's EV timing has been counterintuitive. When EV sales were booming and the federal tax credit sweetened the deal, the automaker crawled through the transition with just one so-so model, the bZ4x. Now that the incentive is gone and EV sales have cooled, Toyota has three new or refreshed models to sell, plus more on the way. The strategy is working: in Q1 this year, the bZ was America's best-selling non-Tesla EV thanks to big range and charging improvements, and Toyota's U.S. EV sales rose 225% in Q2.

**Pricing & basics.** Base pricing with destination: $36,495 for the bZ, $38,695 for the C-HR, $46,975 for the bZ Woodland. The bZ leans toward value and competes with the Tesla Model Y and Hyundai Ioniq 5; the C-HR is compact and sportier; the bZ Woodland is the biggest and most adventure-oriented.

**Range.** All three share the e-TNGA platform and a 74.7 kWh NMC battery (the entry bZ also offers a smaller 57.7 kWh pack, 236 miles). EPA range: bZ up to 314 miles, C-HR 287 miles, bZ Woodland 280 miles (260 on all-terrain tires). Horsepower: bZ 165 (base)/221 (FWD, large battery)/338 (AWD); C-HR 338 hp; bZ Woodland 375 hp. Cargo space: bZ 27.7 cu-ft, C-HR 25.3 cu-ft, bZ Woodland 33.8 cu-ft.

**Efficiency & charging.** All three exceeded 3.0 mi/kWh in mixed driving during testing (summer conditions); a winter test on the bZ in upstate New York saw a maximum of 2.7 mi/kWh. Charging is consistent across the lineup — Toyota rates all three at roughly 30 minutes for a 10-80% DC charge, a big improvement over the old bZ4x's nearly an hour. In real-world testing at an Ionna station: the C-HR went 15-80% in 26 minutes (nearly 200 miles added), 15-100% in 48 minutes; the bZ Woodland went 12-80% in 28 minutes, 12-100% in 46 minutes.

**Driving.** The C-HR is the most fun to drive — standard AWD, 338 hp, a claimed 0-60 of 4.9 seconds, and minimal body roll. The AWD bZ is no slouch either; the FWD 221 hp trim (with 300+ miles of range) also felt quick. The bZ Woodland, with 375 hp, is the most powerful and accelerates ferociously, though its extra length and 4,497-lb curb weight (131 lb more than the C-HR, 264 lb more than the bZ) show up as more body roll in corners.

**Ride quality** varies: the bZ is most comfort-biased with a soft setup; the C-HR felt slightly stiff with lateral jolts over bumps; the bZ Woodland — with longer suspension travel, all-terrain tires, and 8.4 inches of ground clearance — was the reviewer's favorite for ride and off-road capability, with Snow, Dirt, and Mud drive modes.

**Interior & tech.** All three share an identical 14-inch touchscreen, steering wheel, gauge cluster and cabin design, with wireless Android Auto/CarPlay. None get Toyota's newer Arene software platform (found in the new RAV4 and Lexus ES) — a Toyota spokesperson said they lack the hardware/software architecture to support it without a major model-year update. A persistent issue since the 2022 bZ4x: the digital gauge cluster is pushed back and gets blocked by the top of the steering wheel for many drivers. The bZ Woodland has the roomiest cabin and most cargo space (33.8 cu-ft); the C-HR's rear seat is cramped for taller adults.

**Verdict.** None of the three are class-leading, but they're good enough for the average buyer. The bZ Woodland has the best ride quality and most room but costs $10,000 more than the bZ. The reviewer's pick is the AWD bZ XLE: the FWD version with the larger battery delivers 314 miles of EPA range at nearly the same price as the base C-HR, and going AWD adds 338 hp and the same off-road settings as the bZ Woodland for a couple thousand dollars more.
