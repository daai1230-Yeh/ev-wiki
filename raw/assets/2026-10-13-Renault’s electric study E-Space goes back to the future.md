# Renault’s electric study E-Space goes back to the future

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-10-12

---

At the 2026 Paris Motor Show, Renault unveiled its new electric van concept called the E-Space. The name and design reference the 1984 Espace, and the focus is on an interior dedicated to passenger well-being.

The all-electric design study retains the silhouette of a classic large MPV, a body style also returning with models like the Volvo EM90, Lexus LM, or Zeekr 009. It measures 4.70 m long, 2.02 m wide and 1.67 m high, with very short overhangs and 23-inch wheels. No drivetrain details were given, but a retractable front splitter is said to reduce drag at high speed.

Development began with the interior, designed as a “rolling living space”, bright and airy with large glass surfaces and low-set windows. Doors open in opposite directions for pillar-less access. The flat floor holds six individual seats; second-row seats rotate 180 degrees to face the third row, or face forward to use two 12.3-inch screens on the front seatbacks. They can also fold into a table, and game controllers are on hand. A sliding console integrates a mini-fridge and air purifier.

The cockpit has a curved 1,130 x 110 mm widescreen display across the width below the windshield (display only, no touch); operation is via two flexible touchscreens beside the steering wheel. Renault calls this OpenR Panorama, to be introduced in upcoming higher-class models, particularly the Rafale and Scenic. Whether the van gets a production successor is not confirmed.

The interior uses bold colours and 1970s-inspired flair, including a 3 cm-thick wool carpet, coloured straw for table and decorative surfaces, a steering wheel covered in a bio-material from castor oil and cotton, bio-based resins in the paint, and tyres with about 19% sustainable natural rubber. Renault has committed to at least 30% recycled materials across its range and uses this study as a technology carrier for bio-based and renewable alternatives.
