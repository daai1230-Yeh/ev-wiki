# Tesla Model 3 Maintenance Guide What Needs Servicing And What Doesn't

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-11-16

---

EVs need a lot less maintenance than a conventional combustion vehicle. That said, they still need some attention. (Sponsored series presented by ERANGE EV.)

Everybody knows that electric cars are pretty easy on the owner's wallet when it comes to regular maintenance. They may cost more to buy new than a comparable gas car, but as the miles pile on, there's no need to worry about engine oil changes and emissions checks.

While all of this is true, EVs aren't exactly maintenance-free. There might not be an engine under the hood, and no manual or automatic transmission to worry about, but EVs still have transmissions, believe it or not. They also have brake fluid, brake pads, and coolant. And all of these—plus others—need attention over the course of a car's life.

For this first installment of our new series on EV maintenance, I'll focus on the Tesla Model 3, one of the best-selling electric models of all time. I'll go through what maintenance is required by Tesla to keep the warranty intact, and also discuss items that are not part of the company's service schedule but might still be worth considering for the long run.

## Tesla Model 3 Maintenance: What Tesla Recommends

- **Cabin air filter replacement**: every two years
- **HEPA filter and carbon filter replacement**: every three years, for vehicles that have them from the factory
- **Brake fluid test**: every four years. Brake fluid is hygroscopic, meaning it naturally absorbs moisture from the air. If there's a lot of moisture contamination, hydraulic pressure drops, which leads to a softer brake pedal and longer braking distances.
- **Brake cleaning**: every year or every 12,500 miles in areas where roads are salted during winter. Even though all modern EVs use their electric motors to slow down, they still rely on mechanical brakes. Tesla recommends cleaning and lubricating the brake calipers. The thickness of the brake pads also needs to be checked, though there have been plenty of cases of Teslas with over 100,000 miles and the original brake pads still in good condition.
- **Radiator cleaning**: when needed. The Model 3 still needs a radiator to keep the thermal system running cool; debris can build up over time.
- **Precision-cleaning the windshield area in front of the camera**: when needed. Tesla's driver assistance systems rely on cameras; owners can check the Maintenance tab in the infotainment system.
- **Tire rotation**: every 6,250 miles or if the difference in tread depth is 2/32 inches or higher. EVs are harder on tires. Below 4/32nds of an inch, start looking for new tires; below 2/32nds, replace them immediately.
- **Wheel balancing**: when needed (vibrations in the steering wheel).
- **Wheel alignment**: when needed (pulling to one side, uneven tire wear, or after new tires).

All of the vehicle's maintenance needs can be viewed in the Maintenance Summary on the central touchscreen. Tesla says that all of these items need to be performed at an authorized service center, including servicing the low-voltage and high-voltage batteries.

## Tesla Model 3 Maintenance: What Tesla Doesn't Say

We've all heard of "lifetime fluids," but ask any well-versed mechanic out there, and they'll say that they don't exist. The following recommendations are nowhere to be found in Tesla's maintenance plan, but some technicians still recommend them. Tesla has service procedures for all of them.

- **Drive unit fluid change**: between 60,000 and 100,000 miles. Tesla used to recommend changing the drive unit fluid every 100,000 miles; now the transmissions have built-in filters, but plenty of mechanics believe it's good practice to renew the fluid by 100,000 miles at the latest.
- **Coolant check**: every four years. The Model 3 uses coolant to keep the high-voltage battery at an optimal temperature.
- **Drive unit, steering, and suspension bushes**: inspect and replace as needed. Rubber bushes lose elasticity or crack over time, causing knocks or rattles.

## Where to service the Tesla Model 3

Under warranty, Tesla's authorized service centers should handle all maintenance items. After the warranty expires, many of the maintenance actions can be easily done at home. Air filter changes and radiator cleaning are DIY jobs. Tesla has published all of its service manuals online, and plenty of independent EV shops have popped up that can usually handle repairs and maintenance at lower costs than Tesla.

## Key takeaway

Keeping an EV on the road should be much cheaper than a gas car, but it still requires some maintenance. Over a five-year period, the main maintenance items should include at least one set of new tires, multiple tire rotations, two brake fluid tests and maybe one flush, two cabin air filter replacements, several brake checks, and at least one set of wiper blades.
