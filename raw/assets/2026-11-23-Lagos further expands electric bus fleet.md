# Lagos further expands electric bus fleet

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-21

---

Eleven new electric buses for public transport have been introduced in Lagos, Nigeria. The vehicles are from Oando Clean Energy and represent the first batch of a planned delivery of 50 electric buses. In the long term, 552 e-buses are to be deployed on the routes across the megacity.

The Lagos Metropolitan Area Transport Authority (LAMATA) and Oando Clean Energy Limited (OCEL) are advancing the electrification of public transport in Lagos. From humble beginnings, the city has come a long way from a pilot project it launched in 2023; this initially saw two vehicles deployed to test the technology under real-world conditions.

The eleven buses now being introduced represent the first batch of deliveries from a total of 50 electric buses planned under a current agreement. In the long term, the deployment of 552 electric buses on the city's public transport corridors is envisaged. However, LAMATA has not yet provided a concrete timeline for full implementation. There is also no information available on the technical specifications of the vehicles, such as battery capacity, range, or charging power.

The collaboration between LAMATA and Oando Clean Energy began in April 2022 with the signing of a memorandum of understanding. The following year, two electric buses were brought to Lagos for a trial operation. During the subsequent testing phase, the project partners examined, among other things, technical reliability, environmental impact, and passenger acceptance, according to their own statements. The insights gained are now intended to serve as the basis for the gradual expansion of the fleet.

## From pilot project to regular service

Beyond vehicle procurement, Oando Clean Energy plans to establish a local electromobility infrastructure. This includes charging stations, maintenance facilities, the training of technical personnel, and a supply of spare parts. In the long term, the company also aims to assemble EVs in Nigeria.

According to the company, the project is supported, among others, by Nigeria's Bank of Industry, Wema Bank, and the Chinese vehicle manufacturer Yutong. Additionally, potential financing partnerships with the Africa Finance Corporation (AFC) and the International Finance Corporation (IFC) are being explored.

As early as April 2023, Oando Clean Energy, together with Yutong, announced plans to deploy 12,000 electric buses within seven years. The initiative was to begin in Lagos and, in addition to vehicle procurement, include the development of charging infrastructure and a local assembly plant. The relationship between the previously stated target of 12,000 buses and the currently planned 552 electric buses is not clarified in the latest announcement.

With the electrification of bus transport, the Lagos State Government aims to reduce emissions and air pollution in public transport. The expansion is part of the state's climate strategy, which targets net-zero emissions by 2050.
