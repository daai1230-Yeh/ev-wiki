# How Much Quicker Is The Newest Version Of Tesla FSD? This Test Put It Against Old Software To Find Out

**Author**: Rob Stumpf | **Date**: Aug 7, 2026

---

A YouTuber measured how Tesla's FSD hardware and software versions affect reaction time. Hardware 4 outperformed the older Hardware 3, but the real kicker is how much quicker Tesla's new v14 Lite software was on HW3 than older software on the same computer — showing that a mix of hardware and software is needed for best safety and performance.

YouTuber TechGeek Tesla decided to try out how quickly Tesla's vehicles react when they simulate a person walking in front of the car. The test took three different cars and put them through the same route at the same speed, facing the same dummy hoisted out in front to simulate an oblivious person running into the road.

Two cars were equipped with the older Hardware 3 computer and one with the currently deployed Hardware 4. One HW3 car had Tesla's Full Self-Driving version v12.6.4, the other had v14 Lite installed, and the HW4 car had the full-fledged FSD v14.3.4.

The YouTuber built a pulley system across the road, attached a human-looking dummy to the rig, and as the cars approached a specific point traveling at 23 MPH, reeled the dummy into the road while measuring reaction speed to the fraction of a second. Results:

| Hardware | Software | Reaction Time (Seconds) | Delta From Baseline | Delta from v14 Lite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HW3 | 12.6.4 | 1.065 | Baseline | 57.8% Slower |
| HW3 | v14 Lite | 0.675 | 36.6% Faster | Baseline |
| HW4 | 14.3.4 | 0.450 | 57.8% Faster | 30.7% Faster |

Tesla's latest HW4 predictably performed best, outperforming HW3 on FSD v12 by 57.8% and HW3 on v14 Lite by 36.6%. Software matters too: the car with FSD v14 Lite reacted 36.6% faster than HW3 running the older software, while HW3 on the older FSD v12.6.4 reacted 57.8% slower than the HW4 car, which benefits from both software and hardware improvements.

While reaction time isn't the end-all in self-driving performance, it's an important factor and shows how important these powerful onboard computers are in modern cars. Tesla's quest for more compute continues — the automaker has said it plans to double the memory in new HW4 units, and its upcoming AI5 chip will be even more powerful, enabling more intensive inference models for split-second driving decisions.
