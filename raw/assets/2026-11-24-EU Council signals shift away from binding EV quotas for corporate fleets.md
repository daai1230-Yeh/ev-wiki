# EU Council signals shift away from binding EV quotas for corporate fleets

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-11-23

---

When presenting the 'Auto Package', the EU Commission included a draft passage requiring member states to set concrete mandates for the ramp-up of electric company cars from 2030. However, the EU Parliament then downgraded the proposed binding quotas to voluntary targets - and the Council now seems to be following suit.

Following the European Parliament’s vote, the EU Council has now reportedly signalled its rejection of binding national EV quotas for large corporate fleets. According to Automobilwoche, the Irish Council Presidency also intends to allow tax incentives for internal combustion engine company cars to continue. This is evident from a compromise draft by the Council Presidency for a meeting today (Monday), which Automobilwoche claims to have obtained.

Under the ‘Auto Package’ presented at the end of last year, the European Commission proposed imposing specific requirements on member states for the electrification of company cars starting in 2030 – with different targets for each country. The official reasoning was “to support the introduction of zero-emission and low-emission vehicles by large companies.” This was inspired by Belgium’s successful company car taxation policy, which has led to a sharp increase in electric vehicle sales there.

However, both the Parliament and the Council now want to significantly water down this aspect of the Auto Package. The binding quotas originally proposed are to be replaced by so-called aspirational targets. “This means the targets remain voluntary, and member states will not face penalties for failing to meet them,” Automobilwoche reported in late October.

For this vote, the Conservatives (EPP), Social Democrats (S&D), and Liberals (Renew) agreed on a joint opposing position. Together, these three groups form the so-called Centre Platform. EPP member Jens Gieseke, Chair of the European Parliament’s Transport Committee, told Automobilwoche: “For corporate fleets, we have found a good compromise with the other groups that protects fleet operators from major burdens.”

While the Council largely adopts the Parliament’s position on relaxing electric vehicle quotas, opinions differ on the future of company car incentives. According to Automobilwoche, the three parliamentary groups of the Centre Platform want to restrict financial support to zero-emission and low-emission corporate vehicles from 2028 onwards, as originally proposed by the Commission. In contrast, “the Council Presidency wants to continue allowing incentives for internal combustion engine vehicles in principle,” writes Automobilwoche.

These controversies increasingly illustrate how the ‘Auto Package’ draft may change before the final vote. In the trilogue, the company car regulation is not the central issue, but more of a side issue. The primary focus is on the relaxation of the so-called EU internal combustion engine phase-out. Three parliamentary committees have already voted on this. These votes are seen as a guide for the further legislative process. The result: a majority of MEPs support a softening of CO2 regulations, which the Centre Platform had also internally agreed on shortly beforehand. According to der Spiegel, the next step is for the plenary to vote in December.

Regarding CO2 fleet limits, the Centre Platform specifically proposes that manufacturers should only be required to reduce CO2 emissions from new cars by 95 per cent instead of 100 per cent (compared to 2021) by 2035. However, they may achieve an additional 7.5 percentage points reduction if they use climate-friendly European steel in their vehicles as compensation. In such cases, the savings target could therefore drop to 87.5 per cent by 2035. According to the three groups, this would still leave room for further registrations of new internal combustion engine and hybrid vehicles even after 2035.
