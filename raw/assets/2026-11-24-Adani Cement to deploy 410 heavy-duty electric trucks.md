# Adani Cement to deploy 410 heavy-duty electric trucks

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 2026-11-23

---

Indian cement manufacturer Adani Cement (Adani Group) has announced plans to add 410 heavy-duty electric trucks to its fleet. It has already flagged off the first batch as part of the initiative and will commission the remaining trucks by March 2027.

Based on images shared with the announcement, Adani Cement appears to have procured eleven units initially. The company has deployed these heavy-duty electric trucks at its Sanghi plant in the western state of Gujarat and is using them to transport clinker and cement. It says the movement of both these materials is a carbon-intensive part of the cement industry’s operations.

The first batch comprises Chinese manufacturer Sany’s 55-tonne heavy-duty electric trucks. These units appear to be either the 5538E or the 5550E, the two 55-tonne high-roof models the company offers in India, both of which have a 4×2 drivetrain layout and pack 360 kW of power. The 5538E uses a 282 kWh battery pack that supports charging at up to 240 kW and delivers a range of 189 km. The 5550E upgrades to a 376 kWh battery pack that supports charging at up to 360 kW and increases the range to 245 km.

Adani Cement says the economics are now in favour of electric trucks in India’s cement industry. The company says that while diesel prices have increased by 60 per cent over the past decade, lithium-ion battery pack costs and solar tariffs have fallen by approximately 70 per cent and 47 per cent, respectively. It also states that diesel trucks emit approximately 737.5 grams of CO2 per kilometre. The company estimates its 410 electric trucks will reduce its diesel consumption by 11 million litres per year and avoid more than 25,000 tonnes of CO2 emissions annually.

Vinod Bahety, CEO of the Cement Business at Adani Group, said the company has identified multiple routes across the country for transporting clinker and cement using electric trucks. Its plan to deploy 410 electric trucks by March 2027 represents the first phase of electrifying these transport operations. By 2030, the company aims to expand its fleet to over 2,000 electric trucks and use them to handle 10 per cent of its road freight.

Commenting on the recent deployment and expansion plans, Bahety said, “Every truck we electrify reduces our carbon footprint and insulates our logistics cost base from diesel price volatility. This is smart sustainability – it benefits the people, planet and the balance sheet.”
