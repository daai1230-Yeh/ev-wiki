# Horse Powertrain presents methanol range extender

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-07-16

---

Horse Powertrain has unveiled a new range-extender powertrain that runs entirely on methanol. The D20 Methanol combines a 2.0-litre turbo engine with a generator and power electronics, aiming to make EVs more efficient, and uses an axial flux generator for the first time — expected to be more compact and power-dense than conventional designs.

Horse Powertrain, a joint venture between Renault and Geely specialising in internal combustion engine technologies, unveiled the D20 Methanol at Auto China 2026. It is designed for range-extended electric vehicles (REEVs), where the engine does not drive the wheels but generates electricity to recharge the traction battery.

The powertrain delivers up to 105 kW and integrates a 2.0-litre turbocharged four-cylinder engine, a generator and power electronics into a system weighing 170 kg. The engine runs on 100 per cent methanol, with a high-energy ignition system enabling lean combustion and reliable cold starts down to minus 35°C. Nitrogen oxide emissions are expected to comply with both China's CN6b standard and Euro 7.

The generator uses an axial flux design (disc-shaped rotor and stator arrangement) rather than a conventional radial flux machine, resulting in a 46 per cent shorter build length and 63 per cent higher volumetric power density. Horse states the drive system's electrical efficiency at 96.4 per cent and fuel-to-electricity efficiency at 47 per cent; in lab tests, a 40 kWh battery could be fully recharged using just 19.6 litres of methanol.

The introduction advances Horse's strategy of flexibly adapting electrified powertrains. In April the JV presented the X-Range C15 Direct Drive, which lets manufacturers offer pure-electric platforms as full hybrids, PHEVs or range-extenders. Unlike the C15, the D20 Methanol remains a classic range extender, aligning with a China-led trend of REEVs that combine local electric driving with long-distance capability while reusing existing electric platforms.
