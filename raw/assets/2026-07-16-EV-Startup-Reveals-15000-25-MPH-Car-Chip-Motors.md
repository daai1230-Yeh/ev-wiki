# EV Startup Reveals $15,000 25-MPH Car That You'll Never Have To Park

**Author**: Tim Levin | **Date**: 2026-07-15

---

A Miami-based startup called Chip Motors has spent the last few years working on a small, low-speed electric vehicle that it's betting even SUV-obsessed Americans will go for. On Wednesday, it emerged from stealth and opened preorders for its first product: a boxy, oddball car with a tall windshield, no doors, an LED "smile," and a promise to make driving optional.

Cofounder Jameson Detweiler said the idea grew from watching golf carts "escape" golf communities during the pandemic and get adopted widely for short trips in warm-climate markets. Federal rules allow "low-speed vehicles" to travel up to 25 mph and be driven on roads with speed limits up to 35 mph, depending on the state.

The Chip starts at $15,000 for a four-seat model with a soft-top roof — in line with a nice street-legal golf cart, before options like doors, paint, and a surfboard rack. It uses in-wheel motors and a roughly 15-kWh battery for about 100 miles of range at its 25-mph top speed. A six-seater costs $18,000. U.S.-assembled deliveries are scheduled to start in 2027 in Florida before expanding.

Unlike other low-speed vehicles from GEM or Moke, Chip offers a remote-driving feature called Chip Go!, letting owners dispatch a remote human driver to park the car after they get out — starting with U.S.-based teleoperators, and initially only usable when no one is in the vehicle. Chip says it is liable for anything that happens while a teleoperator is in control, and that the service will be governed by rules similar to those covering robotaxi remote assistance; the company is working with Florida state and local officials on the launch.

The vehicle uses surround-view cameras and forward radar but no lidar — Detweiler says that's sufficient for the low-speed environments Chip operates in, though he isn't ruling it out later. Longer term, the company wants to license and adapt autonomous-driving technology to eventually reach Level 4 autonomy, an ambitious goal given no consumer vehicle today can drive itself and rivals like Tesla, Rivian and Lucid — at far higher price points — are still working toward it.

"I want this to be the first mass market American robot," Detweiler said. "It's just going to be on four wheels and sitting in your garage or in your driveway, as opposed to, you know, in your kitchen on two legs."
