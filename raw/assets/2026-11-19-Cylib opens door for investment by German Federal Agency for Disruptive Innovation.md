# Cylib opens door for investment by German Federal Agency for Disruptive Innovation

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-11-18

---

SPRIND, the German Federal Agency for Disruptive Innovation, is participating in Aachen-based battery recycling specialist Cylib. The startup, founded in 2022, is close to commercialising its processes. Its hydrometallurgical recycling plant is being built in Dormagen, North Rhine-Westphalia.

SPRIND is investing an undisclosed amount and becomes a shareholder, helping Cylib "to further develop its technology and scale it to industrial maturity". According to Cylib: "Europe has little access of its own to the raw materials that are critical for battery production, while the number of end-of-life EV batteries grows every year."

The process is based on research at RWTH Aachen. While early methods focused on nickel and cobalt, with lithium recovered last or lost in slag, the water-based OLiC process ("Optimised Lithium and Graphite Recovery") recovers lithium in the very first steps from the black mass. Cylib states recovery rates of over 90% for lithium, graphite, nickel, cobalt and manganese.

"Our technology was born in research, and at cylib we have developed it to the threshold of industrialisation," said Dr. Lilian Schwich, co-CEO and co-founder. "With SPRIND, we gain a partner that understands how important technological innovation is for critical value chains."

"Breakthrough innovations don't only happen in the lab. They also happen where a technology makes a new market possible," added Olav Carlsen, Innovation Manager at SPRIND, noting it strengthens German and European sovereignty in critical raw materials.

In a pilot with Porsche and partners, cathode active material made entirely from recycled raw materials from Porsche's own HV batteries was produced for the first time in September; cells made from it are being tested.

Since September 2026, Cylib has been accepting end-of-life EV batteries at its first industrial site in Wernberg-Köblitz, Upper Palatinate, processing them into black mass, with capacity of up to 10,000 tonnes per year. The Dormagen plant is classified as a "Strategic Project" under the EU Critical Raw Materials Act.
