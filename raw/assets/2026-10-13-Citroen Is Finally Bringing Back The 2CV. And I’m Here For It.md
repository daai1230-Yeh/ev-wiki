# Citroen Is Finally Bringing Back The 2CV. And I’m Here For It

**Author**: Andrei Nedelea | **Date**: 2026-10-11

---

The electric concept channels the original’s charm and previews a European-built production car targeting a €15,000 starting price.

- Citroën’s electric 2CV concept previews a European-built production car targeted at around €15,000 by 2028.
- It’s slightly shorter than the new Renault Twingo and aims to undercut its price by roughly €4,500.
- Retro styling meets a one-ton weight target, but battery size, range and charging speeds remain undisclosed.

Renault is proving that nostalgia sells electric cars. Its new 5 and Twingo are getting good reviews and finding plenty of buyers. Citroën has a 2CV-shaped hole in its lineup, and it’s finally addressing it.

This is the Citroën 2CV Concept, set to debut at the 2026 Paris Motor Show. It brings back the familiar shape of the little French icon, swaps combustion for electricity, and previews a production car due by 2028.

Making an expensive car that reminds people of a cheap one is easy enough. But to stay true to the original 2CV, the reborn EV will be very affordable. Citroën wants to build it in Europe and sell it from around €15,000.

It would even undercut the new Renault Twingo E-Tech, which launched with a French starting price of €19,490 before incentives, putting Citroën’s target roughly €4,500 lower. The fine print on the 2CV’s price, including whether it includes subsidies (up to €5,700 in France), is still needed.

The Citroën is 3.76 meters (148 inches) long, just three centimeters shorter than the Twingo, and shorter than the original 2CV; it would fit between the Ami and C3 in its lineup.

Citroën is aiming for a weight of around 1,000 kg (2,205 lb). Keeping the weight down means a smaller battery, which helps keep it affordable. The future production model’s battery capacity, range, charging speeds, and power output haven’t been announced.

The styling leans into what people remember about the old 2CV: separate round headlight pods, pronounced fenders, an upright windshield, and the iconic curved roof. The hood takes inspiration from the TPV, the project that previewed the original car. The concept’s 19-inch steel wheels will likely shrink for production.

Other exterior details include exposed wipers, air inlets below the windshield, slim turn signals integrated into the rear roof pillars, a contrasting black tailgate, and modern LED lights.

Inside, almost the entire dashboard is removed. A cylindrical assembly holds the steering wheel, controls, and a “Smart Vision Display” that projects information into the driver’s field of view, plus a square eight-inch touchscreen and physical controls. Broad seats use Citroën’s Advanced Comfort technology, and the passenger seat extends. There are a folding fabric roof, removable storage baskets, a removable speaker, and fabric door-pull straps. Door pockets are designed to carry eggs, a nod to the original design brief.

To reach this price, Citroën is getting help from the EU: the production model will be designed to meet future European E-Car rules. The European Commission has proposed an M1E category for electric cars up to 4.2 meters long, aiming to freeze regulatory requirements for ten years to give manufacturers a more predictable, less costly route to building affordable EVs.

The reborn 2CV arrives when car prices have skyrocketed; like the original, it aims to give people a way to get around when more expensive cars are out of reach.
