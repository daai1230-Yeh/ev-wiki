# China NEV retail sales fall 13% in first 15 days of November as penetration reaches 73.2%

**Author:** Lei Kang　｜　**Date:** 2026-11-18

**KEY TAKEAWAYS**
- China's NEV retail sales totaled 447,000 units during November 1-15, compared with 611,000 units for total passenger vehicles.
- NEV wholesale volume rose 3% year-on-year to 592,000 units, with penetration at 82%.

China's new energy vehicle (NEV) retail sales continued to decline in the first half of November, reflecting sluggish demand even as penetration rates climbed further.

Retail sales of passenger NEVs totaled 447,000 units from November 1 to 15, down 13% from a year earlier, though up 7% from the same period in October, according to a weekly report released by the China Passenger Car Association (CPCA) on Wednesday.

NEV retail penetration reached 73.2% during November 1-15, remaining above the record monthly high of 67.5% set in full-month October. So far this year, China's cumulative passenger NEV retail sales have totaled 9.32 million units, down 13% year-on-year.

Wholesale volume of passenger NEVs reached 592,000 units during November 1-15, up 3% year-on-year and 18% higher than the same period last month, putting the wholesale penetration rate at 82%.

Year-to-date, cumulative passenger NEV wholesale volume rose 9% from a year earlier to 13.82 million units.

NEVs significantly outperformed the broader passenger vehicle market. Overall passenger car retail sales stood at 611,000 units from November 1 to 15, dropping 25% year-on-year and 6% compared with the same period in October. Cumulative passenger car retail sales so far this year fell 22% year-on-year to 15.59 million units.

In the first week of November, covering November 1-8, average daily retail sales of passenger cars nationwide stood at 36,576 units, a 21% decline from a year earlier, though up 2% from the same period last month.

In the second week of November, from November 9 to 15, average daily retail sales of passenger cars nationwide were 45,457 units, plunging 29% year-on-year and falling 12% from the same period last month.

Domestic retail sales in November have been characterized by policy bottom-support, a weak recovery, and extreme structural divergence, the CPCA said. Despite a concentrated roll-out of promotional perks for the "Double 11" shopping festival, retail performance remained sluggish over the first 2 weeks.

Weak household income expectations have led to a strong wait-and-see sentiment among consumers, the report noted. Meanwhile, persistently high fuel prices have suppressed demand for internal combustion engine vehicles, causing their market share to shrink further.

Market performance going forward will depend on the foot-traffic boost from new model launches at the Guangzhou Auto Show, as well as year-end replacement demand driven by trade-in subsidies set to expire by the end of the year, according to the report.

From November 1 to 15, passenger car manufacturers recorded wholesale volume of 722,000 units, down 23% year-on-year, though up 11% from the same period in October. Cumulative wholesale volume so far this year fell 7% year-on-year to 23.09 million units.

During November 1-8, average daily wholesale volume for passenger carmakers nationwide stood at 41,675 units, down 20% year-on-year, while surging 63% from the same period last month.

From November 8 to 15, average daily wholesale volume was 55,514 units, declining 26% year-on-year and 13% from the same period last month.

Under pressure to meet year-end sales targets, automakers are accelerating the pace of new car launches to prop up wholesale figures, though this essentially amounts to shifting inventory onto dealership channels, the CPCA said.

Dealers are currently facing widespread cash flow constraints and high inventory pressure, and the wide divergence between wholesale and retail figures indicates that wholesale growth does not reflect genuine end-user demand, meaning future wholesale growth will be constrained by weak retail sales, according to the CPCA.
