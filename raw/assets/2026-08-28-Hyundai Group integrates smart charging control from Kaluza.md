# Hyundai Group integrates smart charging control from Kaluza

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 27.08.2026

---

The Hyundai Motor Group has tasked energy platform Kaluza with natively integrating smart charging controls into the Kia app and Hyundai's digital platforms. This will allow EV drivers to charge when power is cheapest and eventually participate in grid stabilising and storage with vehicle-to-grid (V2G) capabilities. The partnership will initially launch in the UK and subsequently expand to Australia and other countries.

Hyundai and Kia are offering smart charging options in collaboration with the British company Kaluza. The grid-supportive smart charging initiative will be launched internationally, starting in the UK. The offer introduces integration of smart charging control into the group's digital ecosystem, primarily within the Kia App and the myHyundai App. These features will simplify smart charging and, in the future, enable vehicle-to-grid applications planned for Hyundai and Kia from 2027 onwards.

The Hyundai Motor Group announced in July this year that it would launch a new umbrella offering called AllDayEnergy, bundling all services related to smart charging, V2G and Vehicle-to-Home (V2H). These intelligent energy services integrate electric vehicles into domestic power grids and public distribution networks. Initially, smart charging will let drivers charge when convenient and when electricity is cheapest, while full bidirectional applications follow in later phases, enabling the EV battery to serve as temporary storage for the public grid (V2G) and home networks (V2H).

The Kaluza energy intelligence integrated into the app allows BEV drivers to control the charging process via their smartphone. Drivers may choose when their vehicle should be ready and can save money by using the cheapest and cleanest energy. Hyundai Motor Group and Kaluza have natively integrated the charging control into the group's digital platforms so drivers can access the features within their familiar app environment.

In the UK, Ovo will be the first energy provider to offer smart charging via the Kia App in an initial phase, enabling Kia drivers who are OVO customers to manage their car and charging in one place. Kia drivers who currently use the Kia App for their vehicle and the OVO Charge App for charging will be able to switch entirely to the Kia App for both by early autumn. The offer will be available to Hyundai drivers shortly after.

Woong Tae Hwang, Vice President and Head of the EV Energy Strategy Group at Hyundai Motor Group, said Kaluza was selected for its depth of expertise in energy intelligence and its ability to work at the frontier of what is technically possible, describing the work as co-developing the standard for how EVs participate in the energy system.

Stephen Fitzpatrick, CEO of Kaluza, added that the partnership is about more than smart charging — that Kaluza and Hyundai Motor Group are building a new industry standard for how electric vehicles integrate with the energy system, starting in the UK and Australia, and that it is a critical first step in building the foundation for vehicle-to-grid services.
