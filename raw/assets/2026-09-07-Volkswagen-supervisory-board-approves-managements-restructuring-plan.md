# Volkswagen supervisory board approves management's restructuring plan

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 04.09.2026

---

Volkswagen's supervisory board unanimously and unexpectedly approved CEO Oliver Blume's "Future Plan" restructuring, two months after an earlier vote reportedly stalled. A decision on spinning off the core VW brand — opposed by Lower Saxony, a 20%-owning shareholder with veto rights — was deferred, which appears to have eased tensions and helped secure the deal. Unions (IG Metall, the works council) called it a de-escalation and said the brand spin-off is off the table, though they want more constructive engagement from management going forward.

The plan acknowledges 500,000 vehicles of production overcapacity in Europe and flags that competitive follow-on production can't currently be guaranteed at four German plants — Zwickau, Emden, Hanover, and Audi's Neckarsulm — from 2031–2034, affecting over 40,000 workers and ~750,000 vehicles/year of capacity; alternative uses for the plants are being explored in parallel rather than confirmed closures. Globally the workforce is to shrink by about 100,000 positions, the model range cut roughly 50% and product complexity by ~75% by 2035, with Volkswagen investing a "three-digit billion-euro sum" to focus on its most attractive, higher-volume models.
