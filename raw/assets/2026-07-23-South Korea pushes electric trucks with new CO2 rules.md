# South Korea pushes electric trucks with new CO2 rules

**Author**: Carla Westerheide | **Date**: 22.07.2026

---

South Korea plans to mandate CO₂ reductions for medium- and heavy-duty commercial vehicles from 2027, encouraging the rollout of battery-electric and hydrogen-powered trucks and buses. The draft rules also extend compliance incentives for electrified passenger cars.

The new requirements will be introduced in three stages between 2027 and 2030. They will first apply to heavy trucks and tractor units above 15 tonnes, before expanding to medium and large buses and, finally, medium-duty trucks and dump trucks. By 2030, manufacturers must reduce average greenhouse gas emissions from these vehicle categories by 30 per cent compared with the 2021-2022 baseline. Companies failing to meet the targets may face financial penalties, although these will be introduced gradually before the regime becomes fully mandatory in 2031.

To encourage the transition to zero-emission commercial vehicles, South Korea will continue to award "super credits" for battery-electric and hydrogen fuel cell trucks and buses. The draft regulation also introduces compliance credits for hydrogen internal combustion engine vehicles.

The government is also tightening emissions standards for passenger vehicles. By 2030, the fleet-average target for passenger cars and vans with up to ten seats would fall from the previously planned 70 g/km to 54 g/km. For light trucks and vehicles with 11 to 15 seats, the limit would decrease from 146 g/km to 98 g/km.

To help manufacturers meet the stricter requirements, South Korea plans to extend its super credit programme for battery-electric, hydrogen and hybrid vehicles until 2029. Automakers would also benefit from longer repayment periods if they miss compliance targets and from a revised manufacturer classification that introduces a new category for medium-sized companies.

Another proposed measure reflects the growing importance of decarbonising battery production and vehicle manufacturing. Under a pilot scheme, manufacturers could offset up to five per cent of their emissions obligations by generating or using renewable electricity at domestic production sites, introducing a lifecycle element into the regulatory framework.

"The automobile greenhouse gas and fuel efficiency management system is a central pillar driving greenhouse gas reduction in the transportation sector," said Kim Jin-sik, Director General of the Air Environment Bureau at the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment. He added that the revised rules are intended to support "the seamless transition to decarbonization in the transportation sector" while enabling South Korea's automotive industry "to secure future competitiveness."

The ministry has opened a public consultation on the draft regulation until 14 September.
