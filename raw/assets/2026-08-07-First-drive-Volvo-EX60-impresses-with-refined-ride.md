# First drive: Volvo EX60 impresses with refined ride

**Author**: Marie-Kristin Wricke | **Date**: 06.08.2026

---

With the EX60, Volvo has added an all-electric counterpart to its successful XC60. On the first drive, the mid-sized SUV impressed with its high level of comfort, generous interior space and exceptionally quiet cabin. However, the heavy reliance on the central touchscreen for many functions is less convincing.

It is just after 8:30 on a grey morning in Hamburg when a member of the Volvo team hands me a smartphone instead of a key. There is no key fob or card: the Volvo EX60 uses a Digital Key. It is the first time the reviewer has started a Volvo test drive without a conventional key.

**Why the EX60 matters for Volvo**: The EX60 fills a gap in Volvo's electric portfolio between compact models (EX30, EX40) and the large EX90, giving the brand a mid-sized electric SUV to match the popular XC60. Measuring 4.80 metres in length, it is the electric counterpart to the combustion/PHEV XC60, which remains on sale alongside it.

**Driving impressions**: The cabin is exceptionally quiet, helped by an optional Bowers & Wilkins sound system with 28 speakers that actively generates anti-noise. The P10 dual-motor powertrain delivers 375 kW (510 PS) with strong but unaggressive acceleration; the suspension smooths rough surfaces and the adjustable steering offers a refined, comfort-oriented driving character rather than sporty drama.

**Areas needing improvement**: Traffic sign recognition and speed limit detection are only moderately reliable. The new Google Gemini voice assistant (making its EX60 debut) gives mixed results — detailed answers on vehicle-specific questions but generic responses to others.

**Interior**: The nearly three-metre wheelbase gives generous rear legroom. Boot capacity is 523 litres (1,647 litres with seats folded), plus a 58-litre front trunk. Distinctive "Wing-Grip" fin-style door handles are easy to use despite an unconventional look. The test car was finished in a semi-dark metallic "Forest Lake" green.

**Controls**: Nearly all functions run through the central touchscreen, including mirrors, headlights and air vents — a design choice the reviewer found excessive, though mirror and steering controls have dedicated shortcut buttons and there is a physical hazard-light button. The compact, flattened two-spoke steering wheel keeps the high-mounted driver display unobstructed but may not suit all preferences. There is no head-up display option.

**Charging and range**: Built on an 800-volt architecture, the EX60 supports DC charging up to 370 kW, with a claimed 10–80% charge in as little as 16 minutes (not tested during this drive). Real-world consumption during the test was 18.2 kWh/100 km (WLTP quoted range: 16.2–19.2 kWh/100 km) under cool, wet conditions.

**Specs (P10 AWD Ultra test car)**: 92 kWh usable battery (95 kWh gross), 375 kW (510 PS) combined output, 0–100 km/h in 4.6 seconds, up to 2,400 kg towing capacity, WLTP combined range up to 613 km (757 km urban), top speed electronically limited to 180 km/h.

**Pricing**: The Volvo EX60 starts at €62,990, undercutting the XC60 plug-in hybrid by €5,000. A more affordable Core trim is expected later, likely bringing the entry price below €60,000. The optioned test car listed at €77,800.

**Conclusion**: The EX60 prioritises composure over spectacle, delivering strong comfort, quiet cabin and long-distance-friendly driving dynamics in an understated package. Its biggest weakness is an over-reliance on the touchscreen for controls that would benefit from physical switches. Overall it ranks among the most well-rounded battery-electric mid-sized SUVs currently available.
