# 'We're Moving Really Fast': Ionna CEO On 500 kW EV Chargers And Flagship New Location

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-11-18

---

- Ionna is planning to roll out new 500-kilowatt stations in the U.S.
- Its newly opened location in California is its largest and fanciest so far.
- The company just proved that you can now drive coast-to-coast in an EV charging only at Ionna stations.

Ionna, the electric vehicle charging consortium backed by major U.S. automakers including General Motors and Toyota, is growing its footprint in the U.S. at an astonishing pace.

In just over two years after the company was founded, it already has 224 operational sites across dozens of states. Now, it's announcing a new flagship location, better access for the growing pool of Tesla drivers, more powerful stations, and discounted charging rates for Jeep, Dodge, and Fiat EV drivers.

## Ionna's Flagship EV Charging Station In California

The company on Wednesday opened its largest and fanciest charging location in the U.S., in Westminster, California. The new Rechargery has 22 stalls and is full of amenities.

It has canopies over the charging bays, outdoor seating, and a dedicated driver lounge. You also get an arcade machine and round the clock access to Wi-Fi, coffee machine, and restrooms.

Seth Cutler, the CEO of Ionna, told InsideEVs in an interview that the company partnered with Amazon to build a 900-square-foot convenience store at the location which features the e-commerce giant's Just Walk Out technology.

"We're moving really fast to build this network up and provide a differentiated experience for customers," Cutler said.

He added that multiple EVs can simultaneously charge at their maximum speeds at this station. Because it is backed by 4 megawatts of grid power, there are practically no power constraints for everyday EV drivers.

"You could have 10 Lucid Gravitys and they can all pull 400 kilowatts," Cutler said. "There's only load management at the charger level."

Each Ionna charging tower can deliver up to 400 kW, and each tower has two cables serving two parking spots. If only one car is plugged in, the charger can dispense the full 400 kW. If two cars are plugged into the same tower, they share that 400 kW. The towers don't draw from each other, so one busy tower never slows down the one next to it.

## 500-Kilowatt Ionna EV Chargers Are Coming

Cutler said groundwork had already started to build 500-kilowatt stations in the U.S. Its current network comprises 400 kW-capable Alpitronic Hyperchargers. He didn't say when and where these new chargers will open, but the probable timeline is 2028 when more EVs will be able to benefit from such speeds.

Only a handful of EVs in the U.S. can currently accept even 400 kW: the Lucid Gravity, Porsche Cayenne Electric, and the BMW iX3. The upcoming Mercedes AMG GT will be able to charge at 600 kW when deliveries start next year. Owners have reported the Tesla Cybertruck pulling 500 kW briefly at newer V4 Superchargers.

## EV Charging Becomes Easier Than Ever

Tesla drivers can now get auto charge at Ionna stations using fleet charging company Presto's app. Plug & Charge is already enabled at Ionna for BMW, General Motors, Toyota, Volvo, Rivian, Ford, Hyundai, Kia, Mercedes-Benz and Porsche.

Starting in December, Ionna is offering a 10% discount for EVs under the Stellantis umbrella (Jeep Wagoneer S, Dodge Charger Daytona, Fiat 500e). With a connected services subscription, they get an additional 15% discount, for 25% in total.

Ionna's network is now dense enough for coast-to-coast road trips using only Ionna stations — demonstrated this week with drivers setting off from New York, Raleigh, Miami and other cities in a journey that culminated in California.
