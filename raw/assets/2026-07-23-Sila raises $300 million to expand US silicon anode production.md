# Sila raises $300 million to expand US silicon anode production

**Author**: Carla Westerheide | **Date**: 22.07.2026

---

US battery materials specialist Sila has secured $300 million in fresh private funding to expand production of its silicon-carbon (Si/C) anode technology. The company plans to accelerate the next phase of its Moses Lake plant, targeting applications including electric vehicles.

The California-based battery developer and Mercedes partner says it will use the capital to scale production of its Titan Silicon anode material in the United States. The funding will also support the second expansion phase of Sila's production site in Moses Lake. The company completed construction of the facility and started operations in autumn 2025. Located on a 160-acre site, the plant currently has an initial production capacity equivalent to 2 GWh of battery materials per year.

Sila plans to expand the site in stages to a capacity of up to 250 GWh over the next five years. If completed as planned, the company says Moses Lake, Washington, would become the world's largest anode production facility. The expansion is also expected to create several hundred additional jobs in Washington State.

The financing round was led by Atreides Management and Sutter Hill Ventures, with participation from existing and new investors including 8VC, Bessemer Venture Partners, Matrix Partners and funds advised by T. Rowe Price Associates.

"This funding allows us to further scale our operations and deliver on the promise of American innovation, strengthen the foundation of our domestic manufacturing capabilities and ensure that critical industries have a secure and reliable supply of advanced batteries," said Gene Berdichevsky, Co-Founder and CEO of Sila.

Sila is a specialist in silicon anode material and plans to commercialise its proprietary product under the name 'Titan Silicon'. The anode is said to be capable of offering 20 per cent higher energy density than today's graphite cells, and is also expected to improve battery charging times. The battery material specialist's first publicly announced automotive customer is Mercedes-Benz: the carmaker plans to use the Californian company's silicon anode chemistry in the future electric G-Class. Panasonic is also among the future customers.

The announcement comes as the United States seeks to strengthen domestic battery supply chains. Sila argues that China's dominant position in anode material processing and battery cell manufacturing creates strategic dependencies for industries including electric vehicles.

"Sila's transition from a groundbreaking material science inventor to a gigascale manufacturing company is a masterclass in American operational discipline," said Vic Miller of Sutter Hill Ventures.
