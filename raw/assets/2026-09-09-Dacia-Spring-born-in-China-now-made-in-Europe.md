# Dacia Spring, born in China, now made in Europe

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-09-08

---

Dacia has unveiled its second Spring generation and is bringing the former China model to Slovenia. There, the A-class electric car will roll off the production line alongside the Twingo E-Tech, which has the same technical platform. The entire range of the Dacia Spring is priced below €20,000, starting at €17,900 ($20,800).

With subdued colours, robust styling, and most accessories available only as extras, Renault's Romanian subsidiary Dacia stays true to its approach with the Spring, offering an entry-level model for electric mobility. This electric car suits buyers who will compromise on extras for affordability or for a second car.

Although the small A-segment car is now moving to the Renault Group's new RG-EV platform and could theoretically match the technical level of the Twingo E-Tech Electric, corporate strategists think in terms of target groups and positioning, so even in its second generation, the Spring remains an EV that demands tolerance from its drivers — with optional DC charging and a base variant without navigation — while attracting price-sensitive customers.

How the Renault Group aims to prevent cannibalisation can be read, quite literally, in the car's colour palette, as recent Renault models are almost all eye-catching and vibrant. The new Dacia Spring, by contrast, offers just four paint options: Agave, Slate Grey, Diamond Black, and Glacier White. It doesn't get much more understated. While the E-Twingo arrives as a hip, playful retro EV, the Spring exudes its charm as an unpretentious budget model with SUV styling.

**Two trims – same technology**

The most important change is that the Spring will now be produced at Renault's Novo Mesto plant in Slovenia, rather than in Wuhan, China, where the first generation was built by its partner, Dongfeng. For the powertrain, Dacia is adopting a one-size-fits-all strategy. While there are two trim levels called 'Expression' and 'Journey', the underlying technology remains identical, based on a front-mounted 60 kW motor paired with a 27.5 kWh LFP battery. Both components are already familiar from the Twingo E-Tech. The LFP battery, featuring cell-to-pack architecture, is said to deliver a WLTP range of up to 250 km—25 km more than before, though slightly less than the electric Twingo, likely due to less favourable aerodynamics.

The base motorisation of the Spring now accelerates from 0 to 100 km/h in 12.5 seconds — compared to nearly 20 seconds at its initial market launch. Not that this matters much in urban driving. The model remains a city car through and through.

What's far more important for daily use is that the new Spring still only supports AC charging as standard. The built-in 6.6 kW onboard charger allows charging from 15 to 80 per cent in 2 hours and 55 minutes. An optional 11 kW AC charger is also available, which includes V2L capability, so the vehicle can recharge other devices or equipment, while camping, for example. But for those venturing on long trips, there's no alternative but to add the DC charging option with a maximum of 50 kW DC — an improvement over the previous Spring's 40 kW, but still modest.

**Dacia Spring grows 18 cm in width**

In terms of dimensions, the Spring now measures 3.85 metres in length and 1.74 metres in width (excluding mirrors) — 15 cm longer and 18 cm wider than its predecessor. Dacia thus moves to the upper end of the A-segment, promising customers significantly improved interior space. The boot capacity increases to up to 337 litres, or 1,283 litres with the rear seats folded down. Additionally, an 18-litre frunk is available as an option under the bonnet.

According to Dacia, the new Spring is the first Dacia model to benefit from the Renault Group's new development programmes, which aim to accelerate the vehicle development cycle, reduce development time to under two years, and simultaneously lower development costs. The Renault Group's dedicated EV research and development centre in Shanghai, named ACDC, contributed the entire engineering for the critical powertrain, working closely with local partners CATL (battery design) and Shanghai e-drive (powertrain).

**Dacia Spring weighs just 1.2 tonnes**

To keep costs in check, numerous decisions were made to simplify the vehicle. The new Spring is available in just one technical configuration and only the four named colours. In the base version, Dacia omits DC charging, navigation, a central display and a rear-view camera. Overall, the stripped-down Spring weighs just over 1.2 tonnes.

The new Spring Expression standard has 15-inch steel wheels, central locking with remote control, electric front windows, and electrically folding mirrors, plus manual air conditioning, automatic headlights, rear parking sensors, and an electric handbrake. The Journey trim adds a rear-view camera, a 10.1-inch central display with wireless Apple CarPlay/Android Auto, navigation, and connected services, and rides on 16-inch steel wheels.

Dacia sets the market launch of the second generation for September 15; the model will be available for order starting then, with first deliveries scheduled shortly before year-end. The Spring has sold around 210,000 units across Europe since its 2021 introduction, including approximately 50,000 in Germany. The entire model range of the new Spring will be priced under €20,000 (excluding government incentives), starting at €17,900. By 2030, Dacia plans to introduce three more fully electric vehicles.
