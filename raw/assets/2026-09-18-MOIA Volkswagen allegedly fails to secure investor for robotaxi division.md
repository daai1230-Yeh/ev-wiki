# MOIA: Volkswagen allegedly fails to secure investor for robotaxi division

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-09-17 14:30

---

Volkswagen is apparently struggling to find investors for its MOIA robotaxi division. The last two parties interested in a joint venture have now reportedly backed out. Questions have arisen as to whether VW can scale the platform on its own — or if it will sell off MOIA entirely.

For over a year, it's been known that Volkswagen has been considering a "major capital increase" for MOIA – as well as the Volkswagen ADMT unit, which oversees the robotaxi programme and the Group's autonomous driving technology – and is seeking at least one external investor for this purpose. (After all, the Group is in cost-cutting mode.) The multi-billion-euro investment required to commercially scale a future-focused area like robotaxis would be difficult to justify internally – something that has become even more true in light of the restructuring plan, which includes the closure of four German plants.

However, as Manager Magazin reports – citing insider sources – Volkswagen CEO Oliver Blume has reportedly failed in the first round of his search for investors. Recently, only US ride-hailing companies Uber and Lyft were reportedly interested in joining MOIA, but they have now pulled out. This is despite the fact that Uber is already an important partner of MOIA and plans to soon deploy thousands of units of the autonomous electric van VW ID. Buzz AD on its ride-hailing platform in the USA.

## Potential investors expected to contribute hundreds of millions

The sought-after investor for MOIA was reportedly expected to contribute "several hundred million euros" before reaching an agreed-upon milestone, after which an additional payment would have been due. Volkswagen would have benefited not only from the investment itself but also from payments for the use of MOIA's robotaxis. However, according to the report, the potential partners were divided on key issues: should payment be per kilometre or per vehicle? Which car model should be used in the first place? (The ID. Buzz AD was apparently not a given.) And finally: how long should the duration of the contract actually be?

According to Manager Magazin, another reason why negotiations with Uber and Lyft reportedly failed was the question of whether Volkswagen itself would have to invest further in MOIA. Allegedly, the new partner would have had to gradually offset the approximately two billion euros that VW has already invested in MOIA before Volkswagen would have been willing to make new investments.

So what happens next with MOIA, which has also been testing autonomous ridepooling trips in Hamburg under the ALIKE project since July? While there is reportedly a positive commercial outlook for the division starting in the 2030s, reaching that point would require at least another billion euros in investment, according to the report. But Volkswagen neither wants to, nor is it able to, bear this cost alone.

## Could Nvidia or Wayve step in?

This has led to a new possibility: the linking of MOIA with Volkswagen's software subsidiary Cariad. After Cariad ended its cooperation with Bosch for automated driving, the company is now looking for new partners. According to Manager Magazin, potential candidates include the British autonomy startup Wayve and the AI giant Nvidia, which could also replace the Israeli autonomy startup Mobileye as a technology supplier for autonomous driving.

Within the Volkswagen Group, there are now apparently considerations to bring such a technology partner for Cariad on board at MOIA as well. This could offer an advantage over the involvement of mobility platforms like Uber or Lyft: a company like Nvidia or Wayve (with Nvidia already an investor in Wayve) would not only be involved in MOIA's robotaxi business but also in Volkswagen's autonomous driving technology for regular passenger cars – and could therefore take a larger stake in MOIA.

If all else fails, a last-resort solution could reportedly even be a complete sale of MOIA to Uber. This would allow VW boss Oliver Blume to save face — whereas the alternative, shutting down MOIA, would be a disgrace and likely result in a billion-euro write-off.
