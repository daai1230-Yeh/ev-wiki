# Tesla raises Cybertruck prices again in the US

**Author**: Carla Westerheide | **Date**: 26.08.2026

---

Tesla has increased prices for two of the three Cybertruck variants in the US. The entry-level AWD model now costs $74,990 – $15,000 more than when the version launched in February.

Tesla has raised the price of the Cybertruck Dual-Motor AWD by $5,000, from $69,990 to $74,990. The increase of 7.1 per cent is the second price hike for the entry-level version since its launch earlier this year.

The Premium AWD also becomes more expensive, rising by $5,000 from $79,990 to $84,990, an increase of around 6.3 per cent. Tesla has left the price of the range-topping Cyberbeast unchanged at $99,990.

The latest adjustment means the gap between the Premium AWD and Cyberbeast has narrowed to $15,000. It also puts the current entry price of the electric pickup 25 per cent above the introductory price Tesla offered in February.

Tesla introduced the Dual-Motor AWD in February at $59,990, lowering the Cybertruck's entry price by $20,000 compared with the previously cheapest configuration. The existing $79,990 AWD version was renamed Premium All-Wheel Drive at the same time. Tesla also reduced the Cyberbeast from $114,990 to $99,990.

However, the $59,990 price was only available for a limited period. Tesla CEO Elon Musk said at launch that the offer would run for ten days and that subsequent pricing would depend on demand. During that period, delivery estimates moved from June 2026 to September and October and, for some configurations, into 2027.

At the beginning of March, Tesla raised the price of the Dual-Motor AWD by $10,000 to $69,990 and removed the leasing option. The latest increase therefore takes the price $15,000 above its February launch level within just over six months.

The Dual-Motor AWD has an estimated range of 325 miles (approximately 523 kilometres) and features adaptive damping suspension, a powered tonneau cover and bed-mounted power outlets with Powershare vehicle-to-everything functionality. Compared with the Premium AWD, it has a lower towing capacity of 7,500 pounds (around 3.4 tonnes), versus 11,000 pounds (around 5 tonnes).

The price changes come as Cybertruck sales have declined in the US. According to Cox Automotive figures, Tesla sold 3,519 units in the first quarter of 2026, down 45.1 per cent year on year and 15 per cent from the previous quarter. Cox Automotive put US Cybertruck sales at 20,200 units for the whole of 2025, compared with 39,000 in 2024. Tesla does not report Cybertruck deliveries separately.
