# New York Says 'Enough Is Enough' To In-Car Subscriptions

**Author**: Rob Stumpf | **Date**: Nov 24, 2026

---

If the hardware is already installed in your car, a new law in New York says that it (probably) shouldn’t be a subscription.

- A new law prohibits automakers, dealers, and third parties from charging ongoing fees for hardware already built into the car.
- Heated seats and key fob remote start are perfect examples, but premium connectivity and self-driving might not get the same treatment.

Governor Kathy Hochul signed into law a bill prohibiting recurring fees for hardware already installed in the car at the time of purchase, so long as those features don’t create an ongoing cost for the manufacturer or a provider. The state’s announcement says the law guards against car companies charging fees to activate built-in features like heated seats, enhanced navigation, hands-free driving and entertainment options that do not present an ongoing expense for dealers and manufacturers.

Excluded from protection: driver assistance and automation features, connected cellular services, in-car hotspot, satellite radio, vehicle telematics, roadside assistance, navigation updates, and infotainment features. That means Tesla can continue charging for Full Self-Driving, though it calls into question features like Acceleration Boost, a $2,000 software-based performance upgrade with no extra ongoing costs.

The law draws a hard line between paying extra for something that never costs automakers anything to maintain, and a subscription for something that actually costs money to provide (internet-connected streaming, actively updated driver assistance). If a feature just needs a relay flipped on to work completely offline, the manufacturer should work it into the cost.
