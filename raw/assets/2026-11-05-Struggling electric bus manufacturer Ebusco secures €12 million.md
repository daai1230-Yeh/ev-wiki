# Struggling electric bus manufacturer Ebusco secures €12 million

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-11-04

---

Dutch electric bus manufacturer Ebusco is receiving a capital injection of a total of twelve million euros from five existing investors. The sum is intended to finance operations until the second quarter of 2027. Shortly before, Ebusco had to postpone the payment of October salaries due to its strained liquidity situation.

Two strategic investors and three other existing shareholders have committed to providing €12 million for new shares, subject to approval by the general meeting. The new shares are to be issued at five cents each, totalling 240 million new shares. Of the total, €8 million will come from the strategic investors Rhein Holding and Green Innovation International, and €4 million from existing shareholders De Engh, CVI Investments (Heights), and Peter Bijvelds Holding. Rhein Holding had already injected €6 million in August.

## Payout planned in six tranches

The capital is to be disbursed in six tranches aligned with liquidity needs; the final €1 million is scheduled for February 2027. Ebusco aims to finance operating costs and complete existing bus orders. A particular focus is the order from Rouen, which has been waiting for 68 units of the Ebusco 3.0. To complete it, Ebusco announced in October a separate production support package worth €5.86 million from a subsidiary of Gotion, a strategic investor since November 2024.

Bus production, largely outsourced to China, is progressing very slowly: in Q3 2026 Ebusco delivered just three buses, and only 19 since the start of the year. As of 30 September, the order backlog stood at 195 buses, of which only 77 were firm and 118 cancellable.

Less than half of the new capital will benefit operations: part will repay a €7.1 million bridging loan from April 2026, which matures on 1 December 2026.

## Funds expected to last until Q2 2027

This assumes all committed funds arrive in full and on time; otherwise additional capital may be required. Ebusco continues to work on securing around €30 million in working capital financing, which is not yet guaranteed.

## Salaries had to be deferred

Ebusco announced on Tuesday that it had to defer October salaries. It has not specified when the funds will arrive or when outstanding salaries can be paid. The measure still requires approval from an extraordinary general meeting before the end of 2026. For Q1 2027 the company also plans a repair offer of up to €5 million at the same five-cent price.

## Restructuring remains challenging

Ebusco has faced major financial and operational problems since 2024, including production stoppages, delays and order cancellations (for example 23 units by Potsdam's transport operator). It now has vehicles largely assembled by contract manufacturers and has significantly reduced its workforce.
