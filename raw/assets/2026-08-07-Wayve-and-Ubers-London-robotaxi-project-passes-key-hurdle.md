# Wayve and Uber's London robotaxi project passes key hurdle

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 06.08.2026

---

Uber and Wayve have cleared a major hurdle for launching a robotaxi service in London: Transport for London (TfL) has issued Private Hire Vehicle licences for a fleet of fully electric Ford Mustang Mach-E models modified by Wayve for autonomous driving, equipped with Wayve's AI Driver plus surround-view cameras and radar, following TfL compliance inspections. Other approvals are still pending.

The licensing meets the UK's "triple lock" requirement for private hire journeys — operator, driver, and vehicle must all hold licences from the same regulatory authority. Journeys will run under the UK's AV Trialling Code of Practice and Uber's TfL licence.

**Safety driver still required**: Although Wayve's vehicles are designed to drive autonomously, a trained, TfL-licensed safety driver must remain on board for now to monitor trips and take control if necessary.

**Demand**: Over 100,000 people have joined Uber's London waiting list in the past eight weeks; selected users are expected to take initial test rides this summer ahead of public launch.

Sarah Gates (VP Global Affairs Assurance, Wayve): "This licence is an important step towards giving Londoners the chance to experience autonomous driving technology... The responsible deployment of these vehicles will bring us safer, cleaner and quieter streets."

Annie Duvnjak (Global Head of Autonomous Mobility Operations, Uber): "This licence is a key milestone in bringing autonomous rides to London on Uber... Our interest list has seen an incredible response from Londoners who are excited to experience Wayve's British-built autonomous driving technology."

**What's left**: Full driverless robotaxi operation in London still requires additional regulatory approvals under UK testing and approval laws and local legislation, including comprehensive safety demonstrations and an operating licence — the UK only opened its application process for robotaxi pilot operating licences in June.

In February, Uber joined a billion-pound funding round for Wayve and announced plans to launch robotaxi service together in 10+ global markets including London; in Tokyo, the two have also brought on Nissan as a partner for a similar project.
