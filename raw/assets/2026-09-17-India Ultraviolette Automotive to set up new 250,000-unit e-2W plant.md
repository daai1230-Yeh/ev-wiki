# India: Ultraviolette Automotive to set up new 250,000-unit e-2W plant

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 2026-09-16 17:00

---

Indian electric two-wheeler startup Ultraviolette Automotive is planning to build a factory with annual capacity of 250k units. It aims to expand from premium models into lower-priced, high-volume segments.

Ultraviolette will build the new plant in Hosur, a city in the southern state of Tamil Nadu. The facility will be located near manufacturing plants of some of India's leading electric two-wheeler makers, including TVS Motor Company and Ather Energy, which should give the company access to an established supplier ecosystem.

Dubbed 'BIGGA', short for 'Big Global Ambition', the new factory will entail an investment of 7.79 billion rupees (~€70 million), the Tamil Nadu government stated in a recent media announcement. Ultraviolette plans to reach the 250,000-unit capacity in a phased manner. The company says it will design the infrastructure such that it can double the annual production capacity to 500,000 units in the future, as demand grows.

The Tesseract, Ultraviolette's first scooter, will be among the first models to roll out of the new plant. This will be a major launch for the company, as most electric two-wheelers sold in India are scooters. In 2025, scooters accounted for 88.6 per cent of the country's 1.2 million electric two-wheeler sales, as per a report by IMARC Group.

Ultraviolette also plans to invest 2 billion rupees (~€18 million) to expand production capacity at its existing plant in Bengaluru, Karnataka. Located about 50 kilometres northwest of the planned facility in Hosur, the Bengaluru plant can produce up to 30,000 vehicles annually, and the company plans to increase that figure to 50,000 units with this investment.

Backed by companies like TVS Motor Company, Qualcomm Ventures, and TDK Ventures, Ultraviolette currently offers two electric motorcycles in India: X-47 and F77. The company sells the F77 even in Europe, where it is present in 19 countries. It is working on bringing the X-47, as well as the Tesseract and an enduro motorcycle called Shockwave, to European markets.
