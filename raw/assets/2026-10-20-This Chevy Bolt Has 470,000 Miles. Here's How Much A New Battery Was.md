# This Chevy Bolt Has 470,000 Miles. Here's How Much A New Battery Was

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-10-19

---

*At the end of the day, it was probably cheaper than replacing the engine in a combustion car.*

- A 2019 Chevrolet Bolt EV needed a new battery pack after covering nearly half a million miles.
- The replacement battery was sourced from a used car, but it had very low mileage.
- At the right shop, the procedure takes less than a day and costs less than you'd expect.

The original Chevrolet Bolt EV was a fan favorite from the start, but it suffered from an infamous battery recall. Still, it has devoted owners who wouldn't trade it in, even after a ridiculous number of miles.

A 2019 example came into Out of Spec Renew's shop with 471,000 miles on the odometer. The car had its battery replaced under GM's recall in 2022 at roughly 138,000 miles, so the pack that gave up survived over 330,000 miles. A few cell imbalances in two modules prevented the car from going into Drive. The replacement pack was sourced from a used car with an estimated 15,000 miles.

After disconnecting the 12-volt battery and the high-voltage disconnect plug, the car went on the lift, where roughly a dozen bolts, a few electrical connections and two coolant hoses were removed. The old pack came out, the replacement went in, and the cooling system was purged. Programming the replacement battery seemed to take longer than the swap itself; the procedure took roughly four and a half hours of work.

The replacement pack cost about $1,800 (what the shop paid). For the average owner, a good replacement pack should cost around $2,500 to $3,000, plus $800 to $1,000 for labor. Considering the previous battery lasted over 300,000 miles, that is not bad; an engine replacement on a gas car can cost anywhere from $2,000 to $10,000.
