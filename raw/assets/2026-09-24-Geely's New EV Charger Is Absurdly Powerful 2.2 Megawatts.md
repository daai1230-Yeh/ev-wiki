# Geely's New EV Charger Is Absurdly Powerful: 2.2 Megawatts

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-09-23

---

*BYD stunned the industry with its Flash chargers. Geely's 2.25 megawatt system leaves it, and everyone else, behind.*

- The newest Lynk & Co 10 and Zeekr 001 versions can charge from 10-70% in 4 minutes and 30 seconds.
- That's thanks to Geely Auto Group's latest Smart Charging System which can deliver 2,250 kilowatts of peak charging speeds.
- The automaker says it is using AI to protect the battery's long-term health and performance.

Imagine plugging in your electric car at a public fast-charging station and getting back on the road after just four minutes. That's now a reality in China, where Geely Auto Group has revealed a charging system that appears to be the world's fastest.

The automaker revealed its fifth-generation Smart Charging System and a new Ultra Short Blade Battery on Wednesday. The new charger can deliver 2.25 megawatts of peak charging speeds. This one-ups rivals like BYD, which made a big splash last year by rolling out its 1.5 MW Flash chargers. Geely and BYD have been locked in a battle over fast-charging speeds.

Supported by a new battery system, EVs from Geely brands Lynk & Co and Zeekr can charge in the real world from 10-70% in just 4 minutes and 30 seconds, Geely claimed. A 10-97% charge will take 8 minutes and 40 seconds. BYD's Flash chargers can do 10-70% in about five minutes and 10-97% in 9 minutes.

Pumping electrons into a battery at such speeds can be detrimental to the cells if not properly managed. The automaker said it was using an AI-powered system called Xingrui PowerMind to predict battery temperatures and adjust speeds dynamically. It's designed to keep the battery's average temperature at 55°C, with a ceiling of 65°C.

There's also a thermal management system Geely calls "end-to-end five point liquid cooling." It manages heat in a coordinated manner between the battery pack, charging port, charging cable, charging dispenser, and the energy storage batteries supporting the chargers.

Geely also said it had developed a "lithium-ion pulse restoration" technology, which uses micro pulse currents to reactivate lithium ions accumulated at the anode during repeated fast-charging. This targets the lithium plating that can occur during ultra-fast charging, which can degrade the battery. In theory, these micro pulses could dislodge stuck ions and get them moving again.

For reference, some of the fastest-charging EVs currently on the U.S. market are the Lucid Gravity and Porsche Cayenne Electric, both with a maximum charging rate of 400 kW. When the Mercedes-AMG GT 4-Door Coupe becomes available, it will take the crown among western automakers with 600 kW peak and a 10-80% time of just 11 minutes.

But they're nowhere close to what's now on sale in China. (Geely didn't say what peak charging rate its cars can actually reach.)

Where BYD might still have an edge is the scale of deployment. As of August, BYD had already deployed 10,000 of its T-shaped Flash chargers in China and plans to have 20,000 by the end of the year, plus 3,000 in Europe. Whether Geely can match this scale remains to be seen.
