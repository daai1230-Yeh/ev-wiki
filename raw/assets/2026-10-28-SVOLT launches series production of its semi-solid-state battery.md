# SVOLT launches series production of its semi-solid-state battery

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-10-27

---

Chinese battery manufacturer SVOLT has begun series production of semi-solid-state batteries. The first batch of 100 kWh battery packs has rolled off the production line in Changzhou, Jiangsu Province, and is said to support 6C fast charging.

Chairman Yang Hongxin announced the start in a Weibo video. SVOLT produced the first batch of its 'Dragon Armor' battery using series production methods; the 100 kWh pack is being considered by "multiple" customers, with vehicle details to follow. Semi-solid-state batteries sit between conventional lithium-ion cells with liquid electrolytes and solid-state cells, using a solid-liquid hybrid electrolyte that manufacturers hope will deliver better safety, energy density and lifespan.

The pack features liquid cooling on top and bottom and supports up to 900 V. At 6C, the datasheet says around 500 km of range can be recharged in twelve minutes, with a lifespan above 3,000 cycles; test conditions and the scope of the first batch remain unstated.

SVOLT is currently China's tenth-largest battery maker. Yang says 2026 will be the first year hybrid solid-liquid batteries gain broad acceptance. In mid-2025 he announced test production for Q4 2025 and series production for 2027; production has now started this autumn. First-generation cells were stated at 300 Wh/kg, with a second generation aiming for 360 Wh/kg.

An earlier CN EV Post report said SVOLT will supply the batteries for next-generation Mini models; SVOLT was spun off from Great Wall Motor in 2018, which builds the Mini Cooper Electric and Aceman with BMW. Mini has not officially been confirmed as a customer.

SVOLT is also reportedly developing pure solid-state batteries, but Yang said in May that commercialisation will take a very long time. He expects semi-solid-state to be a common path until then, with costs on par with conventional lithium-ion.
