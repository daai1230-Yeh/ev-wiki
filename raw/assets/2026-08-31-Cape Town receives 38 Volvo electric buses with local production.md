# Cape Town receives 38 Volvo electric buses with local production

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-08-29

---

Volvo Buses has announced the delivery of 38 BZR Low Entry electric buses in Cape Town, marking the introduction of the first electric buses with locally manufactured bodies in South Africa. The bodies are produced on-site by the Gauteng Bus and Coach Centre in Johannesburg.

New battery-electric buses to be deployed in Cape Town's electric public transport system are scheduled for delivery from 2027. The public transport operator in the South African capital has chosen the Volvo BZR LE Electric model, based on the BZR platform, which was introduced by Volvo Buses in March 2024. The bodywork is being supplied by South African manufacturer Gauteng Bus and Coach Centre in Johannesburg.

The partners have not disclosed performance specifications, but according to Volvo's website, the Volvo BZR Low Entry Electric is available as a two- or three-axle vehicle with a length of 9.8 to 14.8 metres. The powertrain delivers 200 or 400 kW, and the battery capacity reaches up to 630 kWh nominally. The "LE" in the model name stands for low-entry buses, which feature easy access and improved accessibility.

Regarding the 38 units ordered, Volvo states only that the installed energy capacity is tailored to Cape Town's operational requirements. Transitioning from diesel to electric buses represents a significant shift for the city. The bus manufacturer has said that this requires careful planning, coordination and close collaboration between numerous stakeholders. Volvo Buses is acting as a strategic advisor throughout the process. The charging infrastructure for the electric buses will be managed by the City of Cape Town, while the bus bodywork will be developed and built by Gauteng Bus and Coach Centre.

## Cape Town prepares for the arrival of electric buses

Rob Quintas, City Councillor and responsible for urban mobility in Cape Town, says, "We have ordered a minimum of 38 buses, but this number may increase with funding availability. The new buses will be paid for by means of Public Transport National Grant funding."

"This is a landmark project for both Cape Town and for us. We can be very proud to introduce electric buses to the city, and in doing so, prove that we acknowledge and support a sustainable future," adds Leon Nelson, Director of Volvo Buses in South Africa.

The BZR Electric platform was unveiled by Volvo Buses in March 2024 as the result of a shared e-mobility architecture within the Volvo Group. Three years earlier, in September 2021, Volvo had introduced an electric chassis called the BZL Electric (note the "L" instead of "R"), designed for solo and double-decker buses. Beyond the similar name and powertrain output (200 and 400 kW), the two platforms have little in common.

The BZR Electric is a 600-volt platform and, in its base configuration, supports vehicles with lengths ranging from 9.5 to 14.9 metres. At the heart of the architecture is an electric motor coupled with a two-stage automated transmission, delivering 200 kW or, in a dual-motor configuration, 400 kW. Even during the platform's unveiling, the company from Sweden highlighted the "outstanding versatility" of the new product. It was described as particularly "future-proof," with the design enabling versions for Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) as well as later use in coach applications, as the manufacturer anticipated at the time.

## BZR also serves as the basis for Volvo's e-coach chassis

As the first representative of the new platform, Volvo Buses directly unveiled its first electric intercity bus, the 8900 Electric. Since last year, it has been clear that Volvo Buses are also using the platform in a modified form to develop an even longer-range bus for the coach segment. Instead of the previously supported 540 kWh, the coach chassis outlined a year ago supports up to 720 kWh in energy content. While the earlier version accommodated six battery packs of 90 kWh each, the new product can house eight battery units. In the case of the aforementioned low-entry variant, the BZR LE, up to seven packs are used, totalling up to 630 kWh.

Incidentally, Volvo Buses began production of its first electric chassis for extra-long electric buses last year. The so-called BZRT chassis serves as the basis for both articulated and double-articulated buses with lengths of up to 28 metres. Here, too, the same 720 kWh total capacity is used, distributed across eight battery packs and the same powertrain options (200 or 400 kW). The drivetrains of Volvo's extra-long city buses and coaches are therefore similar.
