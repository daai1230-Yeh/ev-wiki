# Some Owners Say Tesla's New FSD Update Is Causing Their Cars' Computers To Overheat

**Author**: Rob Stumpf | **Date**: Aug 5, 2026

---

Some Tesla owners running FSD V14 Lite claim the update is causing issues.

Some Tesla owners are reporting that their Autopilot computers — the hardware responsible for running Tesla's Full Self-Driving (Supervised) software — are having issues. Specifically, owners of cars with Tesla's last-gen Hardware 3 computers are reporting temporary failures that render FSD unusable for a period of time, while others say the boards are failing outright. No official cause has been confirmed, but some owners point to Tesla's newest FSD V14 Lite update.

Some owners on X report that their cars' computers have reached as high as 102 degrees Celsius (215 degrees Fahrenheit) since updating to FSD V14 Lite — about double the idle temperature of the car computer. For reference, even Nvidia's flagship H100 datacenter GPU has a maximum temperature limit of 87 degrees Celsius.

Millions of Teslas built between 2019 and 2023 with HW3 don't have the compute horsepower to run the full version of FSD's latest release, so Tesla released a distilled version called V14 Lite. HW3 has around an eighth of the memory bandwidth compared to Tesla's current HW4 and half the memory capacity — and Tesla is planning to double the memory in its refreshed HW4 "plus" hardware, meaning HW3 owners will have one-fourth of the available memory compared to modern Teslas. That's why HW3 "simply does not have the capability" for unsupervised self-driving, according to CEO Elon Musk.

Tesla's system is set to force a disengagement of FSD when the computer exceeds 90 degrees, keeping the software locked out until the computer cools. Some owners who checked service mode found an overheating problem; others reported FSD becoming unavailable since upgrading to V14 Lite without noting temperature specifically. A few reported needing a new Autopilot computer after the update — one shared a story of a family member experiencing an FSD failure during a road trip, after which a Tesla Service center said they needed a new Autopilot computer.

Right now these are anecdotes, and it's unclear how widespread the issue is or whether it's directly tied to V14 Lite. Tesla sold between 3 and 4 million HW3-equipped cars between 2019 and 2023, and began rolling out the latest update around two weeks before this report. Tesla didn't respond to a request for comment.

Musk has acknowledged that Tesla will have to build "micro factories" to upgrade customers' HW3 cars. Tesla promised in 2016 that all its cars had the hardware necessary for self-driving, and has since deleted the blog post making that claim, moving from HW2.5 to HW3 to HW4, with HW4.5 and beyond planned to address increased compute needs for unsupervised autonomy. "Unfortunately, HW3 simply does not have the capability to achieve unsupervised FSD. We did think at one point it would have that," Musk said during Tesla's Q1 earnings call earlier this year.
