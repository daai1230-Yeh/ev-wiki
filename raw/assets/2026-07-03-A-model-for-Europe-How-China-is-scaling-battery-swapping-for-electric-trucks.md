# A model for Europe? How China is scaling battery swapping for electric trucks

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-07-02 12:30

---

A battery-swapping station for electric trucks is operating in Chizhou, China, using German technology from Bosch MC Battery Service Innovations — a joint venture founded by Bosch and Mitsubishi Corporation in 2025. In this interview, Managing Director Klaus Sekot explains the business case, market dynamics and prospects for Europe. He argues Europe will eventually follow China's lead, noting "five to ten minutes for a battery swap compared to 30 to 45 minutes of charging time with megawatt charging — this is a huge advantage for long-haul heavy-duty trucks."

Sekot describes Battery-as-a-Service (BaaS) as a model where a third party owns the battery and provides it to fleets as a service; the joint venture's "Battery in the Cloud" technology determines each battery's state of health so financiers can underwrite it. In the Chizhou project, the venture provides intelligent AI-driven charging management, digital-twin asset monitoring, data for insurance and maintenance, and a booking/payment marketplace app for swap stations. Sekot said nearly 30,000 swappable heavy-duty electric trucks were registered in China in 2024, with one dealer telling him 80 per cent of the trucks she sells are electric and more than half of those are swap-capable; three parallel swapping standards currently operate in China, with four truck brands supporting the Chizhou standard.

He attributes China's lead to deliberate industrial policy — pilot cities, coordinated standards and infrastructure support — versus Europe's AFIR framework, which focuses mainly on cable charging (DC and Megawatt Charging System) and gives battery swapping little attention; a new Fraunhofer Institute white paper warns that without its own standards, Europe risks defaulting to Chinese ones. Sekot sees three stages ahead: market penetration in China, expansion into receptive markets like Japan and Europe, and eventual integration of swap stations into smart energy systems as decentralised, potentially bidirectional grid storage. The joint venture's target markets are China, the EU, Japan, the USA and India, with China as "our laboratory" and Europe as "our home market."
