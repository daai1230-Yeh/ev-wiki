# Ubitricity & Deftpower bring smart charging to 1,500 public charging points

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 27.08.2026

---

Ubitricity and Deftpower are expanding their collaboration on smart charging in the Netherlands. At 1,500 public charging points, charging processes are to be deliberately shifted to times of lower grid load using the open OCPI 2.2.1 standard.

According to the companies, Ubitricity is the world's first charge point operator to deploy this functionality at such a scale. Technically this marks a significant shift: until now, smart charging in public spaces has often relied on a direct API connection between the vehicle and the Mobility Service Provider (MSP). The current approach instead uses Charging Profiles, which allow the MSP to send a charging schedule tailored to the driver's preferences directly to the charging point via OCPI. The profile is then implemented on-site, while the Charge Point Operator retains control over the charging processes of its infrastructure. A direct interface with the vehicle is therefore unnecessary, which in principle allows more electric cars to participate in managed charging.

For drivers, this flexibility also offers financial benefits. Through their respective apps they can receive cashback if they reschedule charging sessions to avoid peak grid loads. According to Deftpower, 65 per cent of users who opt for smart charging enable the feature by default. Deftpower CEO Jacob van Zonneveld said that with OCPI 2.2.1 Charging Profiles the companies have unlocked a new level of scale for public smart charging.

Incentive-based smart charging is not entirely new in the Netherlands. Deftpower has already collaborated with Allego on a similar solution: since April, drivers have been able to shift charging sessions from evening peak times to overnight at initially 4,500 AC charging points and receive cashback in return. Allego plans to expand this to around 9,000 charging points across the Netherlands.

What is new is not the principle of time-shifted charging itself, but its implementation via the open OCPI 2.2.1 standard. Control is no longer limited to vehicles with compatible APIs. However, widespread adoption requires further CPOs and Mobility Service Providers to support the corresponding Charging Profiles.

This approach is particularly relevant in the Netherlands, where the power grid in some regions is already reaching capacity limits and new connections for charging infrastructure are sometimes difficult to realise. Shifting charging sessions to off-peak times can reduce peak loads and utilise existing grid capacities more efficiently. While this does not eliminate the need for grid expansion, it can help integrate a growing number of electric vehicles into the existing infrastructure.
