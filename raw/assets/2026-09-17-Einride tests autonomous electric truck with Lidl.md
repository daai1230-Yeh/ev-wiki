# Einride tests autonomous electric truck with Lidl

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-09-16 14:30

---

Einride, a Swedish provider of electric freight transport, is testing its self-developed autonomous electric truck on public roads in Hesse. The driverless vehicle transports goods for longstanding customer Lidl from a logistics center to a nearby store - and does so without a safety driver on board.

Lidl and Einride have now launched a joint pilot project in Edermünde, Hesse, near Kassel. From now on, an autonomous electric truck without a driver's cab, operating at SAE Level 4, will be in service there with official approval from the German Federal Motor Transport Authority (KBA). It transports goods from the local administrative and distribution centre in Edermünde directly to a nearby Lidl store. According to the publicly accessible list of issued trial approvals from the KBA, the permit was granted on 14 August 2026. It is initially valid until at least 28 February 2027.

The prototype, most recently referred to by Einride as the eBot and previously introduced as the Gen 2 Rigid Large, has a loading capacity of 15 Euro pallets, according to Lidl. However, the exact specifications of the solo truck are not known. In the pilot project, the autonomous electric truck is permitted to travel at a maximum speed of 25 km/h – though it is likely capable of much higher speeds.

## A 'purpose-built' autonomous electric truck

Since the vehicle is completely 'purpose-built' and lacks a steering wheel, accelerator, and brake pedal, there is no safety driver on board to intervene in emergencies. However, a technical supervision system is likely required, which can take remote control in critical situations.

"Einride is already running real autonomous deliveries, with real volumes on real schedules with customers in the U.S and Europe. Germany is one of Einride's key markets and we're excited to now bring this technology to public roads in Europe's largest road transport market, and to prove its value together with Lidl and the broader ecosystem of Schwarz Group," said Roozbeh Charli, CEO of Einride. "Germany's approval process is among the toughest in the world, and obtaining clearance there gives us a foundation to scale with confidence."

Thomas Dangelmayer, Head of Operational Logistics at Lidl in Germany, added: "Intelligence creates efficiency, and that is the backbone for permanently reliable product availability. We ensure that the products for our customers are always at the right place, at the right time, and in the right quality. With this innovative project, we are testing a technological milestone step by step and safely in real-world operations to examine how future-proof solutions can be optimally integrated into our site logistics."

For the companies of the Schwarz Group – which includes Lidl, Kaufland, and the environmental service provider PreZero – trucks with autonomous driving functions are not an end in themselves nor the sole answer to the challenges of logistics, according to the group. However, they can be an important building block for transforming the industry. After all, robust logistics are the foundation for reliable goods supply, and highly automated transport routes can help stabilise goods flows, increase operational efficiency, and reduce dependencies on external conditions.

## Einride now listed on the stock exchange

Einride only went public on the US tech exchange Nasdaq in June via a SPAC merger and saw a strong increase at the start of trading. Founded ten years ago, the Swedish company is working towards its vision of driverless goods transport but has already been offering a platform for transport planning and fleet management of electric trucks with human drivers for some time.

The German food discounter Lidl and Einride have been working together for a long time: as early as 2017, Lidl tested Einride's first autonomous prototype, the T-Pod, in Sweden. Since 2020, the partners have been regularly collaborating on electric logistics in Sweden. In the future, Lidl aims to increase the transport capacity in the partnership to 832,000 kilometres per year; a 76 per cent increase.
