# Australia to invest AU$40.5 million in Australia Post fleet electrification

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 22.07.2026

---

Australia has announced plans to invest AU$40.5 million (approx. €24.8 million) in electrifying Australia Post's delivery fleet. The funding forms part of the country's 2026/27 budget.

Most of Australia Post's EV fleet comprises smaller vehicles such as two- and three-wheelers. The new funding will help the company adopt larger EVs such as vans, trucks and prime movers on a large scale. Australia sees the transition as a step towards reducing reliance on diesel and limiting the impact of external disruptions such as fuel shortages on delivery operations.

In a press conference following the announcement, Australia Post's Executive General Manager for Network Operations, Shane Plant, shared additional details about the plan. He said that the new investment will probably add more than 100 EVs to the delivery fleet. This expansion will help Australia Post reduce its diesel consumption by approximately one million litres per year, equivalent to 2.3 per cent of its annual fuel consumption.

Speaking further, Plant said that part of the new investment will go into expanding charging infrastructure over the next two years. He suggested that the new charging stations will feature solar panels, bringing a genuine reduction in emissions. Commenting on the new funding from the government, Australia Post Group CEO and Managing Director Paul Graham said, "Australia Post has been at the forefront of electric vehicle adoption for over a decade, and this investment allows us to go further faster."

In financial year 2025 (2024-25), Australia Post deployed 426 EVs, including 251 electric three-wheelers and 175 UBCO electric motorcycles. In financial year 2026 (2025-26), it put 36 Mercedes eVito vans and one Mercedes eActros electric truck into service, expanding its electrification drive to larger EVs. It also began rolling out 500 Gaius Rapide 3 three-wheeled electric motorcycles. Later, it expanded the UBCO electric motorcycle fleet to 1,000 units and ordered a further 1,500, according to the NZ Herald.

Currently, Australia Post has more than 5,100 EVs in its fleet. These EVs collectively travel 29 million kilometres annually, Australia's Minister for Communications, Anika Wells, said during the press conference.
