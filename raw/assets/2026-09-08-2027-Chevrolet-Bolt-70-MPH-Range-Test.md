# 2027 Chevrolet Bolt 70-MPH Range Test: Here's How Far America's Cheapest EV Goes

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-09-08

*The new Bolt starts at less than $30,000 and promises over 250 miles of range on a full charge.*

---

InsideEVs contributor and State of Charge YouTube host Tom Moloughney range-tested the 2027 Chevrolet Bolt at a constant 70 mph, and it did better than expected.

Despite looking similar, the new Chevrolet Bolt is significantly better than its predecessor. It packs more range, faster charging, a lot more tech inside, and a starting price of less than $30,000 — making it the cheapest new EV in the United States right now.

On paper, the 2027 Bolt is rated for 262 miles on a full charge on the combined cycle, while the EPA highway range rating is 232 miles. GM's entry-level EV topped that highway result in the real world, even though the EPA highway procedure doesn't even exceed 60 mph.

The tester was a base Bolt LT with 17-inch wheels and the same 65-kilowatt-hour lithium-iron-phosphate (LFP) battery pack available on all trims. Tires were set to the manufacturer's recommended pressure, ambient temperature was between 71°F and 80°F, and climate control was set to the most efficient mode. As always, the 70 mph run was done on the New Jersey Turnpike, where Tom does most of his highway range tests due to light early-morning traffic and abundant charging stations.

The 2027 Chevrolet Bolt drove 243 miles on a full charge and still had an estimated 1% and 2 miles of remaining range, according to the car's instrument cluster — 11 miles more than the EPA highway score and 19 miles less than the EPA combined figure. Tom reckoned he could probably squeeze a couple more miles out of the battery but preferred to keep it safe, as the Bolt had already limited power output. Average energy efficiency was 3.6 miles/kWh, with total energy used of 66 kWh — just a smidge over the stated usable capacity of 65 kWh.

Speed has a big impact on the 2027 Bolt's range: earlier this year, the YouTube channel EV Motoring did a 75 mph range test with a similar car under similar weather, and the 5 mph difference resulted in an average efficiency of just 2.8 mi/kWh and a real-world range of only 197 miles.

Still, with a starting price under $30,000, decent real-world range, and usable charging speeds, the new Bolt should make for a great commuter — though GM isn't expected to keep it around for long.
