# Mexico unveils Olinia Carga electric light truck

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 25.08.2026

---

Mexico is expanding its fledgling electric vehicle brand Olinia with a compact electric transporter. The Olinia Carga will offer a range of more than 120 kilometres and a payload of up to 650 kilogrammes, with a market launch planned for late 2027 at around 150,000 pesos.

The Mexican government has unveiled a prototype of the Olinia Carga, also known as the Olinia 2, a compact battery-electric light truck aimed primarily at small businesses, traders and delivery services. The vehicle is designed to fill the gap between three-wheeled transport vehicles and conventional light commercial vehicles.

According to the government, the Olinia Carga can carry up to 650 kilogrammes and will offer a range of more than 120 kilometres per charge. The cab seats two people, while the battery can be charged from a standard domestic socket. The government has not yet disclosed the battery capacity or charging power.

Operating costs are expected to be one of the Olinia Carga's main selling points. Project leader Roberto Capuano Tripp says the electric light truck will cost only around a quarter as much to operate as a conventional light commercial vehicle and about a third as much as a so-called 'motocarro.' The market launch is scheduled for late 2027, with a target price of around 150,000 Mexican pesos, equivalent to approximately 7,000 euros.

Mexico is now looking for a partner to industrialise the vehicle. The tender process will begin at the end of September and run until December. The government aims to work with a company with majority Mexican capital that will manufacture the vehicles domestically. Production is initially planned at 10,000 units in the first year, with commitments already secured for 8,000 vehicles. Annual production capacity could later increase to as many as 50,000 units.

One challenge that remains is the battery. According to Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum, the country currently lacks sufficient domestic production capacity. Capuano estimates that the battery accounts for around 40 per cent of the vehicle's value. The government therefore wants to gradually develop a domestic battery value chain.

The Carga is part of a broader Olinia model family. In June, Mexico presented the Olinia Uno, the first prototype from its domestic electric vehicle brand. The battery-electric city car is also due to launch in 2027 and will offer a range of around 125 kilometres. An Olinia Van with an enclosed cargo area has also been announced.

Olinia forms part of a broader industrial strategy under which Mexico aims not only to manufacture electric vehicles domestically, but also to build up its own development and production expertise. For the Olinia Uno, the initial target is for 50 per cent of the vehicle's value to be generated locally, rising to 75 per cent by 2030. With the Carga, Mexico is now extending this approach to urban logistics and small businesses.
