# Nissan and Honda partner on E/E architecture for smart cars

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-08-31

---

Nissan and Honda have signed a new development agreement aimed at accelerating development cycles for smart cars. Despite having scrapped their merger plans in early 2025, the two Japanese automakers remain open to close collaboration.

The agreement covers standardising several electronic control units (ECUs) that form the core of next-generation software-driven vehicles, letting both automakers reduce investment versus bearing full development costs alone. Beyond ECUs, the pair also plans to standardise and jointly develop the vehicle's internal operating system, plus key parts of the middleware and vehicle control software that run on the ECUs.

The planned E/E architecture — including the jointly developed ECUs and software — is scheduled for implementation in both companies' next-generation smart cars starting in fiscal year 2029. A key objective is establishing uniform specifications for the architecture's components, which the partners see as the foundation for leveraging combined technical expertise and development resources, and ultimately achieving greater economies of scale.

The collaboration underscores how much Nissan and Honda, as relatively small automakers, rely on each other. Two years ago the two sides came close to a full merger and even signed a memorandum of understanding, but merger talks fell through in early 2025 due to differing negotiating positions. With this development partnership, both sides are emphasising their strategic relationship and commitment to keep seeking collaboration opportunities within that framework.

Nissan and Honda have prioritised the software sector related to smart cars, viewing it as crucial to vehicle intelligence and electrification, "given the rapid pace of technological innovation and the need to strengthen competitiveness through improved R&D speed and investment efficiency."

Even major automakers are seeking outside help in this area — Volkswagen, for instance, is relying on E/E architecture developments from the much smaller manufacturers Rivian and Xpeng.
