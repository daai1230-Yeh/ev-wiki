# Volkswagen pauses development of the ID.Golf

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-10-30

---

Volkswagen is temporarily pausing development work on the future ID.Golf. The automaker cites high cost pressure as the reason for the decision and plans to resume work as soon as the necessary investments can be secured.

Brand chief Thomas Schäfer first announced the pause in an internal interview, as reported by Handelsblatt; the company has since officially confirmed it. "We simply cannot afford this work right now," Schäfer said, according to Reuters. In its nine-month statement for the Brand Group Core, VW said development is paused "until we can securely allocate the necessary investments".

The pause is part of a broader cost-saving programme. In the Brand Group Core, net cash flow fell from €4.34 billion to €1.43 billion in the first nine months of 2026; revenue was nearly stable at €106.4 billion, operating profit fell from €4.72 billion to €4.33 billion, and operating return on sales dropped from 4.4 to 4.1 per cent.

The core Volkswagen Passenger Cars brand is under particular pressure: operating profit fell from €1.48 billion to €813 million, with a 1.3 per cent return on sales (2.6 per cent excluding special measures such as restructuring, the planned sale of the Osnabrück plant and the end of ID.4 production in Chattanooga). BEVs have yet to reach the margins of comparable ICE vehicles, so a rising BEV share increases cost pressure. The Wolfsburg plant is reportedly about €300 million behind its cost target, while Emden and Zwickau are on track.

The ID.Golf is intended to be based on the upcoming Scalable Systems Platform (SSP) and is planned for the early 2030s. It is unclear whether the timeline will slip; the Works Council told Reuters lost time could be made up, but sees the spending freeze as a sign of the group's strained finances.
