# 'Most Companies Are Struggling': Executive Shares An Uncomfortable Truth About Solid-State EV Batteries

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: Aug 22, 2026

---

*Korean battery giant LG Energy Solution said key challenges in solid-state battery development remain.*

Solid-state batteries will likely be commercialized at a larger scale in non-automotive applications before widespread electric vehicle adoption, LG Energy Solution said during a media roundtable at its newly opened Lansing, Michigan, battery plant last week.

Battery scientists believe solid-state cells can unlock substantially more driving range, improve thermal profiles to eliminate fire risks, and charge at blistering speeds. The investment and research behind the technology from startups and established players is real.

However, LGES echoed concerns around mass manufacturing. "The problem with solid state is large-scale production," said Robert Lee, North America president for LGES. "It has very good energy density, so if you're making a small form factor, you should be able to get there. If you're making very large form factors, most companies are struggling."

Lee believes EVs won't be the first application for mass-market solid-state batteries. "You will see that in your smartphones probably a decade before you would see it in EVs. I would expect specialized applications first before we're able to contemplate that as fit for EVs or even ESS."

Those specialized applications could be smartphones, drones, and even performance-focused EVs initially. Mercedes-Benz last year covered nearly 750 miles on a single charge in a prototype EQS sedan equipped with American startup Factorial Energy's semi-solid-state battery. BMW has installed an all-solid-state battery from Colorado startup Solid Power in a prototype i7. Stellantis is now testing a semi-solid-state pack in a Dodge Charger Daytona EV.

Over in China, the timeline and development speed on solid-state batteries appears even more aggressive. BYD, CATL, Geely and several other players are racing to begin pilot programs in 2027, followed by wider potential deployment by the end of the decade.

Meanwhile, advancements in current lithium-ion packs will continue to drive progress in EV range and charging performance, LGES said.

The company is working with General Motors to develop lithium-manganese-rich (LMR) batteries, using far less nickel and cobalt while relying more on manganese, which can be processed in the U.S. GM has said LMR batteries will power its full-size trucks and SUVs from 2028 onward, delivering more than 400 miles of range at roughly the cost of LFP.

LGES is also gearing up to produce new 46-series lithium-ion cells for EVs at its upcoming gigafactory in Arizona — larger-format cells better suited to next-generation EVs with structural battery packs, with further advancements potentially enabling 10-minute charging and improved pack safety.

"We just want to have all those options open," said Devon Wilson, VP of sales and marketing at LGES Vertech, adding the company is preparing a pilot program for sodium-ion ESS batteries. CATL has already raced ahead with sodium-ion commercialization, but the U.S. is catching up, with GM planning sodium-ion ESS batteries by the end of the decade.
