# Kempower plans to cut up to 120 jobs worldwide

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-11-16

---

Finnish charging station manufacturer Kempower is planning global job cuts, with up to 120 of its 870 positions potentially affected. The company aims to reduce costs in response to challenging market conditions and restructure its organisation.

Kempower has announced that it is starting negotiations over planned personnel changes. The measures form part of a comprehensive cost-cutting programme aimed at generating annual savings of more than €15 million. This replaces a previously announced savings target of more than €5 million, set in July. The planned changes will affect the company's global operations. In Finland, up to 80 jobs could be cut, with further reductions planned outside the domestic market. Final decisions will be made once the respective consultation procedures have been completed.

## Office-based jobs in Finland primarily affected

In Finland, where Kempower employs around 600 of its 870 staff, negotiations with employee representatives began on 11 November. They involve approximately 420 office-based employees, while production staff are explicitly excluded. Outside Finland, Kempower intends to implement any changes in accordance with the respective national laws and employment practices. The company has not yet disclosed where the remaining up to 40 job cuts might take place.

The new organisational structure is scheduled to take effect in January 2027, with the full savings expected to materialise from the third quarter of 2027. "Proposing these changes is difficult, but necessary. We must align our cost base with current market demand, improve our cost competitiveness, and realign our resources according to strategic priorities. Our goal is to create a leaner, more scalable organisation that prioritises responsiveness to our customers. We will conduct the negotiations openly and fairly and support all those affected," says Bhasker Kaushal, CEO of Kempower.

## Weaker demand weighs on business

The restructuring is being driven by weaker demand for fast-charging infrastructure. On 3 November, Kempower had already revised its annual forecast downwards. Instead of the previously expected revenue growth of 10 to 25 per cent in 2026, the company now anticipates an increase of only around five per cent compared with the previous year. As for operating EBIT, Kempower now expects a weaker result than in 2025, when it recorded an operating loss of €12.4 million. In other words, the company is anticipating another loss in the tens of millions of euros.

Preliminary third-quarter figures also reflect this trend: order intake fell by 19 per cent year-on-year to €60.8 million, while revenue declined by 10 per cent to €66.2 million. Operating EBIT stood at minus €1.7 million, compared with a slightly positive result of €0.2 million in the same quarter of the previous year.

Kempower cites cautious investment by charge point operators (CPOs) as the main reason. These operators are increasingly focusing on improving the utilisation and profitability of their existing infrastructure rather than expanding charging capacity. Market consolidation is also delaying investment decisions. While fleet customers continue to electrify their vehicles, contract negotiations are taking longer and individual investments are being postponed. According to Kempower, new installations of public DC fast-charging points in Europe and North America fell by 18 per cent in the third quarter.

## Kempower already cut jobs in 2024

This is not the first time Kempower has responded to weaker market conditions with workforce reductions. In 2024, the Finnish manufacturer announced plans to cut around 80 full-time positions, including approximately 70 jobs in Finland and another ten at sites abroad. The company also introduced temporary layoffs in Finland at the time, which affected production staff as well.

There are now also signs of reduced activity in the US. On 12 November, the Triangle Business Journal reported job cuts at Kempower's site in Durham, North Carolina. In 2024, the manufacturer opened a production facility for charging stations there, originally promising more than 300 jobs. It is not yet clear how many local positions will be affected by the current measures or whether they are included in the announced global reduction of up to 120 jobs.
