# Mercedes Power Converter decouples high-voltage supply from battery state

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-11-04

---

Mercedes is developing new technology to constantly provide a voltage of 800 volts in electric vehicles. With current technology, the system voltage depends directly on the state of the battery cells. In the future, Mercedes wants to use a so-called Power Converter that bypasses this dependency through individual micro-converters per cell unit.

In conventional electric vehicles, the system voltage is determined by the number of battery cells connected in series. As the state of charge decreases, this voltage drops during operation. Ageing or weakened cells also impair the performance of the entire pack.

## Decentralised microconverters replace conventional cell series connection

Mercedes' Power Converters function as individual decentralised microconverters, controlling individual battery cells or cell clusters. Multiple parallel-connected Power Converters coordinate energy flows and prevent voltage fluctuations by maintaining a stable DC high-voltage supply. The control system selectively relieves weaker cells, increasing overall efficiency. Functions such as AC and DC charging, as well as bidirectional charging, can be integrated. The converter has a power output of up to three kilowatts.

## Omitting the on-board charger saves weight and space

Since the microconverters can handle AC/DC conversion directly, the separate on-board charger (OBC) can be omitted, saving weight, space and production costs. The traction inverter always receives a constant 800 volts and no longer needs to be designed for fluctuating input voltages, improving efficiency and making it easier to use identical powertrain components across drivetrain concepts.

## Potential for OTA features and digital business models

It remains to be seen whether the more complex technology directly at the battery requires greater repair effort. The microconverters allow functions like bidirectional charging and maximum charging power to be controlled purely via software, likely opening up opportunities for paid over-the-air unlocks.
