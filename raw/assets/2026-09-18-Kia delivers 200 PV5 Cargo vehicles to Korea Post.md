# Kia delivers 200 PV5 Cargo vehicles to Korea Post

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-09-17 14:45

---

Kia will deliver 200 units of the electric PV5 Cargo to Korea Post. The vehicles, specially adapted for postal operations, are to be gradually deployed at post offices across South Korea starting this month.

The PV5 Cargo is based on Kia's E-GMP electric platform, specifically developed for Purpose-Built Vehicles (PBVs). For Korea Post, Kia has focused on features such as a low vehicle floor, which reduces physical strain during frequent entry and exit on delivery routes. Additionally, the vehicle includes special driver assistance systems.

Prior to the order, Kia tested the PV5 Cargo in real-world postal operations for approximately two months last year. Between September and November, vehicles were deployed at central post offices across all nine regional postal administrations in South Korea. According to Chosun Biz, the trials assessed how well the vehicle suited daily postal delivery operations. Feedback from employees during this practical test was subsequently incorporated into the version for Korea Post. Through a PBV conversion partner, the cargo area features an aluminium floor and a flat loading surface. Additionally, a device is installed to prevent door damage during loading and unloading.

In addition to the vehicles, Kia aims to support Korea Post in establishing its electric fleet operations. This includes training for fleet managers as well as tailored charging and maintenance solutions.

The PV5 is the first model in Kia's new PBV family and is initially available as a Passenger and Cargo variant. Further versions, such as Open Bed, WAV (Wheelchair Accessible Vehicle) for transporting wheelchair users, and a Chassis Cab, are planned. Kia is also pursuing PBV collaborations with other companies and organisations in South Korea, including the energy provider Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO).

A few days ago, Kia also unveiled the PV7 at the IAA Transportation in Hannover. Compared to the PV5, the second model in the PBV strategy not only offers larger dimensions, increased cargo space, and higher battery energy content but also switches to 800-volt technology.
