# Ford's Radical New EV Manufacturing Process Is Already Building Trucks

**Author**: Rob Stumpf | **Date**: 2026-11-09

---

Ford has built its first Fathom electric pickups on its Louisville production line. Now it needs to prove the process can scale.

- Vehicles are rolling off the line for validation testing, with about 90% of the testing already complete.
- Actual customer deliveries aren't slated until late next year.

During its recent quarterly earnings call, Ford COO Kumar Galhotra said: "It's progressing very well. 90% of the validation testing has been completed. We've built our first production line vehicles at Louisville assembly plant."

Galhotra didn't say these are production vehicles; they are the first Fathoms built on the real production line, the start of Ford proving out its process for both the Fathom and its Universal Electric Vehicle (UEV) Platform.

Ford will build the Fathom using a so-called "assembly tree" rather than a traditional assembly line. The front, middle, and rear sections of the pickup are put together simultaneously, then joined toward the end of the process, which Ford says should make building quicker and more efficient and improve ergonomics for workers. It resembles the "unboxed" process Tesla has introduced for the Cybercab.

Ford is reportedly targeting 100,000 sales in the first year. More than $2 billion was invested in the Louisville plant. Ford's Shelbyville Technical Center has also been producing large castings similar to Tesla's Gigacasting: 146 different parts become just 2 large castings, and Ford has already made 5,000 of these unicastings.

The next challenge, per the COO, is the supply chain: "We need to make sure that the supply base can run at rate with high quality parts. And that's very normal at this stage of the launch, and that's what we're going to be working on for the next few months."

The Fathom could prove to be Ford's EV redemption arc if the automaker gets it right. Affordable? Check. Versatile? Check. Scalable? That's to be seen.
