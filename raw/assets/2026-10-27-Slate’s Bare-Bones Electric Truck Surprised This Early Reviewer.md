# Slate’s Bare-Bones Electric Truck Surprised This Early Reviewer

**Author**: Andrei Nedelea | **Date**: Oct 25, 2026

---

An early drive reveals surprising comfort and punch, though this unfinished prototype still has some rough edges.

- An early reviewer found the Slate surprisingly comfortable, stable, and punchy despite its bare-bones approach.
- The prototype still needs polish, with inconsistent steering effort, abrupt pedal response, and controls that feel flimsy.
- The Slate starts at $26,400 including destination, with a projected 205-mile range that awaits EPA confirmation.

The Slate truck has manual windows, steel wheels, and, in this prototype, an interior with placeholder panels. Expectations for luxury were suitably low. When one YouTuber (Tedward) took it for a drive around Massachusetts, he found rather less to complain about than anticipated.

Acceleration surprised him first. Despite an estimated 8-second sprint to 60 mph, the truck feels quick and responsive around town and has enough muscle to pass at around 40 mph. "It's got plenty. It's got what it needs," he says.

Comfort made an even stronger impression. The Slate handles broken pavement and tram tracks well, and a reviewer recovering from a back injury praises its seating position. One-pedal driving is generally well calibrated, though the accelerator can feel abrupt at first.

At highway speeds, the truck is stable and planted, without the wind whistle expected from the mirrors. It has little body roll in corners, and its rear-wheel-drive layout has him picturing autocross and winter driving events.

This remains an unfinished truck: steering effort changes through a turn, the switchgear should feel more substantial, and placeholder panels rattle over bumps, so the interior can't really be evaluated yet.

Specs: a single 181-hp electric motor powers the rear wheels, drawing from a 65-kWh LFP battery with a claimed 205-mile range (a manufacturer estimate, not EPA). Slate quotes around 30 minutes for a 20–80% DC charge. Base price is $24,950, or $26,400 including destination, before taxes, customization and accessories.

Tedward sees the appeal in a useful little runabout with physical controls and room for personalization. That DIY philosophy extends to accessories such as the $99.99 hitch receiver, which attaches with four bolts and takes as little as 30 minutes to install.

Potential buyers can now try a truck at Slate's first Chicago showroom, while some preorder customers have reportedly been offered December delivery windows. The Jeff Bezos-backed automaker is asking buyers to accept fewer features, and the experience behind the wheel needs to make that bargain not feel like too much of a compromise.
