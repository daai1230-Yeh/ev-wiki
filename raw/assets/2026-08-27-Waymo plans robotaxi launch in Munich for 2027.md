# Waymo plans robotaxi launch in Munich for 2027

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 26.08.2026

---

Waymo is preparing to launch its autonomous ride-hailing service in Germany. In the coming weeks, the first vehicles are expected to hit the roads in Munich, initially with human drivers behind the wheel. Commercial robotaxi operations are planned for the end of 2027.

In the coming weeks, the US company's first robotaxis will begin operating in Munich. However, during this initial phase, trained personnel will still be behind the wheel. Waymo aims to use this period to create high-resolution maps of the city's road network and assess its unique traffic conditions. Following this, the autonomous driving system, 'Waymo Driver', will be prepared for deployment in the Bavarian capital. By the end of 2027, Waymo plans to launch its commercial ride-hailing service for the public.

The launch in Germany has been anticipated for some time. In mid-June, Waymo Germany GmbH, based in Munich, was registered in the commercial register. Its stated purposes included ride-hailing services using autonomous vehicles and support services for related commercial offerings. At the same time, operational structures began to take shape: Waymo started recruiting personnel in Munich to operate autonomous mobility services, while a recruitment agency sought test drivers for autonomous vehicles in Munich and Berlin.

If the announced timeline is met, Munich will become the first city in the European Union to offer a regular driverless robotaxi service. However, this is not Waymo's first foray into Europe. The Alphabet subsidiary has been preparing to launch its ride-hailing service in London since spring.

In Munich, Waymo will use only battery-electric vehicles. The announcement does not yet specify which models will be deployed. In the USA, the company is currently expanding its fleet with the new Ojai, an electric van built by Zeekr. Equipped with Waymo's sixth-generation autonomous driving system, it is now transporting paying passengers in San Francisco, Los Angeles and Phoenix. However, for the commercial launch in Germany, the necessary approvals and licensing steps are still pending. Waymo states that it is in discussions with authorities at municipal, state and federal levels regarding these requirements.

Political support is already in place. Christian Hirte, Parliamentary State Secretary to the Federal Minister of Transport, views Waymo's decision as evidence that autonomous mobility services can operate regularly in Germany. Florian Herrmann, Head of the Bavarian State Chancellery, highlights the region's strengths in the automotive and AI sectors and announces plans to further reduce regulatory barriers.

Waymo brings extensive experience from its commercial robotaxi operations. According to the company, it has completed more than 20 million autonomous rides and driven more than 350 million kilometres fully autonomously. Waymo also highlights a lower accident rate compared to human drivers. However, these statistics are based on its existing operational areas and may not directly translate to Munich's traffic conditions.

Waymo is unlikely to be the only robotaxi provider on Munich's streets. Uber and the Israeli AI company Autobrains have also announced robotaxi programmes for the Bavarian capital. However, with Waymo, one of the world's most experienced robotaxi operators has now set a concrete timeline for launching a commercial driverless ride-hailing service in Germany.
