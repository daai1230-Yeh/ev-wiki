# World EV Day 2026: India's Electric Mobility Transition Enters A New Phase

**Author**: Mohan Gupta | **Date**: 2026-09-09

*(Referenced in ETAuto EV Day Special newsletter as "World EV Day Special | India's EV market moves from early adoption to scale")*

---

As the world observes World EV Day 2026, electric mobility is emerging as an important part of the global transportation system. Electric vehicles are increasingly moving beyond being an alternative to conventional vehicles, supported by the need to reduce carbon emissions, improve energy efficiency, lower dependence on fossil fuels, and create cleaner cities.

In India, electric mobility has expanded across passenger vehicles, two-wheelers, three-wheelers, buses and commercial fleets. Falling battery costs, improving charging infrastructure, policy support and greater consumer awareness are contributing to the growth of the sector. At the same time, the industry is focusing on battery technology, power electronics, vehicle software and connected mobility solutions to improve vehicle performance and reliability.

The link between electric mobility and renewable energy is also becoming increasingly important. Solar and wind power can provide cleaner electricity for EV charging, while battery energy storage and smart charging can help manage additional demand on the electricity grid. Technologies such as vehicle-to-grid and vehicle-to-home could eventually allow EV batteries to support homes and electricity networks during periods of high demand.

However, several challenges continue to affect the pace of EV adoption: limited charging availability in some areas, high upfront vehicle costs, battery recycling requirements, supply-chain dependence, and the need for stronger electricity infrastructure. Addressing these will require greater coordination among policymakers, manufacturers, utilities, technology providers and consumers.

Industry experts believe the next phase of India's EV development will need to focus not only on increasing vehicle sales but also on strengthening domestic capabilities — batteries, motors, controllers, charging equipment and other critical components remain areas where greater local manufacturing can reduce external dependencies and improve supply-chain resilience. The development of reliable charging infrastructure, better integrated with renewable energy, storage and intelligent energy management, is another major priority.

Avinash Punekar, CEO of iCreate, said: "The next five years of India's EV story will be decided not by how many vehicles we sell, but by how much of what goes inside them we have learned to build ourselves. Today, critical elements of an EV, from batteries and motors to controllers and charging systems, still depend significantly on technologies and components developed outside India. That has to change if we want an EV industry that can withstand supply disruptions and external dependencies rather than being derailed by them." iCreate is backing this shift through startups including ChargeZone, Clean Electric, Naxatra Labs, Webber Electrocorp and Cancrie.

Arun Bhardwaj, Managing Director of Smarten Power Systems, said electrification is "also about building the energy infrastructure needed to make electric mobility work reliably in everyday life," arguing India needs to think about generation, charging, storage and consumption as parts of the same equation, and that getting this right — especially through better use of renewable energy — can help build a smarter, more resilient energy system.

Reshu Madan, President and SBU Head at Havells India, said "India's EV revolution is no longer about adoption alone. It is about building the confidence to scale," and highlighted the company's Havells MotrON charging solutions for homes, workplaces, fleets and highways, built on "Safety First, Technology Forward and Reliability Always."

Divyanshu Agarwal, CEO of Zelio E-Mobility, said much of the next wave of adoption is being driven by Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities, where electric two-wheelers and the low-speed EV segment address everyday mobility needs in a practical, accessible and affordable way, and that sustainable adoption happens "when technology directly aligns with ground realities."

World EV Day 2026 highlights both the progress made and the challenges ahead. With continued investment, technological innovation, stronger infrastructure, and greater domestic manufacturing, electric mobility can become a key driver of cleaner transportation while also supporting a smarter, more resilient, and sustainable energy system.
