# New Jersey Just Legalized Cheap Plug-In Balcony Solar Panels

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-09-03

---

Another American state is making it legal to install cheap balcony solar systems that can lower energy bills. New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill signed legislation this week that bars municipalities and landlords from restricting the installation of portable solar panels.

The law, which will go into effect six months from now, allows residents and tenants to install solar systems with a maximum power output of up to 1,200 watts without having to pay additional fees for installation or go through approvals.

The legislation, approved unanimously in the Senate and Assembly in late June, is aimed at cutting residents' power costs as electricity prices continue to climb across the state. Landlords, homeowners associations, housing cooperatives, and similar entities must still be notified about the installation of a plug-in solar system 14 days in advance, and landlords can still restrict placement and size — but the new law bars local governments from requiring licensing or approvals.

"Balcony solar is the ultimate win-win," said Gov. Mikie Sherrill (D) at a bill signing ceremony in Trenton. "It's accessible, affordable, safe, and sustainable. These units cost a fraction as much as rooftop solar, but they can still shave up to $50 off the typical monthly bill."

With a plug-in photovoltaic system, the solar panels connect either to a microinverter or a bigger unit that also includes batteries for storage. The "brain" of the system plugs directly into a household outlet, with the inverter ensuring no power is sent back to the grid during an outage — important for the safety of line workers restoring power.

Balcony solar systems, extremely popular in Germany (over one million systems online there), require a much smaller initial investment than rooftop solar and simpler installation. In New Jersey, the 1.2 kW limit means two or three solar panels can be plugged into a compliant inverter, keeping costs down for apartment dwellers.

"Plug-in solar can save a family hundreds of dollars a year, every year, with no cost to the ratepayers," said Hannah Birnbaum, Co-Founder and Chief of Advocacy at Permit Power. "Governor Sherrill has been going after energy costs since her first day in office, and this law hands families a tool they can use themselves, including the roughly 40 percent of New Jerseyans who rent or live in apartments and have never had a way to lower their own power bill."
