# Study: Batteries lose lithium to the current collector

**Date**: 24 July 2026

---

Researchers have experimentally confirmed a previously suspected ageing mechanism in lithium-metal batteries: using atom probe tomography, scientists demonstrated that lithium ions penetrate and become permanently bound within the copper current collector during charging and discharging. It had previously been assumed lithium does not interact, or only minimally interacts, with the copper collector — the new findings suggest this loss has been underestimated as a cause of capacity degradation.

Lithium was found to accumulate at grain boundaries and interfaces of the copper foil as early as the first charge/discharge cycle. After several cycles, the current collector's structure changes — its surface becomes nanocrystalline and oxidises, creating additional defects. Every loss of lithium reduces available battery capacity and shortens cell lifespan, with the effect increasing as charging cycles accumulate.

The study, published in Advanced Energy Materials, was conducted by researchers from Ruhr University Bochum, Helmholtz Institute Ulm, and Karlsruhe Institute of Technology.
