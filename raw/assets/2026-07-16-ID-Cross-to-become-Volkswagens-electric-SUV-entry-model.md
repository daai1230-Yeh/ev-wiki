# ID. Cross to become Volkswagen's electric SUV entry model

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-07-15

---

With the VW ID. Cross, the last of the four new all-electric compact cars from the Volkswagen Group is celebrating its world premiere: The new model aims to appeal to those looking for an unpretentious, spacious city car with SUV styling. For VW, the ID. Cross, alongside the ID. Polo, is the most important product launch of the year.

Since spring, Volkswagen, Škoda and Cupra have gradually unveiled their new electric compact models. While all four vehicles share the same technical foundation, each targets a different customer group. Within the line-up, the VW ID. Cross takes on the role of the SUV variant alongside the new ID. Polo.

Volkswagen positions the ID. Cross as an electric alternative to the T-Cross, targeting buyers who want the manoeuvrability of a city car combined with greater practicality and interior space. The ID. Cross is scheduled to launch this autumn, with prices starting at €28,000.

At the entry price of €28,000, customers receive the ID. Cross Trend, combining an 85 kW electric motor with a 37 kWh (net) battery, built on Volkswagen Group's new MEB+ platform. Like its sister models (ID. Polo, Škoda Epiq, Cupra Raval), the ID. Cross is based on the updated MEB+ architecture with front-wheel drive, offered with three drivetrain options: 85 kW, 99 kW and 155 kW, and two battery sizes (37 kWh and 52 kWh net).

The new electric compact-car family uses Volkswagen's newly developed Unified Cell in a cell-to-pack architecture, plus the new APP290 electric motor and an in-house-developed "pulse inverter." The standard 37 kWh LFP battery supports DC charging up to 90 kW (10-80% in 27 minutes) and AC charging up to 11 kW, with a preliminary range of up to 316 km. The larger 52 kWh NMC battery raises range to 436 km with 105 kW DC charging (24 minutes for 10-80%).

Key specs: MEB+ platform, front-wheel drive; 37/52 kWh net battery; 85/99/155 kW power; 90/105 kW DC charging; 11 kW AC charging; top speed 150/160 km/h; curb weight from 1,539/1,548 kg; range 316/436 km.

Dimensions: 4.15 m long, 1.79 m wide, 1.58 m tall, 2.60 m wheelbase, 14 cm ground clearance, 475-litre boot (more than the Golf), plus a 22-litre front storage compartment. Volkswagen says the ID. Cross offers more interior space than the combustion T-Cross thanks to a 38 mm longer wheelbase and MEB+'s packaging advantages.

Trim levels are Trend, Life and Style. Trend includes 17-inch wheels, automatic climate control and standard driver-assistance systems (though it's unclear if Trend will be available at launch). Life adds 18-inch wheels, two-zone climate control, adaptive cruise control and a rear-view camera. Style adds LED matrix headlights, 3D LED rear lights, illuminated logos, and keyless entry. The ID. Cross also gets the next-generation "Connected Travel Assist," which can react to traffic lights and includes one-pedal driving.

Optional extras include a 425-watt Harman Kardon audio system, massage front seats, and a panoramic glass roof.

VW brand CEO Thomas Schäfer said the ID. Cross combines "technological competence, clear design, convincing details, and true all-round qualities into an excellent price-performance ratio," positioning it as VW's next potential success story in the tradition of the Golf, Passat and Tiguan.
