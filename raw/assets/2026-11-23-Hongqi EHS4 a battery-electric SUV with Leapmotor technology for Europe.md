# Hongqi EHS4: a battery-electric SUV with Leapmotor technology for Europe

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-11-20

---

Chinese automaker Hongqi is introducing a new eSUV to Europe. The FAW-owned brand will first launch the EHS4 in Italy with a base starting price of €35,900. However, this will be lowered to €32,900 during an initial promotional period.

The Hongqi EHS4 is not the product of an entirely independent vehicle development by Hongqi, a brand long associated with state limousines in China. Instead, the EHS4 is based on Leapmotor's Leap 3.5 electric vehicle platform, meaning it shares its technical foundation with the Leapmotor B10. This close relationship is also evident externally: apart from a uniquely designed front end and a few interior details, the two battery-electric SUVs are nearly identical. Among other differences, the Hongqi EHS4 omits the continuous LED light strip of the Leapmotor B10, opting instead for a red design line on the front bonnet that reflects Hongqi's brand emblem.

## Hongqi EHS4 with 160 kW RWD and 460 km Range

There are also many similarities in the technical specifications. The 4.54-metre-long Hongqi EHS4 features a rear-mounted motor with 160 kW of power. Energy is supplied by a 68.7 kWh LFP battery. The electric compact SUV can achieve a range of up to 460 km WLTP, with Hongqi specifying maximum DC charging power of 178 kW.

The 400-volt system originates from the cooperation between FAW and Leapmotor announced in April 2025. The battery also has ties to another Chinese manufacturer: the cells come from a joint factory operated by FAW and BYD's battery subsidiary FinDreams in Changchun. They are integrated directly into the vehicle floor using the cell-to-chassis principle.

Inside, the Hongqi EHS4 features a central 14.6-inch touchscreen with 2K resolution, along with an additional display for the driver. Apple CarPlay and Android Auto are onboard, as are over-the-air updates. The boot capacity is 460 litres as standard, and can be expanded to up to 1,704 litres by folding down the rear seats. Additionally, there is a 33-litre frunk under the front bonnet.

At the premiere in Venice this week, only pricing for Italy was revealed: the base version starts at €35,900, albeit with an introductory price of €32,900 at market launch. The fully equipped version costs €37,900 as standard, or €34,900 during the introductory period. Prices in Germany are expected to be in a similar range.

## Hongqi seeks partners for European production

The market launch of the EHS4 is part of Hongqi's broader expansion into Europe. The brand was founded in 1958 and belongs to the Chinese state-owned group FAW (First Automotive Works). Originally known primarily for state limousines, FAW Hongqi now positions itself as a premium brand. The EHS4 was designed at the company's own design centre in Munich under the leadership of German designer Christian Winkelmann, who previously worked for the Volkswagen Group for 15 years. Hongqi describes the eSUV as the first model developed specifically for European customers.

Local production in Europe remains a strategic goal. As Xiao Xiao, head of FAW's international business and responsible for Hongqi's European expansion, told Reuters, the group is examining several potential partners for this purpose, including Stellantis and Volkswagen. However, a Volkswagen spokesperson reportedly denied that there are any discussions between Volkswagen and FAW regarding vehicle production in Europe. The long-standing cooperation between the two groups in China continues regardless.

Stellantis is a natural partner for Hongqi for several reasons: the group holds a 20 per cent stake in Leapmotor. Both companies operate a joint venture for the expansion of the brand in Europe and other world regions. And Stellantis produces the European version of the Leapmotor B10 in Spain. Reuters reported in April on discussions between Hongqi and Stellantis regarding the possible use of European production capacities. According to Xiao, FAW prefers partnerships that utilise existing European plants rather than purchasing or building new factories.

## Hongqi appoints new importer for Germany

Hongqi is currently represented in 27 European countries with 90 sales locations. By the end of 2027, the network is expected to grow to more than 150 locations. In Germany, Hongqi is making a second attempt: following its separation from importer Hedin in spring (DE), AEC has recently been appointed as the new importer (DE) – and will also handle homologation and build a dealer network in Germany.

For the coming year, the brand also plans to offer ten models in Europe. In addition to pure electric vehicles, these will include combustion engines, hybrids, and vehicles with range-extender powertrains. A range-extender version of the EHS4 is also announced, set to follow in 2027.
