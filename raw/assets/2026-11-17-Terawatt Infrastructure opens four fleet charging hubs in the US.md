# Terawatt Infrastructure opens four fleet charging hubs in the US

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-11-16

---

The charging infrastructure provider has added a combined 22 MW of charging capacity across four sites in California, Tennessee, Florida and Texas. According to the company, the new depots can charge more than 3,000 commercial electric vehicles per day.

Terawatt Infrastructure announced the expansion on 12 November 2026. The newly opened and upcoming charging hubs are specifically designed for commercial EV and autonomous vehicle fleet operators in metropolitan areas.

Rather than simply providing sites with pre-secured grid capacity and leaving customers to handle the construction, Terawatt develops, builds and operates complete charging depots. Its full-service approach covers land acquisition, regulatory permitting, site design, grid connections and the installation of charging hardware. Once the facilities are commissioned, Terawatt also takes care of ongoing operations, maintenance and energy management using its own charge management software.

The largest of the four new facilities is located near Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport in Texas, where it provides 7 MW of charging capacity for a commercial fleet operator. In California, a 6.6 MW charging hub near San Francisco International Airport supports a single fleet's operations in the San Francisco Bay Area. Terawatt said it completed the South San Francisco project ahead of schedule and reduced development costs by a six-figure sum in US dollars (at least around €90,000) through alternative site-energisation methods and close coordination with regional utility Pacific Gas & Electric Company.

The remaining two locations are a 4.5 MW hub near Nashville International Airport in Tennessee, which supports local fleet operations, and a 3.5 MW site in Miami's Design District/Wynwood area in Florida. While the South San Francisco and Nashville sites are already operational, the Dallas and Miami charging hubs are scheduled to open by the end of 2026.

"We are bringing to life the necessary infrastructure buildout for commercial electric fleets to operate reliably at scale," said Neha Palmer, CEO and co-founder of Terawatt. "We're excited to have new sites up and running across the country, with more on the way in 2027."

With these four additions, Terawatt's portfolio comprises nearly 30 charging hubs in active construction or development across 16 US markets. To finance its expansion, the company secured a debt facility of up to 300 million US dollars (around €275 million) from a syndicate of international banks in June 2026. In addition to light-duty and autonomous vehicle operators such as Waymo and Lyft's fleet subsidiary Flexdrive, Terawatt also serves the commercial freight sector. In April 2025, it opened its first charging hub for medium- and heavy-duty trucks in Southern California. According to the company, its network has delivered more than 30 million kWh of electricity and enabled over 93 million miles (around 150 million km) of electric driving range to date.
