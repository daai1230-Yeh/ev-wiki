# Honda to end Prologue sales in the US

**Author**: Carla Westerheide | **Date**: 2026-07-17

---

Honda has confirmed that it will discontinue the Prologue after the 2026 model year. The move marks the conclusion of Honda's Ultium-based EV partnership with General Motors and leaves the brand without a BEV offering in North America. A Honda spokesperson told CarBuzz: "Honda will conclude sales of Prologue later this year following completion of the 2026 model year," adding that existing customers will continue to receive support through its dealer network.

The Prologue entered the US market in March 2024 as the first volume-production battery-electric Honda for North America. Developed with General Motors, it is based on GM's Ultium platform and produced at the Ramos Arizpe plant in Mexico, which also builds the Chevrolet Equinox EV and Cadillac Optiq.

The decision follows a broader restructuring of Honda's North American electrification plans. Earlier this year Honda shelved several planned EV programmes, including the Honda 0 Series SUV and Sedan and the Acura RSX EV, which were to be built on a dedicated platform at its Ohio EV Hub. The discontinuation also comes after Honda ended production of the GM-based Acura ZDX in 2025. Honda and GM had already abandoned plans to jointly develop affordable EVs and dissolved their fuel-cell joint venture.

Despite its short lifecycle, the Prologue achieved notable success, ranking among the best-selling EVs in the US in its first full year, with more than 80,000 delivered between launch and mid-2026. In the first half of 2026, however, sales fell 48 per cent year-on-year, though it still ranked among the ten best-selling EVs in the country. Dealers are expected to keep selling existing inventory — around 100,000 vehicles reportedly remain in stock — with promotional lease deals clearing remaining stock.

The move leaves Honda without a battery-electric model in the US for the time being. Instead the company is increasingly focusing on hybrids, recently announcing plans to launch 15 new hybrid models globally by 2030 and to expand its hybrid offering in North America, particularly in larger segments.
