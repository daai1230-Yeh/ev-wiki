# JLR delays electric Defender launch to the early 2030s

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 27.08.2026

---

British automaker JLR is reportedly delaying the launch of an all-electric version of its Defender SUV. British media reports that although the off-road-capable vehicle had originally been slated for a new generation in 2028/29, this has now been put off until the early 2030s — and only this new generation will support electric drivetrains.

JLR's electrification strategy has yet to gain significant momentum. Aside from the Jaguar I-Pace, discontinued in 2024, the British heritage group has not introduced a battery-electric production vehicle to market. Several delays have been announced, such as the electric Jaguar Type 01, now expected this year despite originally being scheduled for 2025.

The first electric vehicle from the Land Rover brand is also still pending. The Range Rover Electric has long been announced — journalists tested a prototype as early as spring 2025 — but its market launch was officially postponed to 2026 in the summer of 2025, and the vehicle has yet to materialise.

The Times states that the Defender will also receive an electric variant much later than initially planned. The company maintains the delay is not due to JLR losing faith in electromobility but stems from a platform compatibility issue.

## Platform does not support electric drivetrains

The current Defender, introduced in 2020, is built on the D7x platform, where the "x" stands for off-road capabilities. This platform features a highly rigid aluminium lightweight architecture developed specifically for this off-road vehicle and tailored for internal combustion and hybrid drivetrains. According to JLR, it is too complex and unsuitable for fully electric powertrains — the underbody lacks the space required to accommodate batteries capable of delivering adequate range.

Due to these limitations, JLR plans to introduce electric variants only with the next generation of the Defender, based on a different platform. That is why, according to The Times, JLR has pushed back the launch of the next Defender from 2028/29 to the early 2030s.

This delay also jeopardises the company's goal of offering fully electric models from each of its brands by 2030. Under JLR's current structure there are four brands: Jaguar, Range Rover, Defender and Discovery. Land Rover, which previously encompassed Range Rover, Defender and Discovery, is no longer considered a standalone brand.

The delay is partly attributed to cost-cutting measures introduced by new CEO P.B. Balaji, who joined Tata, JLR's Indian parent company, in November 2025. According to The Times, the executive is under pressure to reduce costs following the carmaker's turbulent last 18 months, including tariffs imposed by the Trump administration that were not fully passed on to the crucial US customer base.

## 'Baby Defender' unaffected by delay

A future 'Baby Defender' — a new, smaller, fully electric variant — is reportedly unaffected by this change of plan. The Defender is currently available in three large sizes: the Defender 90 (4.58 m, three doors), the Defender 110 (5.02 m, classic five-door) and the Defender 130 (5.36 m extended version). A Baby Defender would sit below these.

This Baby Defender (also referred to as the Defender Sport or Defender 80) is set to be based on JLR's new EMA platform (Electrified Modular Architecture), which will also underpin the upcoming electric Range Rover GT and the announced electric Range Rover Velar. It is intended to round off the Defender portfolio at the lower end, appealing to new customer groups as a lifestyle SUV — similar to Mercedes' plans for a smaller electric G-Class. The Baby Defender is expected in 2027, though JLR has not officially confirmed it.
