# Tesla increases profit in Germany despite lower production

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 20.07.2026

---

Tesla concluded the 2025 fiscal year at its Grünheide plant in Germany with higher profits despite declining production and revenue. At the same time, the US electric car manufacturer is preparing a significant production ramp-up at the plant and continues to expand the site with its own battery cell manufacturing.

Tesla Manufacturing Brandenburg SE closed the 2025 financial year with a net profit of €77.1 million, up from €56.8 million the previous year. At the same time, revenue fell from approximately €7.7 billion to €7.1 billion. These figures are taken from the annual report of the German Tesla subsidiary, which has been filed in the German parliamentary electronic lobby register. Production also declined in 2025, when around 202,000 vehicles rolled off production lines in Grünheide, approximately 9,000 fewer than in the previous year. Capacity utilisation dropped from 56% to 54%. These figures have been confirmed by recent reports on the Tesla Manufacturing Brandenburg SE annual report.

According to media reports, the decline in production was partly due to restructuring and model changes. Tesla reconfigured its production lines to prepare for new variants of the Model Y, including the Model Y Performance and an export version for Canada. This reconfiguration led to temporary production stoppages and a subsequent ramp-up phase. Currently, only the Model Y is manufactured in Grünheide. At the end of 2025, around 11,000 people (including temporary workers) were employed at the site.

For 2026, Tesla expects a "significantly higher production volume" and correspondingly greater plant utilisation, according to the annual report. The manufacturer has since outlined more concrete expansion plans to support this forecast, as the company intends to gradually increase production to 7,500 vehicles per week. This would equate to up to 375,000 vehicles per year.

To support the production ramp-up, Tesla is also expanding its workforce. Following an initial phase of hiring 1,000 additional employees, a further 1,000 employees for vehicle production are to be recruited. The increased output is also meant to serve additional markets. According to the annual report, the Gigafactory Berlin-Brandenburg already supplies more than 30 markets, with further expansions planned.

**Tesla also expands cell production**

In parallel, Tesla is advancing battery cell production at its German plant. While the annual report initially mentioned a capacity of up to 8 GWh per year, Tesla has since significantly expanded its plans. In May, the company announced it would increase the planned annual capacity to 18 GWh and invest an additional $250 million. Around 1,500 new jobs are expected to be created in this area. Production is scheduled to begin in the first half of 2027.

However, the expansion is not without risks. The annual report highlights geopolitical tensions and potential supply chain disruptions as key challenges. Additionally, the full integration of battery value creation in Grünheide remains dependent on economic conditions. Given the international competition in cell production, this is a critical factor. Despite these uncertainties, Tesla's recent announcements point to a significant expansion of its German site, where the company aims to ramp up vehicle production while also manufacturing battery cells on a larger scale for the first time.
