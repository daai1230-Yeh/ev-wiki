# World premiere: Xpeng L03 promises rapid charging at a bargain price

**Author**: Kasimir Hupe | **Date**: 2026-07-16

---

At its "Future of Physical AI" Global Brand Event in Munich, Xpeng unveiled its most affordable model to date. The L03 undercuts Tesla and Hyundai on starting price and, despite its 400-volt technology, charges to 80 per cent in just 20 minutes. The brand also outlined its broader vision of an integrated mobility ecosystem linking cars, humanoid robots, and flying vehicles — all running on the same technology stack, powered by Xpeng's self-developed Turing chip and a "World Foundation Model."

## The L03 takes on the competition

At just 4.65 metres long, the L03 is the brand's smallest and most affordable model, with a starting price of €35,600 — positioned below the established G6 in Germany's highest-volume price segment. It undercuts the Tesla Model Y (€39,990), Hyundai Ioniq 5 (€41,900) and Škoda Elroq (€37,390), and its base version already delivers 180 kW versus 140 kW (Elroq) and 125 kW (Ioniq 5) for rivals' entry trims.

The L03 comes in four fully electric LFP variants. The base RWD Standard Range pairs a 58.3 kWh battery with 180 kW for up to 445 km WLTP. The RWD Long Range uses a 71.2 kWh battery for up to 520 km, from €38,600. The AWD Performance (285 kW, 0–100 km/h in 4.5 s) starts at €41,600, or €46,600 for the top "Ultra." Top speed is 180 km/h across the range, and a 0.228 drag coefficient makes it one of the most aerodynamic vehicles in its class. From autumn, a range-extender L03 REEV (37.25 kWh battery plus a 1.5-litre petrol generator, ~1,000 km total range, over 200 km electric) will also arrive from €38,600.

## Charged in 18 minutes with 400 volts

Rather than the 800-volt architecture of the larger G6, the L03 uses a 400-volt system yet still achieves up to 236 kW charging power — 20–80% in 18 minutes, 10–80% in 20 minutes, remarkable for 400 volts and faster than many MEB-based rivals. It does not match top 800-volt models (the G6 does 10–80% in twelve minutes at up to 451 kW). A standard heat pump and vehicle-to-load up to 6 kW complete the package.

## Autonomous driving at Level 2+

The cockpit uses three displays including a 15.6-inch central screen and a 26.8-inch head-up display. The top "Ultra" adds two Turing chips (up to 750 TOPS each) running the Next-Generation Pilot on the VLA 2.0 Vision-Language-Action system. In Germany the L03 offers only Level 2+ partial automation for now, with higher levels tied to a Europe-wide legal framework not expected before 1 January 2027. Standard equipment includes heated/ventilated front seats, panoramic roof, XOPERA sound, seven airbags and 13+ assistance systems. The configurator is live, first deliveries are due in Q4 2026, and Xpeng offers a seven-year/160,000 km vehicle warranty and eight years on the battery.

## Robot and flying car

Xpeng also showed its humanoid robot Iron 2.0 (173 cm, 65 kg, 82 degrees of freedom) which shares the same Turing chip and AI foundation as its cars, with mass production of advanced humanoids planned by end-2026. Its Aridge unit (formerly Xpeng AEROHT) presented the modular "Land Aircraft Carrier" — a six-wheeled ground "Mothership" with a detachable two-seat eVTOL module, with series production announced in China for 2026. Volkswagen, which holds a five-percent stake in Xpeng's development, is also tied to this shared-AI narrative.
