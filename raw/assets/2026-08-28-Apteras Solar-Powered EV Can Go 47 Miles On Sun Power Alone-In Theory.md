# Aptera's Solar-Powered EV Can Go 47 Miles On Sun Power Alone—In Theory

**Author**: Nico DeMattia | **Date**: Aug 27, 2026, 1:36pm ET

---

*Don't just take Aptera's word for it. The results are from independent testing by TÜV Rheinland.*

Aptera's will they/won't they flirtation with production has gone on two decades. It was first founded in 2006, but it was liquidated at one point and went radio silent for a few years. For most of the last 20 years it has promised a three-wheeled electric vehicle with solar panels for a roof, between 250–400 miles of range, and the ability to recoup about 40 miles of range from the sun. Yet it hasn't delivered a single vehicle, so skepticism about its promises is forgivable. However, independent German company TÜV Rheinland recently tested Aptera's solar capabilities, and it seems to deliver.

TÜV Rheinland's team, including its solar expert Dr. Giorgio Bardizza, went to Aptera's Carlsbad, California facility to measure how much solar energy the electric three-wheeler can absorb. Aptera has consistently claimed it can put about four kWh of juice back into its battery from the solar panels on its roof, tailgate, hood and dashboard. That's only a fraction of the launch edition's 44 kWh battery, but it is said to be enough for around 40 miles of range in the ultra-light car, based on the brand's proposed efficiency.

Testing was done over three days, with a different test each day, each from sunrise to sunset in July in Southern California. For accuracy, TÜV Rheinland used its own instruments to measure the electricity actually put back into the battery, not just at the solar panels. A detailed testing report and a video of how it was done are both available.

The first test had the car parked in Aptera's parking lot all day in a fixed position with its solar hatch closed, and it gained 4.23 kWh of energy, said to be worth 42 miles of range. The second test repositioned the car once at noon, simulating someone returning from a lunch break and parking in a different place; that gained 4.4 kWh, worth 44 miles. Finally, the team raised the solar hatch, positioning it toward the sun, and recouped 4.75 kWh — good for 47 miles of range.

The author notes doubts about whether Aptera will ever actually put the three-wheeler on sale, but says it's hard to argue with the test results: they were done by a trustworthy third party and all the data is presented.

Is anyone going to regularly park their car all day in the same spot from sunrise to sunset in perfect sunny conditions? Probably not. But someone with a short commute who parks in the sun for an eight-hour workday might be able to recoup enough range to get home for free on a regular basis, saving a lot of charging.
