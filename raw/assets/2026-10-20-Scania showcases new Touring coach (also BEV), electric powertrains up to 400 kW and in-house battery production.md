# Scania showcases new Touring coach (also BEV), electric powertrains up to 400 kW and in-house battery production

**Author**: Editorial Staff | **Date**: 2026-10-16

---

Scania presented its latest bus and coach electrification developments at its Södertälje headquarters in Sweden: electric powertrains up to 400 kW, internally assembled battery packs, new BEV and PHEV chassis, and the new generation of the Scania Touring coach. The two-day international press event included a visit to the battery assembly facility and vehicle testing at the Scania Demo Center.

**New Scania Touring**: developed with Chinese bodybuilder Higer (which also built the previous generation), already in production and open for orders. It comes in six lengths: two-axle 10.9, 12.3 and 13 m; three-axle 13.2, 13.9 and 14.7 m. It is based on the Scania Super platform; diesel and battery-electric are offered now, gas and hydrogen later. The Super 13 and Super 11 engines (Euro 3, 5 and 6; designed with Euro 7 in mind) cut fuel consumption by about 8% and up to 10% respectively, paired with the new G25CM transmission and an updated rear axle. The cockpit and rear-end noise and vibration were also reworked.

**BEV and PHEV platforms**: low-entry BEV chassis in 4×2 LE and 6×2*4 LE, high-floor BEV in 4×2 HF and 6×2*4 HF, and PHEV in the same high-floor layouts. BEV development began in 2017, a low-floor platform came in 2019 and the low-entry platform in 2023. Scania remains an exception among major European OEMs in e-bus penetration, with deployments limited to relatively small fleets, mainly in Nordic countries.

**Electric powertrains**: six continuous ratings of 240, 270, 300, 330, 360 and 400 kW. The 240 kW machine uses an integrated two-speed gearbox; the others use integrated four-speed transmissions. Scania develops the electric machine, gearbox, batteries and cooling in-house. Installed battery capacities are 267, 356, 445 and 534 kWh.

**Batteries**: modules and packs are assembled in Södertälje with Scania's own BMS. The 1/2MP20 pack holds 89 kWh (about 600 kg) and the MP20 pack 180 kWh (about 1,150 kg), both quoted at 90% SOC.
