# Ford Model e division reports sustained losses in Q3

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-10-29

---

Ford has released its financial results for the third quarter of 2026. For the US automaker, all-electric passenger cars remain a massive loss-making business, with the Model e division accumulating US$1.1 billion in losses between July and September. However, this is a lower figure than in the same quarter last year.

Ford reported a loss of around US$1.1 billion for the third quarter, compared to 1.4 billion in the same period of the previous year. The company improved its position by $300 million, yet its EV division remains deeply in the red. Revenue in the EV segment totalled just $1.2 B in Q3, down from $1.8 B in the corresponding period of 2025, when many US buyers rushed to purchase EVs ahead of the expiry of tax incentives.

Ford's Model e segment includes only fully electric passenger cars such as the Puma Gen-E, the Cologne-built Explorer, or the Mustang Mach-E. It does not include electric commercial vehicles like the E-Transit, which fall under the Ford Pro division. The segment has faced significant challenges in the US, particularly since the $7,500 tax credit for EV purchases was eliminated as of 30 September 2025.

In response, Ford already discontinued the electric pickup F-150 Lightning in autumn 2025. Furthermore, in December 2025, Ford announced extensive write-downs of $19.5 B, primarily in its EV business.

In addition to revenue of $1.2 B (–33 per cent year-on-year) and a negative EBIT of $1.1 B (–21 per cent YoY), Ford also reported approximate sales volumes of around 34,000 BEVs in Q3 (compared to about 50,000 in Q3 2025; –32 per cent YoY). The EBIT margin settled at –86.5 per cent in Q3.

Ford now plans to "further optimise" the profitability of its first-generation EVs while accelerating the market launch of affordable, highly scalable electric vehicles based on the Ford Universal EV platform (UEV), as well as expanding its new battery storage business, Ford Energy.

For the full year, Ford had disclosed it expects an EBIT loss of $4 – $4.5 B for Model e, following $4.8 B losses in 2025. After three of four quarters, the deficit for 2026 stands at $3.5 B.

The Model e division currently represents only a fraction of Ford's overall business.
