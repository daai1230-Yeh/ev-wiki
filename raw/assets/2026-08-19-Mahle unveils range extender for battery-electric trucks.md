# Mahle unveils range extender for battery-electric trucks

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 18.08.2026

---

Mahle will present a new range-extender system for heavy-duty trucks at IAA Transportation in September. It can be installed in battery-electric trucks, effectively turning them into plug-in hybrids. The range extender consists of a high-voltage generator combined with a compact internal combustion engine.

Mahle has unveiled its range-extender system for heavy-duty battery-electric trucks, describing it as a world first that visitors can see at the IAA Transportation in Hanover (15–20 September 2026). The Stuttgart-based supplier states that the on-board generator will enable battery-electric trucks to "cover all daily ranges required" in the future, completely eliminating range anxiety among logistics providers. As Dr Marco Warth, Head of Corporate Development at Mahle, explained: "Our range extender is a smart 'emergency power unit' that is activated at precisely the right moment and significantly increases the range available."

Trucks equipped with this system are known as EREVs, or Extended Range Electric Vehicles – a term primarily used in the Chinese passenger car market to date. EREVs are partially electrified vehicles that can be charged externally with electricity and refuelled with fuel, similar to plug-in hybrids. However, unlike plug-in hybrids, the wheels in EREVs are always driven solely by the electric motor. When the batteries are depleted, a small internal combustion engine on board starts up and generates electricity for the motor. DHL and Scania recently tested similar technology in a field trial.

Mahle's system: a high-voltage generator with a continuous output of 110 kW and a peak output of 130 kW is driven by a compact internal combustion engine, supplying energy to the electric powertrain on demanding routes and when trucks are fully loaded. The generator, internal combustion engine, thermal and fluid management, fuel tank, AdBlue tank, and exhaust aftertreatment are integrated in a compact self-contained enclosure. Thanks to an innovative oil-cooling system, the generator achieves a power density of over 7 kW per kilogramme.

The range-extender unit is installed in the space originally designated for the battery, which consequently becomes smaller – not to reduce the purely battery-electric range, but to accommodate the combustion-based range extender, promising a greater overall range in combination with the remaining batteries. This logically results in higher CO₂ emissions than a pure BEV, though still lower than a diesel truck.

According to Mahle, the system replaces about one-third of a battery-electric truck's existing battery capacity, allowing a smaller battery. As a result, the rear axle load is reduced by about 400 kg and the total vehicle weight by about 600 kg. A range extender BEV truck can reach a total range of more than 800 km, with the battery providing about 400 km and the range extender the remaining 400 km. If the internal combustion engine — biofuel-ready thanks to Mahle components — is operated with renewable fuels such as advanced HVO100 bio-diesel, the truck has virtually zero CO2 emissions in range-extender operation. A thermal management system with 48 kW high-temperature and 15 kW low-temperature cooling capacity is designed to ensure stable operation under heavy-duty conditions.

"Our range extender is the key to disconnecting the market ramp-up of battery-electric trucks from the inadequate pace of infrastructure development," Marco Warth asserts.

Mahle is also expanding its presence among Chinese commercial vehicle manufacturers entering the European market with battery-electric trucks — at least three, Sany, SuperPanther, and Windrose, aim to establish a foothold in Europe this year. At IAA Transportation, Mahle will also unveil a new electric motor, the Mahle Contactless Transmitter (MCT) E-Motor, for electric drive axles of heavy-duty commercial vehicles. It operates without rare-earth magnets, delivers a peak output of 370 kW and torque of over 900 Nm at 3,900 rpm, with motor efficiency of 95 per cent per the VECTO cycle.

Medium and heavy-duty commercial vehicles account for around 20 percent of Mahle's business with automotive manufacturers. Order intake for the first half of 2026 in the commercial vehicle business is significantly above the previous year's period. "We expect that we will grow more strongly than the market with our commercial vehicle products over the next five years," said Arnd Franz, Chairman of the Executive Board and CEO of Mahle, adding that Mahle sees the greatest growth potential in Asia.
