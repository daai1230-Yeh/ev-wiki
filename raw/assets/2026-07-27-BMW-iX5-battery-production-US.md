# Inside BMW's Plan To Produce The iX5's Gigantic Battery Pack In The U.S.

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: Jul 27, 2026

---

Despite a rocky year for EV sales in the U.S., BMW is bringing battery production stateside. The automaker will start mass production of the new iX5's high-voltage battery pack in the U.S. starting in December, at its newly opened Plant Woodruff in South Carolina. Rather than relying on pre-assembled or imported packs, BMW is bringing production in-house — a multi-billion-dollar effort only a handful of U.S. automakers have attempted, intended to build a local supply chain, insulate against geopolitical shocks, and eventually cut costs. Since the iX5 is built at the nearby Plant Spartanburg, BMW also saves on logistics by keeping battery and vehicle production close.

Robert Engelhorn, president and CEO of BMW Manufacturing, said: "With Plant Woodruff, we are further expanding our footprint in South Carolina and strengthening the close integration of battery and vehicle production. This creates the ideal foundation for the next generation of fully electric BMW X models assembled at Plant Spartanburg."

Roughly 300 human workers and 250 robots will assemble the high-voltage packs, using virtual reality and AI to catch errors. Japanese battery maker AESC (now majority owned by China's Envision Group) supplies the cylindrical cells — plans to make those cells in South Carolina were paused last year amid a wave of battery project cancellations after the Trump administration pulled back EV incentives, so AESC will supply them from an undisclosed location instead.

The iX5's cylindrical cells stand 120mm tall, packing 30% more usable energy than the 95mm cells in the iX3. The pack measures 144 kWh usable in the U.S. and 141 kWh in Europe, translating to a BMW-estimated 435 miles of range, and can charge from 10-80% in 22 minutes at a peak rate of 460 kW.

Assembly process: engineers check each cell's voltage on arrival, cluster cells and connect cooling components, then laser-weld everything together. The pack is filled with foam that hardens around the cells for protection, sealed, and fitted with the "Energy Master" battery control unit before a final check and the 15-mile trip to Spartanburg. BMW also assembles battery packs in Debrecen (Hungary), Lower Bavaria (Germany), Shenyang (China), and San Luis Potosí (Mexico) — a "local for local" approach.
