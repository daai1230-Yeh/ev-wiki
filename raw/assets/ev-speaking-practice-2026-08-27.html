1. Warm-Up 暖身 3 min

1.If you were running an electric-vehicle battery startup and demand suddenly dried up, what industries would you look to for new customers?
2.Have you ever seen a company completely change direction — a "pivot" — because its original market slowed down? What happened?
3.Do you think government subsidies should keep supporting EVs, or should the market be left to grow on its own?

2. Read Aloud 朗讀短文 6 min

America's electric-vehicle market has hit a rough patch. A new law called the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, or OBBBA, scrapped federal subsidies for EVs and batteries, and demand has shrunk sharply as a result. To survive, a number of U.S. battery startups are now looking for a lifeline in an unexpected place: the defense industry, supplying components for drones, torpedoes, infantry radios, and even fighter jets.

美國電動車市場正陷入低潮。一項名為《大而美法案》（OBBBA）的新法案取消了聯邦政府對電動車與電池的補貼，市場需求因此大幅萎縮。為求生存，不少美國電池新創企業開始把國防產業視為救命稻草，跨足供應無人機、魚雷、步兵無線電，甚至戰鬥機所需的零組件。

The U.S. Department of Energy recently announced it would release five hundred million dollars in funding, most of which is earmarked for battery startups. The goal is to strengthen America's domestic battery supply chain, reduce reliance on foreign suppliers, and reinforce national security — all while keeping the United States competitive in the global energy race.

美國能源部（DOE）日前宣布，將撥出五億美元資金，其中大部分將流向電池新創企業。目標是強化美國本土的電池供應鏈、降低對外國供應商的依賴、鞏固國家安全，同時讓美國在全球能源競賽中保持競爭力。

Some of that money is already finding a home. Coreshell, a battery materials startup, received fifty million dollars from the Department of Energy to scale up production of silicon anode material for lithium-ion batteries — technology the defense sector is watching closely for lightweight, high-performance power sources.

部分資金已經找到落腳處。電池材料新創 Coreshell 從能源部獲得五千萬美元，用於擴大鋰離子電池矽負極材料的生產規模——這正是國防產業密切關注、能提供輕量化與高效能電力來源的技術。

Two other companies received one hundred million dollars each. Lilac Solutions, which extracts lithium from brine, will use its grant to build a processing plant in Utah, aiming to produce five thousand tons of lithium carbonate — a key precursor for battery manufacturing — every year by 2028. Nth Cycle will use its grant to build a facility that recovers "black mass" from recycled lithium-ion batteries, turning it into new lithium and nickel compounds.

另外兩家公司則各獲得一億美元。從鹽水中提煉鋰的 Lilac Solutions，將利用這筆補助在猶他州興建加工廠，目標在 2028 年前，每年生產五千噸電池製造的關鍵前驅物——碳酸鋰。Nth Cycle 則會用這筆資金興建一座設施，從回收的鋰離子電池中提煉「黑粉」，再轉化為新的鋰、鎳化合物。

Still, the scale of the defense opportunity should be kept in perspective. In 2021, the U.S. Defense Logistics Agency purchased only about two hundred million dollars worth of batteries a year. Compare that to 2025, when the U.S. auto industry alone spent close to eighteen billion dollars on battery manufacturing — a gap that shows defense contracts are a lifeline, but not yet a replacement for the EV market.

不過，國防商機的規模仍需放在正確的脈絡中看待。2021 年，美國國防後勤局（DLA）每年採購的電池金額僅約兩億美元；相較之下，2025 年美國汽車產業光是在電池製造方面就花費近一百八十億美元——這樣的落差顯示，國防合約雖是救命稻草，卻還無法取代電動車市場的規模。

South Korea's LG Energy Solution, or LGES, is facing a similar question. The company has reportedly received an approach from a major U.S. defense contractor about supplying batteries for drones and other uninhabited weapons systems. LGES already has the technology — it showcased a blood-delivery drone powered by its 2170 cylindrical battery cell at the InterBattery 2026 exhibition. But entering the defense business could hurt its ESG rating and brand image, and LG Group has historically kept its distance from defense work after splitting off from LIG Group years ago.

南韓的樂金能源解決方案（LGES）也面臨類似的抉擇。據報導，該公司已收到美國一家主要國防企業的邀約，希望供應無人機及其他無人武器系統所需的電池。LGES 其實已具備相關技術——曾在 InterBattery 2026 展會上，展示搭載其 2170 圓柱型電池的血液運輸無人機。但跨入國防業務，可能損害其 ESG 評級與品牌形象，而樂金集團自多年前與 LIG 集團分拆後，一直刻意與國防產業保持距離。

The numbers, though, are hard to ignore. The global military battery market is projected to grow from two point four billion dollars in 2025 to five point two two billion dollars by 2034, with North America alone accounting for thirty-six point two five percent of that market. And starting in October 2027, U.S. law will ban the military from buying batteries made by six Chinese companies, including CATL and BYD — opening the door even wider for South Korean suppliers like LGES.

然而，這些數字確實難以忽視。全球軍用電池市場預計將從 2025 年的 24 億美元，成長到 2034 年的 52.2 億美元，其中北美市場就佔了 36.25%。而從 2027 年 10 月起，美國法律將禁止軍方採購包括寧德時代、比亞迪在內的六家中國企業所製造的電池——這也為 LGES 等南韓供應商，開啟了更大的機會之窗。

5. Useful Expressions 實用句型 3 min

"___ has become a lifeline for ___."
用來描述某個新機會成為某產業／某公司的救命稻草。例：Defense contracts have become a lifeline for struggling battery startups.
"There's a trade-off between ___ and ___."
用來說明兩件事之間必須權衡取捨。例：There's a trade-off between short-term revenue and long-term brand reputation.
"It remains to be seen whether ___."
用來表示某件事的結果仍不確定，適合評論未來趨勢。例：It remains to be seen whether LGES will actually accept the defense contract.
"On one hand ___, but on the other hand ___."
用來呈現一件事的正反兩面，討論爭議話題時非常實用。例：On one hand, defense contracts bring stable revenue, but on the other hand, they risk the company's public image.
"This could set a precedent for ___."
用來說明某個決定可能為未來立下先例、產生連鎖影響。例：LGES's decision could set a precedent for how other Asian battery makers approach the defense market.

6. Discussion 討論題 5 min

1.Do you think it makes sense for EV battery startups to pivot toward defense contracts when civilian demand slows down? What are the risks of relying on government and military spending?
2.LG Energy Solution is worried that supplying batteries for weapons could hurt its ESG rating and brand image. Is consumer and investor backlash against "dual-use" technology justified, or is it an outdated concern in today's geopolitical climate?
3.Starting in 2027, the U.S. plans to ban Chinese battery suppliers like CATL and BYD from military applications. Is this necessary for national security, or could it just raise costs and slow down innovation?
4.Compare the numbers: in 2025, the U.S. auto industry spent about eighteen billion dollars on battery manufacturing, while defense-related battery procurement was only around two hundred million dollars a few years earlier. Given this gap, is the "defense pivot" a real solution for battery startups, or just a stopgap?
5.Some critics argue that cutting EV subsidies, like under the OBBBA, forces companies into markets they wouldn't otherwise enter. Should government policy stay neutral toward industries, or is it fine for policy to actively steer companies toward certain outcomes?
6.If you were an executive at a battery company like LGES, would you accept a defense contract? What would weigh most heavily in your decision — profit, ethics, geopolitics, or brand reputation?
