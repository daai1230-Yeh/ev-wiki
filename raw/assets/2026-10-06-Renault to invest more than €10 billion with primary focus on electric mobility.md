# Renault to invest more than €10 billion with primary focus on electric mobility

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-10-05

---

Renault intends to invest more than ten billion euros in France over the next five years. The funds will primarily flow into electric vehicles and more affordable models. However, the company is tying these plans to political and social framework conditions.

Renault CEO François Provost revealed the plans in an interview with radio station France Inter. According to the Handelsblatt, the funds will primarily support development of electric vehicles and more affordable models.

The commitment comes with conditions: Provost emphasised that the investments will only proceed if the political and social environment aligns with the company's expectations, without initially specifying the conditions. Over the past five years, Renault has already invested around €13 billion in France, with a key focus on transforming domestic production towards electric mobility.

The plans align with the futuREady strategy programme presented in spring 2026. By 2030, Renault aims to launch 22 new models in Europe, 16 of them fully electric. In parallel, Renault is developing a new 800-volt platform, RGEV Medium 2.0, along with new battery and powertrain technologies. Under futuREady, Renault will integrate range, charging power and manufacturing costs more closely, and use more affordable cell chemistries for high-volume models.

At the end of September, Renault announced a €600 million investment in its Spanish plants in Valladolid and Palencia, where two electric cars based on RGEV Medium 2.0 will be built; Valladolid will also get a new battery assembly line.

France remains the central production hub for Renault's electric cars. The ElectriCity cluster, established in northern France in 2021, produces the Mégane E-Tech, Scénic E-Tech, Renault 5 E-Tech and Alpine A290.

Demand for electric cars in France has risen sharply: in September, media reports indicated that 42 per cent of new passenger car registrations were electric, a new record, attributed to significantly higher fuel prices since the start of the Iran war. Renault manufactured around 500,000 vehicles in France in 2025 and expects an increase of at least 25 per cent in 2026, which would bring production in France to more than 625,000 vehicles. Renault has not yet specified which plants and models will benefit from the new investment.
