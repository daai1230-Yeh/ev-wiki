# China Has An Iron Grip On EV Batteries. Here's America's Plan To Break It

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-11-04

---

- The Trump administration is doubling down on building a domestic battery supply chain by 2030, covering cells and packs as well as extraction, refining and subcomponents.
- Billions of dollars of battery projects were canceled last year.

The U.S. Department of Energy last week released a three-step plan to localize the battery supply chain and reduce reliance on China. It may not mention EVs, but the EV market could be among the biggest beneficiaries if it succeeds.

Citing BloombergNEF, the DOE said battery demand would grow fourfold between 2025 and 2035, from 179 GWh to 707 GWh, across transportation, the grid, AI data centers, consumer electronics and drones. U.S. cell and pack manufacturing has improved, but raw material mining and subcomponents such as anodes, cathodes, electrolytes and separators remain largely overseas, mostly in China.

The plan's objectives:

1. Mine-to-market: establish a continuous domestic value chain by 2030.
2. Discovery-to-deployment: achieve technology and manufacturing leadership by 2035.
3. Enable affordable, safe, and reliable storage applications for the American public.

Part one prioritizes investment across upstream (mining), midstream (refining and engineered materials) and downstream (cells, packs, end products), with targeted investments in extraction, recycling, refining and equipment manufacturing. Part two focuses on next-generation batteries — more affordable, longer lasting, higher performing and less reliant on critical minerals (e.g. solid-state) — and getting them past the "valley of death" from lab to market.

The Biden administration shared similar objectives in 2021 in far more detail, focused on clean energy and jobs; the current blueprint is thinner and leans on national security, the grid, humanoid robots and AI data-center power demand. According to think tank E2, the U.S. canceled, closed or downsized battery projects worth $42 billion between 2022 and 2026, costing over 85,000 jobs (across clean energy broadly: $137 billion, over 240,000 jobs).

The author concludes the blueprint is a step in the right direction, reflecting how the landscape has shifted: EV sales have been up and down while demand for stationary storage has surged, and automakers could still benefit over the long term.
