# Vilnius Airport replaces diesel buses with battery-electric buses

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-16

---

Vilnius Airport is replacing its entire diesel bus fleet for passenger transfers with twelve electric buses from Cobus. The new vehicles will enter service on 16 November. The investment totals €7.2 million, including maintenance and repairs.

Lithuanian Airports (LTOU) has unveiled twelve new battery-electric buses at Vilnius Airport for transporting passengers between the terminal and aircraft. The vehicles fully replace the airport's previous diesel buses. With this transition, the airport aims to reduce both local emissions and noise on the apron. According to LTOU, replacing the diesel buses will avoid around 58 tonnes of CO₂ emissions annually.

Three dual charging stations have been installed at the airport for the new fleet, enabling up to six buses to charge simultaneously. The electricity used will come from renewable sources. Depending on operating conditions, the new buses are expected to run for up to three days on a single charge. By comparison, the previous diesel buses could operate for around twelve hours on a full tank, according to LTOU.

The extended operating range will enable the airport to plan vehicle rotations and charging schedules more flexibly. This is particularly intended to simplify the organisation of passenger transfers during ongoing airport operations. The electric buses, manufactured by Cobus, have been specifically designed for airport use. Each bus can carry up to 100 passengers. Wide doors on both sides of the vehicle and a lowered entrance are designed to speed up passenger boarding and alighting, while making access easier for travellers with luggage, prams or reduced mobility.

The buses also feature automatic climate control. Thanks to their electric drivetrains, they are significantly quieter than the previous diesel models.

## Cobus to handle maintenance for five years

Cobus will remain responsible for the vehicles after they enter service. Under a five-year contract, the manufacturer will handle maintenance, repairs and the supply of spare parts. This also includes remote monitoring of the vehicles' condition and 24/7 technical support.

With the electrification of its passenger buses, LTOU is also advancing its net-zero strategy. The company aims to become climate-neutral by 2030 and reduce CO₂ emissions from airport-controlled operations to zero by 2040.

Electrification in Vilnius is not limited to the airport. In May, the municipal transport operator VVT ordered 112 all-electric MAN Lion's City E buses, including 84 solo buses and 28 articulated buses. Lithuania is also supporting the expansion of electric bus fleets with EU funds. Since 2026, transport operators in the country have been required to procure only buses with alternative drivetrains.

From 16 November, Lithuanian Airports will also take over passenger transport at Vilnius Airport itself. A dedicated unit has been established for this purpose, employing 35 drivers.
