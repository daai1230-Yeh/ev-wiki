# 'Completely Insane': China Has 650 New Car Models So Far This Year

**Author**: Rob Stumpf | **Date**: 2026-07-17

---

Chinese automakers have released around 650 new models since January, of which about 30 models per month are completely brand new cars. Faster development cycles and consumer needs are driving the innovation.

The world has been talking about Chinese EVs more and more lately. They've already made a splash in Europe, and now some Chinese firms are gearing up to enter Canada too. But automakers in China are flooding the local market with hundreds of new models and refreshes this year alone.

Around 650 models have launched, according to Bloomberg — roughly four cars either released or refreshed every single day between January 1st and June 30th of 2026. BYD's Executive Vice President, He Zhiqi, called that number "completely insane" on social media, adding that the domestic market in China is "not just fierce, but brutal."

That 650 figure includes facelifts, refreshes, and all new models. Data from the China Automotive Technology and Research Center shows about 30 all-new vehicles (a "genuinely brand-new model" with no previous entry in China's national vehicle database) have been released each month since January.

For comparison, only 29 new or refreshed vehicles were launched for the U.S. market in 2024, and a Bank of America Securities study predicts just 159 new models will launch in the U.S. over the next four years — a stark contrast to the 650 cars launched in China over six months.

Part of the reason for the uptick is a significant decrease in development cycles. Automakers are competing with one another to gain market share in an industry with slipping sales, ever-increasing competition, and an ongoing price war, prompting them to one-up rivals with updated vehicles. AI has also played a role, from vehicle design to in-car tech, alongside advances in driver assistance systems, denser batteries, and charging tech.

BYD's Zhiqi welcomed the competition in the spirit of fueling innovation. The automaker has said it intends to become the world's largest automaker within the next five years, and it has once again taken the EV sales crown from Tesla. "Competition also breeds prosperity," said Zhiqi.
