# Tesla Q3 results: full order books, but declining figures

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-10-22

---

Tesla's revenue is stagnating, with the US electric car manufacturer witnessing a significant drop in profits during the third quarter. However, the Americans' car business is not doing poorly - order books are full. The reasons lie elsewhere.

Tesla is currently struggling to grow its automotive business. Revenue from the automotive division fell by three per cent year-over-year between July and September – to $20.56 billion. Only through ancillary business areas has Tesla managed to match last year's total revenue ($28 billion). The result: zero per cent growth, but no decline either.

Tesla also roughly achieved the $28 billion revenue mark in the second quarter of this year. However, it is notable that in Q3 2025 and Q2 2026, Tesla reported billion-dollar profits at similar revenue levels. This is no longer the case: GAAP net income plummeted to $787 million in the last quarter – a massive decline of 43 per cent compared to the same quarter last year.

## A quarter marked by pull-forward purchases

Importantly, Q3 2025 should be treated with caution as a basis for comparison, as it was influenced by a surge in pull-forward purchases ahead of the expiration of US incentives, which artificially inflated vehicle sales. Additionally, revenue from CO2 certificates ("regulatory credits") has declined. These fell from $417 million in Q3 2025 to just $113 million now, which already explains a significant portion of the profit drop.

Another indication is the margin: with an operating margin of 2.6 per cent, Tesla has slipped significantly. Only in Q2 2026 did it dip further to 1.4 per cent. Tesla itself cites the lower average selling price of its vehicles as one of many contributing factors. Tesla is currently far removed from the quarterly profits exceeding $2 or $3 billion seen during the peak periods of 2021 and 2022, or double-digit margins.

## Gigafactory Berlin achieves record production

Tesla highlights the positives: "We achieved record deliveries in several markets – including Canada, Australia, Japan, and Taiwan – compared to the previous quarter... we have achieved record production at Gigafactory Berlin." And: "We have begun series production of the Tesla Semi, our Class 8 heavy-duty truck."

After a mixed 2025, the first three quarters of 2026 have shown renewed consistency – 464,000 vehicles produced and just over 486,000 delivered in Q3. Deliveries exceeding production by more than 22,000 vehicles suggests inventory was reduced.

## High activity on the production lines

The 464,000 vehicles manufactured were last matched with nearly 470,000 units in Q3 2024. That is +3.7 per cent versus the same quarter last year and +2.7 per cent versus Q2 2026. Deliveries of 486,000 are only +1.2 per cent versus Q2 2026, but that quarter was itself up around 25 per cent from the seasonally weaker first quarter. Last year Tesla nearly reached 500,000 in Q3 due to pull-forward purchases.

Tesla only mentions in passing it "ended the quarter with the largest backlog for passenger cars in four years", and has long since stopped providing production or sales targets. The report increasingly focuses on future business areas: Super Intelligence (SI), robotics and renewable energy.

## Reduced capital expenditure for the current year

Tesla announces progress on FSD (Supervised) and the expansion of its Robotaxi fleet in the US, plus the debut of the flying Roadster prototype. Elon Musk stated that Tesla could begin delivering its humanoid 'Optimus' robot to external companies by the end of 2027. Capital expenditures are now expected to be around $20 billion for the year.
