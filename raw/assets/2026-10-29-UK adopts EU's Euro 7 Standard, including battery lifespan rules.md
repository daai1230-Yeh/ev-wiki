# UK adopts EU's Euro 7 Standard, including battery lifespan rules

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 2026-10-28

---

The UK Department for Transport has confirmed that Great Britain will adopt the Euro 7 emissions standard. To minimize bureaucratic effort for automakers, there will be no separate GB-specific approval process; instead, EU and UNECE type approvals will apply so that vehicle models can be sold in parallel in both GB and the EU without additional testing.

From November 2027, the UK will require all newly registered passenger cars and light commercial vehicles to comply with the Euro 7 standard. For the first time, these regulations will extend beyond exhaust emissions. The new standard also addresses environmental impacts relevant to electric vehicles, primarily through mandatory limits on brake and tyre wear particles, as well as strict requirements for battery lifespan.

Vehicle brakes will be subject to fixed limits on particulate matter. Electric vehicles have an advantage here, since they recover a large portion of braking energy through regenerative braking. Tyre wear limits will also be introduced gradually to reduce microplastic pollution, affecting heavier, larger electric vehicles most.

Euro 7 also establishes legal minimum standards for the storage capacity of EV batteries. The battery in battery-electric and plug-in hybrid passenger cars must retain at least 80% of its original capacity after five years or 100,000 kilometres. A minimum of 72% is required after eight years or 160,000 kilometres.

In addition, Euro 7 requires manufacturers to install standardised battery state-of-health (SOH) monitors, creating a basis for EV buyers to compare battery condition across manufacturers, models and powertrains.

The UK is adopting all Euro 7 rules based on existing EU and UNECE standards, so manufacturers will not need additional UK-specific testing. For internal combustion engines, the nitrogen oxide limits for diesel passenger cars will be aligned with those for petrol vehicles (maximum 60 mg/km), and emission values must be maintained over ten years or 200,000 kilometres in real-world driving.

For new vehicle types in the case of trucks and buses, the deadline is May 2028, with May 2029 for all new registrations. Automakers with fewer than 2,500 vehicles per year have an extended deadline until 1 July 2030. Existing vehicles are not affected, and the rules for environmental zones such as London's ULEZ remain unchanged for the time being.
