# Nearly 10,000 EVs Were Tested For Battery Degradation. These 2 Models Won Out.

**Author**: Andrei Nedelea | **Date**: Jul 14, 2026

---

A Swedish analysis of nearly 10,000 battery tests found that two EVs retained more than 97% of their capacity after 62,000 miles. The study comes from Sweden's Carla, a used car marketplace, which compiled 9,954 EV battery health tests conducted between 2022 and 2026 to see which models retain the most capacity after 100,000 kilometers (~62,000 miles).

The top three performers were all Korean EVs. The overall winner was the Kia e-Niro (Niro Electric in the U.S.), whose 64-kWh battery pack retained 97.25% of its initial capacity on average, followed by the mechanically related Hyundai Kona Electric at 97.18%. The Kia EV6 (77.4 kWh) came third at 95.95%, followed by the Volvo XC40 Recharge (94.70%), Polestar 2 (94.35%), and BMW i3 with the larger 42.2 kWh pack (93.77%).

One theory: the e-Niro and Kona charge at less than 80 kW, below what many newer EVs handle, which may reduce heat and current linked to accelerated cell aging. Notably, Hyundai replaced entire battery packs in tens of thousands of early Kona EVs after a recall tied to faulty LG Energy Solution cells, which may have skewed the Kona's results upward.

Degradation among the top 20 vehicles tested was under 10%. Even the last-place finisher, the Volkswagen ID.3, retained an average 91.79% battery health after 100,000 km. The study reinforces recent research suggesting EV battery degradation, while real, is typically gradual rather than catastrophic.
