# EU accelerates approvals for power grids and charging stations

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-11-18

---

The European Parliament and EU member states have reached a provisional agreement to streamline approval procedures for grid connections, charging stations, storage systems and more. Charging stations of up to one megawatt in "artificial structures" are expected to become exempt from approval.

The measures are part of the 'European grids package'. The Commission presented a first draft in December 2025, Parliament set its position in the summer, and now a provisional agreement with member states has been reached. Formal approval by the full Parliament and Council is largely seen as a formality.

The draft raises the threshold at which approval is required for small solar installations, energy storage systems and charging stations from 100 to 200 kW. Charging stations with total capacity of 1 MW or less on 'artificial structures' will no longer require approval; the Greens, who initiated this, interpret the term to include motorway service areas.

## Shorter deadlines, higher thresholds

A maximum of six months is set for approval of standalone energy storage systems and charging stations; three months for co-located solar and storage. Grid connection deadlines will regularly be limited to three months, up to six with justification, with exceptions for isolated or small interconnected systems.

Mandatory community participation will apply to projects from 7 MW (previously 10 MW). The agreement also establishes a uniform national digital portal for all approval steps and an EU-wide approval framework for grid infrastructure.

"We took a very important step tonight to make sure that we get a faster rollout of renewables and grid infrastructure," said lead MEP Niels Fuglsang (S&D, Denmark).

Borys Budka (EPP, Poland), Chair of the ITRE Committee: "Acceleration and simplification are not mere political slogans. Progress on digitalisation, faster grid connections for solar panels on Europeans' rooftops or energy storage systems are examples of concrete results."

A breakthrough is the expansion of the principle that renewable and grid projects are of "overriding public interest", removing administrative hurdles; deviations are allowed only in justified cases such as cultural heritage or defence. Member states may also stipulate that a lack of authority response constitutes "tacit approval".

## Member states given 1.5 years for implementation

Transmission network construction takes about ten years, more than half spent on approvals; renewable project approvals can take up to nine years and storage one to seven years. Once approved and published in the Official Journal, member states will have 18 months to transpose the directive.
