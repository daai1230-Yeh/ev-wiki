# World Premiere of the Mercedes GLA: Up to 320 kW of Charging Power and 657 km of Range

**Author**: Kasimir Hupe | **Date**: 29.07.2026 (updated 30 July 2026)

---

Mercedes-Benz presents the all-new GLA in Warsaw. The compact SUV has a range of up to 657 kilometers and charges at up to 320 kW. The electric car can be ordered immediately at prices starting from 48,599 euros. However, a look at the data sheets shows that the peak values only apply to two of the three models.

With the new GLA, Mercedes has reversed its usual approach: previously, the carmaker launched the ICE model first, followed later by an electric variant. This time, it will debut three battery-electric models in November, while hybrid versions will not hit the road until early 2027. For the brand's best-selling compact model, this is a clear statement—and a signal to the industry: electric drive has arrived at Mercedes, even in the volume segment of the A-Class.

The GLA is built on the 800-volt architecture that Mercedes first introduced with the electric CLA and also used in the electric GLB. The entry-level GLA 200 features a 58 kWh LFP battery, while the GLA 250+ and GLA 350 4MATIC models are equipped with an 85 kWh NMC battery. The latter offers a range of up to 657 kilometres (WLTP). Notably, all three variants reach a top speed of 210 km/h. With a starting price of €48,599, the GLA is Mercedes' most affordable electric SUV.

**From 10 to 80% in 22 minutes**

Thanks to its 800-volt architecture, the Mercedes GLA delivers impressive charging performance: the GLA 250+ and GLA 350 4MATIC can draw up to 320 kW at an 800-volt charging station, replenishing the battery from 10 to 80 per cent in just 22 minutes.

During this time, nearly 60 kWh of energy is transferred to the vehicle, corresponding to an average charging power of around 162 kW across the entire charging curve. Such average values are more meaningful than peak figures, as high peak power is of little use if it drops after just four minutes. Mercedes states that ten minutes of charging provides up to 270 kilometres of additional WLTP range—a figure that hardly any other electric vehicle in the compact segment can match.

However, the base model lags behind: the GLA 200 uses a lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery with 58 kWh of usable capacity and charges at a maximum of 200 kW. In ten minutes, it regains 145 to 165 kilometres of range. Due to its smaller battery, it takes just over 20 minutes to charge from 10 to 80 per cent. This makes it nominally faster than the larger models—but with less energy stored. LFP chemistry is cheaper and more durable, tolerates daily full charging better, and is cobalt-free. The trade-off is its lower energy density, which affects range.

One detail that could cause frustration in practice is the DC converter, which enables the GLA to charge at 400-volt charging stations. However, this component is only available as an optional extra. Those who do not order it will be limited to the not yet widely available 800-volt chargers. For alternating current (AC) charging, 11 kW is standard, while 22 kW is available as an option and halves the charging time at home wallboxes or AC charging stations.

Looking ahead, Mercedes will introduce a fourth variant in early 2027 with a 71 kWh NMC battery, closing the noticeable gap between the 58 and 85 kilowatt-hour options.

**Solid consumption, but heavy**

At around 15 kWh per 100 kilometres, the 4.57-metre-long SUV, weighing over two tonnes, achieves an excellent efficiency figure. This is primarily due to its aerodynamics: with a drag coefficient (cW) of 0.27, the new GLA is significantly more streamlined than its predecessor.

However, the weight is less impressive: 2,145 to 2,235 kilograms is substantial for a compact SUV, and the payload of 480 kilograms is quickly reached with five occupants.

**Not everyone will like the illuminated grille**

The new GLA measures 4.57 metres in length, 1.87 metres in width, and 1.60 metres in height, making it 20 millimetres lower than its predecessor. The front is controversial: the grille, featuring 608 backlit polycarbonate dots, 158 micro-LEDs, and a very large, centrally placed star, marks the first time Mercedes has brought this design element to the compact segment.

**AI assistants, software, and CO₂ footprint**

Eight cameras, five radar sensors, and twelve ultrasonic sensors feed a water-cooled control unit with a computing power of 254 tera-operations per second, dedicated to driver assistance functions delivered via over-the-air updates. The MBUX Virtual Assistant, powered by generative AI, combines models from ChatGPT, Microsoft Bing, and Google Gemini.

Across the entire value chain, the electric GLA reduces its CO₂ footprint by around 40 per cent compared to the current ICE model.

**Price and positioning**

At €48,599, the GLA 200 is the "entry-level" model in the trio but offers the lowest range. The €6,378 premium for the GLA 250+ buys an additional 210 kilometres of range and the full 320 kW charging power. The GLA 350 4MATIC costs another €3,130 more, provides all-wheel drive, accelerates to 100 km/h in 5.4 seconds, and has a towing capacity of two tonnes but loses 14 kilometres of range.

Technically, the electric GLA and its big brother, the electric GLB, are twins, sharing the MMA platform, the 800-volt architecture, the 320 kW charging power, and the battery options.
