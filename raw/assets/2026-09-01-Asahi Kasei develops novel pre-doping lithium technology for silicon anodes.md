# Asahi Kasei develops novel pre-doping lithium technology for silicon anodes

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-08-31

---

Asahi Kasei has developed a new pre-lithiation technology for lithium-ion batteries with silicon-based anodes. In internal tests, the Japanese company claims to have increased the energy density of an NMC cell by 10 per cent using the new technology. Tests with potential customers are now set to follow.

Silicon is regarded as a promising anode material for increasing the energy density of lithium-ion batteries. In practice, it is often mixed with graphite. Here, one of the challenges is the irreversible loss of lithium during the first charge and discharge cycle. To compensate for this effect, additional active cathode material may be required, amongst other things – which increases the amount of material used and the cost of the battery cell.

This is where the pre-lithiation process developed by Asahi Kasei comes into play. The company uses relatively inexpensive lithium carbonate as an additional source of lithium. Its decomposition voltage is normally above the typical operating voltage range of lithium-ion batteries, which has made it difficult to use for pre-lithiation until now. Special electrolyte additives are now designed to enable decomposition even at lower voltages. During the first charge cycle, this provides additional lithium, which is intended to compensate for the initial capacity loss of the silicon-containing anode.

In internal tests, Asahi Kasei evaluated the process using an NMC cell whose anode consists of 90 per cent graphite and 10 per cent silicon monoxide (SiO). The energy density reportedly increased by 10 per cent. The company also cites improved cycle stability and lower costs per watt-hour. The company also does not provide specific details regarding absolute energy density, cycle life, or cost savings.

Asahi Kasei also promises benefits for industrial implementation. Pre-lithiation is said to be implementable without significant changes to existing production lines and to be compatible with various cathode and anode materials. Further testing will be needed to determine whether these advantages hold true under real-world production conditions and with different cell chemistries.

Asahi Kasei now plans to test the technology in proof-of-concept projects in collaboration with customers worldwide and subsequently commercialise it through licensing agreements. The company has not yet provided a timeline for potential mass production.

At the same time, the Japanese conglomerate is expanding its business in lithium-ion battery components. Just a few days ago, Asahi Kasei opened a new coating line for battery separators in the U.S. state of North Carolina. Together with a new separator factory in Canada, it is intended to strengthen the North American supply chain for electric vehicle batteries and stationary energy storage systems.
