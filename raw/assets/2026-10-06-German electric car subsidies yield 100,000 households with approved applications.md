# German electric car subsidies yield 100,000 households with approved applications

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-10-05

---

The electric car subsidy has been available for application in Germany since mid-May and has shown high demand. The first 100,000 applications, with a total value of over 430 million euros, have now been approved by the Federal Ministry for the Environment – 90 per cent for purely electric cars, and the remaining ten per cent for plug-in hybrids.

The German Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA) has recently begun publishing monthly statistics on electric car incentives, including key details about applicants and funded models. The third evaluation as of 1 October has been published, and the Federal Ministry for the Environment has highlighted the milestone of 100,000 approved applications, which was exceeded in early October.

Exactly 97,790 applications had been approved by 1 October, compared to 52,473 a month earlier. Federal Environment Minister Carsten Schneider commented that the incentive programme has far exceeded expectations and has already helped 100,000 households protect themselves from high fuel prices, and that the social scaling is working: households that previously would not have considered an electric car can now afford one, helped by the leasing option and by German manufacturers bringing compact cars to market.

The EV incentive programme was established on 19 May but applies retroactively from 1 January 2026. The bonus targets private households and supports the purchase or leasing of new battery-electric, fuel cell, and certain plug-in hybrid and range-extender vehicles.

As of 1 October, €432 million had been disbursed — compared to €230 million as of 1 September and €117 million as of 1 August 2026. 91.6 per cent of applications relate to purely electric cars, with 8.4 per cent for plug-in hybrids. Applications are still almost evenly split between purchase and leasing.

More than half (52.3 per cent) of all approved applications fall into the lowest income bracket (taxable household annual income up to €45,000). The two highest brackets account for less than 3,000. Around 65,000 applications, two-thirds of recipients, come from households without a child relevant for funding, so many do not reach the full €6,000. On average, funding is €4,418 per household (vs €4,380 and €4,350 in the previous two months).

The BYD Seal U DM-i is the most popular plug-in hybrid with 1,436 applications. Premium vehicles are not excluded (no hard price cap, only an income limit), so some electric Porsches also appear.
