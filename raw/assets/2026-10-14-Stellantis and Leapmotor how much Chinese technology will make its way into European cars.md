# Stellantis and Leapmotor: how much Chinese technology will make its way into European cars?

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-10-13

---

*Summary of the electrive report from the Paris Motor Show (condensed, not verbatim).*

The Stellantis-Leapmotor tie-up has grown from an investment and international distribution deal into production and development. Leapmotor builds the B10 at Stellantis' Zaragoza plant (production officially started, announced in Paris alongside the European debut of the B03), and Opel is co-developing an electric SUV with Leapmotor for 2028.

**Strategy.** Since the FaSTLAne 2030 strategy (May), Stellantis pursues shared platforms and efficiency while keeping brand identities distinct. Leapmotor Europe CEO Danilo Annese: "We are in Europe, and we want to stay in Europe." He frames localisation mainly as a logistics and delivery-stability matter rather than tariffs or cost; more models (e.g. the B05) could be built locally. Stellantis is shifting from distribution partner to technology and industrial partner; no new concrete projects were named.

**The Opel SUV.** About 4.50 m long, positioned between Frontera and Grandland, built in Zaragoza, launching 2028. Development began mid-2026 and the styling freeze is done. Opel CEO Florian Huettl says it is not a rebadged Leapmotor: Opel handles chassis, steering, lighting, seats, design and UX; Leapmotor contributes E/E architecture, batteries, electric powertrains and its supplier network. LFP batteries are the affordability lever. Huettl admits Chinese makers lead in electronic components, architectures, batteries and powertrains: "Less planning, more development." Opel expects to develop the SUV in under two years and wants to learn from Leapmotor's OTA-capable software. Reference point: Leapmotor B10 (4.51 m, 56.2/67.1 kWh LFP, up to 168 kW charging, 434 km WLTP), though component carry-over is unconfirmed. Through 2030 Opel plans only this one directly co-developed model.

**Group level.** Stellantis owns 20% of Leapmotor and 51% of Leapmotor International (49% Leapmotor). CEO Antonio Filosa says the relationship has moved from sales to engineering and production; European plant utilisation was about 60% in 2025, so Leapmotor models could fill capacity. He repeatedly calls for a "level playing field" for anyone selling in Europe.

**Peugeot differs.** Brand CEO Alain Favey: "We have absolutely no intention of developing anything with Leapmotor for Europe." Peugeot's Dongfeng collaboration produces China-only models.

**Takeaway.** The open question is how far Chinese core technology can extend into European brands without reducing their independence to design. The Opel SUV is the test case.
