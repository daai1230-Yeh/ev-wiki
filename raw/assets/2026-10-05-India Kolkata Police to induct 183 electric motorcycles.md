# India: Kolkata Police to induct 183 electric motorcycles

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 2026-10-02

---

The police department of Kolkata, the capital of the eastern Indian state West Bengal, has announced plans to add 183 electric motorcycles to its fleet. It has taken delivery of the first 60 units, and once it procures the remaining 123 units, the rollout will represent one of the largest electric motorcycle deployments by any police force in the country.

Kolkata Police plans to use the new electric motorcycles primarily for operations under the 112 Emergency Response Support System (ERSS), Commissioner Ajay Nand said at the flag-off ceremony of the first batch. He also indicated that the department is likely to add electric cars to its fleet soon. All 183 electric motorcycles will be Ultraviolette Automotive's X-47 adventure tourer, although not in any of the regular configurations the company offers to private buyers.

The Bengaluru (Karnataka)-based startup has developed a new patrol version called 'Skyforce', and the 183 units it is supplying to Kolkata Police mark the first deployment of this configuration. The company has equipped these bikes with additional features such as LED blinkers, sirens, hooters, a Public Address (PA) system, microphone holders, walkie-talkie holders, and side panniers. Along with the custom equipment, special decals all around the motorcycles instantly distinguish them from the X-47 versions the company offers to private buyers.

Ultraviolette manufactures the X-47 in two power-electric variants, and the new Skyforce version appears to be in the base variant, which pairs a motor producing 27 kW and 90 Nm of torque with a 7.1 kWh battery pack. This setup allows accelerating from 0 to 100 km/h in 8.2 seconds, achieving a top speed of 145 km/h, and travelling up to 211 km on a single charge, as per the Indian Driving Cycle (IDC).

The top variant combines a motor generating 30 kW and 100 Nm of torque with a 10.3 kWh battery pack. It reaches 0 to 100 km/h in 8.1 seconds but maxes out at the same 145 km/h. It offers a significant gain in practicality, extending the IDC range to 323 km.

Commenting on the deployment, Ultraviolette CEO & Co-founder Narayan Subramaniam said, "This is a landmark moment for Ultraviolette, as we become the first electric two-wheeler manufacturer by scale to have our motorcycles inducted into the police force. Beyond the significance of this milestone, we take immense pride in knowing that our technology will now contribute to the everyday duties of police officers who work tirelessly to maintain law and order and keep our cities safe."

"Adopting electric mobility is a major step towards sustainable and eco-friendly policing. These modern electric bikes will enhance our operational effectiveness, patrolling, rapid response, and community outreach while supporting environmental sustainability," Kolkata Police Commissioner Ajay Nand said.
