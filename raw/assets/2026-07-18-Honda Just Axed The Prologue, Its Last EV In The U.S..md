# Honda Just Axed The Prologue, Its Last EV In The U.S.

**Author**: Tim Levin | **Date**: 2026-07-16

---

Say goodbye to the Honda Prologue. Honda confirmed it will discontinue sales of its sole electric vehicle in the U.S. market later this year, after the 2026 model year. The move follows the demise of the Acura ZDX in late 2025 and the cancellation of the 0 Series EVs, leaving Honda without a fully electric offering in America.

"Customer demand for EV models has shifted significantly over the past 18 months. This decision allows us to better align our product offerings with the needs of our customers and market conditions, as well as our long-term goals," Honda told InsideEVs, adding that Prologue buyers will "continue to receive full support through our dealer network, including service, parts, and warranty coverage."

Policy changes advanced by the Trump administration and Congress have upended America's EV industry — as clean-car regulations eased and the loss of the tax credit dampened demand, carmakers pulled a slew of EVs from the market, including the Ford F-150 Lightning, Nissan Ariya, Volkswagen ID.4, and Volvo EX30. EVs are broadly unprofitable for legacy automakers, so the retreat isn't entirely surprising, though Q2 EV sales had shown the strongest signs of recovery since the tax credit's expiration.

The Prologue, built by General Motors on its Ultium architecture, was a surprise hit in its first year on sale in 2024, selling over 33,000 units and becoming one of the top-selling non-Tesla EVs — helped along by heavy incentives, including a $7,500 price cut earlier this year to offset the end of the federal credit.

Unlike other manufacturers' EV cuts, this leaves Honda with no EV strategy at all in the U.S. In March, Honda scrapped its next-generation "0-Series" EV platform, which was to yield a software-defined sedan this year and at least two crossovers after, and put its Sony joint venture (the Afeela EV project) on ice. A Honda spokesperson said the company is refocusing resources on hybrids, while still "laying the groundwork for introducing a highly competitive EV hardware platform for the future, so that Honda will be well prepared to meet demand when it emerges."

Honda said canceling the 0 Series was its best financial option, warning that starting production "in current business environment where the demand for EVs is declining significantly would likely result in further losses over the long term." The risk: if EV demand rebounds, as most industry watchers expect over time, Honda may have nothing to offer customers.
