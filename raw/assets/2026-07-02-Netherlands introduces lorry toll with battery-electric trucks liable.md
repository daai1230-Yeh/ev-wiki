# Netherlands introduces lorry toll – with battery-electric trucks liable

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-07-02

---

As of 1 July, a truck toll has come into effect in the Netherlands. Trucks weighing 3.5 tonnes or more must now pay per kilometer driven for road usage. The cleaner and lighter the vehicle, the lower the per-kilometer fee. However, even electric trucks must contribute a small amount.

The new toll applies to lorries in vehicle classes N2 and N3 — trucks with a gross vehicle weight rating exceeding 3.5 tonnes — and came into effect this week on nearly all Dutch motorways plus numerous provincial and municipal roads. Distances travelled are recorded via a mandatory toll transponder (OBU) linked to a contract with a toll service provider. Billing is calculated per kilometre, based on the vehicle's weight and emission class: four weight categories (3.5–12 tonnes; 12–18 tonnes; 18–32 tonnes; over 32 tonnes) and five CO₂ emission classes (1 to 5, with 5 being the "cleanest," which includes battery-electric lorries).

Battery-electric lorries are not exempt from the toll, unlike in Germany or Switzerland — the only exemption applies to battery-electric vans up to 4.25 tonnes. A battery-electric lorry with a gross vehicle weight rating of 32 tonnes or more now incurs a charge of €0.038 per kilometre, compared to €0.165 per kilometre for a comparable diesel lorry in the mid-range CO₂ emission class 3.

A significant portion of the toll revenue will be reinvested in the road transport sector, including subsidies for companies investing in battery-electric lorries, charging infrastructure, and more efficient transport solutions. The toll's introduction coincides with the abolition of the Eurovignette requirement in the Netherlands, and the domestic vehicle tax for lorries up to 12 tonnes is also being scrapped. For heavier lorries, Dutch vehicle tax will be reduced to the European minimum level starting in 2027, with a temporary zero rate for the N3 vehicle group from 1 July 2026 to 1 January 2027.

Vincent Karremans, the Dutch Minister for Infrastructure and Water Management, said: "After years of preparation, the lorry toll has been introduced today. The majority of the revenue will flow back into the sector. Thanks to this 'reflux mechanism', the electrification of the sector can be accelerated." He added that this is better for air quality and CO₂ emissions, and ultimately makes the transition to electromobility economically attractive for transport companies, noting the Strait of Hormuz crisis had shown "how important it is to reduce dependence on fossil fuels."

Transitional regulations apply initially: vehicles caught without the mandatory transponder face only half the fine during the first six months, and from 1 September to 31 December 2026 a temporary 22.3 per cent reduction on the lorry toll will apply, aimed at relieving the road freight sector amid high fuel costs from the Middle East conflict. The full toll rate applies from 1 July to 1 September, covering the summer and holiday period.
