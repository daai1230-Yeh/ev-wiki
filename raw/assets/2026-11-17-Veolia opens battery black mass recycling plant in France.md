# Veolia opens battery black mass recycling plant in France

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-16

---

Veolia is commissioning a new plant in the Grand Est region of France to process black mass from lithium-ion batteries. Using its own hydrometallurgical process, the company expects to recover more than 90 per cent of the nickel, cobalt and copper contained in the material.

According to Veolia, the plant can process nearly 7,000 tonnes of black mass annually, equivalent to around 20,000 tonnes of batteries. The material currently comes from France, the UK and Switzerland. Black mass is produced by shredding and processing lithium-ion batteries and contains metals such as nickel, cobalt and copper. Veolia uses a hydrometallurgical process to separate these metals from the material stream, with the aim of making them available again for industrial applications in Europe.

The company initially identifies the automotive, construction and defence industries as potential customers. In the longer term, the recovered materials could also be used in European battery production. However, Veolia has not provided a specific timeline for establishing a closed-loop battery-to-battery recycling process.

Veolia says its process achieves recovery rates of more than 90 per cent for cobalt, nickel and copper. The company describes the plant as the first European project of its kind to use its own hydrometallurgical technology.

## Veolia achieves recovery rates of over 90 per cent

Veolia has been working to establish a European battery recycling chain for several years. In 2022, the company announced its first major electric vehicle battery recycling plant in Minworth, UK. There, end-of-life batteries were to be discharged, dismantled and then processed mechanically and chemically. The newly presented plant in Grand Est operates further down the value chain: it processes the resulting black mass and recovers strategic metals.

In parallel, Veolia is expanding capacity for earlier stages of the recycling process. Battery dismantling projects are currently under development in the UK, Belgium and Slovenia. The resulting black mass could subsequently be processed further at facilities such as the one in Grand Est.

A recent study conducted as part of the EU Safeloop project found that establishing a European battery recycling industry is not just a technical challenge but an economic one as well. Recycling a battery pack in Europe currently incurs an average loss of around €1.90 per kilogramme. Key cost drivers include the transport of hazardous goods, dismantling and uncertainties surrounding material recovery. However, more efficient processes, shorter transport routes and more integrated value chains could significantly improve the economics.

## 700,000 tonnes of batteries expected within five years

The market potential is expected to grow significantly in the coming years. Veolia estimates that nearly 700,000 tonnes of batteries will need to be processed in Europe within five years. The strategic metals contained in these batteries are valued at more than one billion euros.

As the volume of batteries from electric vehicles increases, the question of where the recovered raw materials will be processed becomes increasingly important. Veolia is calling for additional regulatory and economic frameworks in Europe to ensure that more recycled materials are processed within the continent.

The new plant was presented in the presence of, among others, Stéphane Séjourné, Executive Vice-President of the European Commission for Prosperity and Industrial Strategy. It is intended to form part of a European recycling chain in which strategic metals from end-of-life batteries are recovered and reused for industrial purposes within Europe wherever possible.
