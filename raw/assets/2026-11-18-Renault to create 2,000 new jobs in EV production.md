# Renault to create 2,000 new jobs in EV production

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-11-17

---

The Renault Group plans to hire 2,000 production workers at its French plants by 2028 to meet growing demand for electric vehicles. At the same time, up to 1,000 jobs in administrative areas are to be cut through voluntary departures.

As reported by the French newspaper Les Échos, Renault has ramped up production at its Douai plant from one to three shifts per day within roughly two years, driven by the success of fully electric models such as the R5 and the Scénic. However, this expansion has so far relied heavily on temporary workers.

According to the report, Renault now intends to offer permanent contracts to many of these temporary workers, with additional new hires also planned. Speaking at a press conference, Renault’s Head of Production, Thierry Charvet, announced that around 2,000 new jobs will be created in production over the next two years. Of these, 1,650 positions will be for skilled workers in assembly, 250 for production support and 100 for training programmes aimed at university graduates.

Charvet also detailed how the 1,650 skilled worker positions will be distributed across the plants: 600 employees will be hired in Douai, 250 in Maubeuge, and 300 each in Sandouville and Batilly.

## Renault to cut 1,000 office jobs at the same time

At the same time, up to 1,000 office-based employees will leave the company. However, Renault does not plan to make redundancies; instead, it intends to offer voluntary severance agreements and early retirement schemes for older employees. “We want to strengthen the performance of our industrial system in France to guarantee its future,” explained Renault manager Thierry Charvet.

These measures are part of the “France Industrie FutuREady” initiative, which the company’s management presented to trade unions on Monday. Negotiations with the unions regarding job cuts are set to begin at the end of the month.

Just this October, Renault announced plans for a new investment programme of more than ten billion euros in its home market. Renault CEO François Provost announced the plans in an interview with radio station France Inter. According to information from Handelsblatt, the funds will primarily flow into electric vehicles and more affordable models over the next five years.
