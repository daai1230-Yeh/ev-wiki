# Australia allocates AU$30 million for 'smarter grid' projects – including electromobility

**Author**: Ciaran Daly | **Date**: 27.07.2026

---

Australia's Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water (DCCEEW) has announced AU$30 million in funding across 14 projects aimed at making the country's electricity networks smarter and more flexible, under its Grid Enhancing Technologies (GET) Grant Program. The programme runs from 2025-26 to 2028-29, providing individual grants of up to AU$5 million per project on a competitive basis.

The projects aim to help move more renewable energy across the grid and support better use of rooftop solar, batteries, and EVs. On the electromobility front, funded projects include vehicle-to-grid technology and smart EV charging (flexible pricing based on grid demand). Successful recipients include Jet Charge, for its project "Realising Network Benefits from Vehicle-to-Grid," and Charge Hub Australia, for "From Anxiety to Access: Designing Dynamic Pricing that Customers Will Want."

These are funded alongside solutions for renewable energy, grid capacity and battery management — including shared solar and battery solutions for apartment buildings, tools to improve renewable energy connections, and technologies to increase grid stability during high solar generation. The scheme's 14 projects span Victoria, NSW, Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia and the ACT.

Chris Bowen, Australia's Minister for Climate Change and Energy, said: "Australia has the best sun and wind in the world, and we're helping households maximise the benefits. We want to use our sovereign renewables to strengthen our grid and shield Australians from global energy shocks. The Grid Enhancing Technologies grants will support more renewables in the system, put downward pressure on energy bills, and build a stronger, more resilient grid."
