# Subaru Wants Its Own EVs. A 510-HP Mule Shows What's Coming

**Author**: Andrei Nedelea | **Date**: 2026-10-19

---

*A new platform, lighter battery and in-house electronics could help Subaru build electric cars that step out of Toyota's shadow.*

- Subaru is developing its own EV platform to make future models less dependent on Toyota.
- Its 510-hp prototype tests lighter batteries and new motors, but range and charging specs are undisclosed.
- Some technology could reach production around 2029, though no model or launch date is confirmed.

Subaru's electric lineup has grown, mostly thanks to Toyota. Now it wants more control over what comes next. According to Automotive News, Subaru presented the technologies at its proving ground north of Tokyo on October 18. Its all-wheel-drive prototype produces 510 hp and can accelerate from 0 to 62 mph in under four seconds. The prototype, which looks like a hacked-up Outback, is a development tool, not a confirmation of the next production EV's shape or specs.

Engineering executive Takatomo Watamori said controlling the underlying technology would help preserve the safety and driving character that distinguish the brand. Subaru's modern EV ambitions began with the 2023 Solterra, closely related to the Toyota bZ4X; the collaboration has expanded to the Trailseeker, Uncharted, and the three-row Getaway.

The new architecture targets a 100-kg (220-lb) weight reduction versus current EVs, with a redesigned front structure: supports curve over the front wheels to distribute crash forces, and the front motor is designed to rotate during an impact. Aisin developed the prototype's drive units and Panasonic Energy is the battery partner. Subaru claims the motors are smaller and lighter and a 30% efficiency improvement (against an unspecified benchmark).

A reviewer from LOVECARS!TV! described the prototype as combining BRZ-like eagerness to turn with AWD traction, with double-wishbone front and five-link rear suspension, though his drive was limited to two laps under speed restrictions. The battery pack contains 4,000 cylindrical cells, cuts weight by 25% versus the previous battery and targets energy density 20% above the industry average.

Subaru is also developing an integrated ECU for driver assistance, chassis, powertrain and infotainment, using a custom chip developed with AMD, supporting AI functions in a future EyeSight and enabling over-the-air updates. It plans to manufacture the unit itself. Subaru says it developed the technologies in roughly half the usual time to keep pace with Chinese competitors.

Subaru sold about 924,000 vehicles worldwide in 2025 versus over 11 million for Toyota, and aims for 1.2 million annual deliveries in the early 2030s with 10 new vehicles. There is no indication that it plans to end the Toyota partnership. The video also links the prototype to the Subaru Performance-E STI concept shown at the 2025 Japan Mobility Show, though a production electric STI or WRX is unconfirmed.
