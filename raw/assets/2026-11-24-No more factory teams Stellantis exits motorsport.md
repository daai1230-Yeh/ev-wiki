# No more factory teams: Stellantis exits motorsport

**Author**: Victoria Zippmann | **Date**: 24.11.2026 - 12:00

---

Stellantis has exited the WEC and bid farewell to Formula E. This marks a radical shift for the group's motorsport strategy, especially considering Opel's newly developed Gen4 race car and the group's original commitment until 2030. Citroën must also say goodbye to Formula E, and in the WEC, Peugeot's recently updated 9X8 will not race. Here's how Stellantis plans to move forward.

Only in March 2026 did Opel announce its entry into Formula E as a long-term project. The first race of the 2026/2027 season is set to take place in January in Mexico City. Now, before the debut of the newly developed electric racer from Rüsselsheim, Stellantis has announced the withdrawal of all its brands from factory motorsport in the FIA. This means Citroën, like Opel, will also face its final season in Formula E.

Citroën had only taken over the Formula E starting spot of Stellantis sister brand Maserati for the 2025/26 season, while Opel moved up following the exit of DS Automobiles. Opel and Citroën had originally committed to the entire Gen4 cycle until 2029/30. An immediate exit would have incurred contractual penalties, among other issues. According to Stellantis, negotiations regarding the financial consequences of the early withdrawal are still ongoing.

For Mitch Evans, Théo Pourchaire and test driver Sophia Flörsch at Opel, this means just one season. At Citroën, Jean-Éric Vergne and Nick Cassidy will contest their final factory season. As reported by e-formel.de, the independently operated racing team behind Citroën will decide its future autonomously and may continue with a new manufacturer.

Stellantis is taking an even more radical step by exiting the World Endurance Championship (WEC). Unlike Opel and Citroën in Formula E, the Peugeot factory team will not compete in a farewell season. Peugeot has not registered for the 2027 WEC season. By refraining from entering, the group avoided potential contractual penalties. The registration deadline was 23 November, the same day Stellantis announced its withdrawal from factory motorsport.

The heavily revised Peugeot 9X8 Hypercar, planned for 2027, will not make it to the race track. The Peugeot drivers already slated for 2027, Alex Quinn and Robert Shwartzman, thus lose their planned WEC prospects, and Théo Pourchaire’s planned dual programme of WEC and Formula E is now off the table.

The background to Stellantis’ motorsport exit is a group-wide programme called Fastlane 2030. The company presented this programme on 21 May 2026 at its Investor Day in Auburn Hills. It involves around €60 billion euros in investments over five years, alongside a cost-saving target of six billion euros annually by 2028 compared to 2025. What will remain is customer racing. Stellantis intends to continue developing rally race cars and selling them to private teams.

These include, among others, the Lancia Ypsilon Rally2 HF Integrale, the Citroën C3 Rally2, as well as Rally4 models from Lancia and Peugeot. Opel will also continue its electric customer racing commitment with the Mokka GSE Rally. Stellantis Motorsport will additionally work on high-performance models for Alfa Romeo and Maserati, as well as sporty road derivatives (Veloce, GTi, HF and GSE). Affected employees from the world championship teams are to be redeployed within the group.
