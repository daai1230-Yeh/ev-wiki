# Schaeffler and CATL to deepen battery management collaboration

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 20.08.2026 - 16:00

---

Schaeffler and CATL are expanding their collaboration in electromobility. Together, they aim to develop battery management systems and power electronics solutions, primarily for the European market. The starting point is an already secured customer project.

German automotive supplier Schaeffler and Chinese battery manufacturer CATL have signed a letter of intent to collaborate on the joint development of battery management systems (BMS) for EVs, as well as 'X-in-1' Integrated PowerBox (IPB) solutions, which aim to consolidate various components of high-voltage electronics and electromechanical systems into a single unit.

The letter of intent builds on an existing customer project that has already been secured, though the identity of the client and the potential timeline for series production remain undisclosed. In the long term, the partnership is expected to expand beyond Europe to additional international markets.

In developing the BMS, Schaeffler covers hardware and software development, system integration, functional safety, testing and validation, and has corresponding manufacturing capacities in Europe. CATL contributes its experience with battery cells and systems, battery algorithms, and the industrialisation of large volumes. The resulting systems will be specifically tailored to the requirements of European automotive manufacturers.

"Thanks to our established footprint in Europe, Schaeffler offers extensive expertise in local R&D, manufacturing, market entry and regulatory compliance, enabling us to deliver end-to-end support for project rollouts," said Thomas Stierle, Member of the Board for E-Mobility at Schaeffler AG, adding that the goal is to build a competitive portfolio for Europe and other markets together with CATL.

## Exploring AI integration in battery management

The second area of development focuses on the 'X-in-1' boxes. The specific components to be integrated and the exact number of functions are not detailed, but Schaeffler and CATL refer to close integration of high-voltage electronics and electromechanical systems. Such highly integrated solutions can reduce space requirements, cabling and interfaces in the electric powertrain.

The cooperation may extend beyond hardware. CATL has explicitly mentioned cloud computing and artificial intelligence as technologies whose application in battery management will be jointly explored.

This collaboration is particularly significant in light of the increasing localisation of European supply chains. Schaeffler can leverage its existing local development and production structures, while CATL has significantly expanded its presence in Europe in recent years. The partnership targets both European manufacturers and Chinese automakers with international expansion plans.

In parallel, CATL is working on the next generation of its battery technology, recently announcing plans to begin pilot production of solid-state cells in 2027. However, CATL CEO Robin Zeng does not expect volume production before 2030.
