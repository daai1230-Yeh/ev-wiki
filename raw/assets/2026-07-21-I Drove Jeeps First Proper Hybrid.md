# I Drove Jeep's First Proper Hybrid. It Feels More Like A Toyota, And That's Not A Coincidence

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: Jul 21, 2026

---

Jeep has been missing out on the hybrid boom. The new Cherokee fixes that with help from a key rival. While rivals spent years growing hybrid and EV sales, Stellantis struggled to find its footing with battery-powered cars — it discontinued its plug-in hybrids earlier this year, and its long-range fully electric models have stumbled. But with the new 2026 Jeep Cherokee, Stellantis is finally embracing conventional hybrid technology that's been proving itself in the U.S. for more than a quarter century.

The Cherokee, discontinued in 2023, returns as a hybrid-only SUV that's larger and more sophisticated than its predecessor. It rides on Stellantis' STLA Large platform, using a 1.6-liter turbocharged 4-cylinder engine paired with two electric motors and a 1.03 kWh lithium-ion battery, sending power to all four wheels via a CVT. The hybrid system comes from Blue Nexus, a joint venture between Toyota suppliers Denso and Aisin, which also powers Mazda and Subaru hybrids.

Specs: as-tested price $43,590 (base $36,995); 210 combined horsepower; 230 lb-ft of torque; 36 mpg as tested; 8.0 inches of ground clearance; 3,500 lbs towing.

After a week behind the wheel, the reviewer found it drives remarkably close to a Toyota hybrid — a genuine compliment. It returned 36 mpg during testing, mostly highway, with electric motors handling about half the driving in stop-and-go traffic. The 1.6-liter engine gets loud and coarse when pushed hard, and the CVT has a "rubber-band effect," though it smooths out with a lighter throttle foot. It's no match for the RAV4's 2.5-liter setup, which is more polished and returned over 45 mpg in the reviewer's recent testing.

Off-road, the Cherokee has best-in-class approach and departure angles (19.6° and 29.4°) and a Select-Terrain traction system standard across trims, with a more capable Trailhawk variant confirmed to rival the RAV4 Woodland and Forester Wilderness.

The interior is a big step up: a 10.25-inch digital instrument cluster, 12.3-inch touchscreen, and wireless Android Auto that connected flawlessly, though touch-sensitive haptic climate controls don't always register inputs, and a high-pitched AC compressor whine was noted (also reported by Wrangler and Grand Cherokee owners).

At $36,995 to start, the Cherokee costs about $2,000 more than a RAV4 LE AWD and roughly $3,000 more than a Tucson Hybrid AWD, with the Forester and CX-50 similarly priced. The reviewer calls it the best-looking SUV in its class with retro-modern charm, and — with hybrid sales already surging amid high gas prices from the war in Iran — says it's the first Cherokee in a long time that's actually worth considering.
