# Audi returns to efficiency with the Audi A2 e-tron premiere

**Author**: Christoph Schwarzer | **Date**: 2026-09-07

---

The A2 e-tron is Audi's up-market electric car for the compact class. Based on the updated Volkswagen Group Modular Electric Drive Matrix (MEB+), the new model is rolling off production lines at the company's plant in Ingolstadt. Deliveries will begin from December 2026, and prices start at 38,200 euros.

Economically, the Audi A2 compact car was a loss-making exercise for the German carmaker. Its aluminium body made production too costly, and its aerodynamic design proved too controversial. That said, the A2 became a symbol of an era when Audi's brand claim, "Vorsprung durch Technik" – meaning "progress through technology" – still carried real weight. Now, unveiling the A2 e-tron over 20 years later, Audi has succeeded with a new design that still pays homage to the original compact car, especially from the side view. With its high efficiency and bidirectional charging, it also offers compelling practical benefits. In Germany, prices for the complete package start from 38,200 euros.

Audi urgently needed the A2 to expand its electric portfolio into the affordable price range. Exclusively focusing on premium vehicles can be problematic, as Mercedes has found out the hard way. Audi is fortunate enough to be able to leverage the MEB+ platform from Volkswagen Group. The manufacturer, however, omits any mention of this fact, as if it were somewhat embarrassing.

**400-volt system, traction battery in three sizes**

The framework data has already been made public and resembles that of the VW ID.3 Neo, offering batteries with 52, 61, or 84 kilowatt-hours (kWh). These figures are gross values, and the actual usable capacities, derived from the ID.3 Neo, are 50, 58 and 79 kWh. The small and medium versions feature the famously durable and safe LFP cells, which are directly integrated into the crash housing (cell-to-pack) to compensate for the lower energy density of this chemistry mix. The large version uses NMC cells with a cathode mixture of nickel, manganese, and cobalt.

The charging performance of the 400-volt system is already familiar, with a standard charging time from 10 to 80 per cent ranging between 24 and 29 minutes. The electric car's maximum DC charging power varies from 105 to 183 kW, with the lower value applying to the smallest battery and the higher value to the largest.

**12.8 kWh/100 km in the WLTP test cycle**

All three battery sizes are available in combination with the new APP350 drive system, with power levels of 125, 140, and 170 kW, paired with an energy content of 52, 61, and 84 kWh. The largest battery configuration is also available with the high-performance APP550 and 240 kW of motor power. The revised axle-parallel drive (APP) is not only on paper but also in practice, thanks to an improved hairpin winding and an optimised pulse inverter with high efficiency.

Fine-tuning the vehicle's aerodynamic drag (drag coefficient of 0.24) and the new APP have resulted in an efficiency of 12.8 kWh/100 km in the WLTP test cycle. This is significantly lower than any other electric Audi and approaches the efficiency of particularly frugal saloons like the Tesla Model 3 (12.2 kWh/100 km, from 36,990 euros). The A2's WLTP ranges vary between 423 and 646 km, depending on the battery configuration.

While Audi has not provided details on the A2's external dimensions, the wheelbase (2.77 metres) and boot capacity (396 to 1,336 litres) are so close to the ID.3's values that a strong similarity to the ID.3 is plausible — and the ID.3 measures 4.27 metres in length, 1.81 metres in width, and 1.56 metres in height. Unlike the ID.3 Neo, the A2 includes a frunk, though its volume is very small at 13 litres.

**Expanding the home energy storage system**

Of two interesting features, only one is likely to spark much discussion, while the other is genuinely relevant. Like the Volvo EX60, the Audi A2 has pressure-sensitive fins instead of conventional mechanical door handles. These are designed to function flawlessly even in the event of a vehicle electrical system failure, thanks to auxiliary batteries.

For buyers with rooftop solar at home, it is likely more important to know that the A2 supports vehicle-to-home (V2H) energy balancing, meaning that it can not only supply external devices with electrical energy via vehicle-to-load (V2L) energy but can also provide energy storage for a home when needed. This is possible with a charging window between 20 and 80 per cent. The hardware requirement is a "suitable DC wallbox," though Audi has not yet clarified what this entails.

In the press release for the premiere, Audi highlights its comprehensive driver assistance systems. Every A2, for example, comes with adaptive cruise control that can automatically adhere to speed limits and decelerate in curves and before intersections. Standard equipment also includes a rear-view camera, an exit warning system to protect cyclists, and a cross-traffic assist that intervenes when reversing out of a parking space before it is too late. For an additional fee, the A2 offers a full range of driver assistance systems, from automatic emergency stopping on the hard shoulder in a medical emergency to the integration of swarm data for precise speed control, along with a wide array of other options.

**Distance from the mass market**

Audi wants — and needs — the A2 to maintain a meaningful distance from both internal and external competitors. The A2 costs 1,975 euros more than the VW ID.3 Neo Life, for example. And, as one would hope, offers higher quality workmanship, material selection, and noise insulation accordingly.

BMW and Mercedes currently lack direct competitors in this segment. This will only change with the BMW 1 Series from 2028, and at Mercedes, it remains unclear whether and how the segment below the 40,000-euro entry price will be addressed. Volvo is targeting a similar buyer group, though the EX30 is more compact in its dimensions.

The Audi A2 e-tron is unlikely to become a standalone icon like the A2 of the 2000s. Instead, it is a compact premium interpretation of the MEB theme and an alternative to the uniformity of the mass-market ID.3. This differentiation from the ID.3 phenomenon marks this electric vehicle's real opportunity.
