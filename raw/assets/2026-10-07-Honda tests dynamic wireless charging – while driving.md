# Honda tests dynamic wireless charging – while driving

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-10-06

---

Honda, together with its partners Taisei and Taisei Rotec, will develop an inductive charging system to charge the battery while driving. The technological foundation is in place, and the first demonstrations are set to begin next year – including on public roads.

Honda has announced that, since 2021, it has been developing Dynamic Wireless Power Transfer (DWPT) technology to enable wireless energy supply for vehicles while in motion. The Japanese manufacturer is primarily targeting practical applications in logistics, where it expects to see "significant benefits". The largest known developer in this field is the Israeli company Electreon, which already has several pilot projects under way. Research initiatives such as the PES4E|ROAD project are also exploring dynamic inductive charging.

The principle is straightforward: coils embedded in the road surface generate a magnetic field when a specially equipped vehicle drives over them. A receiving coil in the vehicle captures this energy and transfers it directly to the battery.

Honda now aims to enter this potential future market. After five years of development, the vehicle manufacturer now claims that, together with partners, it has developed the underlying technology for such a magnetic wireless road energy transfer system. Alongside Taisei and Taisei Rotec, Honda plans to begin demonstrations starting from the Japanese fiscal year 2027 (beginning 1 April 2027).

The division of labour is as follows: Honda is developing the transmitting and receiving units, which are compatible with DC distribution networks – including applications in large commercial vehicles. Taisei is contributing an associated DC energy supply system for electrifying the road, while Taisei Rotec is responsible for the necessary construction methods.

Regarding the further timeline, a test track is to be constructed at the 'Future Technology Field for Taisei Group/Tamura' (T-FIELD/TAMRA) by the end of 2026. There, the three companies intend to verify both the performance of the energy transfer and the safety of the units embedded in the road. Subsequently, the trio aims to further develop its technology for real-world operation. This includes evaluating "performance at high power output levels of up to 150 kW for potential application to large commercial vehicles, as well as verification of the reliability of WPT performance for vehicles traveling at high speeds".

Additionally, the three companies plan to participate in the 'Tateyama Project' – a demonstration project for DWPT technology to be conducted by East Nippon Expressway Company Limited (NEXCO East) starting from the Japanese fiscal year 2027 on the Tateyama Expressway in Chiba, Japan.
