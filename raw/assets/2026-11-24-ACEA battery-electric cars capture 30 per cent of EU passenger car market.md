# ACEA: battery-electric cars capture 30 per cent of EU passenger car market

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 24.11.2026 - 11:30

---

In the EU, more than 250,000 pure electric cars are now hitting the roads each month. In October, this figure reached 280,000 units, a substantial increase of 61 per cent year-on-year. Across the EU's overall passenger car market, the share of EVs climbed to nearly 30 per cent.

New registrations of battery-electric cars in the EU rose sharply in October. According to the latest monthly statistics from the European Automobile Manufacturers’ Association (ACEA), 279,799 new battery-electric cars were registered, representing a 61.4 per cent increase over last October. In the year to date (January to October), the EU has seen 2,211,071 new battery-electric passenger cars registered – exactly 50 per cent more than in the same period last year. While the share of electric cars reached 29.8 per cent in October, it climbed to 23.4 per cent for the year to date. In other words, nearly one in four new passenger cars in the EU this year has an electric powertrain. This makes battery-electric cars the second most popular powertrain type, behind hybrids.

By far the largest markets are Germany and France, which in October recorded 94,086 and 61,958 new battery-electric passenger cars respectively, each with growth rates of around 80 per cent. ACEA highlights: “Three of the four largest EU markets, which together accounted for 65 per cent of all battery-electric car registrations, recorded strong double-digit growth: France (+79.4 per cent), Germany (+60.7 per cent), and Denmark (+41 per cent). Belgium also posted a more moderate 16.7 per cent increase.”

The overall EU passenger car market also saw a slight increase in October: up 2.6 per cent to 939,447 vehicles. In the powertrain mix, non-plug-in hybrids remain at the top with 316,940 units and a 34 per cent market share, though with no growth (+0.3 per cent). This is followed by battery-electric cars, with the aforementioned high growth rates and just under 30 per cent market share, then petrol cars (158,970, -30 per cent year-on-year) with a 17 per cent share, and plug-in hybrids (102,301, +6 per cent year-on-year) with an 11 per cent share. Completing the statistics are diesel cars (51,177, -31 per cent year-on-year) and other powertrains (30,260, +2.3 per cent year-on-year) with 5 per cent and 3 per cent market shares respectively.

Looking at the year to date, hybrids account for 36 per cent and battery-electric cars for 23 per cent of the market. Meanwhile, the combined market share of petrol and diesel cars fell to 27.7 per cent, compared to 36.5 per cent in the previous year. “The EU powertrain mix continued to shift towards electrified vehicles,” ACEA added.

Plug-in hybrids saw particularly strong growth in Italy: 138,441 vehicles were newly registered there from January to October, a 73 per cent increase over the previous year. In Spain, registrations rose by 32 per cent to 131,411 units, while in Germany they increased by 11 per cent to 275,988 vehicles.

Notably, BEV registrations also increased significantly in other European markets outside the EU. In the EFTA states of Iceland, Norway and Switzerland, 23,369 pure electric cars were newly registered in October, a 52 per cent increase over the same month last year. In the United Kingdom, there were 48,858 vehicles, a 33 per cent rise. From January to October, BEV registrations in the EFTA states increased by 15 per cent to 186,767 vehicles, while in the United Kingdom they rose by 30 per cent to 503,805. However, these four countries do not match the EU’s growth rates.

For the entire region comprising the EU, EFTA and the UK, ACEA reports 352,026 new BEVs in October – a 56 per cent increase. Since the beginning of the year, sales in this region have totalled 2.9 million electric cars, 43.5 per cent more than in the same period last year.

The figures by manufacturer are also interesting, although ACEA does not break them down by powertrain type, so the electric car sales development of domestic manufacturers cannot be illustrated. However, as seen in August and in September, there is generally strong momentum among Chinese manufacturers, who predominantly introduce electrified passenger cars to Europe. In the EU, BYD grew its new registrations in October by 128 per cent to 30,974 vehicles compared to the same month last year. SAIC, the parent company of MG, saw a 26 per cent increase in September to 23,747 registrations. Chery, including Jaecoo, Jetour and Omoda, reached 21,458 vehicles in October (+185 per cent). Leapmotor and Xpeng also showed strong growth, with 11,709 new registrations (+279 per cent) and 4,385 units (+171 per cent) respectively.

But Tesla also recorded an 83.5 per cent increase in October to 10,166 vehicles. In the year to date, the US electric car manufacturer has achieved 70 per cent growth.
