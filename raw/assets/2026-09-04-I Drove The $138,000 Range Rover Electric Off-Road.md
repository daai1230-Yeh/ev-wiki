# I Drove The $138,000 Range Rover Electric Off-Road. It Was Exceptional

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-09-01

---

Five years after the British luxury marque first announced the Range Rover Electric, and over a year after InsideEVs reviewed the prototype in Sweden, the SUV is finally ready for a full reveal. In July, the author flew to England to learn about its powertrain, battery, and software details, and to check out the production SUV and drive one on an off-road course.

**Range and charging.** Land Rover is targeting up to 333 miles of EPA-estimated range in the U.S. (600 km WLTP) from a twin-stacked battery with 118.5 kWh of usable capacity. Rivals like the Porsche Cayenne Electric and Lucid Gravity offer over 400 miles. Martin Limpert, managing director for Range Rover, said getting range and performance right was a balancing act: "The moment you enhance one of your parameters, you sacrifice another one... We could have gone for more energy, but that would mean a heavier battery and less performance capability."

Charging is solid: a 350 kW peak rate means a 10-80% charge takes about 22 minutes, with 125 miles added in 10 minutes at compatible stations. A full 0-100% charge on a 22 kW Level 2 home charger takes about 7 hours. Limpert said a single full charge would cover 97% of customer journeys. U.S.-spec cars get a native NACS port with Plug & Charge compatibility at select networks.

**Design and interior.** JLR kept the Range Rover Electric's design and proportions identical to the gas and plug-in hybrid models — the only visible differences are the charge port and the lack of exhaust pipes. The cabin feels as opulent as the gas-powered Range Rover, with pillow-like headrests in the plush second row and the world's first electrostatic speakers in a production car, offered as a $12,570 option that the author called the best sound system he's experienced in any car.

**Platform and engineering.** The EV rides on the existing Modular Longitudinal Architecture-Flex (MLA-Flex) shared with PHEV, gas, and diesel Rovers, but the electric drivetrain and battery pack improved torsional rigidity by 50%. JLR claims roughly 300 patents filed for the in-house drive units, battery pack, and thermal management system. Limpert said bringing battery and propulsion development fully in-house contributed to the five-year gap between announcement and launch: "it took us longer than we expected because when you do something in-house completely, you identify roadblocks... it was more important for us to get it right than get it fast."

The 344 NMC prismatic battery cells come from Japan's AESC (now owned by China's Envision Group), stacked in a cell-to-pack configuration weighing about 1,622 lbs (736 kg) without coolant. As a result, the EV is only 165 lbs (75 kg) heavier than the plug-in hybrid, though nearly 1,000 lbs heavier than the six-cylinder standard-wheelbase model. Every battery reportedly gets a digital twin so JLR can monitor health remotely and predict faults, updating via over-the-air software where possible.

Dual permanent magnet motors with standard all-wheel drive produce a combined 542 horsepower and 627 lb-ft of torque, enough for a 0-60 mph time of 4.3 seconds.

**Off-roading.** The author drove the Range Rover Electric on JLR's off-road course and came away impressed — throttle calibration in off-road modes is forgiving, smoothing out inconsistent pedal input to maintain a steady pace over challenging terrain. A 30-degree rock crawl felt almost effortless using one-pedal driving with an instructor's guidance. One-pedal driving works up to 33-degree ascents, and the EV can climb up to 45-degree slopes. The SUV can adjust motor speeds every 50 milliseconds and manage wheel slip up to 100 times faster than an equivalent combustion vehicle. JLR reportedly benchmarked the Rivian R1S during development, though on paper the Range Rover's off-road numbers (8 inches of ground clearance, 33.2-degree approach angle, 28.1-degree departure angle, 22.3-degree breakover angle) trail the R1S's more generous figures.

**Price.** Order books are open in the U.S., though delivery timing is still unclear. Starting price is $138,000 before destination — about $25,000 more than an entry-trim Range Rover SE.
