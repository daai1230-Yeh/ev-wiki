# Range Rover GT to launch initially as a battery-electric model

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 22.07.2026

---

Range Rover is expanding its model line-up with the GT. The brand's fifth model is based on the EMA platform and will launch exclusively with a battery-electric powertrain. The vehicle is currently undergoing its final validation tests.

The GT will become the first fully electric Range Rover based on the new Electrified Modular Architecture (EMA). JLR will build it alongside combustion-engined and hybrid models at its Halewood plant in the UK.

At launch, the GT will be available exclusively as a battery-electric vehicle. However, the platform also supports other powertrain options. Range Rover has already confirmed that it will add a full-hybrid variant later in the model's lifecycle. The manufacturer has not yet disclosed technical specifications such as battery capacity, range, power output or DC charging performance.

Within JLR, the EMA platform will underpin smaller electrified models, including the forthcoming electric Range Rover Velar. Larger models such as the Range Rover Electric and Range Rover Sport Electric, by contrast, are based on the Modular Longitudinal Architecture (MLA). JLR will therefore use different platforms for its future electric model range.

Conceptually, the GT is designed to align more closely with the traditional grand tourer formula than previous Range Rover models. The camouflaged prototypes therefore feature a lower, coupé-like silhouette. At the same time, the model is expected to retain the off-road capability that defines the Range Rover brand.

"The combination of effortless electric performance, outstanding long-distance comfort and off-road capability is quintessentially Range Rover," said Range Rover Managing Director Martin Limpert.

The manufacturer has already provided a first look at the interior. Range Rover adopts a minimalist layout featuring a central touchscreen and a separate driver display. The air vents are largely integrated into the dashboard, as are elements of the audio system. Textile materials are set to feature prominently across the instrument panel. For the second row, the manufacturer promises generous legroom but has yet to provide any dimensions.

The Range Rover GT is currently completing its final global validation programme. The brand plans to reveal further details later this year. To assess its credentials as an electric grand tourer, key figures such as battery capacity, energy consumption, range and DC charging performance will be particularly relevant.

The GT also arrives as JLR prepares several new electric models, some of which will launch later than originally planned. Alongside the GT, these include the Range Rover Electric as well as planned electric versions of the Range Rover Sport and Velar. JLR confirmed in 2025 that it had postponed the launch of some models, citing additional testing and changing market conditions.
