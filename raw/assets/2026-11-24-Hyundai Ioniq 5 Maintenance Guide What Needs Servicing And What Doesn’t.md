# Hyundai Ioniq 5 Maintenance Guide What Needs Servicing And What Doesn’t

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-11-23

---

The Ioniq 5 is one of the most popular electric crossovers in the United States. Here’s what it takes to keep it in tip-top shape. *(Presented by ERANGE EV — sponsor content removed)*

It’s no secret that electric cars are more affordable to maintain than their gasoline-powered counterparts. While they may have a higher purchase price than a comparable gas-powered vehicle, EVs can save owners money over time by eliminating the need for regular engine oil changes, emissions tests, and other traditional maintenance tasks. Affordable electric vehicles are also becoming increasingly common, with even more options expected to arrive in the coming years.

However, that doesn't mean electric cars are completely maintenance-free. Like any other vehicle, EVs require regular care and attention to remain reliable and perform at their best for many years.

Although they don't have conventional combustion engines or traditional automatic transmissions, electric cars still feature components that require periodic maintenance. These include reduction gearboxes, brake fluid, brake pads, coolant systems, and tires among other things. Keeping these components in good condition is essential to ensuring an EV's long-term reliability.

For the second installment of our EV maintenance series, I'll be taking a closer look at the Hyundai Ioniq 5, one of the most recognizable electric crossovers on the market. Made in the United States and South Korea, the Ioniq 5 has long been one of the biggest rivals to the ubiquitous Tesla Model Y.

## Hyundai Ioniq 5 Maintenance: What Hyundai recommends

Compared to Tesla, which is known for its rather skimpy maintenance plan, Hyundai includes a generous list of maintenance items in the owner’s manual. The automaker also includes additional service actions that should be performed if the car is used in harsher-than-normal conditions, so owners know what to expect in the long run.

### Normal Maintenance Schedule

Hyundai says that normal driving conditions include distances longer than 10 miles, clean roads without salt or dust, ambient temperatures under 90ºF (32ºC), no towing, no roof rack, and normal speeds. Under these conditions, the required maintenance is:

- Cooling system: Inspect every 8,000 miles or 12 months.
- Coolant: At first, replace at 120,000 miles or 120 months. After that, replace every 24,000 miles or 24 months.
- Brake system (discs, pads, brake pedal, hoses): Inspect every 16,000 miles or 24 months.
- Brake fluid: Inspect every 8,000 miles or 12 months; replace every 48,000 miles or 38 months.
- Tires: Rotate and inspect every 8,000 miles or 12 months; replace when tread dips below 4/32nd of an inch.
- Cabin air filter: Replace every 16,000 miles or 24 months.
- Drive unit (reducer) fluid: Inspect every 32,000 miles or 48 months.
- 12-volt battery: Inspect every 8,000 miles or 12 months.
- Steering gear rack, linkage, and boots: Inspect every 8,000 miles or 12 months.
- Air conditioning system: Inspect every 8,000 miles or 12 months.
- High-voltage battery: Inspect every 8,000 miles or 12 months.

### Maintenance under severe usage and low mileage conditions

Severe conditions include repeated short trips (under five miles in normal temperatures or under 10 miles in freezing temperatures); rough, dusty, muddy, unpaved, graveled or salt-spread roads; very cold weather; heavy dust; heavy traffic above 90ºF while consuming more than 50% of electric energy; repeated mountain driving; towing; roof racks; patrol car or taxi use; frequent high speeds; and frequent stop-and-go driving. In addition to the normal schedule, Hyundai recommends:

- Drive unit (reducer) fluid: Replace every 80,000 miles.
- Steering components, front suspension ball joints, brakes, and drive shafts: Inspect more frequently depending on conditions.
- Cabin air filter: Replace more frequently depending on conditions.

### Owner Maintenance Schedule

- When stopped for charging: check coolant level, windshield washer fluid, and for low or underinflated tires.
- While operating: check for steering vibrations, pulling to one side, unusual brake sounds or pedal feel, the Park function, parking brake, and fluid leaks.
- At least monthly: coolant level, all exterior lights, tire pressures, loose lug nuts.
- At least twice a year: radiator/heater/AC hoses, washer spray and wipers, headlight alignment, seat belts.
- At least once a year: clean body and door drain holes; lubricate door and hood hinges, locks and latches and weather strips; check the AC system; clean the 12V battery and terminals; check brake fluid level.

## Key takeaway

The Hyundai Ioniq 5 is one of the best-selling electric crossovers in the United States, and for good reason. Its unique styling has plenty of fans, and it’s a genuinely nice car to drive. As for its maintenance requirements, the official recommendations have a lot more items compared to the Tesla Model Y, but at the end of the day, most of them are just inspection jobs. As a result, maintenance costs should be quite low compared to a comparable gas vehicle. Just rotate and replace tires, keep an eye on your brakes, and swap out the coolant after 10 years or 120,000 miles.
