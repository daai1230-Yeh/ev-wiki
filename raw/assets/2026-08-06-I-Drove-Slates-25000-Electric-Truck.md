# I Drove Slate's $25,000 Electric Truck. It Could Be A Real Game-Changer

**Author**: Jens Meiners | **Date**: Aug 4, 2026

---

InsideEVs finally got to drive the Slate Truck. Despite the budget price, this simple EV feels planted, solid, and comfortable.

Launching a new carmaker from scratch is one of the most challenging endeavors possible. The graveyard of automotive startups is filling up with brands that failed to cash in on the electric transformation. But unlike other aspirants, Slate doesn't just have massive financial backing, it's also following a far more unique path. Shunning the usual top-down approach used by companies like Tesla and Rivian, Slate's all-electric truck is entering the market right at the bottom.

The writer tested the truck around Newport, Rhode Island, driving a pre-production unit shipped from the Warsaw, Indiana factory in bargain-basement specification, for almost a full hour on back roads and highways.

Judging from photos, the Slate might seem almost toy-like, but in reality it is a substantial truck — virtually identical in size with the first-gen Ford Explorer 3-door and the 1990s Chevy S-10 Blazer. As a pickup, it is shorter than a current Ford Maverick, but only because it has a regular cab with one row of seats; its bed is actually longer than the Ford's.

The basic pickup — the "blank Slate" — is priced at $24,950 (plus a $1,450 delivery fee), undercutting the Ford Maverick by more than $3,000, making it the most affordable truck on the market. Beyond the open-bed truck, there is a $5,000 Squareback SUV kit and a $7,000 Fastback SUV kit.

**Slate Truck specs**: Base price $26,400 (incl. destination) | 65 kWh LFP battery | 20-80% charge in 30 min | 205 miles EV range | 90 mph top speed | 181 hp | 195 lb-ft torque | 0-60 mph in 8 sec | 2,000 lb towing.

The interior is austere, with fabric-clad front seats, a small digital speedometer screen, manual window cranks, and no built-in infotainment or navigation — it's designed to be "bring your own device." Air conditioning, power steering and power locks are always included. The front houses a frunk with 7.0 cu.ft of storage; the pickup bed holds an additional 35.1 cu.ft of cargo.

The single rear-mounted motor's power rating was downgraded from an initially communicated 150 kW (201 hp) to 135 kW (181 hp), but delivers almost 200 lb-ft of torque, moving the 4,048-pound truck to 60 mph in 8 seconds and topping out at 90 mph. The writer reached 85 mph during testing. The 65-kWh LFP battery can charge at up to 90 kW, with range improved from an initial 150 to 205 miles (a larger 240-mile battery option was discarded).

Dynamically, the steering is precise, brakes are linear, and the one-pedal driving recuperation setting (non-adjustable) brings the vehicle to a full stop. It uses a MacPherson strut front suspension and a rear DeDion axle with coil springs. The chassis delivers good dynamics and a comfortable ride despite the short wheelbase, with body roll noticeable but not excessive.

Customization options include multiple wrap colors and decor, alternate head/taillight covers, larger bumpers, fender flares, roll bars, and camping accessories — full color wraps start at $499. Slate will not offer a range-extender gasoline engine or all-wheel drive.

The reviewer concluded the Slate delivers genuine sophistication in its minimalist execution and could be a "game changer," pivoting the market away from ever more screens and features — noting a personal early reservation for the vehicle.
