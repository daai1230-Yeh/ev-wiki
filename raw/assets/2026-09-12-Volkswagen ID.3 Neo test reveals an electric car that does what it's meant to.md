# Volkswagen ID.3 Neo test reveals an electric car that does what it's meant to

**Author**: Christoph Schwarzer | **Date**: September 10, 2026

**Facts**: VW ID.3 Neo Style | Battery: 58 kWh (net) | Range: 485 km (WLTP) | DC charging power: 105 kW | Base price: €41,875

---

The controls in the Volkswagen ID.3 Neo are now haptic push buttons and rotary knobs. No one misses the sliders. But what really sets this VW apart is not the new interior — although it does have a great spacious feel — it's actually the overall package that is most pleasantly convincing. The ID.3 has become a genuinely good electric car.

Did someone say boring? Normally, no one turns to look at a Volkswagen ID.3. But the ID.3 Neo is an exception. Some passers-by glance furtively, while others stare openly. The second major revision of the electric car — which will assume the role of the e-Golf until its new presentation in 2029 — clearly piques people's curiosity. What can the ID.3 Neo do, with its price list starting at €33,995?

In Germany, Volkswagen is offering a flat €2,000 discount on this base price for the Trend trim level. For the ID.3 Life, VW automatically deducts €3,000, and for the Style trim (the test car), it deducts €4,000. Private customers with lower household incomes can receive up to an additional €6,000 from the subsidy fund.

## Only the Škoda Elroq sells better in Germany

Sales figures in Germany prove the ID.3 is popular: from January to July it was the second most-registered electric car in Germany, after the Škoda Elroq. The Neo differs little from previous ID.3 models on the outside, more on the inside, and most of all in its drivetrain. The unlit slider elements for climate control have been replaced by sleek toggle switches, and the choice of materials gives the interior a sense of spaciousness.

## Small turning circle, efficient drivetrain

The ID.3 Neo is nimble and simple to drive, with a turning circle of 10.2 metres. The previous electric motor, the APP 310, has been replaced by the APP 350, delivering 140 kW and accelerating the test car to 100 km/h in eight seconds. The ID.3 Neo averaged 15 kWh/100 km on the onboard computer, even with two-thirds of the distance on motorways. With 58 kWh of usable battery, calculated range came to 387 km — less than the WLTP figure of 485 km but still a good real-world value.

## 550 km on the motorway with one charging stop

Minimum consumption of 10.5 kWh/100 km was recorded in flowing city traffic; 12.4 kWh/100 km in rural driving; and 20 kWh/100 km on the motorway at the recommended speed. Volkswagen specifies max DC charging at 105 kW with a 10–80% charge time of 25 minutes. In practice, the test car exceeded that spec, hitting 126 kW and adding 44 kWh in 25 minutes — enough for roughly 200 additional motorway kilometres, meaning over 550 km achievable with a single 25-minute charging stop.

## LFP cells in the MEB+

The charging curve has two flat sections: around 125 kW, then dropping to about 80 kW past roughly 60% charge. The revised Modular Electric Drive Matrix (MEB) has evolved into the MEB+, which now uses LFP cells in the 50 and 58 kWh battery sizes — cost-effective, safe and durable, though with lower energy density than the NMC cells still used in models like the ID.3 GTI.

Volkswagen confirmed to electrive that the 50 kWh version of the ID.3 Neo actually has a 58 kWh battery limited by software — effectively running permanently in Battery Care Mode, accessing only 80% of the possible energy content, with a range of 410 km, almost matching the discontinued 58 kWh version. Winter testing will show whether Volkswagen has managed LFP's cold-sensitivity as well as Tesla has.

## Traffic light recognition available for a surcharge

The ID.3 Neo's driving automation has also progressed. The IQ. Drive Pro package (€2,305) includes traffic light recognition, which worked precisely in Hamburg city traffic — the car stops automatically, though the driver must confirm the start when the light turns green. VW has also improved steering feel for lane-keeping and refined brake pedal feel for all customers at no surcharge.

## Because it fits so well

With the ID.3 Neo, Volkswagen has consistently returned to its roots: clarity, comfort, and efficiency. How well it sells depends on pricing structures — a similarly equipped ID. Polo is only slightly cheaper. This makes the ID.3 a recommendation for those who want an excellent electric car for everyday use rather than an automotive spectacle.
