# Siemens introduces DC contactor for megawatt charging

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-23

---

Siemens is expanding its DC portfolio with a high-performance DC contactor for voltages up to 1,500 volts and currents up to 600 amperes. The new SIRIUS 3QC1 combines semiconductor technology with mechanical contacts and is intended for use in megawatt charging stations for commercial vehicles.

According to Siemens, this is the world’s first semiconductor-based, high-performance DC contactor of its kind. The company states that its intended applications include battery energy storage systems, industrial DC grids, and fast-charging stations for e-trucks, e-buses, and electric vehicle fleets.

A contactor is used to switch and disconnect electrical circuits. This is particularly challenging with high DC voltages, as opening mechanical contacts can create arcs that lead to wear. Siemens therefore uses hybrid technology: the semiconductors handle the actual switching process, preventing arc formation. The mechanical contacts ensure low-loss current conduction in the closed state and enable galvanic isolation of the circuit during disconnection.

This combination allows the SIRIUS 3QC1 to achieve at least 150,000 switching cycles. The device is also bidirectional, enabling it to switch regardless of current flow direction. This feature is particularly relevant for battery storage systems, which are both charged and discharged.

In charging infrastructure, Siemens sees potential applications primarily in megawatt fast-charging stations for heavy-duty vehicles. The company mentions charging capacities of up to 1,000 kW. Within such systems, the contactor handles the switching and safe disconnection of DC circuits. It is therefore a component of the electrical infrastructure rather than a standalone charging system.

## Siemens plots megawatt charging applications

The SIRIUS 3QC1 is also intended for use in stationary battery storage systems. Siemens cites applications in power racks with 100 kW and high-performance racks with up to 375 kW. The company also sees potential uses in photovoltaic systems, industrial DC microgrids, and electrical systems on ships.

The new contactor will be available in four versions with rated currents of 300, 400, 500, and 600 amperes. The control voltage is 24 volts DC. According to Siemens, the device is designed for ambient temperatures ranging from -25 to +60 degrees Celsius. It also features an integrated display with status indicators and can be operated remotely.

Siemens highlights the potential efficiency advantages of DC systems, as they reduce the need for conversions between AC and DC. The company states that this can achieve energy savings of up to eight per cent. Under suitable conditions, material requirements could also be reduced by up to 50 per cent. These figures apply to DC systems in general and not to the new contactor alone.

Siemens is already active as a provider of fast-charging technology for electric vehicles. For example, at seven of the eight sites along the fast-charging corridor completed in June 2026 by the Portuguese energy company Galp, Siemens’ Sicharge charging stations are in use. A total of 84 charging points were equipped with Siemens technology there. With the new DC contactor, the company is now expanding its portfolio to include another component for high-performance DC applications.
