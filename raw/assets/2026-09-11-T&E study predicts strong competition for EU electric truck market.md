# T&E study predicts strong competition for EU electric truck market

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-09-10

---

Ahead of IAA Transportation, a new Transport & Environment (T&E) analysis warns that European truck manufacturers could face significant competition by 2030. According to the report, a quarter of the electric truck market could be lost to new competitors from China and the US within three years.

The EU truck market, with an annual volume of 245,000 vehicles, is currently dominated by a few established manufacturers — Daimler Truck, Traton (Scania, MAN), Iveco, DAF, and the Volvo Group (Volvo Trucks, Renault Trucks). Electric trucks remain a small share of the market but are growing quickly. T&E's key message: Chinese and US truck manufacturers could capture a quarter of the European electric truck market by 2030 if domestic manufacturers don't accelerate electrification. "Maintaining ambition of the truck CO2 standards will be critical to EU manufacturers remaining market leaders," T&E states.

**Third-country importers eye EU expansion.** A number of Chinese commercial vehicle manufacturers — including Sany, Sinotruk, SuperPanther, FAW Trucks, Foton and others — have registered for IAA Transportation and will primarily enter the European market with electric trucks. Some benefit from a head start in experience thanks to China's more mature electric truck market. From the US, Tesla has announced expansion plans for Europe as the first American electric truck manufacturer, though European Tesla Semi pricing and specs will only be revealed at the trade fair — a limitation T&E acknowledges in its modelling.

**Cost advantage.** T&E concludes the total cost of ownership (TCO) of a Chinese electric truck could be up to 12% lower than an equivalent European model — a potential saving of up to €43,000 over five years, based on typical heavy-duty 4×2 tractor units for long-haul transport purchased in Germany in 2026. "Electric trucks from new entrants perform as well as European trucks on key metrics, including driving range, charging time, maximum payload, and energy efficiency," the report says, adding that new entrants hope to gain up to a quarter of the heavy e-truck market by 2030.

Johanna Braun, Manager of E-Mobility for Trucks and Infrastructure at T&E Germany: "Competitive electric trucks from China and the US are already entering the European market, with more models announced. At this critical juncture, truck manufacturers should learn from the mistakes of the automotive industry. Slowing down electrification now would pave the way for competitors from the US and China."

**Cost-sensitive customers.** T&E argues that a TCO reduction of more than 10% could be decisive in the transport industry, which operates on profit margins of just 1.5–2%. "A German transport operator today, opting for an electric truck from a new entrant instead of a European model, could bring the TCO down from €0.63/km to €0.55/km (including residual value)," the analysts explain. T&E links the findings to a call for Germany to advocate for retaining ambitious CO2 fleet emission standards for trucks at the EU level.

Braun: "The race for market leadership in electric trucks is in full swing. Chancellor Friedrich Merz should advocate for ambitious decarbonisation targets at the European level if he wants to prevent a similar crisis in the truck sector to the one we are currently seeing in the automotive industry."
