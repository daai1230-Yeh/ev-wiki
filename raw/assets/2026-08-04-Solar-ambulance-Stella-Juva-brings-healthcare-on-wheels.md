# Solar ambulance Stella Juva brings healthcare on wheels

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-08-03

---

The Solar Team Eindhoven at TU Eindhoven has unveiled Stella Juva, which it describes as the world's first solar-powered ambulance. Developed to provide medical care in remote regions with limited access to electricity and fuel, the vehicle is now set to undergo field testing in Kenya.

Unlike conventional ambulances, Stella Juva is not intended to transport patients to hospital. Instead, it delivers medical care directly at the point of need. Solar panels integrated into the roof supply energy for both the electric drivetrain and onboard medical equipment, including mobile X-ray systems, ultrasound devices and vaccine refrigeration. Under sunny conditions, the vehicle can achieve an all-electric range of up to 715 kilometres on paved roads, allowing it to operate independently of conventional charging infrastructure.

The project is led by the Solar Team Eindhoven, whose students have developed solar-powered vehicles for more than a decade, including an off-road solar vehicle. Stella Juva was created with organisations including Amref Health Africa to ensure the concept meets real-world operational requirements.

"We were inspired by the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals, which strive to make good healthcare accessible to everyone," said team manager Mathijs van Gerven. Partnerships manager Yarno Basten added: "If twenty-three students can achieve this in a single year, it shows what's possible when the technological industry and social organisations work together."

Femke Maurits, Partnerships Manager at Amref Health Africa in the Netherlands, noted: "For millions of people, access to quality healthcare remains out of reach. However, access to medical care should not depend on the availability of electricity or fuel." Reliable energy is essential for operating medical equipment and refrigerating vaccines.

In August, the team will travel to Kenya to test the vehicle under real-world conditions, simulating scenarios including tuberculosis treatment and preventive health check-ups across two field regions, assessing suitability for unpaved roads. The team previously tested its solar-powered off-road vehicle in the Sahara in 2023.
