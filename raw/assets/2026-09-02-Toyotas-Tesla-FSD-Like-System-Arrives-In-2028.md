# Toyota's Tesla-FSD Like System Arrives In 2028, But With Key Differences

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: 2026-09-01

---

Toyota's next-generation advanced driver assistance system (ADAS) arrives in 2028, and could potentially be more capable than today's system, which offers adaptive cruise control and lane centering. The automaker isn't copying Tesla's Full Self-Driving system, though — Toyota's version will have more guardrails in place.

Japan's Nikkei reported that the system will be "Level 2++" capable — not an official SAE classification, but a marketing term for capabilities between Level 2 (adaptive cruise control and lane centering, driver fully in control) and Level 3 (drivers can take eyes and hands off the wheel but must be ready to take over). Systems marketed as L2+/L2++ can work on highway and in city with point-to-point supervised driving similar to Tesla's FSD, but the driver remains ultimately in charge and liable if something goes wrong — as has played out in multiple Tesla lawsuits. It's unclear exactly where Toyota's system will land on this spectrum; InsideEVs had not heard back from Toyota at time of writing.

The key difference from Tesla: per Nikkei, Toyota isn't betting everything on the end-to-end AI approach Tesla relies on, where a single neural network handles everything at once. Instead, Toyota plans to layer in a "guardrail" system that monitors the AI's behavior and blocks anything unexpected, based on parameters set by humans in advance. Toyota believes this could meaningfully improve safety, claiming it could prevent around 60% of today's traffic fatalities and injuries.

Akihiro Sarada, head of Toyota's Software Development Center, told Nikkei: "We'll develop Level 4 technology from a technical standpoint, but offer it commercially as Level 2++ ... We'll proceed in a way that ensures both safety and explainability based on the technology." In other words, even though the underlying tech is built to a higher standard, drivers will still be on the hook if the car does something it isn't supposed to.

Toyota plans to introduce the system on 2028 models and expand it across more vehicles by 2030. Which countries get it first is unclear, but the U.S. wouldn't be surprising, as Mercedes-Benz (MB.Drive Assist Pro) and Rivian (Autonomy+) work on comparable systems. Separately, Japan is targeting roughly 10,000 Level 4 taxis, buses, and other vehicles on its roads by 2030, having already legalized Level 4 systems.

Toyota is also reportedly in talks to partner with Waymo as its entry point into the robotaxi business, though details and a timeline remain scarce. The moves signal Japanese automakers — who've largely watched from the sidelines as U.S. and Chinese companies push autonomous driving forward — are now working to catch up.
