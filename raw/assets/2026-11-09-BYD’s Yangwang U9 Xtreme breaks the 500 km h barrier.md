# BYD’s Yangwang U9 Xtreme breaks the 500 km/h barrier

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-07

---

503.17 km/h: The Yangwang U9 Xtreme has become the first production car to exceed 500 km/h in a one-way attempt in Abu Dhabi. With this achievement, BYD's luxury brand has broken its own record and demonstrated just how absurdly powerful electric drivetrains have become.

On 1 November, the U9 Xtreme reached 503.17 km/h on a closed section of the E11 in the Al-Dhafra region. Behind the wheel once again was German racing driver Marc Basseng. The FIA has confirmed the speed, and Guinness World Records lists it as the highest speed for a production car and an electric production car in a single-direction run.

This distinction is important. Traditional speed records are typically determined from two runs in opposite directions to account for factors such as wind and gradient. In the case of Yangwang's record, it is explicitly a "single-direction run".

This improves on the BYD subsidiary's previous record of 496.22 km/h, which the U9 Xtreme set in September 2025 at the Papenburg test track in Lower Saxony. Crossing the 500 km/h threshold thus represents an increase of just 6.95 km/h.

## Four motors and over 3,000 HP

The record attempt spanned two days. On 31 October, the maximum speed achieved was initially 490 km/h. Overnight, the engineers adjusted parameters such as battery temperature, motor temperatures, the rear wing angle, suspension software, and tyre pressures. On the second run the following day, the speedometer finally registered 503.17 km/h. The U9 Xtreme features four electric motors that together deliver over 3,000 HP. According to Yangwang, each motor can reach up to 30,000 rpm and up to 555 kW. The power-to-weight ratio is stated as 1,217 HP per tonne.

This is complemented by a 1,200-volt architecture, whereas the standard U9 operates at 800 volts. The system is said to support currents of up to 1,000 amperes. The LFP-based Blade Battery achieves a discharge rate of up to 30C. Even at 20 per cent state of charge, it is claimed to deliver up to 1,800 kW of power. Regenerative braking power is stated as exceeding 700 kW.

The active DiSus-X suspension can intervene individually at each wheel. Combined with the torque vectoring of the four motors, it is designed to stabilise the body at extremely high speeds. Specially developed semi-slick tyres from Giti Tire are also intended to prevent the tyres and rims from shifting under the enormous forces.

The U9 Xtreme also already holds the lap record for electric production vehicles on the Nürburgring Nordschleife with 6:59.157 minutes.

However, the U9 Xtreme will not enter series production: Yangwang plans to build only 30 units. The first customers have already finalised their individual configurations. No price has been disclosed.
