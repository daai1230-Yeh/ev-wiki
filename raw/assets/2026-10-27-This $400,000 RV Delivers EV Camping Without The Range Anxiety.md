# This $400,000 RV Delivers EV Camping Without The Range Anxiety

**Author**: Brett Berk | **Date**: Oct 25, 2026

---

The $400,000 mobile home brings electrification to the RV space in a whole new way.

At this year's iteration of The Quail, held during Monterey Car Week, there were very few electric vehicles on offer. But on the far side of the field there was one, and it really stood out. Intended to go on sale in 2027 at a price of around $400,000, it was nearly 30 feet long, weighed 18,000 lb, and sleeps four people comfortably. The motto on the booth's t-shirts: "Quiet Luxury."

The Entegra Coach Embark is a Class-A motorhome. Constructed by the Jayco family of recreational vehicles and built atop a commercial chassis developed by California-based startup Harbinger, it is powered by a 140-kWh battery mated to a 440-horsepower electric motor. This is good for around 110 miles on a charge. To relieve range anxiety, it also features a 1.6-liter four-cylinder engine that acts as a generator, pumping electrons into the pack. The combustion motor doesn't drive the wheels directly, but its presence allows the RV to travel nearly 450 miles on a full battery and a full tank of gas.

According to Harbinger co-founder John Harris, this makes it the first extended range electric recreational vehicle, or ERERV. "It's a brand new category," Harris says.

The drivetrain suits the intended audience: long drives followed by days of parking while running air conditioners, TVs and kitchen appliances. A traditional RV uses a large 7-plus-liter engine plus an auxiliary generator and separate batteries. The Embark simplifies these layered-up systems. "You get there, and the 140-kWh battery that runs the vehicle is now your house battery. You can run your RV for a week or more," Harris says. To recharge in a campground you can run the engine in Camp Mode or plug into a hookup.

Benefits: Harris had just taken it on a cross-country trip and averaged 16 mpg, about 2-3 times that of an average RV. Tailpipe emissions are reduced significantly, and regenerative braking recoups energy descending mountains. Mitch Johnson, head of Jayco's electric division, says users can camp at more remote locations and "realistically camp for multiple weeks without a hookup."

Drawbacks: Entegra is focused on the dash user interface so owners know what the chassis is doing, since range anxiety now extends to the camping experience. The Embark is also slightly smaller than other Class A RVs and lacks slide-outs, intentionally, to reduce weight and increase range and improve durability and sealing. Interior amenities are high-end; a tour of the prototype felt more like an Airbnb than a typical RV.

Jayco first showed the vehicle in January 2025 and says the market response has been great, but wanted a careful rollout with additional testing. It loans its prototype to its development team and has given a pair to RV rental partner THL in Los Angeles, collecting telematics on charging behaviour and feedback.

The $400,000 price may be a sticking point, but Harris counters that the Embark brings far lower per-mile and maintenance costs. "It's basically what you would spend for a nice RV. But you're getting so much more flexible capability." The cabin silence is also a tangible benefit.
