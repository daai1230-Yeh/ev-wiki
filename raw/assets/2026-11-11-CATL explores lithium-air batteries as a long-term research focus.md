# CATL explores lithium-air batteries as a long-term research focus

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-10

---

CATL is considering lithium-air batteries as a long-term research option. The technology theoretically promises a significantly higher energy density than today's lithium-ion batteries. However, it is still far from market-ready vehicle batteries or a range of 2,000 km.

CATL Chief Scientist Wu Kai has positioned lithium-air batteries as a long-term research focus for the company at the Equipment Powerhouse Forum 2026. Sodium-ion and solid-state technologies remain closer to commercialisation at CATL. Current attention is primarily drawn to the potential for ranges of up to 2,000 km. The basis for such projections is the very high theoretical specific energy of lithium-air chemistry, at around 3,505 Wh/kg. However, this value describes the electrochemical potential of the reaction, not the energy density of a complete vehicle battery.

Lithium-air systems use metallic lithium as the negative electrode and oxygen from the surrounding environment as the reaction partner. During discharge, lithium peroxide forms in a porous cathode; during charging, the reaction is reversed, and oxygen is released again.

Because the oxygen does not need to be carried as a traditional cathode material, this theoretically results in a very high specific energy. However, for a real battery system, electrolyte, electrodes, housing, protective systems, and – above all – components for air processing must be added. Even higher theoretical values of around 12,000 Wh/kg are based on calculations that exclude the mass of the required oxygen.

The gap between theory and a production-ready battery is significant. In 2024, researchers from Jilin University and the Chinese Academy of Sciences achieved 757.5 Wh/kg and 241 charge cycles with a lithium-air pouch cell under defined laboratory conditions.

## CATL's lithium-air batteries still face challenges

Argonne National Laboratory and the Illinois Institute of Technology introduced a solid-state lithium-air cell in 2023 that completed at least 1,000 charge and discharge cycles at room temperature, with a theoretical potential of around 1,200 Wh/kg. Both results show progress but are not equivalent to a validated battery for production vehicles.

A central challenge remains the air supply. The battery requires oxygen, while moisture and carbon dioxide must be reliably kept out, as both can damage electrodes and electrolytes. A vehicle would need a controlled air supply system with filtration and humidity management, which increases weight, space requirements and energy consumption. Further issues include cycle stability, charging efficiency and the stability of the lithium-metal anode. Lithium peroxide can also deposit in the cathode pores, hindering oxygen transport.

A high specific energy alone is not sufficient for EVs: volumetric energy density, power output, fast-charging capability, safety, lifespan and overall system weight are also decisive. The frequently cited 2,000 km cannot currently be applied to a concrete vehicle.

CATL is simultaneously pursuing more market-ready technologies such as sodium-ion and solid-state cells. According to SNE Research, CATL installed 143.7 GWh of battery capacity outside China in the first nine months of 2026, a market share of 33.7 per cent. The LFP share of the market outside China increased from 15.8 to 27.7 per cent within a year.

Whether lithium-air will eventually lead to EVs with four-digit ranges depends less on theoretical energy density and more on whether the numerous chemical and technical challenges can be resolved in a long-lasting, safe and affordable battery system.
