# Lyten to acquire insolvent battery cell manufacturer Morrow Batteries

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 27.08.2026

---

US battery developer Lyten is now seeking to acquire the insolvent Norwegian battery cell manufacturer Morrow Batteries, after acquiring Swedish battery maker Northvolt. If the deal goes through, Morrow's battery technology activities could continue to be based at the company's site in Arendal.

Lyten has signed a letter of intent to acquire Morrow Batteries, in collaboration with Innovation Norway — Norway's state-owned economic development and investment bank — and Morrow's insolvency administrators. The Norwegian battery specialist initiated insolvency proceedings earlier this year for Morrow Batteries ASA, Morrow Technologies AS and Morrow Industrialisation Center AS, and declared insolvency in early summer. Morrow opened a smaller industrial plant for LFP battery cells in 2024 and stated in June this year that it aimed to salvage at least part of its operations.

## Bidding process with multiple interested parties

The insolvency administrators and Innovation Norway had been negotiating with several bidders and finally selected Lyten as the preferred bidder. The three partners agreed to keep the details of the letter of intent confidential.

Leon Bakkebø, Executive Vice President at Innovation Norway responsible for financing and banking, stated that as a secured creditor their priority throughout the process has been to maximise value recovery and reduce execution risk, and that they support the trustee's decision to proceed with Lyten because the proposal provides the strongest overall combination of financial recovery, transaction certainty and industrial credibility. He noted that if completed, the transaction could also create a foundation for future battery-related activity in Arendal.

## 70 jobs secured in the short term

Lyten is already familiar with acquiring insolvent Nordic battery makers from its acquisition of the remaining assets of Swedish manufacturer Northvolt. Christian Bohne, the lawyer administering Morrow's insolvency estate, explained that the goal is to preserve value and seek a solution for the company's continued existence, and pointed out that employees have been severely affected by the insolvency — their commitment having been instrumental in enabling the agreement.

In the short term, Bohne said the process secures around 70 jobs until autumn. In the long term, several hundred jobs at Morrow and orders for local material and service providers could be safeguarded. The insolvency administrator warned that there are circumstances and conditions beyond their control that must be met before the transaction can be completed, but said he is optimistic about Lyten's ability to execute.

## Lyten aims to establish European production base

Dan Cook, CEO and co-founder of Lyten, sees significant potential in Europe, pointing out that Europe has entered a sustained period of infrastructure investment and that batteries are now an important part of this critical infrastructure. Lyten aims to build a financially robust and scalable production base for batteries and energy storage in Europe, coupling technologies, supply chains and production sites in the USA and Europe. Cook said the Morrow team has built a business aligned with this strategy, making the assets a strong strategic fit.

Lyten is evaluating expansion of the Morrow site into a potential "Lyten Industrial Hub", integrating battery production, upstream and downstream activities in the energy storage value chain, and industrial activities supporting other critical sectors including AI data centres, mobility and defence. Lyten also plans a similar hub in Skellefteå at the former Northvolt site.

Northvolt and Morrow Batteries were long regarded as two of the few companies capable of producing battery cells in Europe but were unable to sustain activities. While Northvolt struggled with production quality during its ramp-up phase, Morrow fell victim to its small size in a consolidating market. The plant opened in summer 2024 at its Arendal headquarters for production of prismatic LFP cells with an annual capacity of just 1 GWh — far too small to supply a major car manufacturer, sufficient for only around 25,000 small cars with a 40-kWh battery. For comparison, Volkswagen subsidiary PowerCo states its new cell factory in Salzgitter has an annual capacity of 20 GWh, enough for around 250,000 small cars.

## Morrow lacked investors for expansion to 43 GWh

Morrow Batteries had planned to expand the Arendal site in four stages to eventually reach 43 GWh, but this never materialised due to a lack of investors. Even the ramp-up of the existing 1-GWh plant proved a significant challenge, and the cell factory never achieved series production. As recently as January 2026 — nearly a year and a half after the plant's opening — Morrow stated it was ready for series production but was adopting a "partnership-based approach", seeking long-term investments and strategic alliances to accelerate technology validation and secure offtake agreements.

Morrow's management commented on the insolvency in June, stating the background was complex: the company had been operating in an early and capital-intensive industrialisation phase while the global battery market became more competitive with oversupply and resulting price pressure. Increased capital costs, delays in the industrialisation process and a more restrained investment market made it significantly more difficult to secure financing.
