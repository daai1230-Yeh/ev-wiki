# One Owner Just Did What Tesla Couldn't: Upgrade An Older Car To HW4

**Author**: Rob Stumpf | **Date**: Aug 18, 2026

---

*Tesla has been kicking the HW3 can down the road for nearly two years. One owner got tired of waiting around.*

**Key points**

- A Tesla owner beat the automaker to the punch by retrofitting newer FSD hardware into his older Model 3.
- The HW3-equipped car received new cameras and Tesla's newer HW4 computer.
- It took very few parts to actually complete, according to the successful modder.

Tesla backed itself into a corner with the hardware baked into its cars. Millions of Teslas sold between 2019 and 2023 shipped with computers Tesla marketed as "capable" of Full Self-Driving. The company later admitted this isn't the case, claiming that retrofitting those cars with newer hardware would require a series of micro factories.

One owner didn't want to wait. Polish Tesla hacker Mike Gapinski became the first person to retrofit Hardware 4 into a Hardware 3-equipped car, documenting the process on X. His working Frankenstein car is a 2022 Tesla Model 3 with a scavenged Autopilot computer from a Hardware 4 Model Y.

Mike says piecing together the parts was "not as bad as it seemed," requiring only the Model Y's computer, some Hardware 4 repeater cameras, and some light ECU hacking. He was even able to reuse the car's existing coaxial camera wiring, adapting it to the new HW4 cameras using adapters for the factory FAKRA connectors. Tesla's robust service reference for electrical schematics made identifying connectors straightforward. Fitting the HW4 ECU was fairly easy but required some improvisation.

Tesla has been dragging its feet on offering any retrofit for nearly two years, leaving HW3 owners with a "Lite" version of FSD viewed by the community as inferior to HW4. It has even been suspected of causing Autopilot computers to overheat by stressing the hardware to its limits.

> "Honestly Tesla could have done it ages ago and offer a paid upgrade," wrote Mike on X. "If they end up doing it it doesn't need so much new parts, but you need to replace all the cameras."

A few tweaks remain to resolve trouble codes, but the car is running and driving. Hacking mods on a software-centric car isn't as simple as bolting on a new intake — it's about knowing how to work with the programming, a completely different skill from turning wrenches.
