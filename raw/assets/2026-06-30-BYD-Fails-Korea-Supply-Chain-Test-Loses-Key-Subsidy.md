# BYD Fails Korea Supply Chain Test, Loses Key Subsidy

**Author**: Yoo Min-hwan | **Published**: 2026-06-30 18:11:12

---

BYD, China's largest electric vehicle manufacturer, has been excluded from Korea's purchase subsidy program due to poor performance in domestic supply chain contribution evaluation—the category carrying the highest weight in the assessment process.

Last month, the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment revised subsidy recipient criteria, emphasizing development of the domestic EV ecosystem. This shift immediately disqualified Chinese companies including BYD and Zeekr.

The ministry evaluated 35 companies across five categories: supply chain contribution (40 points), after-sales management and continuity (20 points), safety management (15 points), environmental policy response (15 points), and technology development capability (10 points). Twenty-seven automakers and importers achieving at least 60 points qualified as subsidy program operators.

In the passenger vehicle category, 10 of 14 applicants were selected. Four rejections included BYD, which entered Korea last year. The supply chain contribution assessment evaluates domestic production facility operations, job creation, and impact on parts industry and regional supply chains. BYD scored poorly because it maintains no domestic production facilities and contributes nothing to domestic parts usage or job creation.

Zeekr couldn't apply because domestic certification of its "7X" model remained incomplete during evaluation.

Tesla made the subsidy recipient list, scoring higher in domestic industry contribution partly because LG Energy Solution supplies its batteries. Despite operating only 16 directly-operated service centers in Korea—generating job creation points—Tesla outperformed Chinese competitors.

"BYD expanded aggressively, establishing 34 showrooms and 18 service centers within a year, but received lower evaluation than Tesla because it operates through dealer partnerships," an industry official stated.

BYD faces significant setback to its sales strategy. The approximately 2.5 million won per-vehicle subsidy disappears July 1. BYD entered the import car "Top 10" with 6,107 domestic units last year, and sold 7,023 units through May this year, ranking fourth after Tesla, BMW, and Mercedes-Benz. Since price competitiveness—3-4 million won cheaper than comparable vehicles—constituted its major advantage, sales slowdown possibilities have surfaced.

Additional subsidy recipients: nine cargo companies (Hyundai Motor, Kia, DPECO, Route17, KG Mobility, and Tata Daewoo Mobility) and eight bus operators (Hyundai Motor, Bumhan Auto, iBus, KG Mobility Commercial, and MTR).
