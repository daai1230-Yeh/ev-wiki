# BMW begins Gen6 battery production in Irlbach-Straßkirchen

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-10-02

---

BMW has officially opened its new battery plant in Irlbach-Straßkirchen and launched series production. The site will supply sixth-generation high-voltage batteries initially for the new BMW i3 from Munich and is set to play a central role in Germany's production network in the future.

Just two and a half years after construction began, BMW's new plant in Irlbach-Straßkirchen, Lower Bavaria, has entered series production. Bavarian Minister-President Markus Söder attended the opening of the new facility, emphasising the importance of the location south of Regensburg for industrial value creation and electromobility in Bavaria. Due to high demand for the Neue Klasse models, BMW is ramping up the site faster than originally planned and has already begun operating in two shifts. The manufacturer is investing around one billion euros in the plant.

At launch, Irlbach-Straßkirchen is supplying the Munich plant with sixth-generation high-voltage batteries for the new BMW i3. In the future, the site is set to serve as a central hub, providing Gen6 batteries to other German vehicle plants. The production facility was built on a site covering approximately 60 hectares. Less than two years passed between the start of construction in April 2024 and the production of the first pre-series batteries in March 2026. At full capacity, around 1,600 people will work at the plant. BMW aims to produce several hundred high-voltage batteries per day across two production lines.

The new BMW site initially faced opposition in the region. It was only after a citizen referendum in September 2023 that the way was cleared for the plant's establishment. As early as spring 2025, BMW announced that the central production building had been completed on schedule and that installation of the production equipment could begin.

## BMW adopts 800 volt and cell-to-pack systems for Gen6

The start of production in Irlbach-Straßkirchen is part of the broader Gen6 technology transition for the Neue Klasse. For the first time, BMW is adopting an 800-volt architecture and large-format cylindrical cells with a 46-millimetre diameter. According to BMW, these new cylindrical cells offer around 20 per cent higher energy density compared to the previous prismatic cells.

The cells are integrated directly into the high-voltage battery using the cell-to-pack principle. The battery housing itself becomes part of the vehicle structure through the pack-to-open-body principle. BMW promises faster charging and greater ranges for Gen6 technology compared to Gen5.

Production in Irlbach-Straßkirchen is highly automated. Across approximately 50,000 square metres of production space, 500 robots are in use. More than 3,500 data points are recorded during the production of each battery and monitored in real time. AI-based assistance systems are used to analyse the data and ensure process quality.

For its Gen6 batteries, BMW is pursuing a global "local for local" strategy. In addition to Irlbach-Straßkirchen, new assembly plants are being or have been established in Debrecen, Shenyang, Woodruff, and San Luis Potosí. The goal is to locate battery assembly as close as possible to the respective vehicle plants.

BMW sources the battery cells themselves from external cell manufacturers. At the plant, they are inspected, assembled into cell clusters, connected to coolers, and then welded. A foaming process mechanically bonds the components, increasing stability and protection. Finally, each high-voltage battery undergoes a comprehensive end-of-line inspection.

BMW also aims to improve its CO2 footprint through material usage. The Gen6 cells incorporate secondary materials for cobalt, lithium, and nickel. According to BMW, renewable energy is used in the production of anode and cathode materials as well as in cell manufacturing. This is expected to reduce CO2 emissions per watt-hour by around 33 per cent compared to Gen5 cells.

The new BMW i3 will initially use a 108.7 kWh battery from the Lower Bavarian production site. For the i3 50 xDrive, BMW quotes a WLTP range of up to 906 kilometres. Thanks to 800-volt technology and DC fast charging at up to 400 kW, up to 423 kilometres of range can be added in just ten minutes. The i3 also supports bidirectional charging.

The plant is part of a larger Bavarian battery network. Development and pilot production take place in locations such as Parsdorf, Hallbergmoos, and the Munich Research and Innovation Centre. In nearby Salching, BMW has also been operating a competence centre for direct recycling of battery cells and production residues since the end of 2025.
