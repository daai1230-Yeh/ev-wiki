# EU introduces import quotas for hybrids from China and other countries

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 24.11.2026 - 13:15

---

It's official - from December, the European Union will introduce import quotas for hybrid cars from China, as well as restrict imports from other countries. The regulation will initially apply for 200 days, limiting the import of hybrid cars from China to exactly 191,780 units during this period.

With the so-called hybrid import brake, the EU Commission is responding to the sharp rise in vehicle imports from China. The newly published regulation will enter into force on 1 December and will initially apply for 200 days, until 18 June 2027. A corresponding directive from the EU Commission has now been published in the Official Journal of the European Union. During this period, only the import of 191,780 hybrid cars from China will be permitted.

This figure is derived from the recently agreed annual import quota between the EU and China of 350,000 hybrid vehicles. This figure applies for 365 days, but when adjusted to 200 days, it results in the aforementioned approximately 192,000 units. The regulation covers plug-in hybrids and full hybrids, including mild hybrids. However, it does not apply to range-extender vehicles. This category of vehicles will continue to be classified as battery-electric vehicles and will thus remain subject to the EU’s special tariffs on electric cars.

According to the EU Commission, the trade barriers “follow an investigation that showed imports of hybrid vehicles into the EU have increased significantly since 2023”. The aim is to moderate Chinese exports of hybrid vehicles to the EU, but the rules will also apply to all other hybrid vehicle imports during the 200 days—regardless of their origin. To this end, the EU is introducing specific import quotas per exporting country: “[This sets a] maximum quantity of hybrid vehicles that may be imported into the EU for the duration of the provisional safeguard measures. Imports outside these quotas are not allowed”.

The Commission cites the reason for the sharp increase in hybrid passenger car imports into the EU as “growing global production overcapacity”. In summary, the analysis states that, as other countries close their markets with high trade barriers, much of this overproduction is currently flooding the European market. This, in turn, weakens domestic manufacturers – “despite a growing EU market,” the Commission emphasises, which sees the EU industry threatened by “serious injury” and is therefore now also raising trade barriers.

The import quotas are allocated in two ways: to specific countries that, according to the EU, have a significant interest in supplying hybrid passenger cars to the EU market (‘country-specific quotas’), and to all other countries of origin (a ‘residual quota’). The table above shows the countries that have been assigned their own import quotas. The level of these quotas has been set by the EU “at the level of imports recorded from the respective country in 2025, increased by a little over 15%”. In principle, all countries that accounted for more than two percent of EU imports in 2025 receive a country-specific quota. For all other countries of origin, the aforementioned residual quota is provided.

For now, the quotas apply only for a maximum of 200 days. In principle, the EU can adopt definitive measures within nine months (plus a possible two-month extension) after initiating the so-called ‘Safeguard Investigation’. The EU launched this investigation on 30 October 2026. The period for establishing other protective mechanisms therefore extends until the summer. Specifically for imports from China, there has already been talk of a permanent hybrid import brake, under which the quota would be allowed to grow by six percent annually.
