# South Korea's EV Sales Over 100 Million Won Surge 2.6-Fold; Q4 Sets Stage for Luxury Electric Showdown

**Author**: BigGo Finance | **Date**: 2026-09-27

---

In the first half of this year, new registrations of electric vehicles priced above 100 million won (approximately $74,000) in South Korea reached 7,050 units, a 2.6-fold increase from the same period last year. Their share of total EV sales also expanded from 3.3% to 4.0%. In the fourth quarter, high-priced EV launches from Genesis (GV90), Bentley (Torcal), and BMW (new i7) are expected to accelerate competition in the premium segment. Meanwhile, the 70-million-won (approximately $52,000) range has emerged as the fiercest battleground for imported EVs, with the Volvo ES90, Lexus ES350e, Polestar 3, and BMW iX3 all converging there.

According to data released by CarIsYou Data Research Institute on the 27th, total EV sales in South Korea reached 176,570 units in the first half, more than doubling from 83,088 units a year earlier. While Tesla led the market's growth as the share of entry-level models in the 40–50 million won (approximately $30,000–$37,000) range grew from 42.3% to 51.2%, premium EVs above 100 million won also showed parallel growth.

**Clash of Flagship Electric SUVs**

Among South Korean brands, Genesis will debut the GV90 and GV90 Neolun — the brand's first ultra-large flagship electrified SUV — before year-end. The GV90 is built on Genesis's flagship-dedicated "eMP" platform, equipped with a 123.5 kWh battery and a motor producing up to 490 kW of output.

Global high-end brand Bentley Motors recently unveiled the Torcal, the marque's first luxury all-electric SUV. It delivers 888 horsepower and 137.7 kg·m of peak torque, accelerating from 0 to 100 km/h in 2.9 seconds. Its 113 kWh battery provides up to 600 km of range on the WLTP cycle, and 400 kW fast charging can take the battery from 10% to 80% in 16 minutes.

BMW plans to launch the new i7 luxury sedan in South Korea in the second half of the year, extending WLTP range beyond 720 km. Volvo Car Korea launched the all-electric flagship EX90 SUV in April, featuring a 106 kWh battery and twin-motor all-wheel drive with up to 454 km of range. Porsche's all-electric Cayenne Electric SUV and Cayenne Coupe Electric are slated for a domestic launch in the second half of this year.

**The 70-Million-Won Range: A New Battleground for Imported EVs**

Volvo's next-generation ES90 sedan starts at 72.94 million won (approximately $54,000) in South Korea. Lexus has priced the ES350e at a pre-order starting price of 71.6 million won. The Polestar 3 starts at 77.9 million won for the rear-motor variant. BMW added an SE trim priced at 79.9 million won to the new iX3 lineup.

The convergence on the 70-million-won range reflects consumer price resistance, the 80-million-won regulatory threshold for the green license plate requirement, and falling production costs. Vehicles priced at 85 million won or above are currently excluded from national EV subsidies, with the threshold set to drop below 80 million won next year. Tesla, unlike other brands, has raised prices to enter the range: the six-seat Model Y L launched at 64.99 million won and now stands at 72.99 million won after two increases.
