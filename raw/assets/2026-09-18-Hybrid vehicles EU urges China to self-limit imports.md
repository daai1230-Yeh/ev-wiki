# Hybrid vehicles: EU urges China to self-limit imports

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-09-17 11:30

---

The European Union has reportedly called on China to voluntarily restrict its exports of hybrid cars to Europe. Otherwise, special tariffs — already under discussion within the EU — could be imposed. Currently, these tariffs only apply to pure electric vehicles from China.

The FT reports that the EU is currently still relying on China to voluntarily comply and independently limit the import of hybrid vehicles – but that this could be set to change, citing three individuals familiar with the situation. "If they will not limit their exports to our market then we will," an EU official is quoted as saying. "This is about stopping deindustrialisation. We have to act. It's about managed trade."

Specifically, the EU is seeking a commitment from Beijing to limit the sales of hybrid vehicles manufactured in China to around 15 per cent of the EU market — currently, such vehicles account for more than a third of the market, according to the sources. Whether China will agree to this is expected to become clear during "crunch meetings next month in Beijing." The background is that, in June, the EU set a deadline until October for "concrete results" in reducing its rapidly growing trade deficit with China.

Pressure for action on hybrid vehicles is now mounting, as their imports from China into the EU reached a record high in the second quarter: "While imports of battery EVs subsequently rose gently, imports of Chinese hybrid cars – which only pay a flat 10 per cent EU tariff – soared," states the FT. Their sales have increased more than tenfold, from 3,800 vehicles in October 2024 to 50,000 in July. The business newspaper does not specify the electrification level of the vehicles, so it remains unclear whether the current negotiations primarily concern plug-in hybrids or all types of hybrids.

Since October 2024, pure electric vehicles from China have been subject to a special tariff upon import into the EU, which the Union imposes while citing distortive subsidies. These penalties vary by manufacturer: In addition to the existing base import tariff of 10 per cent, for example, BYD's electric cars incur an additional 17 per cent, totalling 27 per cent. For Geely, the special tariff is 18.8 per cent, resulting in a total tariff of 28.8 per cent. For SAIC, the manufacturer of MG, the special tariff is 35.3 per cent, bringing the total to 45.3 per cent.

As a result, it became evident as early as summer 2025 that particularly active Chinese brands in Europe – such as BYD and MG – were increasingly focusing on plug-in hybrids, which are only subject to the regular car tariff of 10 per cent. This trend has continued throughout the year. In early summer, Handelsblatt reported that the European Commission now intended to intervene, despite having denied plans for special tariffs on Chinese hybrids as recently as January 2026. According to senior EU officials, a corresponding anti-subsidy investigation is already being prepared. Furthermore, if special tariffs on hybrids are introduced, they are expected to be manufacturer-specific as well – based on earlier information from the Handelsblatt.
