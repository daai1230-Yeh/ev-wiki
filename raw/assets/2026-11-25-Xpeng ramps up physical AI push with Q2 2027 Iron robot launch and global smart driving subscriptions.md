# Xpeng ramps up physical AI push with Q2 2027 Iron robot launch and global smart driving subscriptions

**Author:** Phate Zhang　｜　**Date:** 2026-11-25

**KEY TAKEAWAYS**
- Xpeng unveiled its Turing Physical AI Architecture (VLA 3.0) at 2026 AI Day, setting 2027 for both the official release of its Iron humanoid robot and its first purpose-built robotaxi, the Yoyo-01.
- The company also announced paid Turing AI smart driving subscriptions starting in the second quarter of 2027.

Xpeng (NYSE: XPEV) held its 2026 AI Day on Wednesday in its headquarters city of Guangzhou, unveiling a roadmap spanning humanoid robots, robotaxis, and smart driving as it doubles down on physical AI.

He Xiaopeng, Xpeng chairman and CEO, said at the event that the industry has long severely underestimated the difficulty of implementing AI in the real physical world, and that Xpeng's latest solution represents a rethinking of this.

Physical AI faces multiple hard constraints — including regulations, safety, cost, hardware bodies, and power — that cannot be solved merely by scaling up model capabilities, he said.

Unlike autonomous driving firms focusing solely on chassis control or Silicon Valley players betting exclusively on general-purpose large models, Xpeng unveiled its Turing Physical AI Architecture (VLA 3.0) to connect the full stack through what it called a 3-layer coordination.

The architecture links System 0 for underlying real-time hardware control, System 1 powered by the second-generation Vision-Language-Action (VLA 2.0) model, and System 2 for task planning and reasoning.

Through this shared technology foundation, Xpeng's smart vehicles, robotaxis, humanoid robots, and flying cars can reuse computing power, data, and safety systems, diluting marginal R&D costs.

## Iron humanoid robot moves toward real-world roles

At Xpeng's AI Day a year ago, the Iron humanoid robot's gait looked so lifelike that skeptics questioned whether a person was hidden inside, prompting the team to cut open its leg casing onstage to prove otherwise.

This year, Iron walked onto the stage autonomously, held a real-time voice interaction and delivered a speech with He, and then walked back to its charging spot on its own.

The advanced general-purpose humanoid robot stands 170 cm tall and weighs 62 kg. It features 76 degrees of freedom across its body and more than 1,500 tactile sensing points, with tactile coverage exceeding 80% of key areas.

Iron is powered by 3 of Xpeng's proprietary Turing AI chips and a solid-state battery pack with an energy density exceeding 300 Wh/kg. It supports autonomous mapping and round-the-clock scheduling, delivering 1.5 hours of work on a 20-minute charge.

Iron will enter hardware mass production in January 2027, begin training as a sales assistant in Xpeng's domestic retail stores in the first quarter, and see its official public release in the second quarter of 2027, the company said.

Prior to that, Iron will host Xpeng's press conference at the 2026 Guangzhou Auto Show on November 27, which He said could be the first time in history a robot launches a new vehicle at an Auto Show.

Commercially, Xpeng is exploring a mix of hardware sales, software and token subscriptions, and a skills store ecosystem for Iron.

In a post-event media briefing, He described his outlook on the robotics business as "extremely optimistic in the long term and cautious in the short term," saying its medium- to long-term industry scale will undoubtedly surpass that of automobiles.

However, he noted that the global robotics market may currently number only in the tens of thousands of units, with the core challenge being how to build advanced robots that do not require extensive human assistance and can achieve a viable return on investment (ROI).

## Custom robotaxi and low-altitude mobility accelerate

In autonomous mobility, Xpeng announced that its first purpose-built robotaxi model for commercial deployment, the Yoyo-01, will be released in 2027, with the company positioning itself as a technology and solution provider rather than a direct operator.

The program progressed from staffed testing to driverless road tests in 8 months and received L4 demonstration operation approval across all of Guangzhou in October, 11 months after the business was announced.

Xpeng's robotaxi has already begun driverless passenger testing and is slated to launch demonstration operations in selected areas by the end of 2026, before partnering with operators to export replicable solutions overseas.

Technically, the vehicle uses a vision-only approach without LiDAR or high-definition maps, integrating VLA and vision-language models (VLM) onboard while being the first to incorporate VLT brain capabilities for complex task planning.

In aerial mobility, Xpeng's Aridge unit has received Type Inspection Authorization and completed certification flight tests for its manned eVTOL program, and is partnering locally in the United Arab Emirates to establish a low-altitude regulatory sandbox.

## Paid smart driving subscriptions

In passenger vehicle smart driving, Xpeng announced that Turing AI smart driving will be offered as a paid subscription for new global buyers starting in the second quarter of 2027, while customers who place orders beforehand will retain lifetime access.

The move extends Xpeng's push to monetize software; in January, the company already reintroduced tiered paid advanced smart driving packages across 4 updated models.

He said smart driving requires long-term training and continuous iteration, making a healthy and reasonable business model essential to sustaining user experience.

Since VLA 2.0 began rolling out in March, assisted-driving mileage exceeded 50% of total mileage in its first month, and vehicle accident claim rates in China fell by 13.93%, He said.

The latest version supports AI agent-driven autonomous closed-loop training, boosting overall training speed by 10 to 12 times, while enabling recursive self-improvement (RSI) and model self-distillation.

On the global front, following localization tests in Germany, the Netherlands, and France and the launch of public urban NGP test rides at its Paris store in November, Xpeng aims to begin the first global deliveries of NGP and VLA 2.0 in the first half of 2027.

Upcoming OTA updates will also roll out all-scenario reversing out of tight spots, 3 stylized driving preferences, and full-scenario global left- and right-hand-drive coverage to adapt to overseas road features and traffic rules.

For existing owners, Xpeng leveraged distillation from its flagship Turing model and its proprietary XNRT inference engine to begin pushing the VLA 2.0 Lite distilled version to a first batch of 6 Max models equipped with dual Nvidia Orin-X chips in December.

The first batch covers the 2024 P7+ Max, Mona M03 Max, and the 2025 Max variants of the G6, G7, G9, and X9 — matching the capabilities of single-Turing Max models — with remaining dual Orin-X vehicles slated to receive the update in the first quarter of 2027.

Asked about Tesla's recently rolled-out Reality View real-time rendering feature in FSD v15, He said after the event that he had not tried it personally but believes such world-model interfaces are better suited for R&D and evaluation.

He said the feature is not technically difficult to achieve, but consumes significant computing power and electricity, ultimately coming down to whether it is worth committing those resources.

## 6C fast-charging OTA and cabin upgrades

Xpeng is also extending physical AI from assisted driving to whole-vehicle control, launching "Turing AI Co-driving" through its X-VMC 2.0 whole-vehicle motion control system to keep AI actively assisting during manual driving.

Its first feature, VLA Adaptive Deceleration, is scheduled for initial OTA roll-out in December, debuting on the G9L and Mona L05 and reducing brake pedal intervention by 95% in congested traffic.

Xpeng tests showed the feature increased usable driving range by 12.4% in urban congestion compared with having energy recovery turned off, while the AEX 360-degree active avoidance technology under development aims to coordinate responses to collision risks from all directions.

On energy efficiency, Xpeng integrated a proprietary hybrid silicon carbide (SiC) electric drive with 93.5% efficiency, a 31-in-1 integrated thermal management module that cuts thermal energy consumption by 14%, and destination-based energy management that lowers fuel consumption in extended-range models by 6.9%.

Xpeng also announced it will upgrade the charging rate of all existing 5C models to 6C via OTA across both lithium iron phosphate (LFP) and ternary lithium batteries, cutting 10% to 80% charging time to 10.7 minutes.

The company said the upgrade has passed over 1.2 million hours of durability testing, equivalent to more than 25 million kilometers (15.53 million miles); its first X-Energy megawatt flash charging station in Hong Kong has also commenced operations, with global deployment planned for 2027.

AI-driven dynamic adjustments are also entering the cabin, including X-ANC active noise cancellation that cuts noise by up to 7.2 decibels between 0 and 140 km/h, a 3-layer constant-temperature and constant-humidity system, and fully active AI seats supporting up to 32-way adjustment.
