# Opel and Leapmotor formalise battery-electric SUV partnership

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-11-23

---

Opel and Leapmotor have officially agreed to collaborate on the development of a new electric SUV. With a launch date penned for 2028, the model will use key components from the Chinese manufacturer and will be produced at Stellantis' plant in Zaragoza, Spain.

Opel CEO Florian Huettl and Leapmotor founder and CEO Zhu Jiangming have signed an agreement to jointly develop and produce a new battery-electric SUV in the C-segment. This formalises the framework for the already ongoing vehicle project. The EV will be developed in joint project centres at Opel’s headquarters in Rüsselsheim, Germany, and in Hangzhou, China.

The new model is set to expand Opel’s SUV portfolio from 2028, which currently consists of the Mokka, Frontera and Grandland. It will be produced at Stellantis’ Spanish plant in Zaragoza, where the Opel Corsa and, more recently, the Leapmotor B10 are already rolling off the production line. The project is part of Stellantis’ strategic plan FaSTLAne 2030, which aims to accelerate the group’s development processes and advance the electrification of its brands.

Technically, Opel is relying on close collaboration with Leapmotor. The Chinese manufacturer is contributing its expertise in batteries, electric drivetrains and electronic vehicle architecture. Opel is responsible for areas such as design, chassis and steering development, and interior ergonomics. The brand also aims to set its own accents in lighting technology, seating and user experience.

That the cooperation goes far beyond a classic platform takeover was already explained by Opel CEO Florian Huettl in October during a conversation with electrive at the Paris Motor Show. According to Huettl, the approximately 4.50-metre-long battery-electric SUV will be positioned between the Frontera and Grandland. Development began in mid-2026, and the basic design was already finalised at the time of the interview.

## Opel eyes faster development cycles

At the time, Huettl emphasised that this would not be a merely restyled Leapmotor. Instead, the engineering teams in Rüsselsheim and Hangzhou will jointly develop a new vehicle. The goal is to combine Chinese technological expertise with Opel’s brand-typical characteristics. A key reason for the cooperation is development speed. In his conversation with electrive, Huettl acknowledged that Chinese manufacturers have built a technological lead, particularly in electronic components, vehicle architectures, battery technology and electric drivetrains. Opel wants to close this gap both through its own developments within the Stellantis Group and through collaboration with Leapmotor.

Huettl was particularly impressed by the working methods of the Chinese engineering teams. Instead of lengthy planning phases, the focus is on immediate technical problem-solving. His approach to the collaboration was accordingly: “Less planning, more development.” Opel aims to develop the new SUV in less than two years.

Leapmotor is also set to play a major role in software. Huettl highlighted the Chinese manufacturer’s high-performance electronic architecture and its capabilities for over-the-air updates. Opel intends to use this technical foundation but will adapt functions and assistance systems to meet the requirements of the European market.

It is not yet known which specific components will ultimately be used in the new SUV. The already available Leapmotor B10 could at least serve as a technical reference point. The 4.51-metre-long battery-electric SUV is offered with LFP batteries of 56.2 or 67.1 kWh. In the larger variant, it achieves up to 434 km (WLTP) and charges at a maximum of 168 kW. However, it has not been confirmed whether this technology will be adopted unchanged for the Opel model.

## Stellantis further integrates production

The cooperation between Opel and Leapmotor is part of a broader partnership between the two manufacturers. Stellantis holds a 20 per cent stake in Leapmotor and, through its joint venture Leapmotor International – where the group has a 51 per cent stake – controls the Chinese manufacturer’s international activities outside China. Initially, the focus was primarily on the distribution of Leapmotor vehicles in international markets. Meanwhile, the two companies are increasingly collaborating on development and production. The Leapmotor B10 is already being manufactured at Stellantis’ Spanish plant in Zaragoza. With the new Opel SUV, a vehicle from a European group brand will now follow, incorporating Chinese core technology directly into its development.

In an October interview with electrive, Leapmotor Europe CEO Danilo Annese emphasised that the manufacturer wants to expand its presence in Europe in the long term. This is not just about distributing imported vehicles but increasingly about local production and European value creation. On-site manufacturing could, among other things, shorten transport routes and reduce dependence on international supply chains.

Stellantis CEO Antonio Filosa also sees additional opportunities for European plants in the partnership. He estimated their utilisation for 2025 at around 60 per cent. Additional models from Leapmotor could help make better use of existing production capacities. The Zaragoza plant plays a central role here, as it is set to produce both vehicles from the Chinese brand and the jointly developed Opel SUV.

At the same time, the technical cooperation is not automatically being extended to all Stellantis brands. Peugeot CEO Alain Favey explicitly stated at the Paris Motor Show that his brand currently does not intend to pursue a comparable development partnership with Leapmotor for Europe. Opel is thus pursuing an independent approach within the group.

Opel itself also does not plan a comprehensive model offensive based on the Leapmotor cooperation for the time being. According to Huettl, the product plan up to the end of 2030 initially includes only the SUV now contractually agreed upon. However, in the original announcement, Opel had already described the project as a potential blueprint for further collaboration.

With the official signing of the agreement, both manufacturers are continuing their already ongoing collaboration. For Opel, the project will particularly demonstrate whether the development speed and technological expertise of a Chinese partner can be combined with its own brand identity. Whether further joint models will emerge remains open for now.
