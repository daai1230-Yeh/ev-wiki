# ICCT report: China pulls ahead in battery-electric truck and bus sales

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 20.08.2026 - 15:00

---

In China, almost 30 per cent of all new registrations in the heavy truck segment last year were fully electric. While other world regions also report more electric commercial vehicle sales, none can keep up even remotely with China's pace. This is according to a new ICCT report on global demand for electric commercial vehicles.

The International Council on Clean Transportation (ICCT) regularly provides insights into the electrification progress of various world regions. In its latest report, "The ICCT Global EV Market Monitor: Trucks and Buses," analysts examine how battery-electric buses and trucks are establishing themselves in the global market. The study covers 34 countries, accounting for approximately 80 per cent of global truck and bus sales and 99 per cent of zero-emission truck and bus sales.

The study focuses on medium- and heavy-duty zero-emission vehicles (ZE-MHDV), with the truck threshold beginning at a gross vehicle weight over 3.5 tonnes — meaning larger battery-electric vans such as the Mercedes eSprinter (4.25 tonnes) are included.

## Key findings

Across the 34 countries analysed, sales of electric trucks and buses nearly doubled for the second consecutive year in 2025, rising 86 per cent year-on-year. Total sales reached 520,000 electric units, a global market share of around 14 per cent. This growth is primarily driven by China, which accounted for 88 per cent of global ZE-MHDV sales — though the ICCT notes "its share has begun to decline as sales in other markets accelerate."

Of the approximately 520,000 electric units sold, 457,300 were registered in China. Excluding China, global sales in 2025 amounted to around 63,000 units, led by the United States, the European Union and India. The US accounted for about 40 per cent of sales outside China, primarily medium-duty battery-electric trucks.

Composition varied significantly by country. In China, heavy and medium-duty trucks dominated, with buses accounting for only 11 per cent. In the United States, over 90 per cent of sales were medium-duty trucks, with buses and heavy trucks each about 3–4 per cent. In India and Brazil, the market was predominantly driven by buses and coaches, representing over half of EV sales.

## European Union

In 2025, the ICCT recorded over 23,700 new medium- and heavy-duty zero-emission vehicles in Europe. Zero-emission trucks (over 3.5 tonnes) achieved a 4.5 per cent sales share, while battery-electric buses accounted for 24.8 per cent of the total market.

"Sales growth was particularly strong in the zero-emission medium-duty truck and van segment (3.5–12 tonnes), where the ZE sales share more than doubled from 10.4 per cent in 2024 to 21.0 per cent in 2025," the report states. The battery-electric city bus share of total registrations rose from 45 per cent in 2024 to 58 per cent in 2025, while intercity and coach buses stagnated at around 4 per cent.

## Shift from buses to trucks

Between 2020 and 2025 the composition of global sales shifted significantly from battery-electric buses to trucks, primarily due to China's truck sector. In 2020, buses accounted for the majority of ZEVs registered worldwide. By 2022, combined medium- and heavy-duty battery-electric truck sales surpassed buses for the first time, "and the gap widened rapidly thereafter."

In 2025, battery-electric trucks accounted for 86 per cent of all electric vehicles in the statistics. Heavy battery-electric truck sales alone more than doubled from 2024 to 2025, reaching nearly 240,000 units — the largest single category worldwide. In China's heavy truck segment, 28 per cent of all new registrations were purely electric, with sales more than quintupling within two years. Battery-swapping systems and standardised infrastructure have played a key role.

China detail: medium-duty battery-electric trucks 174,700 units (22 per cent electric share); heavy battery-electric trucks 232,000 units (28 per cent); buses and coaches 50,500 units (29 per cent, with city buses at almost 100 per cent electric). Chinese ZE-MHDV sales have increased 527 per cent since 2020, and 98 per cent from 2024 to 2025 alone.

## United States

The 25,427 newly registered medium- and heavy-duty zero-emission vehicles accounted for just 3.1 per cent of the total market. Medium-duty battery-electric trucks led with 23,655 units (4.4 per cent electric share), followed by buses and coaches with 897 units (4.4 per cent), and heavy trucks with 875 units (0.3 per cent). Cumulative sales showed almost no change compared to 2024, up only 2 per cent year-on-year.
