# The 2027 Cadillac Escalade IQ Gets A NACS Port And Faster Home Charging

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-10-03

---

All 2027 GM EVs will come out of the factory with a Tesla-style charging port, but the Escalade EV gets a little extra.

- General Motors is making a few improvements to its flagship electric SUV.
- The 2027 Cadillac Escalade IQ comes with a native NACS port.
- A more powerful on-board charger comes standard across all trim levels.

The all-electric Cadillac Escalade will be a little easier to charge at Tesla Superchargers, and owners who charge at home are in for a treat. GM is ditching the old CCS1 charging port for a native Tesla-style NACS connector on the 2027 Escalade IQ and long-wheelbase Escalade IQL. GM's flagship electric SUV will also get a more powerful on-board charger across all trim levels, as discovered by GM Authority.

For 2027, the electric Escalade is equipped with a 19.2 kW on-board charger on every trim, to top up faster than on the outgoing model. By comparison, the 2026 Escalade came with an 11.5 kW module on the Luxury and Sport trims, whereas the Premium Luxury and Premium Sport were offered with the 19.2 kW OBC.

The upgraded on-board charger doesn't affect DC charging, but it's welcome for those who want to take full advantage of their home grid connection. The 2027 Escalade IQ also comes standard with the Dual Level Charge Cord, usable on 120V or 240V outlets, though charging the massive 205-kWh battery on Level 1 takes days, not hours. On a 120V outlet the cord provides just 1.4 kW, so a full 0-to-100% charge takes almost a week.

On the DC side, the native NACS port removes the need for a CCS-to-NACS adapter at Tesla Superchargers and other NACS stalls. Topping up at a CCS station will require a NACS-to-CCS adapter, though most non-Tesla public networks now offer both connectors.

The same NACS port will accept a NACS home charger — unlike Mercedes-Benz's newest EVs — but if a J1772 charger is already installed, the owner will need an adapter.

All General Motors EVs are scheduled to switch from CCS1 to NACS for the 2027 model year.
