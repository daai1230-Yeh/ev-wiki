# BYD launches third-gen Tang electric SUV starting at $28,000 with up to 850 km range

**Author:** Lei Kang　｜　**Date:** 2026-11-24

**KEY TAKEAWAYS**
- The 5-seat electric SUV forms a dual-flagship SUV series under BYD's Dynasty lineup alongside the 7-seat Da Tang.
- The third-generation Tang features BYD's new-generation drive-by-wire digital chassis.

BYD (HKEX: 1211) officially launched the third-generation Tang electric SUV on Tuesday, advancing the renewal of its flagship lineup with pricing significantly lower than its pre-sales figures.

The 5-seat mid to large-size SUV is available in 3 rear-wheel-drive (RWD) variants, starting at 187,900 yuan ($28,000), which is 16.49% lower than the 225,000 yuan pre-sales starting price announced on November 13.

The 3 variants are priced at 187,900 yuan, 207,900 yuan, and 219,900 yuan, respectively. Buyers of the top-spec trim can pay an additional 20,000 yuan for an all-wheel-drive (AWD) drive-by-wire optional package.

BYD debuted its new-generation drive-by-wire digital chassis on the model, supporting steer-by-wire and brake-by-wire technologies.

By eliminating the mechanical linkage between the steering wheel and the wheels, the system is claimed to improve response speed by more than 25%, it said.

In terms of powertrain, the RWD variants feature a 300 kW (402 hp) motor and offer CLTC battery ranges of 730 kilometers (454 miles) and 850 kilometers.

With the optional dual-motor AWD system, the vehicle delivers a combined power output of 515 kW, accelerates from 0 to 100 km/h in 4.3 seconds, and has a CLTC range of 770 kilometers.

The third-generation Tang is equipped with BYD's second-generation Blade Battery and flash charging technology, alongside the DiSus-A dual-chamber air suspension and rear-wheel steering, achieving a minimum turning radius of 5.6 meters.

The vehicle measures 5,045 mm in length and 1,980 mm in width, with a wheelbase of 2,950 mm. It adopts a 5-seat layout across the lineup, offering 1,003 mm of second-row legroom.

Inside, the cabin features a dual-screen setup combining a 35.4-inch integrated far-end display and a 30-inch panoramic screen. All 4 main seats come with ventilation, heating, and 10-point massage functions, and the car includes a 205-liter power frunk.

For smart driving and safety, the model is equipped with the LiDAR-enabled God's Eye B assisted driving system supporting urban navigation and smart parking, while coming standard with 11 airbags and the TBC high-speed tire blowout stability control system.

Following its launch, the third-generation Tang will join the 7-seat Da Tang rolled out in June to form a dual-flagship SUV lineup for the Dynasty lineup, targeting the large 5-seat and 7-seat family segments, respectively.

In addition to the regular Tang SUV and the Da Tang SUV, the Tang family also includes the Tang L.

According to Chinese automotive media outlet Auto Home, the Tang L will gradually phase out of the Chinese domestic market to focus on overseas expansion.

The intensive refresh of the Tang family is crucial for the new energy vehicle (NEV) giant. According to data compiled by CnEVPost, the Tang series sold 16,664 units in October, up 15.24% year-on-year, though cumulative sales from January to October fell 25.28% year-on-year to 98,278 units.
