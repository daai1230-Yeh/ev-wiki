# BYD to bring its kei car technology to Europe

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-09-01

---

Chinese manufacturer BYD is reportedly planning to develop an electric micro car for Europe and other markets based on its Racco kei car, which has so far only been available in Japan. This will include the use of the X-Pack, a novel battery pack with an inverter.

Kei cars are a special vehicle category promoted in Japan for decades, characterised by their ability to navigate narrow streets with ease. Under current regulations, these vehicles may be a maximum of 3.40 metres long and 1.48 m wide (excluding side mirrors) — significantly smaller than the smallest cars on the European market, such as the Fiat 500e (3.63 m long, 1.68 m wide).

BYD began its push into this market with its first kei car, the Racco. Launched at the Japan Mobility Show in Tokyo last October, it went on sale in July, featuring a permanent magnet synchronous motor on the front axle with 47 kW, a battery capacity of 36 kWh, and a range of 320 km. The base price is 2,145,000 yen (approx. €11,500).

As Nikkei reports, BYD has decided to use the Racco and its technology as a blueprint for other global markets, including Europe — not a direct 1:1 transfer, but one or more electric microcars that are larger and better suited to the European market. BYD is likely to ensure such models do not exceed 4.20 metres in length, allowing them to fit into the EU's newly planned M1E vehicle category, set to receive special support in future. BYD already has a 3.99-metre-long electric car on the European market, the Dolphin Surf, which falls into this category — so models based on the kei car are likely to be positioned between the Racco and the Dolphin Surf.

Another distinctive feature of BYD's kei car is the so-called X-Pack, which consolidates key electrical components, including the inverter and other control units, directly within the underfloor battery unit rather than placing them separately in the engine compartment. In the Racco, the X-Pack is integrated directly with a structural LFP Blade battery as part of a cell-to-body architecture, so the battery serves both as an energy storage system and a load-bearing structural element.

In future, the X-Pack could become the core of BYD's electric microcars for Europe and other global regions. Since its compact integration of the inverter and battery can be scaled to slightly larger A-segment platforms, BYD could use this approach to produce cost-effective, easier-to-manufacture electric cars for the European market — for example, at its new plant in Hungary.
