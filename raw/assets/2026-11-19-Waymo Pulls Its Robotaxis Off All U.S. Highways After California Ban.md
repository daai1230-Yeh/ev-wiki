# Waymo Pulls Its Robotaxis Off All U.S. Highways After California Ban

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: 2026-11-18

---

- Alphabet's Waymo has paused all robotaxi rides on American freeways.
- The suspension comes after the California DMV barred the company's driverless taxis from state highways.
- The ban follows several incidents, including cases where Waymos entered active construction zones.

Waymo has temporarily suspended all freeway rides in the United States after the California Department of Motor Vehicles barred the company's driverless taxis from operating on state highways.

The California ban went into effect last week and doesn't apply beyond state borders, but Waymo said it will pull all its vehicles from U.S. freeways to evaluate their performance. Ride-hailing on city streets is still operational.

According to the California DMV, a series of incidents where Waymo robotaxis entered active construction zones led to the suspension. "The Department determined that Waymo's driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety," the DMV said.

This is the second time this year Waymo has pulled its robotaxis from highways. In June, the company recalled 3,871 vehicles using its fifth-generation Automated Driving System over concerns they could drive at highway speeds into lanes closed for construction.

In a more recent incident, a driverless Waymo was filmed trying to pass a California Highway Patrol traffic break on I-280 last week. While human-driven cars stayed behind the patrol cars, a Waymo Jaguar I-Pace moved into the passing lane and tried to get past the blockade before being stopped by an officer.

Waymo said it is now "working with the California DMV and other state officials diligently and responsibly to resume freeway operations." It operates in 15 American cities and has offered driverless rides on California highways since last November.

Earlier this month, NHTSA opened an investigation into one of Waymo's software fixes, implemented to prevent its robotaxis from passing stopped school buses.

Waymo's fifth- and sixth-generation hardware uses lidars, cameras, and radars to navigate in pre-mapped areas. The company claims its vehicles are 95% safer than human drivers in serious-injury-or-worse crashes.
