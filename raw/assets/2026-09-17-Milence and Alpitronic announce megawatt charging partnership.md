# Milence and Alpitronic announce megawatt charging partnership

**Author**: Florian Treiss | **Date**: 2026-09-16 15:00

---

E-truck charging provider Milence wants to accelerate the expansion of megawatt charging infrastructure for e-trucks along key European transport routes together with charging station manufacturer Alpitronic. The collaboration aims to go beyond just the charging infrastructure itself.

Recently, whenever Milence has opened a new electric truck charging park, the charging stations installed there have almost always been from South Tyrolean manufacturer Alpitronic. To date, the main exception has been where its latest megawatt charging systems are concerned; in which case Milence has repeatedly opted for Spanish manufacturer Power Electronics, such as in Landvetter, Sweden, following a successful practical test in July 2024.

However, Alpitronic – from whom Milence has so far sourced its conventional Hypercharger stations with CCS connectors and up to 400 kW of power – is now also set to become a key partner in the still-nascent MCS sector. This sector is rapidly gaining importance due to its rollout among OEMs like MAN and has moved beyond the testing phase.

Specifically, the two companies have now agreed on a long-term strategic collaboration and signed a Memorandum of Understanding. Together, they aim to accelerate the deployment of a high-performance MCS infrastructure for heavy-duty electric trucks across Europe.

## Milence focuses on key freight transport corridors

The focus will remain on central freight transport and TEN-T corridors, as outlined in Milence's own roadmap, where a reliable charging network for electric trucks is to be established – no longer almost exclusively with CCS charging connectors, but increasingly with the triangular MCS connectors featuring seven contacts.

Milence CEO Anja van Niersen said: "Strong partnerships are crucial if we want to electrify long-haul freight transport more quickly. Together with Alpitronic, we are seamlessly integrating megawatt charging into daily logistics operations: we are reducing charging times, increasing operational efficiency, and getting trucks back on the road quickly and reliably. That's exactly what fleet operators need."

At the heart of the collaboration is Alpitronic's HYC1000 megawatt charging system, introduced in February 2025. With charging power of up to 1,000 kW, it can recharge the batteries of heavy electric trucks in around 30 to 45 minutes – perfectly aligned with legally mandated driver rest periods.

## Powering a growing fleet of e-trucks

Alpitronic CEO Philipp Senoner says: "With Milence, we have found a partner who shares our conviction: if we want to electrify heavy-duty transport, we need a reliable charging infrastructure specifically designed for trucks. Only then can we support a growing number of electric trucks on Europe's roads."

The collaboration will extend beyond the deployment of charging infrastructure: Milence and Alpitronic also aim to drive technological innovations and digital integration forward, ensuring that systems for remote monitoring, diagnostics, and maintenance of charging stations work seamlessly together. This is intended to increase charging availability and make operations more efficient.

## MCS demonstration at IAA Transportation

Meanwhile, at the IAA Transportation in Hanover, Milence – together with Daimler Truck, MAN Truck & Bus, and Scania – demonstrated how quickly electric trucks can be charged using MCS. The system used was Alpitronic's aforementioned HYC1000 megawatt charging system. This was supplemented by a battery energy storage system (BESS) from Pixii, the PowerBase XLP, with a power output of up to 1 MW and a storage capacity of 2 MWh. Milence is already using a similar battery storage system from Pixii at its electric truck charging park near Kassel.

MAN manager Jürgen Wagner commented on the demonstration at IAA Transportation: "MCS charging technology is the key to the cost-effectiveness and widespread adoption of battery-electric lorries in long-haul transport. With charging times shorter than the driver's rest break, the MAN eTGX can achieve daily ranges of over 1,000 kilometres within the same working hours as a diesel lorry. Since July, MAN has been fitting MCS charging technology as standard for customers who wish to adopt it at an early stage. By giving IAA visitors a practical insight into megawatt charging, the VDA and Milence are making an important contribution to the successful market roll-out of MCS and to the success of the comprehensive electrification of road freight transport."

Founded in 2022, Milence is a joint venture between Daimler Truck, Traton (MAN/Scania), and the Volvo Group (Renault Trucks and Volvo Trucks). It aims to establish a network of 70 electric truck charging parks across Europe by the end of 2027. Around 40 of these sites are already operational, including several in Germany.
