# Hyundai invests in product offensive with first EREV models

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 26.08.2026

---

Hyundai Motor Company has presented its growth strategy through 2030. The South Korean group continues to target annual sales of 5.55 million vehicles, including 60 per cent electrified passenger cars. Next year, Hyundai will introduce its first range-extender electric vehicle (EREV).

Hyundai has outlined its refined roadmap for the coming years during its CEO Investor Day. Hyundai Motor Company announced plans to launch or revise more than 100 vehicle models worldwide by 2030. However, the company did not specify how many of these will be battery-electric vehicles. It merely reiterated its overarching target of achieving a 60 per cent share of electrified vehicles in its sales mix by 2030 – up from 23 per cent in 2025. This figure is expected to include full hybrids. Importantly, the entire strategy and these figures apply only to the Hyundai and Genesis brands, not Kia.

The new outlook does not include specific global battery-electric vehicle sales targets for Hyundai and Genesis. The company last addressed this in its 2024 outlook, when it announced a goal of selling two million all-electric vehicles annually by 2030. Since then, Hyundai has explicitly expanded its focus to include full hybrids, and its first range-extender model (EREV) is set to further diversify its electrified powertrain offerings.

Hyundai's product offensive is focused on North America and Korea: of the roughly 100 new or revised models planned by 2030, 58 are allocated to North America and 49 to Korea, followed by Europe (41), India (26) and China (22). Within the next eight months alone, seven new vehicles are set to launch, including the Ioniq 3 in Europe, an A-segment electric SUV for India and the brand's first range-extender model, the Santa Fe EREV, for North America.

**Santa Fe to become the group's first EREV model**

Hyundai's EREVs are scheduled to launch in the first half of 2027 and will offer a range of more than 600 miles, equivalent to 965 kilometres. The Santa Fe EREV will be followed by similarly designed vehicles, including one for the Chinese market. The debut model will be produced at Hyundai Motor Manufacturing Alabama (HMGMA) in the US. Hyundai also announced plans to expand into segments where the brand has not yet been present, including body-on-frame vehicles such as a midsize pickup, and light commercial vehicles.

**Battery-electric vehicles for Europe**

Hyundai aims to introduce a particularly high number of all-electric models in Europe, targeting coverage of 85 percent of the market with a fully electrified portfolio. By 2030, five entirely new electric SUVs and light commercial vehicles are expected to contribute to this goal, alongside the Ioniq 3 and a new B-segment electric SUV. Hyundai's overall target is to increase sales of battery-electric cars in Europe to more than 420,000 units by 2030, up from 116,000 in 2025.

In North America, Hyundai plans to offer more than ten unspecified hybrid models by 2030, with hybrids expected to account for half of the brand's sales in the near future. In China, Hyundai is shifting its strategy towards more localised development and production, including through partnerships, starting with the Ioniq V. In India, Hyundai aims to expand its SUV range, beginning with a new electric SUV in the fourth quarter.

**Mid-nickel cells for lower costs**

Hyundai has developed its own battery cells for the Santa Fe EREV, delivering more than double the output of the high-nickel cells the company used previously, while cutting charging time by 40 per cent. For upcoming battery-electric vehicles, Hyundai plans to use NCM cells with medium nickel content, expected to reduce battery costs by around 30 per cent. Other innovations include an improved cloud-based battery management system intended to extend battery life by an average of 20 per cent by 2028, and a new safety mechanism called "Thermal Runaway Protection" (TRP), debuting in the Genesis GV90.

Genesis, Hyundai's premium brand, aims to expand to more than 40 markets and achieve annual sales of 350,000 vehicles by 2030, with more than 270 sales outlets worldwide. Alongside the GV60, Electrified GV70 and Electrified G80, Genesis will introduce the full-sized electric SUV GV90 this year, followed by the GV80 Hybrid in Q4 as the brand's first full hybrid, and an EREV SUV in early 2027 with a range of more than 640 miles.

Hyundai Motor Company also plans to increase global production capacity by 1.27 million units by 2030: 500,000 in North America, 320,000 in India, 250,000 across CKD sites and 200,000 in Korea.

José Muñoz, President and CEO of Hyundai Motor Company, commented: "Our fundamentals have never been stronger. Hyundai Motor Group is the third-largest automotive group and the second-most profitable, which gives us the ability to invest while others are pulling back. We are bringing more than 100 new models to market by 2030 with multiple powertrain options and raising our operating margin above 9 percent."
