# Mahle unveils range extender system for heavy BEV trucks

**Date**: 21.07.2026

---

Mahle is presenting a range extender system for heavy battery-electric trucks at IAA Transportation in Hanover as a world premiere. The system gives battery-electric vehicles (BEVs) a total range exceeding 800 km by combining a 110 kW high-voltage generator with onboard thermal and fluid management in a compact, self-contained unit.

The unit houses the generator, an internal combustion engine (ICE), AdBlue and fuel tanks and exhaust aftertreatment in a single box, replacing roughly one-third of the host vehicle's battery. This reduces gross vehicle weight by around 600 kg while achieving a power density exceeding 7 kW/kg.

Under real-world conditions, the configuration reduces CO₂ emissions by around 80% against a conventional diesel truck, falling to near-zero when the ICE runs on HVO100 bio-diesel. A 63 kW thermal management system maintains stable operation under heavy-duty loads.

Mahle is also presenting the Mahle Contactless Transmitter (MCT) electric motor, a permanent-magnet-free drive for heavy commercial vehicle axles. The MCT eliminates rare earth requirements, saves up to 3 kg per vehicle, and delivers 370 kW peak at approximately 95% VECTO efficiency.

Mahle sells approximately 8 million electric drive systems annually and is present in every battery-electric and fuel cell truck in Europe. Commercial vehicles represent around 20% of its business.

The range extender fits existing BEV platforms without modification. Replacing roughly one-third of battery capacity with a compact generator unit that slots into standard battery installation space removes the need for OEMs to develop new vehicle architectures, lowering the adoption barrier significantly compared with purpose-built range extender trucks.

Decoupling fleet electrification from infrastructure readiness is the commercial argument. Mahle's framing — that operators with range extenders can continue when charging infrastructure is congested or unavailable — addresses the single most cited barrier to heavy truck electrification without waiting for network investment that has repeatedly slipped behind schedule.

A rare-earth-free traction motor for heavy commercial vehicles is also a supply chain risk reduction measure. Eliminating permanent magnets removes dependence on materials whose supply is concentrated in China and subject to export restrictions.
