# The Mercedes-AMG GT 53 EV Trades Power For Over 500 Miles Of Range

**Author**: Andrei Nedelea | **Date**: 06.08.2026

---

Mercedes-AMG has revealed a cheaper, entry-level GT 4-Door EV with more range than the tri-motor models. The GT 53 drops one motor and more than half the flagship's output but keeps the advanced battery and ultra-fast charging.

Two permanently excited synchronous motors (one per axle) produce a combined 536 hp and 590 lb-ft (800 Nm), reaching 60 mph in 3.4 seconds (one-foot rollout) or 3.8 seconds from a standstill, with an electronically limited top speed of 143 mph (230 km/h). That's far less extreme than the GT 63's three axial-flux motors delivering 1,153 hp and 1,475 lb-ft, hitting 62 mph in as little as 2.1 seconds.

The 53 sits below the GT 55 (three motors dialed to 805 hp/1,328 lb-ft; €154,700 in Germany vs. €196,350 for the 63). The dual-motor 53 introduces a simpler powertrain and should be cheaper still, positioning it closer to the entry AWD Porsche Taycan (€107,300 in Germany).

The front motor acts mainly as a booster, engaging only when extra traction or performance is needed; a mechanical disconnect decouples it otherwise so the car runs as a rear-wheel-drive EV for efficiency. The rear motor uses a two-speed transmission (short first gear for acceleration/urban driving, taller second gear for highway efficiency).

Result: an expected WLTP range up to 503 miles (809 km) from the same 106 kWh battery used across the lineup (GT 63 is rated up to 432 miles/696 km WLTP). Neither model has an EPA rating yet, and Mercedes' figures are preliminary.

Despite lower output, the 53 keeps the 600 kW peak charging rating — thanks to the 800-volt architecture, 10–80% in 11 minutes, adding over 70 kWh. Chassis differs from the 63: the 53 uses AMG Ride Control air suspension with adaptive dampers rather than the 63's AMG Active Ride Control with hydraulically linked dampers and active roll stabilization; the 63 also gets rear-wheel steering and additional track-focused modes (not yet confirmed for the 53). Both use fully variable AMG Performance 4Matic+ AWD and torque vectoring, but only the 55 and 63 can independently control the two rear motors for finer cornering control.

The 53 loses the 63's Race mode, nine-stage traction control, and dedicated Drift/Drag Race/Track Race functions, retaining six drive programs and adjustable accelerator response. It also gets its own synthetic engine sound (AMGForce Sport+ mode recreates the previous AMG E53's turbocharged straight-six, versus the 63's V8 simulation), with paddle-controlled simulated gear changes.

US rollout is staged: the 805-hp GT 55 launches first later this year, with the GT 63 and the newly revealed GT 53 following in early 2027 — currently only the GT 55 appears on Mercedes' US configurator.
