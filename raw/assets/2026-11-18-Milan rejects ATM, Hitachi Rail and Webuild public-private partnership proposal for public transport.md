# Milan rejects ATM, Hitachi Rail and Webuild public-private partnership proposal for public transport

**Author**: Editorial Staff | **Date**: 2026-11-17

---

Milan's public transport authority has rejected the public-private partnership proposal submitted by ATM together with Hitachi Rail and Webuild ahead of the future tender for the management of the city's public transport services.

The news was reported by Italian newspaper Corriere della Sera in its Milan edition of 17 November 2026. According to the newspaper, the technical committee responsible for assessing the proposal found the project unacceptable from a technical, economic and financial standpoint. The proposal was also considered to lack the elements required to establish a public interest.

The decision concerns the project referred to as the "Mobility Project" and comes as the future framework for awarding Milan's public transport services, covering both surface transport and the metro network, remains under definition.

Looking back at the developments preceding the decision, in March 2026 Italian trade media and our sister platform Autobusweb reported on the project financing proposal submitted by ATM together with Hitachi Rail and Webuild to the Municipality of Milan and the local public transport authority. The project covered the future management of public transport services and included possible extensions to the metro network.

The proposal was part of the process leading to a new tender for Milan's public transport services, following the expiry of ATM's concession in 2021 and its subsequent extensions. Meanwhile, other operators had also been linked to the upcoming tender, including French public transport group RATP, although no formal declaration of interest had been issued.

## Milan public transport tender: authority rejects ATM-Hitachi-Webuild proposal

ATM Milano, whose Board of Directors was renewed in April 2026 with Oliviero Baccelli appointed chairman and Alberto Zorzan confirmed as CEO, operates a fleet of approximately 1,200 buses in Milan, more than 600 of which are already electric, serving 40 routes. In October 2026, the operator awarded Karsan a contract for 110 electric buses, worth over €76 million, with the first vehicle expected to enter service by the end of 2027. ATM is also involved in public transport tenders outside Milan: in June 2026, it was among the operators qualified for Piedmont's regional bus tender, covering approximately 56 million vehicle-kilometres annually and worth €112.4 million per year, alongside competitors including ALSA and RATP.

According to Corriere della Sera, the proposal submitted by ATM, Hitachi Rail and Webuild at the end of 2025 was subsequently examined by the public transport authority responsible for the Milan, Lodi, Monza and Brianza area.

The project envisaged a cooperation model between public and private entities, structured as a public-private partnership for the future management of public transport services.

On 18 October, the authority requested clarifications and additional documentation, particularly concerning metro line M4, for which the proposal envisaged changes to the existing management structure.

The negative assessment was issued by a committee comprising experts from the Municipality of Milan and the public transport authority. According to the documentation cited by Corriere della Sera, the project was deemed unacceptable from a technical, economic and financial standpoint.

The authority has therefore ruled out proceeding with the proposal in its current form.

## Milan M4 metro concession and financial issues behind the rejection

One of the main issues identified during the assessment concerns metro line M4, currently operated under a concession and management contract running until 2048.

According to Corriere della Sera, the project submitted by ATM and its partners envisaged changes to the line's operating arrangements, potentially involving two operators in metro management and train operations.

The committee also identified critical issues concerning the residual value of the concession, reported by the newspaper at €840 million. This relates to the so-called terminal value, representing the residual value associated with the concession.

Further objections concerned the economic and financial structure of the proposed partnership. According to the newspaper, the project did not provide a sufficiently detailed financial framework, particularly regarding the sustainability of its individual components.

The proposal also did not envisage increases in public transport ticket prices. As reported by Corriere della Sera, the financial arrangements were among the elements examined by the committee in its negative assessment.

The rejection concerns the specific public-private partnership proposal submitted by ATM, Hitachi Rail and Webuild. No new timetable for the publication of Milan's public transport tender or final decision on its possible division into separate lots has been indicated in the documentation reported by the newspaper.
