# Waymo's New Robotaxi Minivans Are Now Taking Paying Passengers

**Author**: Iulian Dnistran | **Date**: Aug 20, 2026, 8:54am ET

---

The Chinese-made Zeekr vans are fitted with Waymo's sixth-generation hardware and software suite.

- Waymo is now offering paid rides in its new Ojai autonomous vans.
- The Zeekr-made electric robotaxis are available for all riders in three U.S. cities.
- Initially, riders may be matched with an Ojai when they order a Waymo.

Waymo, the biggest robotaxi company in the United States, has started offering paid rides in its new Ojai electric minivans in San Francisco, Los Angeles, and Phoenix. The rollout comes after a limited trial where some riders could hail an Ojai for free.

The larger vehicles feature sliding doors and can seat up to four passengers, making them better suited for families compared to the aging Jaguar I-Pace EVs that have become a staple of San Francisco's roads.

For now, riders who order a Waymo in these three cities may be matched with an Ojai or an I-Pace. Further down the road, when more minivans are available, the Alphabet-owned company will offer customers a choice between the two vehicles.

Built in China by Zeekr, the Ojai minivans feature Waymo's sixth-generation hardware and software suite. Despite facing hiked import tariffs, they are much cheaper to build and operate than the Jaguar I-Paces Waymo has used for years. The vehicles are shipped to the United States without any connected car technologies. Waymo's factory in Arizona then installs the proprietary self-driving system, which includes 13 cameras, four lidars, and six radars. The software was also redesigned to better handle harsh weather conditions including snow and fog.

There are about 300 Ojai robotaxis up and running right now, according to a company spokesperson quoted by TechCrunch. The Ojai will roll out to select riders in Denver, Las Vegas, and San Diego later this year, with thousands of autonomous minivans expected to land in the U.S. by the end of 2026.

The robotaxi market is rapidly heating up in the U.S. and around the world. Stateside, Waymo is still the undisputed leader, though rivals including Tesla and Amazon's Zoox are trying to outrank the Alphabet-owned entity.
