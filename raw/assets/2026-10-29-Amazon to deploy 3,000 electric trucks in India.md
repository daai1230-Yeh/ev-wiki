# Amazon to deploy 3,000 electric trucks in India

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 2026-10-28

---

American e-commerce giant Amazon has announced plans to deploy 3,000 electric trucks in India. This will be the largest electric-truck deployment from an e-commerce company in the country.

Tata Motors, India's largest commercial vehicle manufacturer, will be supplying Amazon with a total of 3,000 electric trucks. The first 20 units were already delivered on Tuesday. H.D. Kumaraswamy, India's Minister of Heavy Industries and Steel, flagged off this fleet.

Under the agreement, Amazon's carrier partners will buy the 3,000 electric trucks from Tata Motors and operate them on its behalf. This asset-light strategy should help the company electrify its transportation fleet without making a major investment, while also limiting its risk exposure.

Amazon says it will deploy the new electric trucks in Delhi during this year's festive season, when freight volumes typically peak. The company is adopting EVs beyond last-mile mobility, with plans to use Tata Motors' light- and medium-duty electric trucks to transport shipments between its facilities.

In the next phase, Amazon will deploy electric trucks on National Highway 48, which runs from Delhi to Chennai. With a length of approximately 2,800 kilometres, this is India's longest freight corridor, traversing seven states and connecting six of the country's largest e-commerce regions. The company is setting up charging infrastructure through a partnership with Yahhvi, a Delhi-based EV charging company for commercial vehicles.

The first batch consists entirely of Ultra E.7 light-duty electric trucks: 7.5 tonne GVW, 14-foot load deck, 3.1 tonne payload, 240 kW permanent magnet synchronous motor and a 96 kWh LFP battery pack, for a range of up to 130 kilometres.

Amazon has more than 15,000 EVs operating across over 500 cities in India. Abhinav Singh, Amazon's vice president of operations for APAC, the Middle East, Africa, and Turkey, said: "We are now setting a bold vision to scale electric trucking, addressing one of the toughest challenges in decarbonising transportation."
