# China further reduces tax incentives for EVs

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 04.09.2026

---

Since September 1, China has applied a 2% consumption tax on lithium-ion batteries (rising to 4% in September 2027), ending an exemption in place since 2015; sodium-ion, solid-state batteries, and fuel cells stay exempt through 2028, signaling a policy tilt toward newer chemistries. CarNewsChina estimates the tax adds roughly $62 (rising to $125 at 4%) to a 60-kWh EV battery, based on Chinese LFP cell pricing.

This is part of a broader wind-down of NEV incentives: since early 2026 the purchase-tax break for EVs and PHEVs dropped from a full exemption to a 50% reduction (capped at ¥15,000/vehicle), and vehicle/vessel tax benefits for electric and hybrid commercial vehicles end January 1, 2027 (passenger BEVs keep that exemption). The state continues supporting charging/battery-swap infrastructure, and remaining tax breaks still apply to foreign automakers' China-built EVs.
