# Japan: Toyota launches all-new Sora hydrogen bus co-developed with Isuzu

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 2026-10-06

---

Toyota has introduced the second generation of its hydrogen fuel-cell electric bus Sora in Japan. The company jointly developed the all-new model with Isuzu.

The second-gen Sora sits on Isuzu's flat-floor battery-electric route bus platform and features a Toyota-developed fuel cell stack. The company still mounts the fuel cell stack on the roof but has relocated its position from the rear section to the front, and it moved the fuel tanks slightly rearward to make room for the same.

While the first-gen Sora features ten 70 MPa hydrogen tanks, the second-gen model uses three new, larger-capacity tanks that take about ten minutes to refill, and they are designed to cut that time by about half once faster-filling hydrogen stations become available. Instead of carrying over the previous staggered layout with diagonally positioned tanks, Toyota has arranged the new tanks transversely in a single row. The fuel capacity has gone down by 129 litres, or 21.5 per cent, to 471 litres. Yet, the company has managed to increase the range by about 100 kilometres to over 300 kilometres.

Toyota attributes the significant gain in driving range to more efficient use of the fuel cell stack and a large-capacity battery. The company has replaced the nickel-metal hydride battery with a lithium-ion unit but has not disclosed its energy storage capacity. Two AC induction motors, each producing 110 kW of power and 485 Nm of torque, drive the wheels. Compared with the old model's AC synchronous motors, they each generate 3 kW less power but 150 Nm more torque.

The second-gen Sora measures 10.5 metres in length, 2.5 metres in width, and 3.4 metres in height. It has a fully flat cabin floor that eliminates the old model's internal steps, improving accessibility, particularly for passengers who have mobility challenges or are travelling with strollers.

Toyota has designed the all-new Sora in four variants, two for urban use and two for suburban operations. For one of the urban variants, the company states a maximum capacity of 69 passengers, including 19 seated and 50 standing. By comparison, the old model accommodates up to 78 passengers, comprising 22 seated and 56 standing people.

For enhanced safety, Toyota equips the second-gen Sora with EDSS (Emergency Driver Stop System) that works in conjunction with DSM (Driver Status Monitor). If the system detects an issue with the driver, the vehicle decelerates, stops, and applies the parking brake automatically. The latest model also features a front blind spot monitor that can look for pedestrians and cyclists in front of the bus and notifies the driver.

Toyota has not disclosed pricing or detailed commercial plans for the all-new Sora in Japan. The company has not shared any export plans either. In Europe, however, Toyota has so far relied on its Portuguese partner Caetano for hydrogen buses. Hydrogen buses co-branded by both companies are in service in the Austrian capital, Vienna, for example.
