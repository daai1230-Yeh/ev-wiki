# Megawatt charging network: Tesla to launch in Europe with 21 sites

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-09-16 16:30

---

Tesla will roll out its truck charging network next year, starting with Germany, France, Benelux, the UK, and Sweden. According to Dan Priestley - head of Tesla's truck programme - the plan is to start with 21 public locations featuring over 100 megawatt charging points.

In a keynote at IAA Transportation, Dan Priestley provided further details on Tesla's plans to build its own truck charging network in Europe. In parallel with the planned launch of the Tesla Semi next year, a network of charging sites will be established, primarily for Tesla's own truck but also open to trucks from other brands. Priestley explained that the system would follow the example of the firm's existing charging network for passenger cars in this regard.

According to a graphic presented by Priestley, Tesla is planning an initial phase start with 21 sites featuring over 100 MCS charging points, averaging roughly five charging points per location. It is clear that the UK will have two sites and Sweden three. The majority of the remaining 16 sites will be distributed across Germany and France, with a few in the Benelux region.

## Up to 1.2 MW of power

Interestingly, while the European Semi can draw a maximum of 800 kW at MCS stations according to Tesla's specifications, Tesla's Megachargers will offer up to 1.2 MW – mirroring North America. They are therefore already designed for higher charging power, which could indicate that Tesla plans to later export its longer-range Semi version to Europe. After all, this version requires more power to quickly recharge its significantly larger battery.

For context: in the US, Tesla offers its electric truck in Standard Range and Long Range variants. As electrive learned at the trade fair in Hanover, the former features two battery packs, while the latter has three. Each pack has an energy content of 274 kWh, giving the Standard Range truck a battery capacity of 548 kWh, while the range-optimised variant has 822 kWh. The batteries for the Semi come from Tesla's own production and are based on 4680 round cells with NCMA chemistry.

## Tesla Semi to be "as maneuverable as a two-axle truck"

In his speech in Hanover, Priestley confirmed that all Semis for the domestic and global markets are rolling off the production line at Tesla in Nevada, and the Semi now announced for Europe largely corresponds to the North American Standard Range version.

The configuration of the powertrain was previously unclear, but the trade fair debut has shed some light: the Semi is a 6×4 tractor unit with three electric motors. As electrive learned, the main drive unit is located on the rear axle and provides continuous power. The middle axle houses the other two units. These can be disengaged and only activate when high power and torque are required. This means that in many situations – such as motorway driving – the main motor alone suffices. Priestley describes it as a sophisticated system that serves as the foundation for high efficiency. Tesla's lead truck developer also emphasised that "despite being a three-axle truck, [the Semi] has the manoeuvrability of a two-axle truck despite its three axles" with a tight turning circle.

## Customers offered a "very competitive price"

Priestley argued that the Tesla Semi, in the form it will be offered in from 2027, offers an exceptionally good package for a range of tasks in Europe and that it will also boast a "very competitive price". Notably, Tesla does some things differently from local truck manufacturers: in the Semi, the driver sits in a central seat, and the cab does not include a bed (only an additional seat offset to the right rear). Additionally, Tesla installs only one MCS port; an adapter will be available for CCS charging.

Fundamentally, Tesla is designed around the MCS 3.2 charging standard. According to an overview of the charging technology available on the Belgian Tesla website, the 'Megachargers' are dispensers connected to a remote cabinet that houses all the charging technology. This cabinet measures 1,175 x 1,390 x 1,925 mm, weighs 1.1 tonnes, and can deliver 1.2 MW split across two satellites. These satellites can be located up to 60 metres away. In contrast, the Basechargers that Tesla will also offer in future for slower overnight charging are an all-in-one solution, with the charging technology integrated directly into the column.

| | Tesla Megacharger | Tesla Basecharger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Max. output power | 1.2 MW | 120 kW DC, 125 kW AC |
| Amperage | 1,500 A | 150 A |
| Voltage range | Up to 1,000 V | 180–1,000 V |
| Cable length | 3 m | 6 m |
| Dimensions | 334 x 891 x 1,946 mm | 340 x 1,200 x 2,000 mm |
| Weight | 100 kg | 100 kg |
| Supported open protocols | ISO 15118-2, OCPI-compatible | ISO 15118-2, OCPI-compatible |
| Payment terminal | Optional | Optional |
| Charging standard | MCS 3.2 | MCS 3.2 |

In addition to building an "expanding" public charging network, Tesla also plans to offer both charging solutions to companies for private use from the outset – just as it now does with passenger car chargers. According to Priestley, the Basecharger will cost €17,000, while the Megacharger (two satellites and one power cabinet) will be priced at €163,000 in the configurator. Delivery is scheduled to begin in "mid-2027".
