# Exicom to sell DC fast charger manufacturer Tritium

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 24.11.2026 - 16:00

---

Just under two years after acquiring the insolvent Australian fast charger manufacturer Tritium, Indian charging infrastructure provider Exicom is looking to divest the company once again. A non-binding agreement values the company at US$85 million.

Exicom has justified the decision by citing Tritium’s ongoing high capital requirements and the growth opportunities in its Indian home market, as well as other export markets. The identity of the potential buyer has not yet been disclosed, and no binding purchase agreement has been signed.

Exicom acquired Tritium out of insolvency in August 2024. As industry expert Syed Haseeb Hassan reported in his eTechvolution newsletter, citing earlier statements from Exicom management, the purchase price at the time was US$37 million.

Under Exicom’s leadership, Tritium has brought new products to market maturity and expanded into additional markets. The DC fast charger manufacturer currently has the highest order backlog since the acquisition. However, the business remains capital-intensive. Exicom itself recorded consolidated revenue of 3.88 billion Indian rupees in the second quarter of the 2026/27 financial year – a year-on-year increase of around 38 per cent. Net losses decreased from 688 million to 578 million rupees.

Even after the planned sale, Exicom intends to benefit from the Australian manufacturer’s technology. Tritium’s liquid-cooled power modules are now being produced at Exicom’s plant in Hyderabad, India. Additionally, Exicom retains an indefinite licence for the TRI-FLEX technology.

“We remain convinced of Tritium’s potential to be a leading power electronics solutions provider to EV charging, Industrial and Data Centre segments,” explained Anant Nahata, CEO and Managing Director of Exicom. The Indian manufacturer plans to focus its financial resources more heavily on expanding its own charging infrastructure business in the future.

Following its acquisition by Exicom, Tritium fundamentally revised its strategy. As Chief Sales Officer Aaron Jones explained in a July 2025 interview with electrive, the company aimed to focus more on charging electric commercial vehicles and fleets while expanding its service offerings. A key role was played by the modular fast-charging system TRI-FLEX, which, according to Jones, can distribute a total power of up to 1.68 megawatts across up to 64 charging points.

Jones was also optimistic about the financial outlook at the time: “Right now, Tritium’s in a great spot: we don’t need to raise capital or worry about debt, so financially we’re solid.” Exicom was committed for the long term and saw Tritium as part of its global growth. Around 16 months later, the Indian parent company now cites the high capital requirements as the reason for the planned separation.

At the end of 2025, Tritium reported more than 21,000 of its DC fast chargers deployed worldwide in over 50 countries. The company is increasingly positioning itself as a provider of power electronics for data centres and energy infrastructure. In December 2025, Tritium introduced GRID-FLEX, a solution based on an 800-volt DC architecture that uses the same liquid-cooled power module technology as the company’s fast chargers.

According to eTechvolution, the first GRID-FLEX system was commissioned at a major data centre operator in June 2026. Another potential order worth around $20 million is reportedly in an advanced stage. The future role of the fast-charging business under a potential new owner remains unclear.
