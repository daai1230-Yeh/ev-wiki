# Chery Group starts assembling Lepas EVs in Thailand

**Author**: Sagar Parikh | **Date**: 24.11.2026 - 17:30

---

Chinese conglomerate Chery Group has started assembling electric cars of its newest brand in Thailand, Lepas, beginning with the L6 EV. The company has an ICE L6 and a L6 PHEV also in its global line-up, but in Thailand, it produces and sells only the L6 EV.

Chery Group imports the Lepas L6 EV’s completely knocked-down (CKD) kits from China and assembles them at its new factory in Nikhom Phatthana, Rayong, which it inaugurated in April. Omoda & Jaecoo Manufacturing (Thailand), a joint venture between Chery Group and King Gen Auto, owns the plant. The latter, a subsidiary of Thailand’s King Gen, holds a 43.70 per cent stake in the venture.

The Chinese automaker has not stated the projected production volume of the Lepas L6 EV in Thailand, but it did say earlier that the production capacity of the Rayong plant, which covers an area of 104 rai (16.64 hectares or 166,400 square metres), will increase to 80,000 units by 2030 in a phased manner. It also assembles the Chery V23, Omoda C5 EV, Jaecoo 5 EV, and Jaecoo 6 EV at this site.

Chery Group also plans to assemble the Lepas L4 EV, Chery QQ, Omoda C4 EV, Omoda C2 EV, and Jaecoo 3 EV at the Rayong plant in the future. The company will utilise this facility to localise six plug-in hybrid models as well, including the Lepas L8.

Thailand recently approved an increase in the excise tax on pure electric vehicles and range-extended electric vehicles imported as completely built-up (CBU) units from 10 per cent and 15 per cent, respectively, to a uniform 35 per cent from January 2027. With the hike approaching, companies appear to be pushing their localisation plans.

Leapmotor officials recently met Thailand’s Board of Investment (BoI) to discuss plans for establishing a local production facility. MGC-Asia, the official distributor of Xpeng, has said that the two companies are evaluating setting up a local EV manufacturing plant as part of their long-term plans. Dongfeng Motor-owned Voyah and its local distributor, AAS Group, are also exploring local assembly, The Nation reported.

Commenting on the start of local assembly of the Lepas L6 EV, Jittisak Wongsiri, Director of Sales and Distributor Network Development at Lepas Thailand, said: “We assess the impact of the tax structure alongside market demand to ensure that product decisions and offerings to customers are appropriate. At the same time, we remain committed to our manufacturing plans in Thailand, which are a crucial foundation for our long-term business operations.”
