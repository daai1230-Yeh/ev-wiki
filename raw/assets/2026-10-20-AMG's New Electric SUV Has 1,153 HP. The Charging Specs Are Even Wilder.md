# AMG's New Electric SUV Has 1,153 HP. The Charging Specs Are Even Wilder

**Author**: Andrei Nedelea | **Date**: 2026-10-17

---

*How does 10-80% in 12 minutes sound?*

- The AMG GT SUV packs up to 1,153 hp, with three versions arriving in the U.S. in summer 2027.
- GT 55 and GT 63 models promise 10–80% charging in 12 minutes, with peak power reaching 710 kW.
- A larger battery and active suspension that controls pitch, not just roll, set the SUV apart from the GT 4-Door.

Mercedes unveiled the high-riding sibling of the AMG GT 4-Door EV on Saturday, simply called the AMG GT SUV. Mercedes claims it can add 48 kWh of energy in five minutes.

Like the 4-Door, the GT SUV is a bespoke AMG model built on the AMG.EA platform, but with a bigger battery, higher peak charging power, and uprated suspension. Three versions will reach U.S. dealerships next summer: the GT53, GT55, and GT63. All have variable all-wheel drive and can mechanically disconnect their front motors to improve efficiency. The GT 53 gets a two-speed rear transmission, while the three-motor versions control each rear wheel independently for torque vectoring.

Inside, a 14-inch central touchscreen pairs with a 10.2-inch instrument display, plus an optional second 14-inch passenger screen. The panoramic glass roof can switch between transparent and opaque.

The four-door's claimed 11-minute 10–80% time remains shorter; the SUV needs 12 minutes because it has more battery to fill. On a 400-kW charger, Mercedes quotes 16 minutes. The GT 53 retains the four-door's smaller battery and 600-kW peak, promising 10–80% in 11 minutes, or 14 minutes at 400 kW. All versions support 11-kW AC charging, NACS in America, and automatic switching to 400 volts. Its claimed WLTP range is 371–415 miles.

The GT 53 uses two synchronous motors producing 536 peak hp and 590 lb-ft (0–60 mph in 4.1 s, top speed 143 mph). The GT 55 uses three axial-flux motors delivering 805 peak hp and 1,372 lb-ft (0–60 mph in 3.0 s). The flagship GT 63 shares the GT 55's battery, charging and range, but raises peak power to 1,153 hp and torque to 1,475 lb-ft, with 0–60 mph in 2.6 s and a standard 168-mph top speed.

The GT 55 and GT 63 get 128 kWh of usable capacity versus the four-door's 106 kWh. Peak charging rises from 600 to 710 kW, adding 83 kWh in ten minutes at an average of about 498 kW. Both target a WLTP range of 385–435 miles (620–700 km).

The new Active Ride Control+ suspension adds active pitch stabilization to roll control. It is standard on the GT 63 and optional on the GT 55, as are carbon-ceramic brakes. All variants get standard six-degree rear steering. The SUV also keeps simulated shifts, combustion engine soundtracks and seat vibrations; the two top versions mimic a V-8, the 53 a six-cylinder.

U.S. pricing has not been announced. For reference, the AMG GT 4-Door EV starts at €115,430 for the GT 53 in Germany and rises to €196,350 for the GT 63.
