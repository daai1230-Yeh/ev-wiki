# NextGO Epi secures funding for gallium oxide semiconductor development

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-07-16

---

Berlin-based deeptech company NextGO Epi has secured €2 million in pre-seed funding. The company, spun out of the Leibniz Institute for Crystal Growth (IKZ) in 2025, develops gallium oxide epitaxial wafers for next-generation power semiconductors. The round was led by Vireo Ventures, with participation from Ultratech Capital Partners, IBB Ventures and business angel Boris Habets. The capital will accelerate product development, expand the team and strengthen its European and international presence.

NextGO Epi says it is currently the only European manufacturer producing industrial-grade gallium oxide epitaxy wafers with diameters of up to four inches. These wafers form the electrically active layer of next-generation power semiconductors, supporting future applications in electric vehicles, charging infrastructure, renewable energy systems and data centres.

Gallium oxide is an ultra-wide-bandgap semiconductor that, compared with the silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride (GaN) semiconductors widely used today, offers potential for higher voltage resistance and lower energy losses. The company says gallium-oxide power semiconductors could significantly reduce EV fast-charging times while lowering component production costs, though these advantages still need to be proven in mass-produced components and real-world applications. NextGO Epi already supplies customers in Europe, North America and Asia; its founders have over ten years of research experience and hold two international patents, and it is supported by Jochen Linck, former COO of Aixtron.

The funding coincides with European efforts to expand semiconductor production, including the EU Commission's recently introduced Chips Act 2.0. While established manufacturers like Infineon expand silicon and SiC production, and Bosch expands SiC in Roseville, California with US CHIPS Act support, NextGO Epi is working on material alternatives — with gallium oxide potentially joining the spectrum if its advantages are confirmed industrially.
