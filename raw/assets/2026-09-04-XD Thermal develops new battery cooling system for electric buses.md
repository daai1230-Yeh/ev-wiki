# XD Thermal develops new battery cooling system for electric buses

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-09-03

---

Chinese thermal management specialist XD Thermal has developed a new battery cooling system for an unspecified European electric bus platform. The platform uses battery modules with a capacity of 35.3 kWh, and XD Thermal contributes liquid-cooled battery housings with stamped cover plates that dissipate heat from both sides of the modules.

Unlike solutions with separate cooling plates, parts of the thermal management system are directly integrated into the housing structure, reducing the number of cooling components by around 25 percent and eliminating several assembly steps. The multiple thermal interfaces also ensure more uniform heat distribution, which should help lithium-ion battery lifespan — though XD Thermal has not published specific figures on cooling performance, temperature distribution, or lifespan, and independent verification is not yet available.

Integrating cooling into the housing raised sealing challenges: with multiple interfaces, joints and sealing paths, XD Thermal developed its own testing devices and used air and helium leak tests to catch minor leaks. Coating the varied surfaces with different insulating and thermal barrier materials required new suspension and masking processes for complex geometries.

"Once cooling becomes part of the housing, the design boundary changes," said Xu, Technical Director at XD Thermal — the focus shifts from optimizing a single cooling plate to the thermal performance of the whole structure while staying suitable for series production.

The first prototype was delivered in around six weeks, reaching series-production readiness after about two months, with thermal management, design, process, quality and production teams working in parallel. The architecture is said to be adaptable to different vehicle platforms, battery dimensions and capacities without redeveloping the basic cooling concept. XD Thermal has built additional production capacity for large-format battery cooling components since early 2025, now also serving further electric bus and heavy-duty projects.

The article notes that integrated battery/thermal management is gaining importance across electric commercial vehicles as space and weight efficiency become more critical — Mitsubishi Heavy Industries recently unveiled a similar flexible cooling system for e-trucks.
