# ASTAG Charge launches with 100 companies across 60 locations

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-11-16

---

The Swiss Commercial Vehicle Association has launched ASTAG Charge, a network that allows members to share their truck charging infrastructure. A total of 100 companies are now participating, providing around 200 charging points for a fleet of 585 electric trucks.

Earlier this year, two initiatives – Utopia and ASTAG Charge – emerged in Switzerland to promote shared charging for electric trucks. Both were built on the Depotcharge platform developed by Munich-based Evolve Energy. The initiatives have now merged and gone live under the unified ASTAG Charge brand.

According to the Swiss Commercial Vehicle Association ASTAG, the initiative initially involved seven companies, but the number of participants quickly grew. At its launch, the network comprised 100 companies with 585 electric trucks and around 200 charging points across 60 locations. Franco Digirolamo, a member of ASTAG's executive board, sees the rapid increase in participation as a sign that the solution has struck a chord with the industry. He comments: "Instead of everyone building their own charging network, we are connecting the existing infrastructure of our members."

The platform, he explains, was designed by members for members: simple, efficient and transparent. "This way, ASTAG Charge leverages one of the industry's great strengths: its dense network of companies and operational sites. Every additional location creates new charging opportunities, and every additional fleet increases network utilisation."

Participation is exclusively open to association members. According to Digirolamo, the registration process is deliberately designed to be straightforward. "Companies register on astag-charge.ch. Fleet operators can then use the available charging options. Those who wish to provide their own charging infrastructure can have their location technically connected to the platform."

Joscha Specks of technology partner Depotcharge says this connection can often be completed within a few working days and, in some cases, remotely. Participants do not pay setup or basic fees, according to the organisers. Costs are incurred only when charging actually takes place. Charging sessions and billing are handled digitally via the platform.

Several months have passed since the depot charging network was launched in Switzerland. The Utopia project initially saw Swiss energy company Repower and logistics specialists DPD and G. Leclerc Transport begin an open rollout of Depotcharge, aiming to establish charging facilities at partner companies. In parallel, the Swiss Commercial Vehicle Association ASTAG launched a closed network through which its members could share their charging infrastructure. The technical merger of the two initiatives is scheduled for 1 December.

In both cases, the underlying technology is the Depotcharge platform from Evolve Energy GmbH. "Depotcharge connects site operators with available charging capacity to fleet operators who need cost-effective charging away from their own depots," the Munich-based company explains. The sharing platform went live in Switzerland in early May. Registration has also been available in Germany for some time.

The Swiss launch is part of the "Electromobility 2030" roadmap of the Swiss Federal Office of Energy. The background is the rapid electrification of truck fleets in Switzerland, which is progressing faster than in the rest of Europe. According to the latest ACEA statistics, the Alpine country recorded 605 new registrations of electric trucks weighing more than 16 tonnes in the first nine months of the year, up 62 per cent year on year. Electric models accounted for 24 per cent of registrations in this weight class, putting Switzerland at the top of the European market.

However, "the newly emerging charging infrastructure often remains underutilised today. At the same time, fleet operators charge at public charging stations at prices that are usually significantly higher than at depots," Evolve Energy points out. The sharing platform aims to bridge this gap.

The principle is straightforward: "Depotcharge enables companies in the heavy-duty transport and logistics sector to share their charging points. Underutilised charging slots can be made available to other registered fleet operators. These operators enter usage agreements with site operators, book the desired time windows, and gain access to the charging points. Access times and prices are set individually by the site operators," the organisers explain. Depotcharge handles access management, reservations and automated billing.

With 100 participants, the network has attracted considerable interest from the outset. For comparison, ASTAG states on its website that it represents 3,700 members and partner organisations across Switzerland, operating a total of 30,000 commercial vehicles in goods and passenger transport. According to ASTAG, the Depotcharge-based sharing system offers site operators the potential to generate "50% additional revenue through monetising unused charging capacity". For fleet operators, the association promises "50% cost savings compared to public charging infrastructure".
