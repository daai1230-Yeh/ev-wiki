# Why America Built Fewer EV Fast-Charging Stations In Q2

**Author**: Suvrat Kothari | **Date**: Jul 14, 2026

---

The buildout of America's public fast-charging stations slowed in the second quarter as charging networks shifted their focus toward profitability and improving reliability for EV drivers, charging data platform Paren said.

Charging companies added 4,382 new ports across 806 new stations in Q2, down from the 4,865 ports and 891 stations added during the same period last year — a 10% year-over-year decline. The numbers also fall well short of the fourth quarter's record, when operators added 5,966 new ports and 937 new stations.

Loren McDonald, chief analyst at charging data analytics firm Chargeonomics, attributed the slowdown to operators prioritizing profits and the overall quality of the charging experience over sheer expansion. Many new stations now come with amenities like restrooms, cafes, and WiFi.

Quarter-over-quarter growth remained strong, however: just 3,521 new ports went in during Q1, meaning Q2 installs rose 24% quarter-over-quarter.

Tesla continued to lead the buildout of public fast-charging infrastructure, but its all-time market share dropped below 50% for the first time as rival networks picked up the pace. The company accounted for just 27% of new deployments in Q2, with 1,185 new ports. Walmart and ChargePoint followed with 368 and 333 new ports, respectively, while Red E and Electrify America rounded out the top five with 315 and 202.

Average utilization rates held steady at 15.8%, suggesting new capacity is being absorbed by drivers at roughly the same rate stations are being built.

Growth was heavily regional, with 40% of new ports landing in just five states — led by California with 120 new stations, followed by Texas, Florida, Illinois, and New York. North Dakota added zero new public fast-charging ports in Q2, while Montana, Wyoming, and South Dakota each added just one new station.
