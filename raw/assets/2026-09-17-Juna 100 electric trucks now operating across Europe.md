# Juna: 100 electric trucks now operating across Europe

**Author**: Jasmin Apitzsch-Krause | **Date**: 2026-09-16 18:30

---

The Scania-Sennder joint venture Juna now has around 110 electric trucks on the road in four European countries. By the end of 2026, the size of the fleet is expected to increase to around 150 vehicles. Juna relies on a pay-per-use model designed to make it easier for transport companies to transition to electromobility.

Just over two years after launching its first electric trucks in 2024, Juna has surpassed the milestone of 100 vehicles. The fleet is currently operating in Germany, Italy, Poland and the Netherlands. By the end of the year, the joint venture between Scania and Sennder aims to expand its fleet to around 150 electric trucks. Juna not only brokers the vehicles but also analyses suitable routes in collaboration with shippers and freight forwarders, supporting transport companies in their deployment. Payment is usage-based, meaning transport operators do not need to purchase or finance the electric trucks themselves.

This approach primarily addresses the high investment costs of electrification. According to the company, electric trucks currently cost two to three times as much as comparable diesel models. At the same time, around 70 per cent of Europe's trucks are operated by transport companies with fewer than ten vehicles.

A recent example of this model is provided by DHL Freight: through its Partner Store Programme, logistics provider Pflaum gains access to 20 electric trucks from Juna, which are deployed for DHL transport operations. The agreement runs for five years and enables Pflaum to use the e-trucks without making its own investment in the vehicles. The 20 Juna trucks complement Pflaum's 45 own electric trucks; by the end of 2026, its e-fleet is set to grow to 65 vehicles.

Since the launch of its first vehicles, the Juna fleet has covered around 3.9 million kilometres, according to the company. Deployments range from cross-border transport to daily recurring shuttle services. Other customers include Nestlé and Beiersdorf, for which Juna supports regional logistics operations in Hamburg.

Juna was founded in 2023 by Scania and digital freight forwarder Sennder. The joint venture combines Scania's vehicle and technical expertise with Sennder's experience in digital transport networks. By the end of 2026, the fleet is expected to grow from the current approximately 110 to around 150 electric trucks.
