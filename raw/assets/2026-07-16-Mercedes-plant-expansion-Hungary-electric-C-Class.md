# Mercedes celebrates plant expansion in Hungary – and the production launch of the electric C-Class

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-07-14

---

Mercedes-Benz has inaugurated the expansion of its plant in Kecskemét, Hungary, and started production of the new all-electric C-Class there. The automaker has also officially confirmed that the compact G-Class will roll off the production line in Hungary in the future.

With the expansion, Kecskemét has become the largest Mercedes plant in Europe, with the site more than doubling from 200 to 440 hectares. Two new halls for bodywork and assembly, a second press shop, a new paint shop, and a battery assembly facility have been added, at a total investment of around one billion euros. Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar attended the opening.

Michael Schiebe, Mercedes-Benz board member for Production, Quality and Supply Chain Management, said the extension "increases the resilience and flexibility of our global production network" and enables competitive production of models like the GLB and the new electric C-Class.

Around 5,000 employees currently work at Kecskemét, which produces the combustion A-Class and the battery-electric GLB. With this expansion, the electric C-Class becomes the first larger "Core" volume-segment Mercedes model built there, including AMG derivatives with axial flux motors.

The electric C-Class had its world premiere in April as a lower-profile alternative to the GLC 400 4MATIC SUV, sharing the same MB.EA-M platform (800-volt system), a 94 kWh battery, 330 kW charging power, 360 kW drive power, and up to 762 km WLTP range.

Mercedes says key components, including body parts and drive batteries, are made on-site in Hungary to shorten supply chains. Kecskemét's new hall is dedicated exclusively to fully electric vehicles, and Mercedes plans flexible production links between Hungary and German plants — for example, the electric GLC could be built at both Bremen and Kecskemét. Automobilwoche has reported some GLC production could shift to Kecskemét if Mercedes moves the SUV from Bremen to its Tuscaloosa, US plant.

Mercedes also confirmed the upcoming compact G-Class will be exclusively manufactured in Kecskemét, based on the MMA platform for compact vehicles.

Mercedes hasn't officially disclosed the new capacity, but Automobilwoche reports it will roughly double to between 300,000 and 400,000 units, bringing Kecskemét to a 30% share of Mercedes' European production, up from 15%.
