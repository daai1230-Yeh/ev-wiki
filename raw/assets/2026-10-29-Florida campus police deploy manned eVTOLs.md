# Florida campus police deploy manned eVTOLs

**Author**: Cora Werwitzke | **Date**: 2026-10-28

---

The Florida Polytechnic University Police Department is the first law enforcement agency in the USA to integrate a manned electric aircraft into its operational service. The vertical take-off aircraft is "in the final phase of the approval process," according to the university.

The eVTOL named "Astro" from US manufacturer Jetson is being used as a patrol aircraft. It is still under review by the FAA for this specific use case, and approval is reportedly in its final stages. In the future it is expected to help monitor the campus and surrounding areas, access hard-to-reach locations, assist in clean-up efforts after storms, and deliver emergency supplies quickly.

The aircraft is the single-seat Jetson ONE: 2.7 x 1.6 x 1.22 metres with foldable wings, 115 kg total weight including lithium-ion batteries, roughly 20 minutes of flight time and a maximum speed of 102 km/h.

"Advanced air mobility is a technology on the rise, and we can either choose to be proactive or reactive as it develops," said Aaron Murray, the University's police chief. Jetson CEO Stéphan D'haene said the company prioritised the order despite a three-year waiting list, citing the use case beyond recreation.

Astro will also serve as a learning and research tool; the university introduced a new "Aerospace Engineering" degree programme this autumn. Florida was recently selected for the FAA's "eVTOL and Advanced Air Mobility Integration Pilot Program", and the nation's first dedicated air test track, the SunTrax facility in Auburndale operated by the Florida DOT, lies four miles away, with initial eVTOL tests set to begin by the end of this year.
